**中文:** 摘要

<span id="S004"></span>

**Original:** Efficient sampling from Bayesian posterior distributions in high dimensions remains a hard problem, despite advances in theory and algorithms. Multiple steps are usually required to draw unbiased samples, for example, iterations of an MCMC algorithm or integration steps in a diffusion-based amortised sampler. However, scalable one-step amortised inference remains a challenging open problem. In this work, we build upon recent progress in data-driven generative modelling to offer a possible solution for one-step amortised sampling. Our one-step implicit sampler (OSIS) uses short-time MCMC dynamics to define a local target for the evolution of an implicitly modelled distribution. In the asymptotic limit, the modelled distribution is a fixed point of the MCMC kernel and thus samples from the target. We analyse the convergence properties of OSIS and show connections with other generative modelling and sampling algorithms. We study various design choices and show that OSIS performs well compared to multi-step amortised samplers and non-amortised MCMC baselines while being far more efficient at sampling time.

**中文:** 尽管理论与算法已经取得进展，从高维贝叶斯后验分布中高效采样仍然是一个困难问题。通常需要多个步骤才能得到无偏样本，例如迭代执行 MCMC 算法，或在基于扩散的摊销采样器中进行数值积分。然而，可扩展的单步摊销推断仍是一个具有挑战性的开放问题。本文基于数据驱动生成建模的近期进展，提出一种实现单步摊销采样的可行方案。本文的单步隐式采样器（one-step implicit sampler，OSIS）利用短时 MCMC 动力学，为隐式建模分布的演化定义一个局部目标。在渐近极限下，所建模的分布是 MCMC 核的不动点，因此从该分布采样即可得到目标分布的样本。本文分析 OSIS 的收敛性质，并说明它与其他生成建模和采样算法之间的联系。本文还研究了若干设计选择，结果显示，OSIS 的表现良好，可与多步摊销采样器及非摊销 MCMC 基线相比，同时在采样时具有高得多的效率。

**Original:** The sampling problem. Many problems in physics, biology, statistics, and machine learning (Welling & Teh, 2011; Wirnsberger et al., 2020; Noé et al., 2019; Holdijk et al., 2023) require drawing samples from a distribution whose density is known up to a normalising constant. Formally, this means sampling from a distribution defined via an energy function E: R^d → R, with density

$$\pi(x)=e^{-E(x)}/Z,\qquad Z=\int_{\mathbb R^d}e^{-E(x)}\,dx.\tag{1}$$

**Original:** Problem setting: one-step amortised sampling. Suppose that $本地资料：mathbb R^{d_z}\to\mathbb R^d$ is a measurable function and $q$ is a simple distribution (e.g., $\mathcal N(0,I)$) over $\mathbb R^{d_z}$, where $d_z$ is not necessarily equal to $d$. Define a random variable $z\sim q$ and $x=G(z)$. The distribution of $x$ is the pushforward of $q$ by $G$, denoted $\rho=G_\#q$. If $G$ is not (efficiently) invertible or differentiable, the density of $\rho$ is intractable to compute. Given a target distribution $\pi$ over $\mathbb R^d$ defined by an unnormalised density as in (1), the goal of one-step amortised sampling is to find $G$ such that $\pi\approx G_\#q$. If the function $G$ depends on parameters $\theta$, we denote it $G_\theta$ and abbreviate $\rho_\theta=(G_\theta)_\#q$. The goal is then to find $\theta$ such that $\rho_\theta\approx\pi$.

$$K(y\mid x)=\alpha(x,y)q(y\mid x)+(1-A(x))\delta_x(y),\tag{52}$$

$$\alpha(x,y)=\min\left(1,\frac{\pi(y)q(x\mid y)}{\pi(x)q(y\mid x)}\right),\qquad A(x)=\int\alpha(x,y)q(y\mid x)\,dy.\tag{53}$$
