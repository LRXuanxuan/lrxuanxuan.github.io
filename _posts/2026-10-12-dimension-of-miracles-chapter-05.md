<h2>第五章</h2>
<p>“好吧，”奖品说，“这事算完了。希望我们以后再也见不到那个丑东西。卡莫迪，我们回你家去吧。”</p>
<p>“好主意。”卡莫迪说。“使者！我现在想回家！”</p>
<p>“这种感觉完全正常，”使者说，“而且非常符合现实。我也认为你应该回家，越快越好。”</p>
<p>“那就送我回去。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>使者摇摇头。“那不是我的工作。我只负责把你带到这里。”</p>
<p>“那是谁的工作？”</p>
<p>“是你的工作，卡莫迪。”办事员说。</p>
<p>卡莫迪心里一沉。他开始明白卡莫德刚才为什么那么轻易就放弃了。“听着，各位，我不想给你们添麻烦，可说真的，我需要一点帮助。”</p>
<p>“唉，好吧，”使者说。“把坐标给我，我亲自送你过去。”</p>
<p>“坐标？我根本不知道什么坐标。那颗行星叫地球。”</p>
<p>“它就算叫绿奶酪我也不在乎，”使者说。“我要帮你，就必须知道坐标。”</p>
<p>“可你刚刚才去过那里，”卡莫迪说。“你去了地球，把我带到了这里！”</p>
<p>“从你的角度看起来也许如此，”使者耐心地解释，“可事实根本不是那样。我只是前往办事员交给我的坐标；那些坐标来自抽奖计算机。然后你恰好在那里，于是我就把你带了回来。”</p>
<p>“那你不能把我送回同一个坐标吗？”</p>
<p>“当然可以，轻而易举。不过你到了那里什么也找不到。银河系可不是静止的。银河里的所有东西都在运动，每一样都有自己的速度和方式。”</p>
<p>“不能根据那些坐标算出地球现在会在哪里吗？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“我连一列数字都加不明白，”使者骄傲地说。“我的才华在别的方面。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪转向办事员。“那你能算吗？或者抽奖计算机能算吗？”</p>
<p>“我的加法也不怎么样。”办事员说。</p>
<p>计算机又窜回房间。“我加法算得极其出色，”它说。“但我的职能仅限于在允许的误差范围内选择并定位抽奖获胜者。我已经把你定位完毕——你就在这里——因此我被禁止承担‘找出你们行星当前坐标’这项有趣的理论工作。”</p>
<p>“就不能帮个忙吗？”卡莫迪恳求道。</p>
<p>“我的系统里没有‘帮忙’这一商数，”计算机回答。“我没法替你找行星，就像我不会煎鸡蛋，也不会把一颗新星三等分。”</p>
<p>“难道就没人能帮我？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“别绝望，”办事员说。“旅行者援助处眨眼就能替你搞定，我亲自带你去。你只要把‘家乡坐标’告诉他们。”</p>
<p>“可我不知道啊！”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>一阵短暂而震惊的沉默。</p>
<p>随后使者说：“你连自己家的地址都不知道，还指望别人怎么知道？这个银河也许不是无限大，可无论如何都大得很。任何不知道自己‘位置’的生物，就根本不该离开家。”</p>
<p>“我当时不知道还有这回事。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“你本来可以问。”</p>
<p>“我没想到……听着，你们必须帮我。查明我的行星在哪里，不可能困难到什么程度吧。”</p>
<p>“难得不可思议，”办事员告诉他。“‘在哪里’只是必需的三个坐标之一。”</p>
<p>“另外两个是什么？”</p>
<p>“我们还必须知道‘在何时’和‘是哪一个’。我们把它们叫作定位的三个 W：Where、When、Which。”</p>
<p>“你们就算把它们叫绿奶酪我也不在乎，”卡莫迪突然发火。“其他生命形态到底怎么找到回家的路？”</p>
<p>“它们利用与生俱来的归巢本能，”使者说。“顺便问一句，你确定自己没有吗？”</p>
<p>“我想没有。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“他当然没有归巢本能！”奖品愤愤不平地插嘴。“这家伙一辈子从来没离开过自己的母星！他上哪儿去发展归巢本能？”</p>
<p>“说得也是。”办事员疲惫地揉揉脸。“这就是跟低等生命打交道的后果。该死的计算机，还有它那些虔诚的错误！”</p>
<p>“五十亿次才一次，”计算机说。“这要求应该不算很过分吧。”</p>
<p>“没人怪你，”办事员说。“事实上谁也没怪谁。可我们还是得想出怎么处理他。”</p>
<p>“责任重大。”使者说。</p>
<p>“确实。”办事员同意。“要不我们把他杀掉，然后把整件事忘了？”</p>
<p>“喂！”卡莫迪叫道。</p>
<p>“我没意见。”使者说。</p>
<p>“如果你们都没意见，”计算机说，“那我也没意见。”</p>
<p>“别算上我，”奖品说。“我眼下说不清具体哪里不对，可这个主意整个就有问题。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪发表了一系列措辞强烈的声明，大意是自己不想死，也不该被杀。他呼吁对方唤起善良本性与公平竞赛精神。这些发言被裁定带有倾向性，从记录中删除。</p>
<p>“等等，我有办法了！”使者突然说。“作为替代方案，这样如何？我们不杀他；我们真心实意地、竭尽所能地帮助他活着回家，并且让他在精神和肉体两方面都保持健康。”</p>
<p>“倒是个思路。”办事员承认。</p>
<p>“这样一来，”使者说，“我们就能完成一项堪称典范、功德无量的善举；而且它明明注定徒劳，因此更加可贵。因为显而易见，他十有八九还是会在旅途中被杀掉。”</p>
<p>“那我们最好赶紧开始，”办事员说。“免得我们还在说话，他就先死了。”</p>
<p>“这到底是怎么回事？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“以后我再全告诉你，”奖品低声对他说。“前提是还有‘以后’。如果时间允许，我还会给你讲一个关于我自己的、相当迷人的故事。”</p>
<p>“准备好，卡莫迪！”使者大声喊。</p>
<p>“我准备好了，”卡莫迪说。“希望如此。”</p>
<p>“不管准备好没准备好——走你！”</p>
<p>于是，他走了。</p>
</section>
<section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="en" lang="en" aria-label="English original" hidden>
<p><b>CHAPTER 5</b></p>
<p>‘Well,’ the Prize said, ‘that’s <i>that.</i> I trust we have seen the last of that ugly creature. Carmody, let us go to your home.’</p>
<p>‘An excellent idea,’ Carmody said. ‘Messenger! I want to go home now!’</p>
<p>‘The feeling is quite normal,’ the Messenger said, ‘and also quite reality-oriented. I would say that you <i>should</i> go home, as rapidly as possible.’</p>
<p>‘So take me home,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>The Messenger shook his head. ‘That’s not my job. I am only supposed to bring you here.’</p>
<p>‘Whose job is it?’</p>
<p>‘It is your job, Carmody,’ the Clerk said.</p>
<p>Carmody experienced a sinking feeling. He was beginning to understand why Karmod had given up so easily. He said, ‘Look, fellows, I hate to impose on you, but really, I need some help.’</p>
<p>‘Oh, very well,’ the Messenger said. ‘Give me the co-ordinates and I’ll take you there myself.’</p>
<p>‘Co-ordinates? I don’t know anything about that. It’s a planet called Earth.’</p>
<p>‘I don’t care if it is called Green Cheese,’ the Messenger said. ‘I need to know the co-ordinates if I’m to be of any assistance.’</p>
<p>‘But you were just there,’ Carmody said. ‘You went to Earth and brought me here!’</p>
<p>‘So it may have appeared to you,’ the Messenger explained patiently. ‘But it is not the case at all, I simply went to the co-ordinates which were given me by the Clerk, who got them from the Sweepstakes Computer; and there you were, and I brought you here.’</p>
<p>‘Can’t you bring me back to the same co-ordinates?’</p>
<p>‘I can, with the greatest of ease. But you would find nothing there. The galaxy is not static, you know. Everything in the galaxy moves, each thing at its own rate and in its own manner.’</p>
<p>‘Can’t you figure out from the co-ordinates where Earth will be now?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘I can’t even add up a column of figures,’ the Messenger said proudly. ‘My talents lie in other directions.’</p>
<p>Carmody turned to the Clerk. ‘Then can you figure it out? Or can the Sweepstakes Computer?’</p>
<p>‘I can’t add very well, either,’ the Clerk said.</p>
<p>The Computer scuttled back into the room. ‘I can add magnificently,’ it said. ‘But my function is limited to selecting and locating the winners of the Sweepstakes within my margin of permissible error. I have you located (you are here) and therefore I am forbidden the interesting theoretical job of learning your planet’s present coordinates.’</p>
<p>‘Can’t you do it just as a favour?’ Carmody pleaded.</p>
<p>‘I have no quotient for favours,’ the Computer replied. ‘I can no more find your planet than I can fry an egg or trisect a nova.’</p>
<p>‘Can’t anyone help me?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Don’t despair,’ the Clerk said. ‘Travellers’ Aid can fix you up in a jiffy, and I’ll take you there myself. Just give them your Home Co-ordinates.’</p>
<p>‘But I don’t know them!’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>There was a short, shocked silence. Then the Messenger said, ‘If you don’t know your own address, how do you expect anyone else to know it? This galaxy may not be infinite, but it’s a pretty big place all the same. Any creature that doesn’t know its own Location should never leave home.’</p>
<p>‘I didn’t know that at the time,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘You might have asked.’</p>
<p>‘I didn’t think of it … Look, you have to help me. It can’t be too difficult to find out where my planet is.’</p>
<p>‘It’s incredibly difficult,’ the Clerk told him. ‘“Where” is only one of the three co-ordinates that are needed.’</p>
<p>‘What are the other two?’</p>
<p>‘We also need to know “When” and “Which.” We call them the three W’s of Location.’</p>
<p>‘I don’t care if you call them Green Cheese,’ Carmody said in a sudden burst of anger. ‘How do other life-forms find their way home?’</p>
<p>‘They utilize their inherent homing instinct,’ the Messenger said. ‘Are you sure you don’t have one, by the way?’</p>
<p>‘I don’t think so,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Of course he doesn’t have a homing instinct!’ the Prize burst out indignantly. ‘The fellow’s never been off his home planet! How would he develop a homing instinct?’</p>
<p>‘True enough,’ the Clerk said, and rubbed his face wearily. ‘This is what comes of dealing with lower life-forms. Damn that computer and his pious errors!’</p>
<p>‘Only one in five billion,’ the Computer said. ‘Surely that’s not asking very much.’</p>
<p>‘No one’s blaming you,’ the Clerk said. ‘No one’s blaming anyone, as a matter of fact. But we still have to figure out what to do with him.’</p>
<p>‘It’s a heavy responsibility,’ the Messenger said.</p>
<p>‘It certainly is,’ the Clerk agreed. ‘What do you say we kill him and forget the whole thing.’</p>
<p>‘Hey!’ Carmody cried.</p>
<p>‘It’s OK with me,’ the Messenger said.</p>
<p>‘If it’s OK with you fellows,’ the Computer said, ‘then it’s OK with me.’</p>
<p>‘Count me out,’ the Prize said. ‘I can’t put my finger on it at the moment, but there’s something wrong with the whole idea.’</p>
<p>Carmody made several vehement statements to the effect that he did not want to die and ought not to be killed. He appealed to their better instincts and their sense of fair play. These remarks were judged tendentious and were struck from the record.</p>
<p>‘Wait, I have it!’ the Messenger said suddenly. ‘As an alternative solution, what about this? Let’s <i>not</i> kill him; let’s help him, in utter sincerity and to the best of our abilities, to return to his home alive and in good health both mental and physical.’</p>
<p>‘It’s a thought,’ the Clerk admitted.</p>
<p>‘In that way,’ the Messenger said, ‘we can perform an exemplary action of the greatest merit, all the more noteworthy because it will be utterly futile. For obviously, he will probably be killed in the course of the trip anyhow.’</p>
<p>‘We’d better get on with it,’ the Clerk said. ‘Unless we want him to get killed while we’re talking.’</p>
<p>‘What is this all about?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘I’ll explain everything later,’ the Prize whispered to him. ‘Assuming that there is a later. And, if we have time, I’ll also tell you a rather fascinating story about myself.’</p>
<p>‘Get ready, Carmody!’ the Messenger called out.</p>
<p>‘I’m ready,’ Carmody said. ‘I hope.’</p>
<p>‘Ready or not, here you go!’</p>
<p>And he went.</p>
</section>
