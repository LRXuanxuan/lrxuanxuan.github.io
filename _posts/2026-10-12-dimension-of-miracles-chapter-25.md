<h2>第二十五章</h2>
<p>西斯赖特大概在传送时出了点小差错。短暂的一片空白之后，卡莫迪发现自己坐在一辆出租车后座。他身处一座非常像纽约的城市，而且似乎正处在一场谈话的中间。</p>
<p>“你刚才说啥？”司机问。</p>
<p>“我什么也没说。”卡莫迪回答。</p>
<p>“哦。我还以为你说了什么。好吧，我刚才是在说，那边就是新建的佛拉马里翁大厦。”</p>
<p>“我知道。”卡莫迪听见自己说道。“我参与修建的。”</p>
<p>“真的？好家伙！不过现在工程结束了，是吧？”</p>
<p>“对。”卡莫迪说。他把嘴里的香烟拿出来，皱眉看着它。“这些烟我也抽够了。”</p>
<p>他摇摇头，把香烟扔出窗外。</p>
<p>这些话和动作，对他的一部分——行动意识——来说完全自然；可他的另一部分——反思意识——却在一旁看得津津有味。</p>
<p>“嗨，你早说啊！”出租车司机说。“来，试试我的。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪看着司机伸过来的开口烟盒。“你抽 Kool，嗯？”</p>
<p>“我一直抽这个。”司机说。“Kool 有淡淡的薄荷清凉，味道就是这么对！”</p>
<p>卡莫迪挑起两边眉毛，以表示怀疑。尽管如此，他还是接过烟盒，抽出一根棺材钉似的香烟点燃。司机笑眯眯地从后视镜观察他。</p>
<p>卡莫迪吸了一口，脸上露出惊讶而愉快的表情，又慢慢、陶醉地吐出烟雾。</p>
<p>“嘿！”卡莫迪说。“你这玩意儿还真有点东西！”</p>
<p>司机睿智地点点头。“我们不少 Kool 烟民都这么觉得……好了，先生。华尔道夫-阿斯托里亚酒店到了。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪付钱，正要下车。司机仍然笑着，向后探过身子。</p>
<p>“嘿，先生，”他说，“我的 Kool 呢？”</p>
<p>“哦！”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>他把烟盒还回去。两人彼此微笑，然后出租车开走了，卡莫迪独自站在华尔道夫-阿斯托里亚门前。</p>
<p>他穿着一件结实的 Burberry 风衣。他之所以知道，是因为读到了商标——商标没有缝在领子里面，而是牢牢缝在右袖外侧。</p>
<p>现在仔细一看，他才发现自己身上所有标签都在外面。任何人一眼就能看出：他穿的是 Van Heusen 衬衫、Countess Mara 领带、Hart, Schaffner &amp; Marx 西装、Van Camp 袜子和 Lloyd &amp; Haig 科尔多瓦皮鞋。头上戴的是米兰 Raimu 制作的 Borsolino 礼帽。双手套着 L.L. Bean 的鹿皮手套。手腕上是一只自动上弦天文计时表（Audemars Piccard），集计算尺、计时器、累计时间指示器、日历和闹钟于一体；除此之外，它每年走时误差还保证不超过正负六秒。</p>
<p>最后，他身上还隐约散发着 Abercrombie &amp; Fitch 的 Oak Moss 男士古龙水味。</p>
<p>卡莫迪觉得，这套行头还算不错，不过绝对称不上一流。勉强过得去，但他对自己有更高要求。他很有上进心，打算继续往上爬，希望有朝一日成为那种不只在圣诞节才拿出芝华士威士忌招待客人的男人；穿 Brooks Brothers 衬衫，套 F. R. Tripler 西装外套，用 Lentheric 的 Onyx 须后水，再随手披上一件 Paul Stuart 的 Country Warmer 夹克……</p>
<p>可要买得起——更准确地说，要<strong>配得上使用</strong>——这种商品，他需要 A-AA-AAA 级消费者评级，而不是由于出生时一点小小的不幸，硬塞给他的普通 B-BB-AAAA 评级。</p>
<p>他需要那个评级！</p>
<p>难道他还不够好吗？妈的，他在斯坦福读书的时候，“消费技术”可是全班第一！过去三年，他的使用指数一直保持在第九十百分位！他的 Dodge Ferret 汽车永远一尘不染！类似的证据他还能举出一大堆。</p>
<p>为什么他们还不给他升级？</p>
<p>难道他们根本没有注意到他？</p>
<p>卡莫迪赶紧把这种异端思想赶出脑海。他眼下还有更紧迫的问题。今天，他面前有一项吃力不讨好的任务。接下来一小时里他必须做的事，很可能让他丢掉工作；一旦如此，他就会被贬入那些面无表情的无产阶级消费者行列，只配购买“不规则东方商品次品”（IOMS）。</p>
<p>时间还早，可他需要先给自己壮壮胆，才能迎接接下来的考验。于是他走进华尔道夫酒店的男士酒吧。</p>
<p>他抓住酒保的目光。还没等对方开口，卡莫迪便抢先说道：“嘿，朋友，再来一次。”</p>
<p>事实上，这名酒保此前压根没给他倒过酒，所以严格说来不可能“再来一次”。不过这并不重要。</p>
<p>“给你，老兄。”酒保笑着说。“Ballantine’s 啤酒，深度酿造的浓郁风味，味道就是这么对。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪知道，这句话本来应该由自己说。他走神了，让对方抢了先。</p>
<p>他若有所思地啜了一口啤酒。</p>
<p>“嘿，汤姆？”</p>
<p>卡莫迪转过身。那是新泽西州利奥尼亚的内特·斯廷，他的老朋友兼老邻居，正喝着可口可乐。</p>
<p>“说来也怪，”斯廷说，“你有没有注意过？有了 Coke，事事更顺心。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪这回一句广告词也没接上。他一口灌掉啤酒，对酒保喊：“嘿，朋友，再来一次！”</p>
<p>这招很蹩脚，但总比什么都不说强。</p>
<p>“最近怎么样？”他问斯廷。</p>
<p>“老婆去度假了。她决定去迈阿密，搭美国航空偷偷溜走一星期——阳光之旅第一选择。”</p>
<p>“那不错。”卡莫迪说。“我刚把海伦送去拿骚；如果你觉得从空中看巴哈马已经很美，那等你落地再瞧。你知道吗，前几天晚上我还问她，在我们这个高速运转的世界里，为什么还有人愿意花时间坐船横渡大西洋去欧洲？然后她说——”</p>
<p>“好主意。”斯廷打断他。</p>
<p>他当然完全有权这么做；那整套荷美邮轮广告实在太长，长到已经不够逼真。</p>
<p>“至于我嘛，我本来想把全家都送去万宝路乡野。”</p>
<p>“好主意。”卡莫迪说，“毕竟——”</p>
<p>“——万宝路，确实有太多值得喜欢的地方。”斯廷替他把句子说完。</p>
<p>这也是他的特权：广告是他先起的头。</p>
<p>“当然。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>他急忙把啤酒灌下去，又喊：“嘿，朋友，再来一次！Ballantine’s 啤酒！”</p>
<p>可他知道，自己根本没有尽到谈话中应尽的那一份责任。到底出了什么问题？此时此刻，这个具体场景明明存在一套<strong>规定好的对话</strong>。可他想不起来，怎么也找不到那句该说的话……</p>
<p>斯廷镇定自若，毛茸茸的腋窝里牢牢附着着全新改良冰蓝色 Secret 止汗剂，终于先抓到了正确台词。</p>
<p>“老婆们都不在家，”他笑道，“所以洗衣服就轮到咱们啦。”</p>
<p>又被抢先！</p>
<p>卡莫迪只能顺着往下演。</p>
<p>“对啊。”他说，干笑一声。“还记得以前那句‘我的衣服比你的白’吗？”</p>
<p>两个人一起发出轻蔑的笑声。</p>
<p>随后斯廷看看自己的衬衫，再看看卡莫迪的衬衫；皱眉、挑眉、张嘴，用脸依次表演出怀疑、难以置信和震惊。</p>
<p>“嘿！”斯廷说。“我的衬衫真的比你的白！”</p>
<p>“哎呀，还真是！”卡莫迪说，连看都懒得看。“这可怪了。我们用的是同一个型号的洗衣机，设定的洗涤程序也一样。而且漂白剂也是同一种……对吧？”</p>
<p>“我用的是高乐氏（Clorox）那玩意儿。”斯廷漫不经心地说。</p>
<p>“Clorox。”卡莫迪若有所思地重复。“对，肯定就是它！我的漂白剂太弱了！”</p>
<p>他装出懊恼的样子，斯廷则假装得意。卡莫迪本想再点一杯啤酒，可前两杯喝得一点也不开心。他决定，斯廷对这种游戏反应太快，自己不是对手。</p>
<p>卡莫迪用美国运通信用卡付了酒钱，然后继续前往办公室。他的办公室位于第五大道 666 号五十一层。他以一种民主而同志般的亲切态度和同事们打招呼。好几个人试图把他拖进各自的广告套路，他全都没有理会。</p>
<p>卡莫迪很清楚，从“人生位置”这个意义上说，他的处境已经岌岌可危。昨晚一整夜，他都在思考自己还有哪些选择。焦虑让他急性偏头痛发作，胃也不舒服，差点错过查尔斯顿舞比赛。不过他的妻子海伦——其实她根本没去度假——给了他一片阿尔卡-塞尔策（Alka-Seltzer），一下子就把他治好了。于是两人按计划参赛，并且多亏 Alka-Seltzer 拿到了一等奖。</p>
<p>可问题依然存在。</p>
<p>凌晨三点，海伦告诉他，小汤米和小廷克今年的蛀牙比去年少了百分之三十二。卡莫迪回答：“你知道吗……我敢打赌，是佳洁士（Crest）的功劳！”</p>
<p>可他说这话时一点真心也没有——尽管海伦主动给他递出这句台词，确实很体贴。</p>
<p>他知道，妻子再怎么给丈夫递广告词，也不足以真正改变什么。</p>
<p>如果你想提高消费者评级，如果你想证明自己配得上生命中真正重要的东西——比如缅因州“无羁荒野”深处由 Tech 建造的瑞士式木屋，一辆专供“自认卓尔不群之士”购买的 Porsche 911S，以及那种“无法忍受次等产品的人”才会使用的 Ampex……好吧，如果你想要这一类东西，你就必须证明自己<strong>值得拥有</strong>。</p>
<p>光有钱不够。</p>
<p>社会地位不够。</p>
<p>头脑简单地坚持不懈也不够。</p>
<p>你必须证明，自己真正属于那个为这些商品而存在的“卓尔不群的族类”。</p>
<p>你必须为了得到一切，冒险押上一切。</p>
<p>“见鬼！”卡莫迪对自己说，同时右拳重重砸进左掌，“我说了要做，就一定要做！”</p>
<p>于是他勇敢地走向老板于伯曼先生办公室的门，又勇敢地把门猛然推开。</p>
<p>房间里没人。</p>
<p>于伯曼先生还没到。</p>
<p>卡莫迪走进办公室。他会在这里等。下巴绷紧，嘴唇紧抿，眉心出现三道竖纹。他努力保持自制。</p>
<p>于伯曼随时都会出现。</p>
<p>等他进来，汤姆·卡莫迪就要对他说：</p>
<p>“于伯曼先生，您完全可以因为这句话开除我——可是您有口臭。”</p>
<p>然后他会停顿一下。</p>
<p>“<strong>口臭。</strong>”</p>
<p>想的时候多么简单，真正执行起来却多么困难！</p>
<p>然而，一个男人必须挺身而出，必须为清洁以及清洁的各种延伸概念而战，必须拼命争取向上晋升。</p>
<p>就在这一刻，卡莫迪知道，那些半是传说的伟大存在——<strong>厂商们</strong>——正在注视自己。</p>
<p>只要他们判定他值得……</p>
<p>“早上好，卡莫迪！”</p>
<p>于伯曼迈着两条长腿大步走进房间。他长着一张鹰隼般英俊的脸，两鬓夹杂灰白——那是特权的标志。他的玳瑁框眼镜比卡莫迪的足足宽三厘米。</p>
<p>“于伯曼先生，”卡莫迪声音发颤地开口，“您完全可以因为这句话开除我——”</p>
<p>“卡莫迪，”老板说道。他从横膈膜发出的雄浑声音穿透卡莫迪虚弱的胸腔音，就像 Personna 外科不锈钢刀片切开肥肉一样，“今天我发现了一种最不可思议的漱口水。叫 Scope。我相信，我的口气可以连续好几个小时保持清新。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪露出讽刺的微笑。</p>
<p>多么不可思议的巧合！老板居然碰巧发现了卡莫迪正准备推荐给他的那款漱口水。</p>
<p>而且有效！</p>
<p>于伯曼先生的口气再也不像暴雨过后的垃圾坑。现在已经甜美到适合接吻——当然，是给女孩子接吻；卡莫迪本人对那一套没兴趣。</p>
<p>“听说过吗？”于伯曼问。</p>
<p>没等回答，他便离开了办公室。</p>
<p>卡莫迪脸上的讽刺笑意更浓了。</p>
<p>他又失败了。</p>
<p>然而与此同时，他却清清楚楚感到一种失败带来的解脱。</p>
<p>高管级消费生活实在太折磨人，消耗大得不可思议。它确实适合某一类男人；可也许卡莫迪并不属于那一类。</p>
<p>假如刚才他成功了呢？</p>
<p>他几乎已经能感到，自己将多么依依不舍地放弃那些属于第五十八百分位消费者的旧物：Raleigh 香烟赠券、猪皮绒面帽、会发光的圣诞领带、Skai 材质的“Quick-Trip”高管商务包、KLH Model 24 立体声音响系统，尤其还有那件 Lakeland 顶级外套——进口、柔软、顺滑的新西兰仿麂皮剪羊毛制成，衣领和翻领还特别做了框饰。</p>
<p>而且，其他所有亲切熟悉的东西也都必须处理掉。</p>
<p>“有时候，事情看着像出了岔子，最后反而恰到好处。”卡莫迪对自己说。</p>
<p>“是吗？你到底他妈在说什么？”卡莫迪自己回答自己。</p>
<p>“我的天。”卡莫迪对自己说。</p>
<p>“对啊。”卡莫迪的自我回答卡莫迪。“适应得稍微有点太快了，是不是？”</p>
<p>两个卡莫迪互相看了看，交换了一下情况，达成结论。</p>
<p>随后重新合为一体。</p>
<p>“西斯赖特！”卡莫迪大喊。“把我弄出去！”</p>
<p>而忠实可靠的西斯赖特立刻照办了。</p>
</section>

<p><b>CHAPTER 25</b></p>
<p>Seethwright must have mishandled the transition, for, after a brief blank spell, Carmody found himself in the back seat of a taxi. He was in a city very much like New York, and he seemed to be in the middle of a conversation.</p>
<p>‘What didja say?’ the driver asked.</p>
<p>‘I didn’t say anything,’ Carmody replied.</p>
<p>‘Oh. I thought you were saying something. Well, what <i>I</i> was saying is, I was saying that’s the new Flammarion building over there.’</p>
<p>‘I know,’ Carmody heard himself say. ‘I helped build it.’</p>
<p>‘Is that a fact? Some job! But now you’re finished, huh?’</p>
<p>‘Yes,’ Carmody said. He took the cigarette out of his mouth and frowned at it. ‘I’m finished with these cigarettes, too.’ He shook his head and threw the cigarette out of the window. These words and actions seemed perfectly natural to one part of him (the active consciousness). But another part of him (the reflective consciousness) was watching with considerable amusement.</p>
<p>‘Well, why didn’t you say so?’ the cabby said. ‘Here, try one of mine.’</p>
<p>Carmody looked at the open pack in the driver’s hand. ‘You smoke Kools, eh?’</p>
<p>‘It’s my regular smoke,’ the cabby said. ‘Kools have that light touch of menthol and the taste that’s right!’</p>
<p>Carmody raised both eyebrows to show disbelief. Nevertheless, he accepted the pack, extracted a coffin nail and lit up. The smiling cabby was watching him in the rearview mirror. Carmody inhaled, looked surprised and pleased, exhaled slowly and luxuriously.</p>
<p>‘Hey!’ Carmody said. ‘You got something there!’</p>
<p>The driver nodded sagely. ‘A lot of us Kool smokers think so … Here we are, sir. The Waldorf-Astoria.’</p>
<p>Carmody paid and began to step out. The cabby leaned back, still smiling. ‘Hey, mister,’ he said. ‘How about my Kools?’</p>
<p>‘Oh!’ Carmody said. He gave back the pack. He and the cabby smiled at each other. Then the cabby drove off and Carmody stood in front of the Waldorf-Astoria.</p>
<p>He was wearing a sturdy Burberry topcoat. He could tell this by reading the label, which, instead of being inside the collar, was sewn securely to the outside of his right sleeve. Now that he looked, he saw that all his labels were outside: anyone could tell that he had on a Van Heusen shirt, a Countess Mara necktie, a Hart, Schaffner &amp; Marx suit, Van Camp socks, and Lloyd &amp; Haig cordovans. Upon his head was a Borsolino made by Raimu of Milan. His hands were encased in deerskin gloves from L.L. Bean. His wrist was covered by a self-winding chronometer (Audemars Piccard) which had a slide rule, a timer, an elapsed-time indicator, a calendar, and an alarm; all this in addition to keeping time within a guaranteed accuracy of plus or minus six seconds a year.</p>
<p>Finally, he smelled faintly of Oak Moss men’s cologne from Abercrombie &amp; Fitch.</p>
<p>He considered it a <i>fairly good</i> outfit, though by no means first-rate. It would pass muster, but he expected more of himself. He was ambitious, he planned to move up, he expected to become the sort of man who serves Chivas Regal on days other than Christmas, wears Brooks Brothers shirts, blazers from F. R. Tripler, uses Onyx after-shave lotion by Lentheric, slips into Country Warmer jackets by Paul Stuart …</p>
<p>But he would need a class A-AA-AAA Consumer Rating for goods like those, instead of his commonplace B-BB-AAAA which a mishap of birth had stuck him with. He <i>needed</i> that rating! Wasn’t he good enough? Why, damn it, at Stanford he’d been first in his class in Consumer Techniques! His Use-Index for three years now had been in the ninetieth percentile! His car, a Dodge Ferret, was immaculate! He could cite other examples.</p>
<p>Why hadn’t they moved him up?</p>
<p>Was it possible that they did not have their eye on him?</p>
<p>Carmody quickly put such heretical thoughts out of his mind. He had more immediate concerns. Today he had a thankless task before him. What he had to do in the next hour might well cost him his job, in which case he would be relegated to the empty-faced ranks of the proletarian users of Irregular Oriental Merchandise Seconds (IOMS).</p>
<p>It was still early, but he needed fortification for the ordeal ahead. He walked into the Men’s Bar of the Waldorf.</p>
<p>He caught the bartender’s eye. Quickly, before the man could speak, Carmody said, ‘Hey, friend, do it again.’ The fact that the man had not done it for him previously, and therefore, technically, could not do it again, was of no significance.</p>
<p>‘Here you go, Mac,’ the bartender said, smiling. ‘Ballantine’s got the deep-brewed flavour and the taste that’s right.’</p>
<p>Carmody knew that he should have said that himself. He had been caught napping. He sipped his beer thoughtfully.</p>
<p>‘Hey, Tom?’</p>
<p>Carmody turned around. There was Nate Steen from Leonia, New Jersey, an old friend and neighbour, drinking a Coke. ‘It’s funny,’ Steen said, ‘but did you ever notice? Things go better with Coke.’</p>
<p>Carmody was caught without a line. He drained off his beer at a gulp and called to the bartender, ‘Hey, friend, do it again!’ It was a poor expedient, but better than nothing at all. ‘What’s new?’ he asked Steen.</p>
<p>‘Wife’s gone on vacation. She decided to come on down to Miami and sneak a week via American Airways, number one to the sun.’</p>
<p>‘That’s great,’ Carmody said. ‘I just sent Helen to Nassau; and if you think the Bahamas are lovely from the air, wait until you land. And do you know, I was asking her just the other night why, in this fast-moving world of ours, would anyone want to take the time for an ocean voyage to Europe? And she said –’</p>
<p>‘Nice idea,’ Steen interrupted. He had a perfect right, of course; the Holland-American bit was entirely too long for verisimilitude. ‘Now me, I thought I’d pack us all off to Marlboro Country.’</p>
<p>‘Fine thought,’ Carmody said, ‘after all –’</p>
<p>‘– you really <i>do</i> get a lot to like in a Marlboro,’ Steen finished (his privilege: he had begun the plug).</p>
<p>‘Sure,’ Carmody said. Hastily he slopped down his beer and called out, ‘Hey, friend, do it again! Ballantine beer!’ But he knew that he wasn’t holding up his end. What on earth was wrong with him? For this very moment, this particular situation, there was an obligatory dialogue. But he couldn’t remember, he couldn’t seem to find it …</p>
<p>Steen, calm and collected with new improved ice-blue Secret clinging to his hairy armpits, came to it first. ‘With our wives away,’ he chuckled, ‘we get to do the wash.’</p>
<p>Beaten to the punch! Carmody could do no more than to string along. ‘Yeh,’ he said. He gave a hollow laugh. ‘Remember that stuff about “my wash is whiter than yours”?’</p>
<p>Both men indulged in scornful laughter. Then Steen looked at his shirt, looked at Carmody’s shirt, frowned, raised his eyebrows, opened his mouth, portrayed disbelief, incredulity, amazement.</p>
<p>‘Hey!’ Steen said. ‘My shirt <i>is</i> whiter than yours!’</p>
<p>‘Gee, so it is!’ Carmody said, not bothering to look. ‘That’s funny. We used the same model of washing machine set for the same cycle. And we also used the same bleach … didn’t we?’</p>
<p>‘I used that Clorox stuff,’ Steen said carelessly.</p>
<p>‘Clorox,’ Carmody said thoughtfully. ‘Yeah, that’s gotta be it! <i>My bleach was too weak!</i>’</p>
<p>He portrayed mock exasperation while Steen feigned triumph. Carmody thought of ordering another beer, but he hadn’t enjoyed the last two. He decided that Steen was too quick for him.</p>
<p>Carmody paid for the beers with his American Express credit card and then continued to his office, which was on the fifty-first floor of 666 Fifth Avenue. He greeted his fellow workers with democratic camaraderie. Several people tried to involve him in their gambits, but he ignored them. Carmody knew that his situation, life-positionwise, was desperate. He had thought about his alternatives all last night. Worry had brought on an acute migraine and an upset stomach, and he had almost missed the Charleston contest. But his wife, Helen (who hadn’t really gone on vacation), had given him an Alka-Seltzer, which had fixed him up in a jiffy, and they had gone as planned and had taken first prize thanks to Alka-Seltzer. But the problem had remained. And when Helen told him, at three in the morning, that Tommy and little Tinker had had 32 per cent fewer cavities this year over last, he had replied, ‘Do you know … I’ll bet it’s the Crest!’ But his heart hadn’t been in it, although it had been sweet of Helen to feed him the line.</p>
<p>He knew that no wife could feed her husband enough lines to make any real difference. If you wanted to advance in the Consumer Ratings, if you wanted to show yourself worthy of the things that counted in life – a Tech-built Swiss-type chalet deep in the Untrammelled Wilderness of Maine, for example, and a Porsche 911S, which people who considered themselves a breed apart purchased, and an Ampex for people who couldn’t be bothered with anything less than the best … well, if you wanted that sort of thing, you had to deserve it. Money wasn’t enough, social position wasn’t enough, simple-minded perseverance wasn’t enough. You had to prove that you really <i>were</i> of that Breed Apart for whom those goods were intended. You had to risk everything in order to gain everything.</p>
<p>‘By jingo!’ Carmody said to himself, striking his right fist into the palm of his left hand, ‘I said I’d do it and I will do it!’ And he boldly advanced to the door of Mr Übermann, his boss, and boldy threw open the door.</p>
<p>The room was empty. Mr Übermann had not arrived yet.</p>
<p>Carmody entered the office. He would wait. His jaw was tight, his lips were compressed, and three vertical lines had appeared between his eyes. He fought to keep himself under control. Übermann would be here any moment. And when he came, Tom Carmody would say to him, ‘Mr Übermann, you could have me fired for this but you’ve got bad breath.’ He would pause, ‘<i>Bad breath.</i>’</p>
<p>How simple it seemed in contemplation, how difficult in execution! Yet still, a man must stand up, must fight for cleanliness and its extensions, must scramble for advancement. At this very moment, Carmody knew, the eyes of those half-legendary figures, the Manufacturers, were on him. If he were found worthy …</p>
<p>‘Morning, Carmody!’ said Übermann, striding long-legged into the room! He was hawk-faced and handsome; his temples were streaked with grey, a mark of privilege. His horn-rimmed glasses were a full three centimetres wider than Carmody’s.</p>
<p>‘Mr Übermann,’ Carmody began in a quavering voice, ‘you could have me fired for this –’</p>
<p>‘Carmody,’ the boss said, his diaphragmatic voice cutting through Carmody’s weak chest tones as a Personna surgical-steel blade cuts through flab, ‘today I have discovered the most amazing mouthwash. Scope, it is called. I believe my breath will be sweet for hours and hours.’</p>
<p>Carmody gave an ironic smile. What a fantastic coincidence! The boss had lucked into <i>the very mouthwash</i> that Carmody had been about to recommend. And it had worked! No longer did Mr Übermann’s breath smell like a rubbish pit after a heavy rain. Now it was kissing-sweet (for girls, of course; Carmody himself was not interested in that sort of thing).</p>
<p>‘Ever hear of it?’ Übermann asked, and then left the office without waiting for an answer.</p>
<p>Carmody smiled even more ironically. He had failed again. And yet, he could feel an unmistakable sense of relief at the failure. Executive consumption was terribly trying, fantastically wearing. It was proper for a certain kind of man; but perhaps he was not that kind. Suppose he had made it? He could sense even now the regrets with which he would have given up his fifty-eighth-percentile consumption artifacts – his Raleigh coupons, his pigskin suede cap, his light-up Christmas tie, his Executive ‘Quick-Trip’ Business Case made of Skai, his KLH Model 24 stereo music system, and particularly his Lakeland top-of-the-line coat of imported, soft, supple New Zealand Sueded shearling with the framed collar and lapels. And he would have had to dispose of all the rest of his dear, familiar objects as well.</p>
<p>‘Sometimes things just work out right even when you think they’re going wrong,’ Carmody said to himself.</p>
<p>‘They do? Just what in hell are you talking about?’ Carmody replied to himself.</p>
<p>‘Oh my God,’ Carmody said to himself.</p>
<p>‘Yeah,’ Carmody’s self answered Carmody. ‘Acclimatized a little too quickly, didn’t you?’</p>
<p>The two Carmodys looked at each other, compared notes and reached a conclusion. They coalesced.</p>
<p>‘Seethwright!’ Carmody shouted. ‘Get me out of here!’</p>
<p>And Seethwright, that faithful man, did just that.</p>
