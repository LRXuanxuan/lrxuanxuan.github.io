<h2>第十七章</h2>
<p>首先，也是最重要的，莫兹利给卡莫迪讲起捕食者：它们的风俗习惯、行为规范、习性与反应，以及各种手段和办法。卡莫迪必须弄清楚，刚才究竟发生了什么，又为什么会发生——哪怕这些知识来得比事件本身晚了一步。</p>
<p>“尤其是来得比事件晚一步的时候。”奖品插嘴说。</p>
<p>莫兹利继续解释：正如每个男人都能找到一个女人一样，每一种活着的生物也都有自己的捕食者。“伟大的进食之链”——这是对宇宙中处于动态状态的全部生命所作的一种诗意描绘——必须持续运转，哪怕仅仅出于内在必然性。我们所知道的生命包含创造；而没有死亡，创造便不可想象。因此——</p>
<p>“为什么没有死亡就不能想象创造？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“别问蠢问题。我说到哪儿了？哦，对。因此，杀戮是正当的，尽管伴随杀戮而来的某些现象不那么容易让人接受。一个生活在自然栖息地里的生物，会以某些其他生物为食，同时又被另外一些生物取食。通常，这个过程自然、简单而且保持着极其精细的平衡，以至于捕食者和被捕食者往往很长时间都懒得去想它，而会把注意力放在别的事情上——创作艺术品、收集花生、沉思绝对者，或者某个物种碰巧觉得有趣的任何事情。这也正合适，因为‘自然’——我们不妨把她人格化成一位穿赤褐色和黑色衣服的老太太——并不喜欢自己的规章制度成为每一场鸡尾酒会、每一个蜂拥巢穴、每一次秘密集会，或者随便什么场合的谈资。</p>
<p>“可是你，卡莫迪，你无意中逃出了自己母星原有的制衡体系，却仍然没有逃出不可抗拒的‘过程法则’。因此，假如在遥远太空里原本不存在适合你的捕食者，那就必须给你找一个；如果找不到，就必须给你造一个。”</p>
<p>“好吧，是这么回事。”卡莫迪说。“可是那艘飞船，那些人——”</p>
<p>“——并不是它们看起来的东西。”莫兹利告诉他。“这一点现在对你应该很明显了。”</p>
<p>“现在是明显了。”</p>
<p>“严格说来，‘它们’其实是‘它’——一个单一实体，一种专门为你创造出来的生物，卡莫迪。它就是你的捕食者，而且它几乎完全按照那套简单而标准的捕食法则行动。”</p>
<p>“什么法则？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“是啊，‘什么法则’。”奖品叹了口气。“说得多么漂亮！我们可以对命运和世界大声咆哮，可到最后，仍然只能面对一个赤裸裸的命题：存在的东西，就是这些东西。”</p>
<p>“我刚才不是在评论。”卡莫迪说。“我是在问问题。那些捕食法则到底是什么？”</p>
<p>“哦，抱歉，我误会你了。”奖品说。</p>
<p>“没关系。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“谢谢。”奖品说。</p>
<p>“不客气。”卡莫迪说。“我不是想——不，我就是想问！那些简单而标准的捕食法则究竟是什么？”</p>
<p>“你非得问吗？”莫兹利说。</p>
<p>“恐怕是的。”</p>
<p>“当你把它以问题形式提出来，”莫兹利严厉地说，“捕食立刻就不再简单，也不再标准，甚至它作为‘法则’的地位都开始可疑。关于捕食的知识，是所有生物与生俱来的，就像手臂、腿和脑袋一样——甚至比这些还更确定。你看，它比任何科学定律都更加根本，因此不适合接受那种简单粗暴的还原。仅仅把这种问题问出口，本身就已经严重限制了答案可能是什么。”</p>
<p>“可我还是觉得，我应该尽可能知道关于捕食的一切。”卡莫迪说。“尤其是关于我自己的那个。”</p>
<p>“对，你确实应该知道。”莫兹利说。“或者更准确地说，你本来就应该知道——这和‘现在才知道’绝不是一回事！不过算了，我尽量讲。”</p>
<p>莫兹利用力揉了揉额头，然后宣布：“你吃，因此你也被吃。这个你已经知道。可你究竟会怎样被吃？你会怎样被诱捕、抓住、固定、处理？最后上桌时，你会热气腾腾，还是冰镇得恰到好处，又或者保持室温？显然，这取决于吃掉你的那个东西的口味。你的捕食者会不会从一个方便的高处跳下来，扑向你毫无防备的后背？会不会替你挖一个坑，织一张网，向你发起单打独斗的挑战，或者伸着利爪从天空俯冲而下？这取决于捕食者的本性，而本性决定它的形态与功能。与此同时，它的本性又受到你自身本性的限制，并对你的本性作出响应；而你的本性和它的一样，都受到自由意志的塑造，所以归根结底不可测知。</p>
<p>“再说具体一点。俯冲、挖坑或者织网都很直接，可面对具有记忆能力的生物，这些办法会失去效力。像你这样的生物，卡莫迪，只要成功躲过一次简单直接的致命袭击，以后也许就再也不会被同一种把戏骗到。</p>
<p>“不过，直来直去从来不是自然的作风。有人说，自然在幻象这件事上拥有既得利益，因为幻象正是通往死亡与出生的高速公路。反正我不会和这个命题争辩。只要接受这个概念，我们就会发现：你的捕食者若想诱捕像你这样复杂的生物，就必须实施复杂的机动。</p>
<p>“而且问题还有另一面。你的捕食者并不是纯粹为了吃你才被创造出来。没错，你是它生命里最重要的单一事物；但它和你一样拥有自由意志，因此并不会被严格限制在‘进食功能’那套逻辑里。谷仓里的老鼠也许会以为，椽子上的猫头鹰之所以被构想、被塑造成那副模样，唯一目的就是捕猎老鼠。可我们知道，猫头鹰脑子里还有好几件别的事情。所有捕食者都是这样，你那个也不例外。由此我们可以得到一个重要结论：一切捕食者都因为拥有自由意志，而在功能上必然是不完美的。”</p>
<p>“我以前从没这么想过。”卡莫迪说。“这对我有帮助吗？”</p>
<p>“嗯，其实没有。不过我还是觉得你应该知道。实际上，你也许永远都无法利用自己捕食者的缺陷。甚至你可能永远也弄不清那些缺陷是什么。在这种处境下，你和谷仓里的老鼠并没有区别。听到翅膀呼呼作响时，你也许能找到一个洞钻进去，却永远没法分析那只猫头鹰的本性、才能和局限。”</p>
<p>“太好了。”卡莫迪用沉重的讽刺口气说。“还没开始，我就已经输了。或者借用你们的术语：虽然现在还没有谁把叉子插进我身体，我也已经跟被吃掉差不多了。”</p>
<p>“别发脾气，别发脾气。”奖品劝道。“事情还没坏到那个程度。”</p>
<p>“那到底坏到什么程度？你们两个能不能告诉我一点真正有用的东西？”</p>
<p>“我们正在努力。”莫兹利说。</p>
<p>“那就告诉我，这个捕食者到底长什么样。”</p>
<p>莫兹利摇头。“这完全不可能。你觉得有哪个猎物能够知道自己的捕食者长什么样？如果可以，猎物岂不是就永生了！”</p>
<p>“那违反规则。”奖品插嘴说。</p>
<p>“至少给我个大概。”卡莫迪说。“它是不是总伪装成宇宙飞船到处跑？”</p>
<p>“当然不是。”莫兹利说。“从你的角度看，它是个变形者。你听说过老鼠自己走进蛇嘴里吗？苍蝇自己落到青蛙舌头上吗？小鹿自己走到老虎两只前爪之间吗？这才正是捕食的精髓！你必须问问自己：那些受到迷惑的猎物以为自己正走向哪里？它们以为眼前是什么？同样，你也应该问问自己：当你和捕食者的三根‘手指’聊天，又一路跟着它们径直走进那张嘴时，你眼前真正存在的究竟是什么！”</p>
<p>“它们看起来就像人。”卡莫迪说。“可我还是不知道捕食者自己到底长什么样。”</p>
<p>“我没有任何办法让你明白。”莫兹利说。“关于捕食者的信息很难获得。它们和你自身之间的互补关系太强。它设置的陷阱与伪装，都是建立在你自己的记忆、梦境、幻想、希望与欲望上。就像你刚才亲眼看见的那样，捕食者拿出你最珍爱的戏剧，再亲自演给你看。想认识你的捕食者，你就必须先认识你自己。而认识整个宇宙，都比认识自己更容易。”</p>
<p>“那我能怎么办？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“学习！”莫兹利说。“永远保持警惕，以最高速度行动，什么都别信，谁都别信。在抵达自己的家以前，别想放松。”</p>
<p>“家！”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“是的。回到自己的行星以后，你就安全了。你的捕食者不能进入你的巢穴。你当然仍然会遭受一切普通灾难，不过至少能免掉这个。”</p>
<p>“你能把我送回家吗？”卡莫迪问。“你说过你正在造一台机器。”</p>
<p>“我已经造好了。”莫兹利说。“不过你必须理解它的局限；那些局限是我自身局限的伴生物。我的机器只能把你送到地球<strong>现在所在的何处（Where）</strong>，除此以外什么都做不了。”</p>
<p>“可这不就是我需要的吗！”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“不，不是。‘何处’只能给你定位的第一个W。你仍然必须解出<strong>何时（When）</strong>以及<strong>哪一个（Which）</strong>。我的建议是按顺序来：先处理时间性，再处理特殊性——用一句常见的话说，就是先问何时，再问哪一个。你必须立刻离开这里；那个捕食者已经被你愚蠢地勾起了食欲，随时可能回来。这一次，我未必还能那么幸运地把你救出来。”</p>
<p>“你刚才是怎么把我从它嘴里弄出来的？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“我仓促做了一个诱饵。”莫兹利告诉他。“看起来和你一模一样，只不过我把它做得比真人略大一点，还多给了它一点生命活力。捕食者把你吐出来，流着口水扑向诱饵。不过这招不能再用第二次。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪宁愿不去问那个诱饵有没有感到疼痛。“我准备好了。”他说。“可我究竟要去哪里？接下来会发生什么？”</p>
<p>“你会前往一个地球——几乎可以肯定，是错误的那个。不过我会写信给一个认识的人；他非常擅长解决时间问题。如果他愿意接你的案子，就会来找你。再之后……谁说得准？卡莫迪，发生什么就接着什么吧；只要还有事情愿意发生，你就该心怀感激。”</p>
<p>“我确实很感激。”卡莫迪说。“不管最后结果怎样，我都非常感谢你。”</p>
<p>“没什么。”莫兹利说。“如果你最后真能回家，别忘了替我给那位老先生捎口信。都准备好了？机器就在我旁边。我没来得及把它做成可见，不过它看上去几乎和一台真力时牌电池短波收音机一模一样。见鬼，它跑哪儿去了？啊，在这儿。奖品带好了吗？”</p>
<p>“我带着他呢。”奖品说，两只手牢牢抓住卡莫迪的左臂。</p>
<p>“那就准备出发。我先调这个旋钮，再调这个，然后是这边这两个……卡莫迪，离开宏观宇宙，重新回到某颗行星上，你会觉得很舒服，哪怕那不是你的行星。从性质上说，原子、行星、银河系或者宇宙之间当然没有区别；问题只在于你最习惯生活在哪一种尺度上。现在，我按下这个——”</p>
<p>砰！啪！喀——嚓！慢叠化，快叠化，重叠溶解；电子音乐表示外太空，外太空表示电子音乐。日历纸一页页翻飞，卡莫迪在模拟失重中头朝下翻滚。定音鼓奏出不祥的音符，不祥的音符奏响定音鼓；鲜艳色彩猛然闪烁；一个女人的声音在回声室里哀号；孩子们发出笑声；一组被灯光照得像行星的雅法橙蒙太奇；一幅被照得像溪流涟漪的太阳系拼贴画。磁带减速，磁带加速；淡出，淡入。</p>
<p>这趟旅程真他妈够呛，不过没有任何事情超出卡莫迪的预料。</p>

<p><b>CHAPTER 17</b></p>
<p>First and foremost, Maudsley talked about predators, their folkways and mores, habits and reactions, ways and means. It was important for Carmody to know just what had happened to him, and why, even if that knowledge came subsequent to the event.</p>
<p>‘<i>Especially</i> if it comes after the event,’ the Prize put in.</p>
<p>Maudsley went on to say that, just as for every man there’s a woman, so for every living organism there’s a predator. The Great Chain of Eating (a poetic image for the totality of life in a state of dynamism in the Universe) must go on, for reasons of inner necessity if for nothing else. Life as we know it involves creation; and creation is inconceivable without death. Thus –</p>
<p>‘Why is creation inconceivable without death?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Don’t ask stupid questions. Where was I? Oh, yes. Thus murder is justified, though some of its concomitants are less readily appreciated. A creature in its natural habitat lives off certain other creatures and is lived off by still other creatures. This process is usually so natural and simple, and in so fine a state of balance, that preyers and preyed-upon alike tend to ignore it for great stretches of time, putting their attention instead upon the creation, of art objects, the gathering of groundnuts, the contemplation of the Absolute, or whatever else the species finds of interest. And that is as it should be, because Nature (whom we may personify as an old lady dressed in russet and black) does not like to find her rules and regulations the subject of every cocktail party, swarming nest, Konklave, or what you will. But you, Carmody, by inadvertently escaping the checks and balances of your native planet, still have not escaped the inexorable Law of Process. Thus, if there were no existing predator for you in the farflung reaches of space, then one would have to be found. If one could not be found, then it would have to be created.’</p>
<p>‘Well, yeah,’ Carmody said. ‘But that spaceship, those people –’</p>
<p>‘– were not what they seemed,’ Maudsley told him. ‘That must be evident to you.’</p>
<p>‘It is now.’</p>
<p>‘<i>They</i> were actually <i>it,</i> a single entity, a creature created especially for you, Carmody. <i>It</i> was your predator, and it followed almost classically the simple, standard Laws of Predation.’</p>
<p>‘Which are?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Yes, which are,’ the Prize sighed. ‘How nicely you put it! We may rant against fortune and the world, but we are left at the end with the stark proposition: <i>These are the things which are.</i>’</p>
<p>‘I wasn’t commenting,’ Carmody said, ‘I was asking. What are these Laws of Predation?’</p>
<p>‘Oh, sorry, I misunderstood you,’ the Prize said.</p>
<p>‘That’s quite all right,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Thank you,’ the Prize said.</p>
<p>‘It’s nothing,’ Carmody said. ‘I didn’t mean … No, I did mean! What are these simple, standard Laws of Predation?’</p>
<p>‘Must you ask?’ Maudsley said.</p>
<p>‘Yes, I’m afraid I must.’</p>
<p>‘When you put it in the form of a question,’ Maudsley said severely, ‘predation ceases to be simple and standard, and even its status as a law becomes dubious. Knowledge of predation is inherent in all organisms, just like arms and legs and heads, but even more certain. It is much more basic than a law of science, you see, and therefore not subject to simplistic reductionism. The mere asking of that sort of question imposes a severe restriction upon the answer.’</p>
<p>‘Still, I think I should know everything possible about predation,’ Carmody said. ‘Particularly mine.’</p>
<p>‘Yes, definitely you should know,’ Maudsley said. ‘Or rather, you <i>should have known,</i> which is by no means the same thing! Still, I’ll try.’</p>
<p>Maudsley rubbed his forehead vigorously and stated: ‘You eat, therefore you are eaten. That much you know. But <i>how,</i> precisely, are you to be eaten? How are you to be trapped, seized, immobilized, and prepared? Will you be served up piping hot or nicely chilled or at room temperature? Obviously, that depends on the tastes of that which feasts upon you. Shall your predator leap at your undefended back from a convenient height? Shall it dig a pit for you, or spin a web, or challenge you to single combat, or dive upon you with outstretched talons? That depends upon your predator’s nature, which determines his form and function. That nature is limited by and respondent to the exigencies of your own nature, which, like his, is informed by free will and thus ultimately unfathomable.</p>
<p>‘Now to particulars. Diving, digging or spinning are straightforward, but they lose effectiveness against a creature with the faculty of memory. A creature like youself, Carmody, could you avoid the simplistic deadly attack once, might never be deceived again.</p>
<p>‘Straightforwardness is not Nature’s way, however. It has been said that Nature has a vested interest in illusions, which are highways to death and birth. I for one will not argue the proposition. If we accept the concept, we see that your predator must engage in complex manoeuvres in order to snare a complex creature such as yourself.</p>
<p>‘There is another side to the problem also. Your predator was not conceived solely in order to eat you. You are the single most important thing in his life, granted; but he does possess free will, just as you do, and therefore is not limited to the strict logic of his eating function. Barn mice may think that the owl in the rafters was conceived and delineated for the sole purpose of hunting mice. But we know that the owl has several other things on his mind. This is how it is with all predators, including yours. From this, we draw an important conclusion: that all predators are functionally imperfect by virtue of their free will.’</p>
<p>‘I never thought of it that way before,’ Carmody said. ‘Does that help me?’</p>
<p>‘Well, not really. But I thought you should know it anyhow. You see, practically speaking, you may never be able to exploit your predator’s imperfections. Indeed, you may never even learn what they are. In this situation you are just like the barn mice. You may find a hole to scamper in when you hear the whirr of wings, but you will never be able to analyse the nature, talents, and limitations of the owl.’</p>
<p>‘Well, that’s just great,’ Carmody said, with heavy sarcasm. ‘I’m licked before I start. Or, to use your terminology, I’m as good as eaten even though nobody’s stuck a fork in me yet.’</p>
<p>‘Temper, temper,’ the Prize cautioned. ‘It isn’t quite as bad as that.’</p>
<p>‘So how bad is it? Can either of you tell me anything useful?’</p>
<p>‘That’s what we’re trying to do,’ Maudsley said.</p>
<p>‘Then tell me what this predator looks like.’</p>
<p>Maudsley shook his head. ‘That is quite impossible. Do you think any victim can learn what his predator looks like? If he could, the victim would become immortal!’</p>
<p>‘And that’s against the rules,’ the Prize put in.</p>
<p>‘At least give me an idea,’ Carmody said. ‘Does he always go around disguised as a spaceship?’</p>
<p>‘Of course not,’ Maudsley said. ‘From your point of view, he is a shape-changer. Have you ever heard of a mouse walking into the jaws of a snake, or a fly lighting on a frog’s tongue, or a fawn stepping between the forepaws of a tiger? <i>That</i> is the essence of predation! And you must ask yourself: where did those deluded victims think they were going, and what did they think was in front of them? Similarly, you must ask yourself what was really in front of your eyes when you talked to three of the predator’s fingers and followed them straight into his mouth!’</p>
<p>‘They looked like people,’ Carmody said. ‘But I still don’t know what the predator looked like.’ ‘There is no way I can enlighten you,’ Maudsley said. ‘Information about predators is not easily gained. They are too complementary to oneself. Its traps and concealments are based upon your own memories, your dreams and fantasies, your hopes and desires. The predator takes your own treasured dramas and plays them for you, as you just saw. To know your predator, you must know yourself. And it is easier to know the entire Universe than to know oneself.’</p>
<p>‘What can I do?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Learn!’ Maudsley said. ‘Be eternally vigilant, move at top speed, trust nothing and no one. Don’t think of relaxing until you have reached your home.’</p>
<p>‘Home!’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Yes. You will be safe on your own planet. Your predator cannot enter your lair. You will still be subject to all commonplace disasters, but at least you will be spared this.’</p>
<p>‘Can you send me home?’ Carmody asked. ‘You said you were working on a machine.’</p>
<p>‘I have completed it,’ Maudsley said. ‘But you must, understand its limitations, which are a concomitant with my own. My machine can take you to Where Earth is now, but that is all it can do.’</p>
<p>‘But that’s all I need!’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘No, it is not. “Where” gives you only the first W of location. You will still have to solve for When and Which. Take them in order, is my advice. Temporality before particularity, to use a common expression. You will have to leave here at once; your predator, whose appetite you foolishly aroused, may be back at any instant. I may not be so lucky this time in my rescue attempts.’</p>
<p>‘How did you get me out of his mouth?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘I hastily fabricated a lure,’ Maudsley told him. ‘It looked just like you, but I built it a little larger than life-size and gave it a bit more vitality. The predator dropped you and bounded after it, dribbling saliva. But we can’t try that again.’</p>
<p>Carmody preferred not to ask if the lure had felt any pain. ‘I’m ready,’ he said. ‘But where am I going, and what is going to happen?’</p>
<p>‘You are going to an Earth, almost undoubtedly the wrong one. But I will send a letter to a person I know who is very clever at solving temporal problems. He’ll look you up, if he decides to take your case, and after that … well, who can say? Take it as it comes, Carmody, and be grateful if anything comes at all.’</p>
<p>‘I am grateful,’ Carmody said. ‘No matter what the outcome, I want to thank you very much.’</p>
<p>‘That’s quite all right,’ Maudsley said. ‘Don’t forget my message to the old fellow if you ever do get back home. All set to go? The machine is right here beside me. I didn’t have time to make it visible, but it looks almost exactly like a Zenith battery-operated shortwave radio. Where the hell did it go? Here it is. Got your Prize?’</p>
<p>‘I’ve got him,’ the Prize said, holding on to Carmody’s left arm with both hands.</p>
<p>‘Then we’re ready. I set this dial here and then this one, and then these two over here … You’ll find it pleasant to be out of the macrocosm, Carmody, and back on a planet, even if it isn’t yours. There’s no qualitative difference, of course, among atom, planet, galaxy or universe. It’s all a question of what scale you live on most comfortably. And now I push <i>this</i> –’</p>
<p><i>Bam! Pow! Crrrrunch!</i> Slow dissolve, quick dissolve, lap dissolve, electronic music denoting outer space, outer space denoting electronic music. Pages of a calendar flip, Carmody tumbles head over heels in simulated free fall. Kettledrums sound ominous note, ominous note sounds kettledrums, bright flash of colours, woman’s voice keening in echo chamber, laughter of children, montage of Jaffa oranges lighted to look like planets, collage of a solar system lighted to look like ripples in a brook. Slow the tape, speed the tape, fade out, fade in.</p>
<p>It was one hell of a trip, but nothing Carmody hadn’t expected.</p>
