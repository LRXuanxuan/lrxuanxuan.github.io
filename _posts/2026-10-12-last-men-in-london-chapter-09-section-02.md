<h2>ii. 保罗与汉普蒂</h2>
<p>恰好有这样一个罕见的孩子，被父母送进了保罗任校长的学校。从外表看，他像一个粗笨的大块头，仿佛把一个怪诞的八岁儿童放大了来瞧。粗壮的脖子托着一颗硕大、几乎秃顶的脑袋，在正常人眼里很令人反感。只有后脑还长着头发，稀疏、坚硬，颜色像灰沙。光秃而凹凸不平的颅顶悬在两只浅褐色的大眼睛上方，那双眼睛大得仿佛占据了脸部中央的全部区域。一个不起眼的鼻子下面，是一张巨大而轮廓鲜明的嘴。下颌如此短小，以至于虽然下巴形状端正，看起来却仿佛大嘴下面又长了一个鼻子。他总把头高高挺直，仿佛头顶正托着一只水罐。这种姿态使他的脸带上一种滑稽的尊严，而那双机敏、警觉的眼睛又偶尔使这种尊严显得阴险。他敦实的身躯和巨大而总在动的双手，会让女人不寒而栗。</p>
<p>难怪这个不幸的孩子——同学给他起了个绰号叫“汉普蒂”——会遭到同伴欺负。课堂上，他通常懒散，间或突然来一阵活动，还会说出一些粗野古怪的话，引得男孩们哄笑，却有时令保罗很不安。班主任起初报告说，汉普蒂愚笨而且懒惰得无可救药；后来才看出，他其实一直在活动，只不过不是沉入遥远的思索，就是观察、批评眼前除了课业之外的一切。保罗对这孩子仿佛在愚钝和天才之间来回跳跃的表现困惑不已，于是设法让全班做了一系列智力测验。汉普蒂的成绩根本无法分析。某些最简单的题能把他难倒，另一些原本为“高智商成年人”设计的题，他却毫不迟疑地答出。保罗追究他为何会在低难度题上失败，结果发现每一次都是因为题目里存在某种细微的歧义。心理学家们还没有聪明到足以测量这样一个独一无二的男孩。</p>
<p>保罗试着赢得汉普蒂的信任。他带他出去喝茶，周末偶尔同他散步。保罗长期以来都习惯带一群男孩去乡间、去河上活动，夏天还组织露营假期和欧洲大陆旅行。起初，他希望把汉普蒂也纳入这类集体活动，但很快便明白，只要同龄人在场，这个古怪生物就根本无法融入。因此，他开始抽出时间单独同男孩相处。汉普蒂起初抱有敌意，后来变成礼貌的沉默寡言。等他发现保罗——在我的帮助下——确实意识到了他与同伴之间的鸿沟，态度才谨慎地友善起来。</p>
<p>一开始，两人交谈极其困难。保罗过去很擅长根据谈话对象调整自己的角色，这种本领现在价值无比，却仍无法产生他所希望的那种突然、自然的亲近。问题在于，汉普蒂不属于任何已知类型。不能把他当成正常青春期少年去接近；但他虽然有许多幼儿特征，也不能当儿童看待，因为某些方面的兴趣早已属于成年人。另一方面，保罗惯用的那套把学生“当男人和男人一样谈”的办法，在这里也失效了，因为汉普蒂似乎根本没有正常少年那种渴望进入成年人兄弟会的愿望。不过，凭着耐心，以及一种自己正在探索一种比以往所有冒险中遇见的心灵都更遥远的心态，保罗还是逐渐标出了汉普蒂性格上的几个主要地标。研究中慢慢显出一条重要原则：汉普蒂之所以显得幼稚，是因为那些普通儿童会忽略的幼儿经验侧面，反而牢牢吸引了他的注意。例如有时游泳前，他会赤身裸体躺在河岸上，整整一刻钟只顾拨弄脚趾，像婴儿躺在摇篮里一样，对保罗兴高采烈的谈话，或“该下水了”的提醒，全无反应。这种举动一度让保罗绝望，直到他发现，真正迷住这孩子的，是自己控制脚趾和控制手指之间的差别。并且保罗有理由怀疑，这情形里还有某种更为深奥的方面，要么英语根本无法表达，要么过于微妙，超出了保罗的理解能力。</p>
<p>保罗刚认识汉普蒂时，发现男孩正处在一种病态的怯懦中，只偶尔闪现出对同伴的轻蔑。但在保罗的同情与洞察影响下，汉普蒂开始意识到，自己毕竟不只是一个低等者。随着心智发展，也随着他明白自己是按另一种模式造成的，他渐渐确信自己在许多方面确实优越：在根本上更聪明，更能作出连贯一致的行为，更少受返祖冲动困扰；最重要的是，他经验中存在某些层面——其中有些还是最令人陶醉的部分——除最敏感的少数同类之外，别人根本无法进入。</p>
<p>同汉普蒂接触一年后，保罗开始感到双方的位置已经倒转：过去扮演优越者的是自己，如今却被一步步迫使让出上位。这变化始于某一天。那时保罗正试图激励汉普蒂更用功，采用的办法是诉诸他的竞争性自尊。训话结束后，男孩望着他，神情里混着惊奇和失望。然后他发出一声短促的笑，说：“可我到底为什么要打败小约翰逊他们呢？”这问题本身并不特别，可从汉普蒂嘴里问出来，却似乎有一种特殊分量。保罗突然觉得，自己刚才策略上的错误暴露了一种感情上的粗俗。</p>
<p>再过了一阵，保罗惊讶地发现，自己竟然在若干学校政策问题上非常认真地征求汉普蒂的意见。保罗本来就习惯向学生讨建议，以培养他们的责任感；但这一次令他不安的是，他真的需要这建议，而且确实准备采用。此时汉普蒂已经极其敏锐地摸清了同类的心智方式；在自己有经验的领域，例如学校里，他表现出一种冷静而常带犬儒色彩的聪明。</p>
<p>又过了一年，一向懒散的汉普蒂突然让保罗吃了一惊：他开始认真学习，并以惊人的速度补回落下的进度，很快就成了全校最聪明、同时也最难对付的学生。他给教师们留下的印象是，无论做什么功课，他都只是在玩一场游戏；或者说，他只是在学习人类这个种族的各种心智把戏，却既不相信这些把戏，也不认可它们。人们很难不把他想成丛林里的博物学家，正在研究猿类的心智。六个月后，他作为学术神童的名声已经传遍全国。汉普蒂的这一变化令保罗欣慰，但另一个变化却既难理解，又令人不安。此前，保罗一直觉得他危险地缺乏自我关切。在学生中间，他对玩具、糖果和金钱过分慷慨，于是被别人毫不留情地榨取；在功课上，保罗也以为正是他根深蒂固的“不把自己当回事”，使他如此低效。可如今，随着学习狂热一同出现的，却是一种为了自身利益牺牲他人快乐或福祉的倾向。多数方面，他仍保持着本来正常而无私的天性，只是染上一层轻蔑，好像随手把恩惠像垃圾一样丢给狗；但凡涉及自己真正关心的事，他现在却变成了毫不留情的自利者。保罗花了几个月才弄清这种异常变化、以及男孩越来越沉默冷淡的原因。终于有一天，两人在利斯山散步时，汉普蒂宣布，他决定把自己的秘密告诉校长，因为他必须得到某个有智力的成年人的帮助。于是保罗听到了一番很长的说明，讲男孩如何看待自己、如何看待这个不幸让他居住其中的世界，以及如何看待自己的未来。保罗强烈地感到，双方的位置这次真是彻底倒转了：这个怪诞青年以一个成年人对待儿童般的自信优越感来对待自己，而校长本人竟不由自主地摆出了自己手下某位级长听训时那种恭敬姿态。然而按照任何正常标准衡量，汉普蒂的计划都荒唐透顶。</p>
<p>汉普蒂说，经过仔细的心理学与生物学调查，他已经发现自己与正常人类有根本区别，而且在心智能力上确实高出许多。不幸的是，他的天性已经被野蛮的成长环境严重扭曲。“不过，”他补充道，“你本人确实一直同情地对待我，而且就你们这种人所能达到的程度而言，也算相当理解我。实际上，要不是你，我在青春期阶段大概已经彻底毁掉了。”说完这种带着轻蔑的感谢后，这个令人生畏的少年宣布，尽管保罗尽了力，自己如今大概仍已受损太深，未必能完成必须尝试的事业。不过，他不是胜利，就是战死。保罗此时不智地插嘴，说自己很高兴汉普蒂终于决心展示能力，而且无论选择什么职业，他肯定都会在世界上留下印记。男孩停下脚步，转身面对校长，用一种若有所问的神情盯着他；在这种目光下，保罗很难保持自尊。</p>
<p>过了一会儿，汉普蒂用一种仿佛只是在说“我得去买包烟”或“我得换身衣服”那样平静而笃定的口气说：“我要做的，是造一个新世界。我还没确定，到底是应该把这个世界改造掉，还是必须把它毁了，再造一个。”保罗如释重负地大笑起来。可对方只说：“我原以为你更聪明。事实上我知道你更聪明。想一想！我是认真的。”保罗惊骇地意识到，汉普蒂确实是认真的；更令他困惑的是，他还发现自己心中有某个部分竟在赞同。尽管如此，他还是提醒自己，这个自封的世界建造者毕竟只是个古怪的男孩，于是开始试图说服汉普蒂：他是在出洋相；无论一个人多么优越，任何单个个体都不可能完成这样的事业。男孩回答：“你说的是健全的常识，也就是迄今使你们这个物种取得胜利的那种常识。但像我这样的异类心智能非常清楚地看见，常识同样也是你们的毁灭之源；以你们目前的处境而言，你们作为一个物种，既没有足够的智力，也没有足够的德性来自救。一个更高等的心灵，也许能够把你们训练好，或者至少赐给你们一种仁慈的灭绝。至于我，你说得没错，我几乎肯定会失败。你们这个世界既不容易推动，也不容易毁灭；而我早年同一个迟钝、野蛮的种族接触，又已经遭到可怕的残害。但我必须尝试。并且在接下来几年里，我会需要你的帮助。你花几周认真想一想，就会明白我是对的；除非你愿意背叛自己最高的理想，否则从今以后，你必须把其他一切都置于为我服务之后。”</p>
<p>保罗再次认真抗议这种疯狂想法；但为了迁就男孩，接下来的路上还是听他讲完那套惊人的计划。首先，汉普蒂宣称，他要把全人类的一切文化吸收到自己身上。他相信这不会花太久，因为一旦发现自己的优越性，他也同时获得了一种独到洞察，能看清最优秀的人类心灵究竟弱在哪里，又取得了哪些有限但坚实的成就。等他掌握人类全部智慧之后，就会凭借自己更精细的感知与更高的智力去纠正它。这样产生的许多成果会超出同类理解，但他会根据自己对低等心智的透彻了解，为全部工作出版简化版本。完成这一初步、容易而纯理论性的任务后，他便开始实际改造世界。他确信，等自己真正成熟时，凭借更高明的处世技巧和独一无二的人格力量，他对待正常人将像牧羊人对待羊群一样容易；不过他也没有欺骗自己，知道要面对的不只有羊，还有狼，而且等到需要摧毁世界上那些庞大的返祖组织与既得利益时，会出现极严重的困难。他承认，这项工作需要自己全部技巧，而且很可能最终仍会把自己击败。不过与此同时，他会着手制造更多高级类型的个体。如果自己的性发育最终正常，其中一些可能是亲生后代；也可能他会发现散布在世界各处的其他独特生物；又或者，他能够利用自己对生物学、生理学更精细的理解，从正常卵细胞制造高级男女。无论如何，靠这种或那种办法，如果最坏情况发生，而正常物种确实不可救药，他就会在世界某个偏远地区建立一个小型超人殖民地。那里将成为一个新人类物种与新世界秩序的胚芽。它会一点点夺取整个星球的控制权，要么灭绝低等物种，更可能的是驯化其中那些对自己有用的亚人群体。</p>
<p>讲完这份政策宣言，汉普蒂停了一会儿，然后重新开口，声音里出乎意料地带着犹豫和痛苦。“这套计划，”他说，“在你看来当然荒诞，但以我的能力，在一个全由低等者构成的世界里，它本来应当可行。我并不是什么完美典范。未来总有一天，会出现远比我精妙得多的心灵。可就算是我，只要身体能承受压力，原本也该足以完成这工作——要不是你们这个物种已经把我严重残害。直到现在，我从没把最严重的问题告诉过你。我必须逼自己把秘密揭开，因为你必须帮助我恢复完整，让我能去完成该做的事。”</p>
<p>接着汉普蒂告诉保罗，童年遭受的苦难使他对同类所谓的道德产生了猛烈仇恨。事情已经发展到这种地步：每当某种行为看来很可能赢得普遍赞许，他就会产生一种无法抗拒的欲望，偏要走相反方向。比如最近这阵拼命学习，本来社会的称赞几乎逼得他重新退回懒散状态。只有不断提醒自己，努力学习真正的目标正是摧毁低等物种所谓的道德，他才能控制住自己。他对他人福祉日益增长的漠视，表面上可以用“把自己献给伟大责任”来解释，但真正给这种漠视力量的，是意识到自己的行为正在招致同类谴责。还有另一种更严重、至少更戏剧化的方式，他对道德主义恶臭的反感也正在威胁着毁掉自己。有时他会突然被一种不可控制、近乎疯狂的冲动攫住，想要以当时能想到的最骇人方式侵犯公众的体面规范。他本人性发育迟缓，可早年长辈对性的敬畏与羞耻曾把他吓住；后来终于看清同胞在这问题上的愚昧和卑劣迷信时，他便产生了一种强烈欲望，要把他们震骇得无以复加。</p>
<p>一旦逼自己说起这个主题，汉普蒂便被激情卷走了。他向惊愕的校长倾倒出一股怪诞而多半淫秽的幻想洪流，从男学生式的下流笑话，一直到残酷的性侵害行为。说到其中最可怕的一桩骇人想象时，他压低声音，发出一种近乎虔诚的笑，然后说：“这事应该在皮卡迪利圆环中央那个安全岛上干，前提是你能在那里逮到合适的女人——那种看起来像公主的。可他们冲过来之前，我来得及做完吗？”</p>
<p>保罗如释重负地感觉到，主导权终于又回到了自己手中。他劝说这个不幸的男孩，并答应帮助他。后来，他把汉普蒂带去见一位精神分析师；这人过去处理学校里的棘手个案常常很成功。但这一次，专家失败了。要通过唤醒无意识欲望来治愈汉普蒂根本不可能，因为在这个古怪心灵里，一切都完全处于意识之中。汉普蒂把自己看得通通透透。暗示和催眠同样无效。见过几次后，分析师开始坐立不安，因为汉普蒂恶意地享受着一步步迫使他获得一种极不讨喜的自我认识。这个可怜人不仅开始发现自己的技巧大多不过是盲目猜测，自己所谓的智慧遗漏的东西远比包括的更多；更令他恐惧的是，他发现自己其实受一些宗教性质的秘密欲望与忠诚支配，而过去从没有勇气承认。汉普蒂第十五次来会面时，被告知分析师失踪了。后来才弄清，这个心神大乱的男人经历了一次宗教皈依，逃到僻静处冥想去了。和他那个行业里的许多人一样，他骨子里其实是个简单的人；一旦发现自己内心存在某些宗教可以满足、而弗洛伊德教义无法满足的需要，他想不出更好的办法，只好从一种正统观念的煎锅跳进另一种正统观念的火堆。几周之内，他就加入了修会，开始研究圣托马斯的心理学原则。</p>
<p>汉普蒂残缺的心灵现在似乎每况愈下。他越清楚地认识童年造成的损害，就越屈服于仇恨——不是对同胞本身的仇恨，而是对他们虚假正义感的仇恨。</p>
<p>在神志清醒的时候，他会告诉自己，这种激情荒谬透顶；情绪一发作时，自己只想侵犯和摧毁，无论珍贵之物还是可鄙之物都可能伤害；这种强迫冲动正在毁掉他的心智，使他不再适合承担伟大任务；他绝不能让自己被拖到周围那些不幸而盲目的野兽的水平。</p>
<p>保罗日复一日看着自己的保护对象进行这场绝望斗争，被一种重大生物悲剧的感觉压得肃然无言。因为他不能不猜想：倘若这个孤独而强大的生物没有遭到残害，他也许真的能够创立一种新人类。汉普蒂已经惹过几次不算严重的麻烦，每一次保罗都费了很大力气才把他救出来。校长如今日日害怕最终灾难到来，既毁掉汉普蒂，也顺带使学校声名扫地。不过学校最终还是免于最坏的结局。某天早晨，保罗收到一封信，汉普蒂在其中说，他已经明白自己败了，而且随时可能对某个无辜的人造成严重伤害，因此决定去死。他最后的愿望，是把遗体交给某位世界著名的神经学家解剖。调查证明，汉普蒂确实已经停止了生命活动，尽管死因从未查明。不用说，尽管他一直是家里的害群之马，父母还是为他安排了一场体面的基督教葬礼。</p>
<p>大自然第一次试验性地造出人类之后，又一次笨拙的改良尝试，就这样结束了。还有另一个更高等、也幸运得多的个体，后来几乎真的完成了汉普蒂只在想象中设想的事业。关于这个人、他建立的乌托邦殖民地，以及这个殖民地如何被一个充满嫉妒的世界毁灭，也许我会在另一个场合再讲。</p>
</section>

<h2>ii. PAUL AND HUMPTY</h2>
<p>IT SO HAPPENED that one of these rare beings was sent by his parents to the school where Paul was head master. In appearance he was a great lout rather like a grotesque child of eight seen through a magnifying-glass. His thick neck and immense, almost bald head, was very repulsive to the normal eye. Only the hinder part of his head bore hair, which was sparse, wiry, and in colour like grey sand. The bare and lumpy dome of his cranium overhung two pale brown eyes, so large that they seemed to occupy the whole middle region of his face. Beneath an inconspicuous nose was a huge and clear-cut mouth. So small was the lower jaw that the chin, though well moulded, seemed but another nose under the great lips. The head was held erect in the attitude of one supporting a pitcher on his crown. This carriage gave to the face a farcical dignity which the alert and cautious eyes rendered at times malignant. The thick-set body and great restless hands made women shudder.</p>
<p>No wonder that this unfortunate child, who received the nickname Humpty, was persecuted by his schoolfellows. In class his laziness was relieved now and then by fits of activity, and by uncouth remarks which roused derision among the boys, but which to Paul were sometimes very disturbing. The form master at first reported that Humpty was stupid and incorrigibly indolent; but later it appeared that he was perpetually active either in remote meditations or in observing and criticizing everything save the work in hand. Puzzled by the lad’s seeming alternation between stupidity and brilliance, Paul contrived to give the whole class a series of intelligence tests. Humpty’s performance defeated analysis. Some of the simplest tests floored him, yet some of those’ intended for ‘superior adults’ he solved without hesitation. Inquiring into the failures in the low-grade tests, Paul found that they were always due to some subtle ambiguity in the problem. The psychologists were not intelligent enough to test this unique boy.</p>
<p>Paul tried to win Humpty’s confidence. He took him out to tea, and walked with him occasionally at week-ends. Paul had long been in the habit of taking parties of boys into the country and on the river, and in the summer he organized camping holidays and trips on the Continent. At first he had hoped to fit Humpty into this communal activity, but very soon he realized that nothing could be done with the strange creature in the presence of his contemporaries. He therefore devoted some time to treating the boy separately. Humpty was at first hostile, then politely reticent. When he found that Paul (with my aid) realized the gulf that lay between him and his fellows, he became cautiously well-disposed.</p>
<p>Conversation was at first extremely difficult. Paul’s old gift of adopting a persona suited to his companions was now invaluable, but failed to produce the sudden and easy intimacy which he desired. The trouble was that Humpty did not belong to any known type. It was impossible to get in touch with him on the assumption that he was a normal adolescent; yet in spite of his infantile traits he could not be successfully treated as a child, for in some ways his interests were already those of an adult. On the other hand, Paul’s well-tried policy of speaking to his boys ‘as man to man’ was in this case unsuccessful, because Humpty seemed to be without the normal craving to be a member of the adult fraternity. With patience, however, and with a sense that he was exploring a mentality more remote than anything he had discovered in earlier adventures. Paul plotted out some of the main landmarks of Humpty’s nature. Gradually an important principle emerged in this study. In so far as Humpty was infantile, his attention was arrested by aspects of infantile experience which the normal child would miss. Sometimes, for instance, before a swim he would lie naked on the bank twiddling his toes for a solid quarter of an hour, like a baby in its cot, untouched by Paul’s bright talk, or his suggestions that it was time to take the plunge. This conduct filled Paul with despair, till he discovered that what fascinated the boy was the difference between his control of his toes and his control of his fingers. Moreover, Paul had reason to suspect that there was some other more recondite aspect of the situation, which either could not be expressed in the English language, or was too subtle for Paul’s apprehension.</p>
<p>When Paul first made the acquaintance of Humpty, he found the boy in a state of morbid diffidence punctuated now and then by flashes of contempt for his fellows. But under the influence of Paul’s sympathy and insight Humpty began to realize that he was not, after all, merely an inferior being. As his mind developed, and as he came to understand that he was made on a different pattern from his fellows, it was borne in on him that in many ways he was superior, that he was basically more intelligent, more capable of coherent behaviour, less beset by atavistic impulses, and above all that there were certain aspects of his experience (in some cases the most delectable) to which none but the most sensitive of his fellow human beings had any access whatever.</p>
<p>A year after his first contact with Humpty, Paul began to feel that the tables had been turned, that he who had formerly played the part of the superior was being forced step by step to yield precedence. The change began one day when Paul had been trying to rouse Humpty to work harder at school by appealing to his competitive self-regard. When the sermon was over, the boy looked at him with a wonder tinged with dismay. Then he gave out a single bark of laughter, and said, ‘But why on earth should I want to beat Johnson Minor and the rest?’ This was not a very remarkable question, but coming from Humpty it seemed to have a peculiar significance. And Paul felt that somehow in his error of tactics he had displayed a gross vulgarity of feeling.</p>
<p>Somewhat later Paul discovered with dismay that he had been quite seriously asking Humpty’s advice about certain matters of school policy. It was his custom to ask his boys for advice, so as to give them a sense of responsibility; but this time he was perturbed to find that he actually wanted the advice, and intended to use it. By now Humpty had sized up the mentality of his fellow-mortals very shrewdly; and in spheres of which he had experience, such as the school, he displayed a cold and often a cynical intelligence.</p>
<p>After another year had passed, the indolent. Humpty surprised Paul by settling down to work, and making up for lost ground so successfully that he soon became the school’s most brilliant, but most difficult, pupil. He gave his teachers the impression that in all his work he was but playing a game, or that he was learning the mental tricks of the human race without believing in them or approving of them. It was impossible to avoid thinking of him as a naturalist in the jungle studying the mentality of apes. After six months his fame as a prodigy of scholarship had spread over the whole country. This change in Humpty gratified Paul, but there was another change which was both incomprehensible and distressing. Hitherto he had seemed to Paul to be dangerously lacking in self-regard. Amongst the boys his generosity over toys, sweets and money had caused him to be mercilessly plundered, while in his work it was inveterate carelessness of self (so Paul thought) that had made him so ineffective. But now, along with his fever of work he developed a propensity for sacrificing the pleasure or well-being of others to his own interests. In most respects he retained his normal and unselfish nature though tinged with a contemptuousness that made him cast favours about him as one might fling refuse to the dogs; but in matters in which he felt a serious concern he was now a relentless self-seeker. It took Paul some months to discover the cause of this extraordinary change, and of the boy’s increasing reticence and frigidity. At last, however, Humpty, during a walk on Leith Hill, announced that he would take his head master into his confidence, because he must have the help of some intelligent adult. Paul was then given a lengthy account of the boy’s conclusions about himself, about the world which he had the misfortune to inhabit, and about his future. Paul had a sense that the tables had indeed been turned with a vengeance, and that the grotesque youth was treating him with the confident superiority with which normally a grown man condescends toward a child; while the head master himself involuntarily adopted the respectfulness of one of his own prefects receiving instructions. Yet according to all sane standards Humpty’s plans were preposterous.</p>
<p>After careful psychological and biological inquiry, so Humpty said, he had discovered that he was profoundly different from the normal human being, and indeed very superior in mental calibre. Unfortunately his nature had been seriously distorted by his barbarous upbringing, ‘Though’, he added, ‘you yourself have certainly treated me with sympathy, and with as much comprehension as can be expected of your kind. Indeed it is to you I owe it that I was not completely ruined during my adolescent phase.’ Having made this contemptuous acknowledgement, the formidable boy declared that, in spite of Paul’s care, he was probably by now too much damaged to win through in the enterprise which he must attempt. However, he would triumph or die fighting. Here Paul unwisely interrupted to say how glad he was that Humpty was at last determined to show his ability, and that undoubtedly he would make a mark in the world whatever career he chose. The boy stood still and faced his head master, gazing at him with a quizzing expression under which Paul found it hard to preserve his self-respect.</p>
<p>Presently Humpty remarked, with all the assurance and quietness of one who says he must buy cigarettes, or change his clothes, ‘What I must do is to make a new world. I am not sure yet whether I shall have to destroy it and produce another.’ Paul, with immense relief, burst into laughter. But the other said only, ‘I thought you had more intelligence. In fact I know you have. Think! I mean what I say.’ With dismay Paul realized that Humpty did mean what he said; and with bewilderment he realized further that something in his own mind applauded. However, he reminded himself that this would-be builder of worlds was merely an eccentric boy; and he set himself to persuade Humpty that he was making a fool of himself, that no single individual, no matter how superior, could achieve such a task. The boy replied, ‘What you say is sound common sense, the kind of sense by which your species has hitherto triumphed. But an alien mentality such as mine can see very clearly that common sense is also your undoing, and that, as a species, you have neither the intelligence nor the virtue to save yourselves in your present plight. A superior mind may perhaps be able to discipline you, or to afford you a merciful extinction. With regard to myself, you are right that I shall almost certainly fail. Your world is not easy to move, or to destroy; and I have been terribly mutilated by early contact with an insensitive, a brutish, species. But I must make the attempt. And for a few years I shall need your help. Think the matter over for a few weeks, and you will see that I am right, and that unless you would betray your own highest ideals you must henceforth subordinate everything else to my service.’</p>
<p>Once more Paul earnestly protested against this folly; but to humour the lad he listened during the rest of the walk to his amazing plans. First, Humpty declared he would assimilate all the cultures of the human race. He was convinced that this would not take him long; for, having discovered his own superiority, he had also gained unique insight into the weakness and the limited but solid achievement of the best human minds. Having made himself the master of all man’s wisdom, he would proceed to correct it by his own finer percipience and intelligence. Much that he would thus produce would be beyond the comprehension of his fellows, but he would publish simplified versions of all his work, based on his thorough knowledge of the inferior mentality. When this preliminary, easy, and purely theoretical, task was completed, he would set about the practical reform of the world. He was confident that by the time he had reached maturity, his superior tact and the unique power of his personality would enable him to deal with normal individuals much as a shepherd deals with his sheep; but he did not disguise from himself the fact that he would have to cope not only with sheep but with wolves, and that very grave difficulties would arise when the time came to break down the great atavistic organizations and vested interests of the world. This task, he admitted, would need all his skill, and would probably defeat him. Meanwhile, however, he would have taken steps to produce other individuals of superior type. Possibly, if his sexual development turned out normally, some of these would be his own offspring. Possibly he would have encountered other unique beings scattered up and down the world. Possibly he would be able to use his finer understanding of biology and physiology to produce superior men and women from normal ova. Anyhow, by one means or another, if the worst came to the worst, and the normal species proved incorrigible, he would found a small colony of supermen in some remote part of the world. This would become the germ of a new human species and a new world-order. Little by little it would gain control of the whole planet, and would either exterminate the inferior species, or more probably domesticate such members of the subhuman hordes as it required for its own uses.</p>
<p>At the close of this announcement of policy Humpty paused, then began again in a voice which betrayed an unexpected hesitation and distress. ‘This programme,’ he said, ‘sounds to you fantastic, but it should be possible to one of my powers in a world of inferiors. I am no paragon. There should some day be minds incomparably finer than mine. Yet even I, if my health can stand the strain, should prove fit for the task, but for the severe mutilation which I have already received at the hands of your species. Till now I have never told you of my most serious trouble. I must bring myself to lay bare my secret, since you must help me to make myself whole for the work which I am to do.’</p>
<p>Humpty now told Paul that his sufferings during childhood had filled him with a violent hate of whatever passed as morality amongst his fellows. Things had come to such a pitch that, whenever any conduct seemed likely to earn general approval, he conceived an irresistible desire to take the opposite course. In his recent burst of hard work, for instance, public commendation had almost forced him to plunge back into indolence. Only by reminding himself that the real aim of his work was to destroy the so-called morality of the inferior species could he keep himself in hand. His increasing contempt for the well-being of others, though it found its excuse in his self-dedication to a great duty, drew its vigour from the sense that he was earning the condemnation of his fellows. There was also a more serious, or at least more dramatic, way in which his revulsion from the stink of moralism threatened to undo him. He was sometimes seized with an ungovernable, an insane, impulse to violate public decency in whatever manner would seem at the time most outrageous. He himself was sexually backward, but the awe and shame with which his elders regarded sex had in early days intimidated him; and when at last he came to realize the folly and abject superstitiousness of his countrymen in this matter, he conceived a violent craving to shock them.</p>
<p>Having forced himself to broach the subject, Humpty was carried away by his passion. He poured out on the bewildered head master a torrent of grotesque and mostly obscene fantasies ranging, from schoolboy smut to acts of brutal sexual aggression. Of the most horrible of these gruesome titbits he said, after a hushed, almost it seemed a reverent, laugh, ‘That ought to be done on the island in Piccadilly Circus, if you could catch the right woman there, one of those who look like princesses. But could I do it before they got at me?’</p>
<p>Paul felt with relief that leadership had once more come into his hands. He reasoned with the unhappy boy, and promised to help him. Subsequently he took Humpty to a psycho-analyst who had often dealt successfully with difficult cases in the school. But this time the expert was defeated. It was impossible to cure Humpty by bringing to life his unconscious cravings, for in this strange mind everything was fully conscious. Humpty knew himself through and through. Suggestion and hypnotism proved equally impossible. After a few meetings the analyst began to be ill at ease, for Humpty was taking a malicious pleasure in forcing him step by step to a most unflattering self-knowledge. Not only did the unhappy man begin to realize that his skill was mostly blind guesswork, and that his wisdom left out of account far more than it embraced, but also to his horror he discovered that he was dominated by secret desires and loyalties of a religious type which he had never had the courage to recognize. When Humpty appeared for the fifteenth meeting he was told that the analyst was missing. Later it turned out that the distracted man had suffered a religious conversion, and had fled away into solitude to meditate. Like so many of his profession, he was at bottom a simple soul; and when he found in himself needs that could be satisfied by religion but not by the doctrines of Freud, he could discover nothing better to do than to leap from the frying-pan of one orthodoxy into the fire of another. Within a few weeks he had joined a monastic order, and was studying the psychological principles of St Thomas.</p>
<p>The crippled mind of Humpty seemed now to go from bad to worse. The more clearly he realized the damage that had been inflicted on him in childhood, the more he succumbed to hatred, not of his fellow-men, but of their false righteousness.</p>
<p>In sane periods he told himself that his passion was fantastic; that when the mood was on him he lusted merely to violate and smash, and would harm what was precious no less than what was contemptible; that his obsession was ruining his mind, and making him unfit for his great task; that he must not let himself be dragged down to the level of the unhappy and unseeing beasts who surrounded him.</p>
<p>Paul, watching from day to day the desperate struggle of his protégé, was overawed by the sense of a momentous biological tragedy. For he could not but surmise that, if this lonely and potent being had not been mutilated, he might indeed have founded a new mankind. Humpty had already fallen into several minor scrapes, from which Paul had with difficulty extricated him. The head master now lived in dread of the final catastrophe which would ruin Humpty and incidentally disgrace the school. But the school was, after all, spared the extreme disaster. One morning Paul received a letter in which Humpty declared that, realizing that he was defeated, and that at any moment he might do grievous harm to some innocent person, he had decided to die. It was his last wish that his body should be given to a certain world-famous neurologist for dissection. Inquiry proved that Humpty had indeed ceased to live, though the cause of his death was never determined. Needless to say, though he had always been the black sheep of his family, his parents secured for him a decent Christian burial.</p>
<p>Thus ended one of Nature’s blundering attempts to improve upon her first, experimental, humanity. One other superior and much more fortunate individual was destined almost to succeed in the task that Humpty had merely imagined. Of this other, of the utopian colony which he founded, and of its destruction by a jealous world, I may tell on another occasion.</p>
