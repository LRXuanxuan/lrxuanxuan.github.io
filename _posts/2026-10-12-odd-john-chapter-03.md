<h3>第三章　可怕的孩子</h3>
<p>终于，到六岁时，约翰把注意力转向了移动身体这件事。在这门本领上，他此前甚至比他那副身体的外观所暗示的还要落后。智力和制作方面的兴趣，使他把其他一切都忽略了。</p>
<p>但这时，他终于发现了独立行动的必要，也发现了征服一门新技艺本身的诱惑。和往常一样，他的学习方法独出心裁，进步也快得惊人。他从来没有爬过。起初，他扶着一把椅子直立起来，轮流用一只脚保持平衡。这样练了一个小时，他便筋疲力尽；有生以来第一次，他显得彻底灰心。这个曾把数学家当作迟钝儿童的人，如今却带着一种新鲜而近乎忧郁的敬意，看待自己十岁的哥哥——全家最活跃的成员。整整一周，他执着而敬畏地观察汤米走路、奔跑、同姐姐打闹。每一个动作都被焦虑的约翰仔细记下。他同时勤奋练习平衡，甚至牵着母亲的手走了几步。</p>
<p>然而一周结束时，他像是发生了一次神经崩溃，随后好几天再也不肯让脚落地。带着明显的挫败感，他重新退回阅读，甚至又退回数学。</p>
<p>等身体恢复到足以再次下地时，他一口气、不借任何帮助地横穿了整个房间，随后竟喜极而泣，哭得近乎歇斯底里——这实在太不像约翰了。至此，这门技艺已经被征服，剩下的只不过是通过锻炼把肌肉练强。</p>
<p>可是约翰并不满足于会走路。他已经给自己设定了一个新的生活目标，并以他一贯的决绝投入其中。</p>
<p>起初，他那尚未发育的身体给他造成了极大妨碍。两条腿仍然几乎像胎儿的腿，又短又弯。但在持续使用——以及看起来同样重要的、他那不可屈服的意志——作用下，它们很快开始变直、变长、变强。七岁时，他已经能像兔子一样跑，像猫一样爬。就整体体格来说，他看上去大约只有四岁；可身上那种精瘦、结实的感觉，又像一个八九岁的街头小鬼。他的脸在形状上仍是婴儿的脸，可有时神情却几乎属于四十岁的成年人。巨大的眼睛和贴头的白色绒发，又使他显得没有年龄，甚至不像人类。</p>
<p>这时，他对肌肉的控制已经达到极其惊人的程度。所谓“学习”熟练动作，似乎不再存在。他的四肢，甚至每一块单独的肌肉，都完全照他的意志行动。这一点在他第一次尝试走路后的第二个月尤其明显：他学会了游泳。他在水里站了一会儿，观察姐姐已经练得很熟的划水动作，然后把脚从池底抬起来，照着做就是了。</p>
<p>接下来的好几个月里，约翰把全部精力都用来在各种体能本领上赶超别的孩子，并把自己的意志强加给他们。起初，孩子们都很喜欢他的这些努力。除了汤米——汤米已经意识到，自己正在被这个小弟弟全面超过。街上那些年纪更大的孩子倒更大方些，因为一开始约翰的成功还没有真正威胁到他们。但随着时间推移，约翰越来越把所有人都压了下去。</p>
<p>有一次，一个大家珍爱的球卡在屋顶排水槽里。约翰看上去不过是个略显瘦长的四岁孩子，却顺着落水管爬上去，沿着檐槽匍匐前进，把球扔了下来；随后只为了觉得好玩，他又沿两面斜屋顶之间的沟槽继续往上爬，最后骑在屋脊上。帕克斯当时进城买东西去了。邻居们当然全被吓坏，以为孩子随时会摔死。约翰预见到这里有乐子可寻，于是假装突然惊慌失措、动弹不得。他看起来像是彻底吓傻了，哆哆嗦嗦抓着瓦片，凄惨地呜咽，眼泪还顺着脸颊往下流。大家赶紧打电话给附近一个建筑承包商，对方派来工人和梯子。等救援者终于出现在屋顶上，约翰冲他做了个鬼脸，接着撒腿奔向自己的落水管；在一群目瞪口呆、继而愤怒不已的围观者注视下，他像猴子一样顺着管子滑了下来。</p>
<p>托马斯听说这次胡闹后，又惊又喜。“这个神童，”他说，“已经从数学进军杂技了。”帕克斯却只说：“我真希望他别老让人注意到自己。”</p>
<p>这时支配约翰的，是一种吞噬般的激情：个人本领和支配力。可怜的汤米从前是个颇爱发号施令的小魔头，如今却黯然失色，心里苦不堪言。姐姐安妮则崇拜才华横溢的约翰，成了他的奴隶。她过的是一种相当辛苦的生活。我尤其能同情她，因为许多年后，我也要接替她的位置。</p>
<p>此时，约翰已经成了附近每个孩子眼中的英雄，或者最讨厌的敌人。起初，他完全没有直觉去理解自己的行为会给别人造成什么感受，因此大多数人都把他视作“一个讨厌、自大、古里古怪的小东西”。问题其实很简单：别人不知道的时候，他总知道；别人做不到的时候，他几乎总能做到。奇怪的是，他没有表现出任何傲慢；但同样，他也绝不装出虚假的谦逊。</p>
<p>下面这个例子，标志着他此后对待同伴的策略发生转折，也最能说明他起初在这一方面的弱点，以及他那令人难以置信的心智柔韧性。</p>
<p>隔壁那个高大的学生斯蒂芬正在旁边花园里，对着一台拆得七零八落、结构颇复杂的割草机苦战。约翰翻过篱笆，默默看了几分钟。不久，他笑了。斯蒂芬没有理他。于是约翰弯下腰，从少年手中一把夺过一个齿轮，把它装到正确的位置；接着重新装好其他零件，这里转一颗螺母，那里拧一枚紧定螺钉，工作就完成了。斯蒂芬站在一旁，尴尬得说不出话。约翰朝篱笆走去，一边说：“真遗憾你不擅长这种活儿，不过我有空时随时可以帮你。”令他大吃一惊的是，对方突然扑过来，两次把他打倒，随后干脆把他扔过了篱笆。约翰坐在草地上揉着身体各处，肯定至少有那么一下感到愤怒；可好奇心压过了怒火。他几乎还算友善地问：“你为什么想这么干？”斯蒂芬却一句话也没说，径直离开了花园。</p>
<p>约翰坐在那里沉思。接着，他听见父亲在屋里说话，立刻冲进去找他。“喂！医生！”他喊道，“假如有个病人你怎么也治不好，后来某一天另一个人来了，一下把他治好了，你会怎么做？”托马斯正忙着别的事，漫不经心地回答：“不知道！大概会因为他多管闲事，把他揍一顿吧。”约翰倒吸一口气：“可是为什么？那不是非常愚蠢吗？”父亲仍心不在焉地说：“我想是吧，可人又不总是理智。这得看另一个家伙怎么表现。如果他让我觉得自己像个傻瓜，我肯定也会想揍他。”约翰盯着父亲看了好一会儿，然后说：“我明白了！”</p>
<p>“医生！”他忽然又开口，“我得变强，变得跟斯蒂芬一样强。要是我把那些书都读完——”他朝那些医学大部头看了一眼，“——就能学会怎么变得特别特别强吗？”父亲笑了。“恐怕不能。”他说。</p>
<p>此后的半年里，有两个目标支配着约翰的行为：第一，成为一个战无不胜的斗士；第二，理解他的同类。</p>
<p>后一项对约翰来说反倒更容易。他开始研究我们的行为和动机，一部分靠提问，一部分靠观察。很快，他发现了两个重要事实：第一，我们对于自己的真实动机，常常无知得令人吃惊；第二，在许多方面，他都和我们其余的人不同。多年以后，他亲口告诉我，正是在这一时期，他第一次开始意识到自己的独一无二。</p>
<p>不用我说也能猜到，不到两个星期，约翰看上去就像换了个人。他极其准确地给自己披上了一层英国人最典型的外壳：谦逊而慷慨。</p>
<p>尽管年纪小，外表看起来更小，约翰如今却成了许多恶作剧中那个不情愿、也不显山露水的领头人。孩子们总会喊：“约翰知道该怎么办！”或者：“把那个小魔鬼约翰叫来，这种活他最厉害。”他们同公立学校孩子之间时断时续地打着一场小型战争——那些孩子每天四次从街口经过——负责策划伏击的是约翰；在败局中突然发动一次近乎奇迹般的猛烈反冲、把失败变成胜利的，也是约翰。他简直像个幼年的朱庇特，只不过拳头就是他的雷霆。</p>
<p>这些战斗，一部分当然是欧洲那场更大战争的余波；但我相信，它们也是约翰为了自己的目的有意煽动和维持的。它们既给他展示体能的机会，也给他一种不被正式承认的领导地位。</p>
<p>于是也难怪，附近的孩子们会互相说：“约翰现在可真够意思。”而他们的母亲并不怎么懂他的军事天才，却被他的礼貌打动，于是也会互相说：“约翰这阵子真讨人喜欢。他以前那股讨厌的怪劲和自大已经没了。”</p>
<p>就连斯蒂芬也夸起他来。他对母亲说：“那小子其实挺不错。挨那顿揍对他有好处。他还为割草机的事向我道歉，说希望自己没把它瞎鼓捣坏。”</p>
<p>可命运给斯蒂芬留了一份惊喜。</p>
<p>尽管父亲泼过冷水，约翰还是一直抽零碎时间翻看医学和生理学书籍。里面的解剖图对他尤其有吸引力，而要真正看懂这些图，他就必须读文字。当然，他的词汇量远远不够，于是便仿照维克托·斯托特的办法：先从头到尾读完一本大型英语词典，接着又读完一本生理学术语词典。没过多久，他的阅读已经流畅到这种程度：目光只需沿一页印刷文字的中线迅速扫下去，就能把内容理解，并无限期地记住。</p>
<p>不过约翰并不满足于理论。有一天，帕克斯惊恐地发现，他正在餐厅地板上解剖一只死老鼠，而且还很周到地先铺了报纸，免得弄脏地毯。从此以后，他的解剖学研究——无论实践还是理论——都由“医生”监督。有几个月，约翰完全沉醉其中。他在解剖和显微镜观察方面都显示出极高的技巧。他逮住一切机会盘问父亲，而且常常把父亲答案中的混乱暴露出来；最后帕克斯想起那两位数学家的下场，坚持说疲惫的医生必须得到喘息。从那以后，约翰独自学习。</p>
<p>随后，他又像抛弃数学一样，突然抛弃了生物学。帕克斯问：“你是不是像把‘数字’弄完那样，把‘生命’也弄完了？”“没有，”约翰回答，“可是生命不像数字那样能严丝合缝地扣起来。它拼不出一个完整图案。那些书全有问题。当然，我经常一眼就看出它们哪里愚蠢，可一定还有某种更深的问题，是我现在看不出来的。”</p>
<p>顺便说一句，大约就在这时，约翰还真的被送进学校读过书，可他的学校生涯只持续了三个星期。“他的影响太扰乱秩序，”女校长说，“而且完全没法教。我担心这孩子虽然在几个有限方向上很机灵，实际上却低于正常水平，需要特殊教育。”从此以后，为了应付法律要求，帕克斯自己假装在家教他。为了让母亲高兴，他会扫一眼那些课本，然后想复述多少就复述多少。至于理解：凡是他感兴趣的内容，他理解得不比作者差；凡是令他厌烦的，他就直接忽略。在这些东西上，他完全可以表现得像个低能儿。</p>
<p>结束生物学以后，约翰放弃一切智力研究，把注意力全部集中到自己的身体上。那年秋天，他除了冒险小说，只读了几本柔术著作。他花大量时间练习柔术，也做自己发明的体操。此外，他还按照自己的一套原则，极其严格地控制饮食。约翰的消化器官一直是他身体唯一明显的弱点。它们保持婴儿状态的时间，似乎比身体其余部分还长。直到六岁以前，他除了特制牛奶和果汁以外几乎什么都消化不了。战争造成的食物短缺又使喂养约翰更加困难，他也一直很容易出现轻微的消化问题。可是现在，他把事情接管到自己手里，设计出一种复杂却分量极少的饮食方案：水果、奶酪、麦乳、全麦面包，并把进食与休息、锻炼精确错开。我们都笑他；除了帕克斯，她认真保证他的每一项要求都得到满足。</p>
<p>究竟是因为饮食、体操，还是纯粹靠意志力，他的力量的确变得异常惊人——至少相对于他的体重和年龄而言。附近的男孩一个接一个地被卷进同约翰的争执，又一个接一个地落败。当然，使他能够对付远比自己高大的对手的，并不是蛮力，而是敏捷和心计。大家常说：“只要那小子按他想要的方式抓住你，你就完了。你又打不中他，他太快了。”</p>
<p>奇怪的是，每一次争斗在旁观者看来，主动挑衅的似乎都不是约翰，而是另一个人。</p>
<p>高潮发生在斯蒂芬身上。此时的斯蒂芬已经是学校橄榄球第一队的队长，而且同约翰成了真正的好朋友。</p>
<p>有一天，我正在托马斯的书房里同他谈话，忽然听见花园里传来异常激烈的扭打声。我们望出去，只见斯蒂芬徒劳地一次又一次扑向灵活闪避的约翰；约翰每次侧身跃开时，都会用那只婴儿般的小拳头一次又一次狠狠击中斯蒂芬的脸。那张脸因为愤怒、困惑而几乎认不出来，同平时和善的斯蒂芬相比简直骇人。两个人身上都沾满了血，看样子全来自斯蒂芬的鼻子。</p>
<p>约翰也像变成了另一个生物。他的嘴唇向后扯开，形成一种非人的、介于龇牙咆哮和微笑之间的表情。一只眼睛因为斯蒂芬唯一一次打中的拳而半闭着，另一只却像面具上的眼洞一样深黑空阔。约翰一旦暴怒，虹膜就几乎完全缩到看不见。</p>
<p>这场冲突既前所未有，又荒诞得不像现实，以至于托马斯和我有好几秒钟都僵在原地。最后，斯蒂芬总算抓住了那个恶魔般的孩子——或者说，是约翰允许他抓住。我们冲下楼去救人。可赶到花园时，斯蒂芬已经趴在地上，一边扭动一边喘气；他的双臂被反剪到身后，牢牢锁在约翰那双狼蛛般的手里。</p>
<p>那一刻，约翰的样子给了我一种极其强烈的恶魔印象。他蜷伏着，死死扣住对手，看上去真的像一只蜘蛛，正准备把身下那个受折磨的少年吸干。我记得，光是看着这一幕就让我一阵恶心。</p>
<p>面对事态这突如其来的转折，我们全都不知所措。约翰转过头来，目光正好同我相遇。我从未在一张孩子的脸上见过如此傲慢、如此丑陋的权力欲。</p>
<p>我们互相凝视了几秒。显然，我的眼神把自己的恐惧全暴露了出来，因为他的情绪迅速改变。愤怒肉眼可见地褪去，先变成好奇，继而又变成出神。突然，约翰发出了他那种谜一样的笑声。里面没有胜利的味道，反而有一种自嘲，或许还有一点敬畏。</p>
<p>他放开受害者，站起身说：“起来吧，老斯蒂芬。抱歉把你气得失去理智。”</p>
<p>可是斯蒂芬已经昏过去了。</p>
<p>我们始终没弄清这场冲突到底是怎么回事。我们问约翰，他只说：“已经结束了。忘了它吧。可怜的老斯蒂芬！不过不，我自己不会忘。”</p>
<p>几天以后，我们再问斯蒂芬，他说：“我根本不愿意想。其实是我的错，我现在明白了。不知怎么，我突然就疯了——偏偏那时他还打算格外友善。可是，被那么一个小孩子打败！……但他根本不是小孩子，他是闪电。”</p>
<p>我并不假装自己有能力理解约翰，不过关于他，我确实忍不住形成过一两个理论。就这件事而言，我的看法是这样：当时他显然正经历一个高度集中的自我肯定阶段。然而我不相信，自从割草机那件事以后，他就一直怀着报复心。我认为他只是冷静地决定，要同自己认识的人里最强大的一个较量力量——或者更准确地说，较量技巧；而为了达到这个目的，他刻意而又巧妙地一步步把倒霉的斯蒂芬刺激到暴怒。至于约翰自己的愤怒，我怀疑完全是人为制造的。他发现自己在一种冷酷的狂怒状态下打得更好，于是就把这种状态主动制造出来。依我看，这场最高级别的测试绝不能是一场友好的比试，而必须是一场真正像野兽搏斗那样绝望、凶猛的冲突。好吧，约翰终于得到了自己想要的。而一旦得到，他又在一瞬间、彻底地看穿了它，也看到了它以外的东西。至少我是这样相信的。</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" lang="en" hidden>
<h3><span id="a333"></span>CHAPTER III. ENFANT TERRIBLE</h3>
<p><span class="t28">A</span><span class="t31">T</span><span class="t30"> </span><span class="t31">LAST</span><span class="t30">, </span><span class="t31">AT</span><span class="t30"> </span>the age of six, John turned his attention to locomotion. In this art he had hitherto been even more backward than the appearance of his body seemed to warrant. Intellectual and constructive interests had led to the neglect of all else.</p>
<p>But now at last he discovered the need of independent travel, and also the fascination of conquering the new art. As usual, his method of learning was original and his progress rapid. He never crawled. He began by standing upright with his hands on a chair, balancing alternately on each foot. An hour of this exhausted him, and for the first time in his life he seemed utterly disheartened. He who had treated mathematicians as dull-witted children now conceived a new and wistful respect for his ten-year-old brother, the most active member of the family. For a week he persistently and reverently watched Tommy walking, running, “ragging” with his sister. Every movement was noted by the anxious John. He also assiduously practised balancing, and even took a few steps, holding his mother’s hand.</p>
<p>By the end of the week, however, he had a sort of nervous breakdown, and for days afterwards he never set foot to ground. With an evident sense of defeat, he reverted to reading, even to mathematics.</p>
<p>When he was sufficiently recovered to take the floor again, he walked unaided right across the room, and burst into hysterical tears of joy — a most un-John-like proceeding. The art was now conquered. It was only necessary to strengthen his muscles by exercise.</p>
<p>But John was not content with mere walking. He had conceived a new aim in life; and with characteristic resolution he set himself to achieve it.</p>
<p>At first he was greatly hampered by his undeveloped body. His legs were still almost fetal, so short and curved they were. But under the influence of constant use, and (seemingly) of his indomitable will, they soon began to grow straight and long and strong. At seven he could run like a rabbit and climb like a cat. In general build he now looked about four; but something wiry and muscular about him suggested an urchin of eight or nine. And though his face was infantile in shape, its expression was sometimes almost that of a man of forty. But the huge eyes and close white wool gave him an ageless, almost an inhuman look.</p>
<p>He had now achieved a very striking control of his muscles. There was no more learning of skilled movements. His limbs, nay the individual muscles themselves, did precisely as he willed. This was shown unmistakably when, in the second month after his first attempt to walk, he learned to swim. He stood in the water for a while watching his sister’s well-practised strokes, then lifted his feet from the bottom and did likewise.</p>
<p>For many months John’s whole energy was given to emulating the other children in various kinds of physical prowess; and in imposing his will upon them. They were at first delighted with his efforts. All except Tommy, who already realized that he was being outclassed by his kid brother. The older children of the street were more generous, because they were at first less affected by John’s successes. But increasingly John put them all in the shade.</p>
<p>It was of course John, looking no more than a rather lanky four-year-old, who, when a precious ball had lodged in one of the roof-gutters, climbed a drain-pipe, crawled along the gutter, threw down the ball; and then for sheer joy clambered up a channel between two slopes of tiles, and sat astraddle on the crest of the roof. Pax was in town, shopping. The neighbours were of course terrified for the child’s life. Then John, foreseeing amusement, simulated panic and inability to move. Apparently he had quite lost his head. He clung trembling to the tiles. He whimpered abjectly. Tears trickled down his cheeks. A local building contractor was hurriedly called up on the phone. He sent men and ladders. When the rescuer appeared on the roof, John “pulled snooks” at him, and scuttled for his drain-pipe, down which he descended like a monkey, before the eyes of an amazed and outraged crowd.</p>
<p>When Thomas learned of this escapade, he was both horrified and delighted. “The prodigy,” he said, “has advanced from mathematics to acrobatics.” But Pax said only, “I do wish he wouldn’t draw attention to himself.”</p>
<p>John’s devouring passion was now personal prowess and dominance. The unfortunate Tommy, formerly a masterful little devil, was eclipsed and sick at heart. But his sister Anne adored the brilliant John, and was his slave. Hers was an arduous life. I can sympathize with her very keenly, for at a much later stage I was to occupy her post.</p>
<p>John was now either the hero or the loathed enemy of every child in the neighbourhood. At first he had no intuition of the effect his acts would have on others, and was regarded by most as a “beastly cocky little freak.” The trouble was simply that he always knew when others did not, and nearly always could when others could not. Strangely he showed no sign of arrogance; but also he made no effort to assume false modesty.</p>
<p>One example, which marked the turning-point in his policy towards his fellows, will show his initial weakness in this respect, and his incredible suppleness of mind.</p>
<p>The big schoolboy neighbour, Stephen, was in the next garden struggling with a dismembered and rather complicated lawn-mower. John climbed the fence, and watched for a few minutes in silence. Presently he laughed. Stephen took no notice. Then John bent down, snatched a cog-wheel from the lad’s hands, put it in place, assembled the other parts, turned a nut here and a grub-screw there, and the job was finished. Stephen meanwhile stood in sheepish confusion. John moved toward the fence saying, “Sorry you’re no good at that sort of thing, but I’ll always help when I’m free.” To his immense surprise, the other flew at him, knocked him down twice, then pitched him over the fence. John, seated on the grass rubbing various parts of his body, must surely have felt at least a spasm of anger, but curiosity triumphed over rage, and he inquired almost amiably, “Why did you want to do that?” But Stephen left the garden without answering.</p>
<p>John sat meditating. Then he heard his father’s voice indoors, and rushed to find him. “Hi! Doc!” he cried, “if there was a patient you couldn’t cure, and one day some one else came and cured him, what would you do?” Thomas, busy with other matters, replied carelessly, “Dunno! Probably knock him down for interfering.” John gasped, “Now just why? Surely that would be very stupid.” His father, still preoccupied, answered, “I suppose so, but one isn’t always sensible. It depends how the other fellow behaved. If he made me feel a fool, I’m sure I’d want to knock him down.” John gazed at his father for some time, then said, “I see!”</p>
<p>“Doc!” he suddenly began again, “I must get strong, as strong as Stephen. If I read all those books” (glancing at the medical tomes), “shall I learn how to get frightfully strong?” The father laughed. “I’m afraid not,” he said.</p>
<p>Two ambitions now dominated John’s behaviour for six months, namely to become an invincible fighter, and to understand his fellow human beings.</p>
<p>The latter was for John the easier task. He set about studying our conduct and our motives, partly by questioning us, partly by observation. He soon discovered two important facts, first that we were often surprisingly ignorant of our own motives, and second that in many respects he differed from the rest of us. In later years he himself told me that this was the time when he first began to realize his uniqueness.</p>
<p>Need I say that within a fortnight, John was apparently a changed character? He had assumed with perfect accuracy that veneer of modesty and generosity which is so characteristic of the English.</p>
<p>In spite of his youth and his even more youthful appearance John now became the unwilling and unassuming leader in many an escapade. The cry was always, “John will know what to do,” or “Fetch that little devil John, he’s a marvel at this kind of job.” In the desultory warfare which was carried on with the children of the Council School (they passed the end of the street four times a day), it was John who planned ambushes; and John who could turn defeat into victory by the miraculous fury of an unexpected onslaught. He was indeed an infant Jove, equipped with thunder-bolts instead of fists.</p>
<p>These battles were partly a repercussion of a greater war in Europe, but also, I believe, they were deliberately fostered by John for his own ends. They gave him opportunities both for physical prowess and for a kind of unacknowledged leadership.</p>
<p>No wonder the children of the neighbourhood told one another, “John’s a great little sport now,” while their mothers, impressed more by his manners than his military genius, said to one another, “John’s a dear these days. He’s lost all his horrid freakishness and conceit.”</p>
<p>Even Stephen was praiseful. He told his mother, “That kid’s all right really. The hiding did him good. He has apologized about the mower, and hoped he hadn’t jiggered it up.”</p>
<p>But fate had a surprise in store for Stephen.</p>
<p>In spite of his father’s discouragement, John had been spending odd moments among the medical and physiological books. The anatomical drawings interested him greatly, and to understand them properly, he had to read. His vocabulary was of course very inadequate, so he proceeded in the manner of Victor Stott, and read through from cover to cover, first a large English dictionary, then a dictionary of physiological terms. Very soon he became so fluent that he had only to run his eye rapidly down the middle of a printed page to be able to understand it and retain it indefinitely.</p>
<p>But John was not content with theory. One day, to Pax’s horror, he was found cutting up a dead rat on the dining-room floor, having thoughtfully spread a newspaper to protect the carpet. Henceforth his anatomical studies, both practical and theoretical, were supervised by Doc. For a few months John was enthralled. He showed great skill in dissection and microscopy. He catechized his father at every opportunity, and often exposed the confusion of his answers; till at last Pax, remembering the mathematicians, insisted that the tired doctor must have respite. Henceforth John studied unaided.</p>
<p>Then suddenly he dropped biology as he had dropped mathematics. Pax asked, “Have you finished with ‘life’ as you finished with ‘number?’ ” “No,” replied John, “but life doesn’t hang together like number. It won’t make a pattern. There’s something wrong with all those books. Of course, I often see they’re stupid, but there must be something deeper wrong too, which I can’t see.”</p>
<p>About this time, by the way, John was actually sent to school, but his career lasted only three weeks. “His influence is too disturbing,” said the head mistress, “and he is quite unteachable. I fear the child, though apt in some limited directions, is really subnormal, and needs special treatment.” Henceforth, to satisfy the law, Pax herself pretended to teach him. To please her, he glanced at the school books, and could repeat them at will. As for understanding them, those that interested him he understood as well as the authors; those that bored him he ignored. Over these he could show the stupidity of a moron.</p>
<p>When he had finished with biology, John gave up all intellectual pursuits and concentrated on his body. That autumn he read nothing but adventure stories and several works on jiu-jitsu. Much of his time he spent in practising this art, and in gymnastic exercises of his own invention. Also he dieted himself extremely carefully upon principles of his own. John’s digestive organs had been his one weak spot. They seemed to remain infantile longer even than the rest of his body. Up to his sixth year they were unable to cope with anything but specially prepared milk, and fruit juice. The food-shortage caused by the war had added to the difficulty of nourishing John, and he was always liable to minor digestive troubles. But now he took matters into his own hands, and worked out an intricate but very scanty diet, consisting of fruit, cheese, malted milk, and whole-meal bread, carefully spaced with rest and exercise. We laughed at him; all but Pax, who saw to it that his demands should be fulfilled.</p>
<p>Whether through diet, or gymnastics, or sheer strength of will, he certainly became exceptionally strong for his weight and age. One by one the boys of the neighbourhood found themselves drawn into a quarrel with John. One by one they were defeated. Of course it was not strength but agility and cunning that made him fit to cope with opponents much bigger than himself. “If that kid once gets hold of you the way he wants, you’re done,” it used to be said, “and you can’t hit him, he’s too quick.”</p>
<p>The strange thing was that in every quarrel it seemed to the public that not John but the other was the aggressor.</p>
<p>The climax was the case of Stephen, now captain of his school’s First Fifteen, and a thoroughly good friend to John.</p>
<p>One day when I was talking to Thomas in his study we heard an unusual scuffling in the garden. Looking out, we saw Stephen rushing vainly at the elusive John; who, as he leapt aside, landed his baby fist time after time with dire effect on Stephen’s face. It was a face almost unrecognizable with rage and perplexity, shockingly unlike the kindly Stephen. Both combatants were plastered with blood, apparently from Stephen’s nose.</p>
<p>John too was a changed being. His lips were drawn back in an inhuman blend of snarl and smile. One eye was half closed from Stephen’s only successful blow, the other cavernous like the eye of a mask. For when John was enraged, the iris drew almost entirely out of sight.</p>
<p>The conflict was so unprecedented and so fantastic that for some moments Thomas and I were paralysed. At last Stephen managed to seize the diabolic child; or was allowed to seize him. We dashed downstairs to the rescue. But when we reached the garden, Stephen was lying on his stomach writhing and gasping, with his arms pinned behind him in the grip of John’s tarantula hands.</p>
<p>The appearance of John at that moment gave me a startling impression of something fiendish. Crouched and clutching, he seemed indeed a spider preparing to suck the life out of the tortured boy beneath him. The sight, I remember, actually made me feel sick.</p>
<p>We stood bewildered by this unexpected turn of events. John looked around, and his eye met mine. Never have I seen so arrogant, so hideous an expression of the lust of power as on that childish face.</p>
<p>For some seconds we gazed at one another. Evidently my look expressed the horror that I felt, for his mood rapidly changed. Rage visibly faded out and gave place first to curiosity then to abstraction. Suddenly John laughed that enigmatic laugh of his. There was no ring of triumph in it, rather a note of self-mockery, and perhaps of awe.</p>
<p>He released his victim, rose and said, “Get up, Stephen, old man. I’m sorry I made you lose your hair.”</p>
<p>But Stephen had fainted.</p>
<p>We never discovered what it was all about. When we questioned John, he said, “It’s all over. Let’s forget about it. Poor old Stephen! But no, I won’t forget.”</p>
<p>When we questioned Stephen a few days later, he said, “I can’t bear to think of it. It was my fault, really. I see that now. Somehow I went mad, when he was intending to be specially decent, too. But to be licked by a kid like that! But he’s not a kid, he’s lightning.”</p>
<p>Now I do not pretend to be able to understand John, but I cannot help having one or two theories about him. In the present case my theory is this. He was at this time plainly going through a phase of concentrated self-assertion. I do not believe, however, that he had been nursing a spirit of revenge ever since the affair of the mower. I believe he had determined in cold blood to try his strength, or rather his skill, against the most formidable of his acquaintances; and that with this end in view he had deliberately and subtly goaded the wretched Stephen into fury. John’s own rage, I suspect, was entirely artificial. He could fight better in a sort of cold fury, so he produced one. As I see it, the great test had to be no friendly bout, but a real wild-beast, desperate encounter. Well, John got what he wanted. And having got it, he saw, in a flash and once for all, right through it and beyond it. So at least I believe.</p>
