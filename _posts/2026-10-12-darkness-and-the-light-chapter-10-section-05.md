<h2>五、绝望与新的希望</h2>
<p>宏伟计划原本如此，可一个无法预见的事件使它彻底受挫。大约在预定高潮——也就是完全停止进食——前一年，已经衰弱而老化的人口中出现一种陌生的新疾病。我始终无法确定，它的根源究竟完全属于自然、完全内在于我们这个物质宇宙——也就是我们这片“雪花”——还是某些我无法理解的幽暗力量，以某种方式侵入我们的宇宙，刺激或直接制造了这场骇人的瘟疫。疾病本身的形式，以及它恰好出现的时机，看起来几乎经过精密计算，专门为了破坏光明即将取得的胜利。</p>
<p>疾病最初的症状是剧烈呕吐和腹泻。痉挛有时猛烈到食道和直肠都可能撕裂，甚至翻出体外。许多患者在这个初始阶段便死亡。活下来的人则出现严重的腺体紊乱，由此可能产生各种可怕症状中的一种或多种。极为常见的一种，是飞速发生的衰老：一个年轻男子只需几周就会变成牙齿掉光、喃喃自语的老头。身体与心智的幼儿化也几乎同样常见。另一种后果，是骨骼疯狂生长，以至于被过度撑紧的肌肉与皮肤在四肢各处裂开，露出白骨。可骨质软化同样是常见症状：四肢会在本来不该弯曲的位置折弯，头颅也软得像一个过熟橙子。或者，皮肤会不停生长，最后变成一件宽大松垮的衣服；患者甚至可能被自己腿上拖曳的皮肤褶皱绊倒。另一种常见结果，是性别特征迅速混乱。男人明显出现女性特征，女人则越来越男性化。也许最令人痛苦的，是性欲经常以荒诞方式被无限夸大。生殖器官严重肿胀，女性乳房等第二性征也膨大到令人厌恶的程度。肉体中过度旺盛的性冲动完全奴役心智，以至于每一个物体、每一个概念都被赋予性的意义；即使自律最强的人，也会被卷入持续不断的淫乱和各种性变态之中。腺体紊乱的另一些后果则纯粹属于情绪。有些患者反复陷入毫无对象、疯狂爆发的暴怒；另一些则被无理性的恐惧，或同样无理性的逞强支配。有时，一阵突然袭来的仇恨会迫使患者杀死或折磨恰好在身边的人。有时，一种永久而冰冷的仇恨会集中到某个完全无辜的受害者身上。疾病也可能表现成伤感得令人恶心的多愁善感，感情对象可能是某个人、某种动物、整个人类，甚至是患者为了满足特殊心理需要而自行虚构出的某位神祇。一种常见效果，是疯狂害怕独处；另一种恰好相反，患者会对他人的存在产生极端恐惧，以至于有人突然来访时，他可能从高层窗户跳出去，或者像受惊的鸟一样一头撞死在墙上。还有一种后果，是感官能力被大幅削弱。患者失明、失聪，失去味觉和嗅觉，也几乎失去触觉；这个悲惨生物只剩下一种还能让自己产生些微兴趣的感觉——疼痛。于是他会带着笨拙而病态的急切，掀掉自己的指甲，压碎自己的眼球，把舌头咬成血肉模糊的烂肉。</p>
<p>这些症状有些永久存在，有些几周后便会消退。然而所有病例最后都会进入同一种永久情绪状态：极深的冷漠。最严重的人除了满足进食和排泄等肉体需要之外，对任何事情都不再关心；甚至连这些需要也可能无法引起兴趣，如果无人照料，他们会从早到晚一动不动地躺着。如此极端的病例不算常见，可平均而言，疾病造成的损害虽然没有那么显眼，却几乎同样灾难性。多数患者最终能恢复到足以在一切简单动物冲动上表现正常，却再也无法从那些真正具有人类特色的活动中获得任何满足。于是，冲动性的动物依恋他们还能体验，但持久、真正意识到对方是独立主体的人类之爱却完全超出能力。其他较不亲密的真正共同体关系也一样不可能。过去形成的社会行为习惯仍会继续存在，起初甚至能让患者在熟悉的社交场景里看起来毫无变化；可是火已经熄灭。渐渐地，就连体面社会行为的外壳也会被抛弃。抽象思考——即使他们的智力仍足以完成——也会令他们感到无聊到难以忍受。艺术对他们不再具有任何意义。更准确地说，他们在理智上可能仍能理解艺术技巧，却再也不会受到感动。精神生活在他们看来完全愚蠢。那项过去曾热情接受的伟大共同纪律与冒险，如今只会令他们打哈欠、耸肩。他们在理智上明白那是什么，却对它毫无感觉。</p>
<p>不同类型的心灵，对这种自身深层变化作出不同反应。所有人都在过去已经形成的精神习惯与新的性情之间经历强烈冲突。许多人半心半意地努力恢复过去那种感受方式，却因失败而迷惑、压抑。有些人虽然内在的光已经熄灭，仍无精打采地继续旧有行为形式，只是越来越敷衍。另一些人成了举止得体的犬儒主义者。还有一些人逐渐对一切曾经无比珍贵、如今却已经无法感受的事物产生冷酷而恶毒的仇恨，对那些没有被疾病摧毁的人产生不肯罢休的报复欲。仇恨有时似乎反而给他们带来一种新的强烈感受，于是成为生命的主导动机，驱使他们竭尽所能使仍忠于光明的人痛苦，并使他们失败。</p>
<p>这种疾病还有一个严重特征，起初并没有被人发现，直到资料越来越多才渐渐显现。总体而言，情感发展程度最高的人虽然较不容易感染，可一旦微生物真正突破防线，他们受到的损害反而远比一般人严重。最初抵抗越强，一旦崩溃，他们越容易在疾病早期直接死亡。处在另一端、情感发展程度最低的人则完全相反：他们极容易感染，却很容易恢复，后遗症也较轻。年轻人特别容易染病；不过只要挺过第一阶段，往往恢复得很好，不仅较少留下严重后遗症，有时甚至能够避开最终那种冷漠状态。</p>
<p>为了准备圣年，医疗服务此前已经大幅削减，因此治疗和研究瘟疫根源都受到严重妨碍。疾病似乎最初在雨季的马来亚出现，很快便扩散到亚洲，再传遍每一块大陆。短短几个月内，数百万人死亡，又有更多数百万人虽然活下来，却只能作为无助的病人或残疾者继续生活。整片整片人口在身体健康恢复之后，情感能力却已经退化成冷漠或犬儒。研究证明，这种疾病由一种微生物造成，它寄居在雨滴、河流和湖泊之中。多云的大气与丰沛降雨尤其有利于传播。微生物从口腔进入人体，在消化器官中繁殖，再通过血液进入各类腺体。如果能够足够早发现，消灭它并治愈病人其实非常简单：大量饮酒即可。于是，一代已经把自己奉献给最高精神生活的人，竟被迫靠把烦恼浸进酒精来拯救生命。</p>
<p>圣年不得不推迟。这是一个极其严重的决定，因为人口正在老去，而且已经没有儿童。但除此之外别无选择。禁止生育的命令被取消，各民族都被号召尽可能多生孩子。可那些已经陷入冷漠的人群，对这个号召几乎没有反应。</p>
<p>与此同时，疾病继续传播，只是速度有所减慢，毒力也逐渐下降。瘟疫还有一个奇怪特征，让人怀疑真正的敌人并非微生物本身，而是某种在背后指挥攻击的恶魔式智慧。例如人们发现，一旦某个地区刚刚肃清疾病，往往很快会出现一段坏天气。被污染的雨水重新浸透土地、灌满水库。此外，把每个月病例分布绘成地图以后，人们发现微生物的推进方式带有令人吃惊的目的性。瘟疫不仅仿佛受到人口密集地区吸引；为了抵达一个巨大人口中心，它甚至会伸出一道由湿润天气和感染构成的巨大“伪足”，横跨原本干旱的沙漠。从亚洲向南非扩张时，这一点尤其醒目。伊朗仍在遭受瘟疫时，一条暴雨天气形成的巨大舌状地带便跨过阿拉伯沙漠和阿比西尼亚，伸进湿润的中非；随后恶劣天气继续南移，直到触及南非人口稠密区域。为了进入美洲，它似乎曾数次尝试从英国跨越大西洋，可“人工制造”出来的东风都被当地盛行西风击败。最终，一条云带却从西非伸向亚马孙河流域，并从那里扩散遍整个美洲。澳大利亚则从东印度群岛的原始疫区受到侵袭。唯独新西兰，它似乎始终没有“发现”。</p>
<p>这种看似有目的的行动可能只是错觉。某种自然原因完全可能造成这样的现象。然而，如果再考虑到疾病偏偏在人类因普遍营养不足而身体抵抗力最低、同时精神力量尚未完全发展的时候发动攻击，那么假设背后存在某种隐秘的邪恶目的，似乎也并非毫无道理。</p>
<p>直到世界人口多数已经被削弱为冷漠状态，瘟疫才最终受到控制。大多数国家里，能够保持完整人类精神素质的人不超过大约百分之三；而这些人又常常因为邻人的麻木而深受打击，最后自己也陷入迟钝。只有两个地区基本不受影响：西藏由于气候异常干燥、海拔很高，同时人口发展水平也较高而幸免；新西兰则纯粹因为瘟疫没有“发现”它。</p>
<p>拉萨明智地放弃恢复圣年的一切希望，号召全人类暂时把主要力量投入生育和重建物质文明。新西兰热烈响应。其他地方也有小团体和孤立个人真诚接受号召，其余人则不是口头赞成而什么也不做，就是根本不理会。</p>
<p>普遍的懒散使多数国家的村庄生活严重退化。亚原子农业和手工业仍然继续，却做得马虎敷衍。普布里的生活逐渐退化得像我们这个时代的酒馆，有时甚至远远更不健康。许多曾经靠酒精治愈的人已经对这种成瘾性药物形成依赖，也根本不试图控制。懒散、缺乏进取心的淫乱十分普遍，但避孕又阻止了真正生育。幸存的前行者也懒洋洋维持着旧生活形式，精神实质却已经消失。迟钝不可避免地使一切社会行为与制度迅速恶化。追逐私利和群众盲从这些旧恶再次出现，只是失去了过去那种活力和激情。人口持续下降，因为几乎没有孩子出生；只有西藏和新西兰例外，那里每一个育龄女性都以献身精神每年生一个孩子。不久，研究发现一种能够稳定获得三胞胎的方法，于是出生率立刻提高到三倍。即使所有照护、技术和荣誉都集中到母亲身上，这种负担仍极其沉重。她们却坚持完成任务；尽管产妇死亡率很高，人口还是迅速增长。儿童当然得到一切可能的优待，并由国家监督。两个民族的全部社会组织都围绕他们的利益重新安排。</p>
<p>后来，来自西藏和新西兰的移民开始大量进入其他国家，与当地残余人口通婚，并重新组织那些垂死的社会。村庄生活逐渐恢复活力。文明世界熟悉的一切活动再次展开。前行者也重新开始探索；不过眼下当然完全谈不上恢复那场已经放弃的精神冒险。整个人类最主要的任务，是恢复自身力量，并找出阻止瘟疫再次发生的方法。因为后来仍会偶尔出现小型疫情，而且每当、也几乎只在前行者工作最活跃的地点与时刻发生。仿佛精神探索的先锋恰恰因为自己的成功而感染疾病。即使某些人已经进化到本身具有免疫力，他们似乎也会变成携带者——甚至是微生物真正的“生成者”？——通过呼吸污染周围空气。</p>

<h2>v. DESPAIR AND NEW HOPE</h2>
<p>SUCH WAS THE great plan, but an unforeseen event frustrated it. About a year before the appointed climax and the complete cessation of eating there appeared among the frail and ageing population a new and strange disease. I was never able to determine whether its source was wholly natural, wholly intrinsic to our physical cosmos, to our snowflake, or whether in some manner beyond my comprehension some obscure powers of darkness had somehow made incursion into our cosmos to stimulate or create this hideous epidemic. Its form and the time of its onset seemed nicely calculated to undermine the impending victory of the light.</p>
<p>The first symptom of the disease was violent vomiting and diarrhoea. So formidable were the spasms that the gullet and rectum might be torn and even forced outwards. Many patients succumbed in this initial phase. Those that recovered were left with terrible glandular disturbances which might result in any or several of a number of frightful symptoms. A very common trouble was galloping senescence, which turned the young man into a maundering and toothless gaffer in a few weeks. But infantilism of body and mind was almost as common. Another effect was an extravagant growth of the skeleton, such that the overstrained flesh and skin would split on every limb, revealing the bare bone. But a softening of the bony structure was also a frequent symptom, causing the limbs to bend in unnatural places and the head to turn as soft as an over-ripe orange. Or the skin might grow till it became a loose voluminous garment. Sufferers were often in danger of tripping on the folds of skin trailing from their own legs. Another frequent result was rapid confusion of sex. Men would visibly acquire female characters, women would turn mannish. Most distressing of all, perhaps, was the frequent and fantastic exaggeration of sexuality. The organs became grossly distended. The secondary sexual characters, such as the female breasts, were repulsively enlarged. The mind became so enslaved to the pressure of the body’s superabundant sexuality that every physical object and every concept became charged with sexual meaning, and even the most self-disciplined found themselves swept away in a continuous orgy of fornication and all kinds of perversion. Other consequences of glandular disorder were purely emotional. Some sufferers were obsessed by recurrent fits of objectless and frantic rage, others by irrational terror or equally irrational bravado. Sometimes a sudden access of hate would force the patient to kill or torture whoever was at hand. Sometimes a permanent and icy hatred would be concentrated on a wholly innocent victim. The disease might take the form of maudlin sentimentality, directed either on human persons or animals, or the human race as a whole, or some fictitious deity invented to suit the patient’s peculiar needs. One common effect was a crazy dread of isolation. Another was such panic fear of the presence of other human beings that, when the patient was surprised by a visitor, he might leap out of an upper window or dash himself against the wall like a terrified bird. Yet another effect was a reduction of sensibility. Blind and deaf, without taste and smell, almost without touch, the wretched creature would snatch a morbid pleasure from the only sense that remained to rouse him to some faint interest, namely pain. With fumbling eagerness he would tear back his finger-nails, crush his eyes, bite his tongue to bloody pulp.</p>
<p>Some of these symptoms were permanent, some passed off in a few weeks. But in every case the final emotional state was identical and permanent. The patient emerged into profound apathy. In extreme cases he cared for nothing but the satisfaction of bodily needs of nutrition and excretion. Even these might cease to interest him, so that, if left to himself, he might lie inert from morning till night. Such extreme cases were uncommon, but on the average the damage caused by the disease, though less obvious, was scarcely less disastrous. Most people recovered so far as to behave in a normal manner in respect of all simple animal impulses, but they no longer found any satisfaction whatever in the activities which are distinctively human. Thus an impulsive animal affection might be within their reach, but persistent and genuinely other-conscious human love was beyond them. Impossible also were all the other, less intimate forms of true community. Old habits of community-behaviour would persist and might at first carry the sufferers through the familiar social situations without any manifest change; but the fire was quenched. Little by little even the forms of decent social behaviour were abandoned. Abstract thought, even when their intelligence was still capable of it, they found unutterably boring. Art had no longer any meaning for them. Or rather, though intellectually they might still understand its technique, it could no longer stir them. The life of the spirit was wholly fatuous to them. The great common discipline and adventure, which they formerly accepted with enthusiasm, now stimulated them only to yawn and shrug their shoulders. Intellectually they understood it, but they had no feeling for it.</p>
<p>Different types of mind reacted differently to this deep change in themselves. All suffered from a severe conflict between their established mental habit and their new disposition. Many put up a half-hearted struggle to feel in the old way, and were bewildered and oppressed by their failure. Some, though the inner light was extinguished, listlessly carried on all the old forms of behaviour, but with increasing slovenliness. Others became well-bred cynics. Others gradually conceived a cold and spiteful hatred of all that was once so precious to them and now escaped them, and a relentless vindictiveness against those who had not been affected by the disease. Hate sometimes seemed even to provide them with a new intensity of feeling, and become the dominant motive of their lives, leading them to do all in their power to distress and defeat those who were still faithful to the light.</p>
<p>One serious aspect of the disease was not at first realized. It emerged into view as data accumulated. On the whole the emotionally most developed individuals, though rather less susceptible, were also, when the microbe secured a hold on them, far more gravely damaged. Their initial resistance was greater, but once it had been broken down, they were specially liable to die in the early phase. At the other end of the scale the lowest emotional types, though very liable to contract the disease, recovered easily and suffered only mild after-effects. The young were specially susceptible, though if they succeeded in surmounting the first phase of the disease, they tended to make a good recovery, escaping serious after-effects, and sometimes even the final apathy.</p>
<p>In preparation for the sacred year the medical services had been greatly reduced. Both cure and research into the causes of the plague were seriously hampered. It seems to have begun in Malaya during the wet season. Thence it soon spread into Asia, and into every continent. Within a few months millions had died and more millions had recovered only to live on as helpless invalids or cripples. Whole populations, though their bodily health was restored, were emotionally reduced to apathy or cynicism. Research proved that the disease was caused by a micro-organism which infested rain-drops, rivers, lakes. A cloudy atmosphere and a heavy rainfall were peculiarly favourable to the spread of the plague. The microbe entered the human body by the mouth, multiplied in the digestive organs, and spread thence by way of the blood into the glands. If it was detected early enough it could be destroyed, and the patient cured by a very simple method, namely the drinking of large quantities of alcohol. Thus it came about that a generation which had consecrated itself to the most exalted life was forced to drown its troubles in drink.</p>
<p>The sacred year had to be postponed. This was a very grave step, for the population was ageing, and there were no children. But no other course was possible. The ban on procreation was removed, and the peoples were urged to have as many children as possible. The apathetic populations made little response to this appeal.</p>
<p>Meanwhile the disease continued to spread, though less rapidly, and with decreasing virulence. One strange aspect of the scourge suggested that the real enemy was not the micro-organism itself but some devilish intelligence which was directing its attack. It was noticed, for instance, that when a district had been cleared of the disease, a spell of bad weather was apt to occur. Contaminated rain drenched the ground and filled the reservoirs. Moreover, maps plotting the incidence of the disease from month to month had revealed a startlingly purposive movement in the advance of the microbe. Not only was the plague mysteriously attracted to populous districts, but in order to reach a great centre of population it might extend a vast pscudopodium of wet weather and infection, even across an arid desert. This was particularly striking in its advance from Asia to South Africa. While Iran was in the throes, a great tongue of drenching weather was protruded across the Arabian Desert and Abyssinia into moist Central Africa. Thence the bad weather extended southwards till it reached the crowded areas in South Africa. In order to reach America it appeared to make several attempts to bridge the Atlantic from Britain, but its ‘artificial’ east winds were overcome by the prevailing westerlies. Finally, however, it stretched out an arm of cloud from West Mica to the Amazon, whence it spread throughout the Americas. Australia it invaded from its original foothold in the East Indies. New Zealand it failed to discover.</p>
<p>This seeming purposiveness may have been illusory. Some natural cause may very well have produced it. But when it is taken in conjunction with the fact that the disease attacked the human race just when its physical resistance was weakest owing to universal under-nourishment, and when its spiritual power was not yet fully developed, some occult evil purpose seems plausible.</p>
<p>The plague was not finally stamped out until a majority of the world population had been reduced to apathy. In most countries not more than about three in a hundred persons retained their full human calibre, and these became generally so disheartened by their neighbours apathy that they too sank into lethargy. Two regions alone were unaffected, namely Tibet, through the fortunate combination of its exceptionally dry climate, its altitude, and the high development of its population and New Zealand, which the plague had not ‘discovered’.</p>
<p>Lhasa wisely abandoned all hope of restoring the sacred year, and called upon mankind to devote itself for the present mainly to reproduction and the re-establishment of material civilization. New Zealand responded eagerly. Elsewhere small groups and isolated individuals answered the call in full sincerity. The rest either professed agreement and did nothing, or ignored the appeal.</p>
<p>Owing to the prevailing lethargy, village life in most countries gravely deteriorated. Sub-atomic agriculture and handicrafts were still carried on, but in a slipshod manner. The life of the poobs degenerated into something like the life of the pubs in our own day, often into something far less wholesome. Many persons who had been cured by alcohol had contracted an addiction to this habit-forming drug, and maqe no effort to restrain themselves. Fornication of a lazy, unenterprising sort, was general, but procreation was prevented by birth-control. The surviving forwards indolently carried on the outward forms of their old life, but its spirit was lost. Sluggishness inevitably produced a rapid deterioration in all social behaviour and institutions. The old vices of self-seeking and mob mentality reappeared, but without the old vigour and passion. Population steadily declined, for very few children were born; save in Tibet and New Zealand, where every woman of child-bearing age was devotedly producing a child every year. Presently research discovered a method of securing triplets, and the birth-rate was promptly trebled. Under the strain, and in spite of all the care and skill and honour that was lavished on them, the mothers were heavily overstrained. They clung to their task, however, and though maternal mortality was high, population increased rapidly. The children were of course given every possible advantage, under state supervision. The whole social organization of the two peoples was arranged for their benefit.</p>
<p>There came a time when emigrants from Tibet and New Zealand were flooding into other countries to intermarry with the remnants of the native populations, and to reorganize their moribund society. Gradually village life was revitalized. All the familiar activities of the civilized world were once more afoot. The forwards once more explored; though for the present there was no question of reviving the abandoned spiritual venture. The main task of the race was to recover its strength and to find out how to prevent any recurrence of the plague. For there were occasional incipient epidemics. They occurred whenever and wherever the work of the forwards was most active. It was as though the pioneers of spiritual activity contracted the disease through the very success of their adventure. Even if they happened to be individuals of so developed a type that they were immune, they apparently became carriers (or actual generators?) of the microbe, infecting the atmosphere through their breath.</p>
