<h2>第二十二章</h2>
<p>卡莫迪站在一片修剪得整整齐齐的平原上。头顶是蓝天，一轮金黄色的太阳悬在空中。他慢慢环顾四周。大约半英里外，有一座小城。</p>
<p>这座城市并不是按照美国城市通常的方式建起来的——没有最外围的加油站，没有像触手一样伸出去的热狗摊，也没有一圈汽车旅馆，更没有废车场构成的防护外壳。它更像某些意大利山城，也像一些瑞士村庄：毫无铺垫地突然出现，又毫无解释地戛然而止，城市主体一下子整个呈现在眼前，丝毫不作缓冲。</p>
<p>尽管外观很异国，卡莫迪却确信自己看到的是一座美国城市。于是他朝那里走去，步子很慢，感官高度警觉，一旦发现什么不对便准备立刻逃跑。</p>
<p>不过，一切似乎都很正常。这座城市显得温暖、开阔；街道规划得很宽裕，店铺宽大的凸窗也有种坦率直白的气质。越往里走，卡莫迪越发现不少令人愉快的东西。刚进城不久，他便走进一座广场，和罗马的广场一模一样，只是小得多。广场中央有一座喷泉，喷泉里立着一尊大理石雕像：一个男孩抱着海豚，清澈的水流从海豚嘴里喷出来。</p>
<p>“真希望你喜欢。”一个声音从卡莫迪左肩后方说道。</p>
<p>卡莫迪没有受惊跳起来，甚至都没猛地转身。他已经习惯有声音从自己背后开口。有时候他觉得，银河里相当多的东西似乎都喜欢用这种方式接近他。</p>
<p>“挺不错。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“是我亲自建造并安放在这里的。”那个声音说。“我觉得，喷泉这个概念虽然古老，在审美上却依旧具有功能性。至于这个广场——长椅、遮阴的栗树等等——则复制自博洛尼亚的一种样式。我同样没有因为害怕过时而束缚自己。在我看来，真正的艺术家需要什么就用什么，无论那东西已有一千年历史，还是刚诞生一秒钟。”</p>
<p>“我赞赏这种态度。”卡莫迪说。“请允许我自我介绍。我叫托马斯·卡莫迪。”他微笑着转过身，伸出手去。</p>
<p>可他左肩后面没有人，右肩后面也没有。整个广场空空荡荡，视野之内一个人影都没有。</p>
<p>“请原谅。”那个声音说。“我不是故意吓你。我还以为你知道。”</p>
<p>“知道什么？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“知道我的事。”</p>
<p>“这个我可不知道。”卡莫迪说。“你是谁？又是从哪里跟我说话？”</p>
<p>“我是这座城市的声音。”那个声音说。“或者换一种说法，我就是这座城市本身——真正的城市本体——正在和你说话。”</p>
<p>“是吗？”卡莫迪讥讽地说。随后他自己回答自己：“对，我想这确实就是事实。好吧，你是一座城市。了不起啊！”</p>
<p>事实上，卡莫迪已经有点烦了。他遇见过太多规模宏大、力量神奇的存在。从银河这一头到那一头，他不断被更高一级的东西压过一头。各种力量、造物和人格化存在没完没了地跳出来，一次又一次把他的镇定搅得粉碎。</p>
<p>卡莫迪是个讲道理的人；他知道星际社会也有啄食次序，而人类在这张排行榜上的位置不怎么靠前。但他同样是个自尊心很强的人。他相信，一个人总得代表点什么——哪怕只代表他自己。一个人总不能一天到晚对周围那些非人存在“哦！”“啊！”“我的老天！”地惊叹个不停；那样下去，什么自尊都剩不住。</p>
<p>卡莫迪相当看重自己的自尊。到了这个阶段，那已经是他所剩无几的财产之一。</p>
<p>于是，卡莫迪转身离开喷泉，悠闲地穿过广场，仿佛他一辈子每天都在和城市聊天，而且对此已经有点厌倦。他走过几条街，又沿几条大道往上走；扫了几眼商店橱窗，估量了一下房屋大小；经过雕像时也会停一停，不过只停很短时间。</p>
<p>“怎么样？”过了一阵，城市问。</p>
<p>“什么怎么样？”卡莫迪立刻反问。</p>
<p>“你觉得我怎么样？”</p>
<p>“还行。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“只是还行？”</p>
<p>“听着，”卡莫迪说，“城市就是城市。看过一座，基本也就看遍了。”</p>
<p>“这不是真的！”城市有些恼火地说。“我和其他城市有明确区别。我是独一无二的。”</p>
<p>“是吗？”卡莫迪不屑地说。“在我看来，你不过是一堆拼装得不怎么样的零件。一个意大利广场，两尊希腊风格的雕像，一排都铎式住宅，一栋老纽约公寓楼，一个做成拖船形状的加州热狗摊——天知道还有些什么。这有什么独一无二的？”</p>
<p>“这些形式被组合成一个有意义的整体，这一点就是独一无二。”城市说。“我在内在一致性的框架中呈现多样性。你要明白，这些较老的形式并不是时代错置；它们代表不同的生活风格，因此完全适合被纳入一台精心打造的居住机器。”</p>
<p>“那只是你的看法。”卡莫迪说。“顺便问一句，你有名字吗？”</p>
<p>“当然。”城市说。“我叫贝尔韦瑟，是新泽西州一个依法注册的自治镇。你想喝杯咖啡吗？也许再来个三明治，或者一些新鲜水果？”</p>
<p>“咖啡听起来不错。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>他任由贝尔韦瑟的声音指引自己拐过街角，来到一家露天咖啡馆。咖啡馆名叫“哦，你这小子”，完全复制了十九世纪九十年代沙龙酒吧的样子：蒂芙尼灯、刻花玻璃吊灯、自动钢琴，一样不少。和卡莫迪在城里见到的其他一切一样，这里洁净得一尘不染，却没有任何人。</p>
<p>“气氛不错，你不觉得吗？”贝尔韦瑟问。</p>
<p>“挺坎普的。”卡莫迪评价道。“喜欢这一套的人大概会觉得不错。”一只不锈钢托盘降到桌面，上面放着一大杯泡沫丰厚的卡布奇诺。“不过至少服务很好。”卡莫迪补充道。他啜了一口咖啡。</p>
<p>“好喝吗？”贝尔韦瑟问。</p>
<p>“嗯，很好。”</p>
<p>“我的咖啡一直让我颇为自豪。”贝尔韦瑟安静地说。“还有我的厨艺。真不想吃点什么？煎蛋卷？或者舒芙蕾？”</p>
<p>“什么都不要。”卡莫迪坚决地说。他往椅背上一靠，问道：“所以你是座模范城市，嗯？”</p>
<p>“是的，我有幸承担这一身份。”贝尔韦瑟说。“我是所有模范城市中最新的一座，而且我相信，也是最令人满意的一座。最初构想我的，是耶鲁大学和芝加哥大学组成的联合研究小组，他们当时拿着洛克菲勒基金的资助。我的大多数实用细节由麻省理工学院设计，不过某些特殊部分来自普林斯顿和兰德公司。实际建造工程由通用电气负责，经费则来自福特基金会以及其他几家我无权透露名称的机构。”</p>
<p>“履历倒挺有意思。”卡莫迪用一种令人难以忍受的漫不经心语气说。“街对面那栋是哥特式大教堂吧？”</p>
<p>“是的，完全哥特式。”贝尔韦瑟说。“同时也是跨教派的，对所有信仰开放，设计座位容量三百人。”</p>
<p>“这么大的建筑，只坐三百人，好像不算多。”</p>
<p>“当然不算。可我的构想是把庄严感和温馨感结合起来。很多人都很喜欢。”</p>
<p>“说起来，人都在哪儿？”卡莫迪问。“我一个都没看见。”</p>
<p>“他们走了。”贝尔韦瑟悲伤地说。“全都离开了。”</p>
<p>“为什么？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>贝尔韦瑟沉默了一阵，然后说：“城市与社区关系发生了破裂。其实只是误会；或者更准确地说，是一连串不幸的误会。我怀疑有煽动分子在这次出走中起了作用。”</p>
<p>“可到底发生了什么？”</p>
<p>“我不知道。”贝尔韦瑟说。“我真的不知道。有一天，他们就这么全走了。就这么一下子！不过我相信他们会回来的。”</p>
<p>“那可难说。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“我对此深信不疑。”贝尔韦瑟说。“不过眼下，卡莫迪先生，你为什么不先住在这里呢？”</p>
<p>“我？我真不觉得——”</p>
<p>“你看上去旅途劳顿。”贝尔韦瑟说。“休息一下肯定对你有好处。”</p>
<p>“最近确实一直在赶路。”卡莫迪承认。</p>
<p>“谁知道呢，也许你会发现自己很喜欢这里。”贝尔韦瑟说。“无论如何，你都能获得一种独一无二的体验：让全世界最现代、最先进的城市专门为你服务。”</p>
<p>“听起来确实挺有意思。”卡莫迪说。“我得考虑一下。”</p>
<p>贝尔韦瑟这座城市的确勾起了他的兴趣。</p>
<p>但他同样感到不安。</p>
<p>他真希望自己知道，这座城市原来的居民究竟发生了什么。</p>
</section>
<section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="en" lang="en" aria-label="English original" hidden>
<p><b>CHAPTER 22</b></p>
<p>Carmody was standing on a neatly trimmed plain, beneath a blue sky, with a golden-yellow sun overhead. He looked around slowly. Half a mile ahead of him he saw a small city. This city was not constructed in the common manner of an American city – with outliers of gas stations, tentacles of hot-dog stands, fringe of motels, and a protective shell of junkyards; but rather, as some Italian hill towns are fashioned, and some Swiss villages as well, suddenly rising and brusquely ending, without physical preamble or explanation, the main body of the town presenting itself all at once and without amelioration.</p>
<p>Despite its foreign look, Carmody felt sure that he was looking at an American city. So he advanced upon it, slowly and with heightened senses, prepared to flee if anything was amiss.</p>
<p>All seemed in order, however. The city had a warm and open look; its streets were laid out generously, and there was a frankness about the wide bay windows of its store fronts. As he penetrated deeper, Carmody found other delights, for just within the city he entered a piazza, just like a Roman piazza, although much smaller; and in the middle of the piazza there was a fountain, and standing in the fountain was the marble representation of a boy with a dolphin, and from the dolphin’s mouth a stream of clear water issued.</p>
<p>‘I do hope you like it,’ a voice said from behind Carmody’s left shoulder.</p>
<p>Carmody did not jump with alarm. He did not even whirl around. He had become accustomed to voices speaking from behind his back. Sometimes it seemed to him that a great many things in the galaxy liked to approach him that way.</p>
<p>‘It’s very nice,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘I constructed it and put it there myself,’ the voice said. ‘It seemed to me that a fountain, despite the antiquity of the concept, is aesthetically functional. And this piazza, with its benches and shady chestnut trees, is copied from a Bolognese model. Again, I did not inhibit myself with the fear of being old-fashioned. The true artist, it seems to me, uses what he finds necessary, be it a thousand years old or one second new.’</p>
<p>‘I applaud your sentiment,’ Carmody said. ‘Permit me to introduce myself. I am Thomas Carmody.’ He turned, smiling, his hand outstretched. But there was no one behind his left shoulder, or behind his right shoulder, either. There was no one in the piazza, nobody at all in sight.</p>
<p>‘Forgive me,’ the voice said. ‘I didn’t mean to startle you. I thought you knew.’.</p>
<p>‘Knew what?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Knew about me.’</p>
<p>‘Well, I don’t,’ Carmody said. ‘Who are you and where are you speaking from?’</p>
<p>‘I am the voice of the city,’ the voice said. ‘Or to put it another way, I am the, city itself, the veritable city, speaking to you.’</p>
<p>‘Is that a fact?’ Carmody said sardonically. ‘Yes,’ he answered himself, ‘I suppose it is a fact. So all right, you’re a city. Big deal!’</p>
<p>The fact was, Carmody was annoyed. He had encountered too many entities of great magnitude and miraculous power. He had been one-upped from one end of the galaxy to the other. Forces, creations and personifications had jumped out at him without cessation, causing him time and time again to lose his cool. Carmody was a reasonable man; he knew there was an interstellar pecking order, and that humans did not rate very high on it. But he was also a proud man. He believed that a man stood for something, if only for himself. A man couldn‘t very well go around all the time saying ‘Oh!’ and ‘Ah!’ and ‘Bless my soul!’ to the various inhuman entities that surrounded him; he couldn‘t do that and keep any self-respect. Carmody cared more than a little for his self-respect. It was, at this point, one of the few things he still possessed.</p>
<p>Therefore, Carmody turned away from the fountain and strolled across the piazza like a man who conversed with cities every day of his life, and who was slightly bored with the whole thing. He walked down various streets and up certain avenues. He glanced into store windows and noted the size of houses. He paused in front of statuary, but only briefly.</p>
<p>‘Well?’ the city said after a while.</p>
<p>‘Well what?’ Carmody answered instantly.</p>
<p>‘What do you think of me?’</p>
<p>‘You’re OK,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Only OK?’</p>
<p>‘Look,’ Carmody said, ‘a city is a city. When you’ve seen one, you’ve pretty much seen them all.’</p>
<p>‘That’s untrue!’ the city said, with some show of pique. ‘I am distinctly different from other cities. I am unique.’</p>
<p>‘Are you indeed?’ Carmody said scornfully. ‘To me you look like a conglomeration of badly assembled parts. You’ve got an Italian piazza, a couple Greek-type statues, a row of Tudor houses, an old-style New York tenement, a California hot-dog stand shaped like a tugboat, and God knows what else. What’s so unique about that?’</p>
<p>‘The combination of those forms into a meaningful entity is unique,’ the city said. ‘I present variety within a framework of inner consistency. These older forms are not anachronisms, you understand; they are representative styles of living and as such are appropriate in a well-wrought machine for living.’</p>
<p>‘That’s <i>your</i> opinion,’ Carmody said. ‘Do you have a name, by the way?’</p>
<p>‘Of course,’ the city said. ‘My name is Bellwether. I am an incorporated township in the State of New Jersey. Would you care to have some coffee and perhaps a sandwich or some fresh fruit?’</p>
<p>‘The coffee sounds good,’ Carmody said. He allowed the voice of Bellwether to guide him around the corner to an open-air café. The café was called ‘O You Kid’ and was a replica of a Gay Nineties saloon, right down to the Tiffany lamps and the cut-glass chandelier and the player piano. Like everything else that Carmody had seen in the city, it was spotlessly clean, but without people.</p>
<p>‘Nice atmosphere, don’t you think?’ Bellwether asked.</p>
<p>‘Campy,’ Carmody pronounced. ‘OK if you like that sort of thing.’ A foaming mug of cappuccino was lowered to his table on a stainless-steel tray. ‘But at least the service is good,’ Carmody added. He sipped the coffee.</p>
<p>‘Good?’ Bellwether asked.</p>
<p>‘Yes, very good.’</p>
<p>‘I rather pride myself on my coffee,’ Bellwether said quietly. ‘And on my cooking. Wouldn’t you like a little something? An omelette, perhaps, or a soufflé?’</p>
<p>‘Nothing,’ Carmody said firmly. He leaned back in his chair and said, ‘So you’re a model city, huh?’</p>
<p>‘Yes, that is what I have the honour to be,’ Bellwether said. ‘I am the most recent of all model cities and, I believe, the most satisfactory. I was conceived by a joint study group from Yale and the University of Chicago, who were working on a Rockefeller fellowship. Most of my practical details were devised by MIT, although some special sections of me came from Princeton and from the RAND Corporation. My actual construction was a General Electric project, and the money was procured by grants from the Ford Foundation, as well as several other institutions I am not at liberty to mention.’</p>
<p>‘Interesting sort of history,’ Carmody said, with unbearable nonchalance. ‘That’s a Gothic cathedral across the street, isn’t it?’</p>
<p>‘Yes, completely Gothic,’ said Bellwether. ‘Also, interdenominational and open to all faiths, with a designed seating capacity for three hundred people.’</p>
<p>‘That doesn’t seem like much for a building that size.’</p>
<p>‘It’s not, of course. But my idea was to combine awesomeness with cosiness. Many people liked it.’</p>
<p>‘Where are the people, by the way?’ Carmody asked. ‘I haven’t seen any.’</p>
<p>‘They have left,’ Bellwether said mournfully. ‘They have all departed.’</p>
<p>‘Why?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>Bellwether was silent for a while, then said, ‘There was a breakdown in city-community relations. A misunderstanding, really; or perhaps I should say, an unfortunate series of misunderstandings. I suspect that rabble-rousers, played a part in the exodus.’</p>
<p>‘But what <i>happened</i>, precisely?’</p>
<p>‘I don’t know,’ Bellwether said. ‘I really don’t know. One day they simply all left. Just like that! But I’m sure they’ll be back.’</p>
<p>‘I wonder,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘I am convinced of it,’ Bellwether said. ‘But for the nonce, why don’t you stay here, Mr Carmody?’</p>
<p>‘Me? I really don’t think –’</p>
<p>‘You appear to be travel-weary,’ Bellwether said. ‘I’m sure the rest would do you good.’</p>
<p>‘I have been on the move a lot recently,’ Carmody admitted.</p>
<p>‘Who knows, you might find that you liked it here,’ Bellwether said. ‘And in any event, you would have the unique experience of having the most modern, up-to-date city in the world at your service.’</p>
<p>‘That does sound interesting,’ Carmody said. ‘I’ll have to think about it.’</p>
<p>He was intrigued by the city of Bellwether. But he was also apprehensive. He wished he knew exactly what had happened to the city’s occupants.</p>
