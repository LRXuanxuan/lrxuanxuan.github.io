<h2>ii. 哲学狐猴</h2>
<p>你们的历史学家在追溯这场战争的起源时，通常只会追到此前一个世纪。他们挑出这样一些偶然因素：一位大公遇刺，一位皇帝的野心，两个彼此嫉妒民族之间的宿怨，一个新帝国咄咄逼人的成长和一个旧帝国的抵抗，种族文化之间的不相容，唯物主义造成的精神堕落，或者两个相互竞争的经济体系之间不可避免的冲突。</p>
<p>这些因素，每一个从某种意义上说都是战争的原因；可是，若说其中任何一个，或者它们全部加起来，就构成了战争真正而本质的原因，那也不过比说“战争之所以发生，是因为有人移动钢笔，签下了第一份动员令”高明一点。</p>
<p>海王星观察者当然会把这些事实一一记下，但他还会继续越过它们，寻找那个更加普遍、更加深层的原因——正是因为有了这个原因，那些较小的诱因才有机会发生作用。他甚至不会停在欧洲各民族于战争前许多个世纪中的诞生。对于你们彼此屠杀的解释，他最终会把它看成你们物种那场漫长精神戏剧中的一个关键事件。</p>
<p>我们的观察者已经通过直接检查，追踪了人从类猿祖先中逐步醒来的全部阶段。事实上，随着我们的技术不断进步，我们的探索甚至能够沿着世系继续后退，进入人类尚未成为类猿动物以前的祖先。最出色的研究者甚至已经成功进入过极遥远过去几个孤立个体的心灵——那时哺乳动物还没有真正从爬行动物中出现。</p>
<p>他们品尝过所有人与兽那些冷血祖先所能拥有的迟缓、僵硬心态；也以强烈对比品尝过另一种全新的温暖，以及意识里最初那一点轻灵闪动——那是最早的试探性哺乳动物开始长期生活在一种近似“持续发烧”的高体温状态时，第一次被点燃的经验。</p>
<p>不过，为了理解你们的战争，我们没有必要把过去追得那么远；只需回到猿类从前猿类祖先中出现的阶段即可。</p>
<p>即使在如此早期的年代，我们已经能观察到心智成长中的两个主题。它们共同构成第一人类整个历史的核心动机，也构成你们如今困境的钥匙。</p>
<p>第一个主题，是对外部世界越来越清楚的认识；第二个主题，则是对人类精神自身性质的洞察——而后者增长得慢得多。</p>
<p>第一个主题虽然过程中也有起伏，却总体不断取得胜利，因为它只不过是另一件事情在心理层面的表现：你们通过智能掌握物质自然，而正是这种能力使你们这个物种取得支配地位。</p>
<p>第二个主题，则依赖于把智能应用到另一个领域：欲望和恐惧构成的内部世界。与理解外部世界不同，这种内在洞察并没有直接的生存价值，所以它的发展断断续续。也正因为这种“内部智慧”很早以前就已经被“外部知识”远远甩在后面，最终才会出现你们的战争，以及战争后来引起的一切后果。</p>
<p>我们的观察者细致研究过这场跨越漫长时代的戏剧里所有关键事件。甚至在那些昏暗的前猿类心灵中，我们也会搜寻每一代里那些超越同类的个体：无论他们超前的是对外部世界的实际掌握与理解，还是对内部自我的认识。</p>
<p>就像拿一块磁铁在垃圾堆表面扫过，可以把其中所有铁屑吸出来一样，我们也可以把自己的心灵调到略高于所研究时代平均水平的“实际智能”或“自我意识”程度，于是便能从庞大众生中挑出那些心智确实领先同时代人的稀有个体。</p>
<p>当然，我们也发现，这些天才里只有少数成功把自己的成就传给未来；更多人则被不利环境完全压倒。</p>
<p>在前猿类心灵之间漫游时，我们曾发现过一次令人惊讶的精神繁盛。它的悲剧历史，对于理解你们自身那种完全不同的命运很有意义。</p>
<p>早在真正的猿类出现以前，地球上曾生活着一种眼睛很大的小型狐猴。它比那种后来终于产生猿与人的微小跗猴更为发达。像跗猴一样，它生活在树上，行为主要由双眼立体而细致的视觉所支配；也像跗猴一样，它拥有取之不尽的躁动好奇心。</p>
<p>不过，与跗猴不同，它经常会陷入一阵阵安静与内省。</p>
<p>命运特别眷顾这个种族。时间以几万年、几十万年流逝，它们逐渐产生体形较大、脑容量也大得多的个体。它们很快达到类人猿水平的智能，随后又超过了那个水平。</p>
<p>这个种族中，有一天诞生了一个属于自身类型的天才。他既具有异常突出的实践智能，也具有异常突出的内省能力。成年以后，他发现可以用一根树枝把够不到的果实打下来。</p>
<p>这个新发明使他如此快乐，以至于每次完成动作、其他较不聪明的同伴已经争抢落果时，他还会因为单纯陶醉于技巧本身，继续在那里挥舞树枝。他不仅品味那个物理事件，也品味自己对它产生的快乐。</p>
<p>有时，他甚至会掐自己一下，用熟悉的痛苦同这种新鲜喜悦形成鲜明对照。随后，他会陷入一种深沉而兴奋的“冥想”——如果我们可以把那种尚未形成任何理论、只是单纯品尝自己全新心理过程的活动叫作冥想的话。</p>
<p>一天傍晚，当他又这样沉浸其中时，一只树栖猫科动物把他吃掉了。</p>
<p>幸运的是，这位早期哲学家留下了后代。从这些后代中，通过一连串幸运突变，最终产生了一个脑容量很大的非猿类种族。你们的地质学家至今还没有挖到它们那点稀少遗骸，也还没有把它们登记为进化主干上一条旁支。</p>
<p>在整个地球研究领域里，这些生物比任何别的对象都更让我们的探索者着迷。</p>
<p>它们对于自我认识与相互理解的能力，起初不过是突变偶然造成的怪异结果，从生物学意义上说完全没有用途。可仅仅因为这种能力在遗传上恰好同具有巨大生存价值的实践智能联系在一起，它也跟着得到了发展；终于，它在一件从人类角度看近乎奇迹的事件里证明了自己的价值。</p>
<p>在这个新种族发展的早期，它一度面临分裂成一团彼此战争部落的危险。每个部落都嫉妒地守护自己的果树林，又不断袭击邻居。大量果实在争斗中被毁掉，饥饿和相互屠杀也开始折磨这个神经非常敏感的种族。人口逐渐下降。</p>
<p>就在这时，奇迹发生了。</p>
<p>一位非凡的雌性——她是整个种族最伟大的天才——成功组织了一次停战，并召集所有部落来到一片林中空地周围的树上聚会。</p>
<p>在那里，在一些同盟者和一大堆果实的帮助下，她表演了一出惊人的哑剧。可以说，那是后来一切布道和一切宣传戏剧的祖先。</p>
<p>首先，她用舞蹈和长声呼喊，把观众引入一种近似催眠、又极度紧张专注的状态。随后，她仅靠姿势和富有感情色彩的声音，就在他们心里煽起强烈的部落激情。</p>
<p>当树冠里各群体眼看就要彼此扑上去开战时，她突然改变了表演的调子。</p>
<p>单凭惊人的表演能力，她向那些困惑、却仍然能够理解她的同类展示了自己对仇恨的强烈厌恶与痛苦；展示了自己如何审视内心、剖析自我；也展示了自己怎样发现那些此前一直被忽略的深层欲望。</p>
<p>她没有任何语言工具，只有姿势和声调，而其中一部分甚至必须当场创造。可她仍然赞美了爱与美，赞美纯粹认知，也赞美精神和平。</p>
<p>被迷住的观众不自觉地模仿她每个动作。每一个人都在自己心里认出了她正在表达的那种更深天性。</p>
<p>结果有些像你们后来所谓的宗教复兴大会，只不过里面完全没有任何超自然成分。</p>
<p>就在高潮时，这位已经精疲力竭的女先知无法承受巨大感情压力，心脏衰竭而死。于是，她用死亡给自己的福音盖上了最后一道印章。</p>
<p>这件事本身已经够像奇迹，更惊人的却是后果。这个种族仿佛只迈出一步，就突然达到了一种自我认识与相互忠诚的高度；第一人类后来终其历史都徒劳追求这种水平，而正因为你们从未真正达到，我也几乎无法向你们准确描述它是什么样子。</p>
<p>这些可爱的狐猴在全盛时期，也发展出一种粗糙的物质文化。它们把编枝住所搭在树杈之间，使用简单木制工具，也制作陶器，把器皿像果实一样挂在枝头。它们甚至学会种树，以扩大自己的森林；还会把果实晒干，储存在内衬黏土的篮子里。</p>
<p>不过，在我们的观察者看来，这种实践能力远不如与之相连的那种早熟内在生活有趣。</p>
<p>大自然把它们放在一座巨大而繁茂的岛屿上，可以说格外优待。它们唯一的敌人是大型猫科动物，而狐猴最终用陷阱把它们捕到灭绝。</p>
<p>与大陆上那些后来成为猿和人祖先的近亲不同，这些狐猴既没有过度强烈的性欲，也没有过度强烈的攻击性。因此，它们的人口始终保持有限，感情上则高度统一。</p>
<p>在社会性方面，它们甚至可以同蚂蚁相比；但它们的社会性是有智能、有自我意识的。基础不是盲目的群居本能，而是彼此洞察。</p>
<p>当它们建立了一种非常朴素的舒适生活标准，并形成相对稳定的社会秩序之后，它们那种天生的自我认识能力和相互理解能力，又同对杂技动作以及视觉形式的真正审美趣味结合起来，打开了一个充满精致精神冒险的世界。</p>
<p>它们发展出歌曲和有清楚音节的语言，发展出树上的舞蹈，也发展出一种以编织和陶土为基础的抽象艺术。</p>
<p>人格本身，以及人格之间交流的花朵，在整个种族中普遍开放。它们开始进行概括和推测，方式虽然天真，却很有希望。通过冥想与自我纪律，它们还主动培养神秘体验。</p>
<p>最早研究它们的观察者，曾带着惊讶、甚至近乎敬畏的心情，感受到这些长尾巴、柔软毛皮、生活在树上的生物，怎样摸索着走向、甚至超过你们的苏格拉底和耶稣所达到的某些精神直觉。</p>
<p>甚至可以说，这个在基督纪元以前几百万年就已经繁盛过的种族，是地球上唯一真正存在过的“基督徒种族”。</p>
<p>事实上，它们是一个由精神天才组成的种族。假如它们能够在野蛮世界的压力中保存下来，那么完全可能达到一种远比第一人类后来真正达到过的任何精神状态更加优美的心灵形式。</p>
<p>但这不可能发生。</p>
<p>在它们延续的一百万年里，只有极其罕见的好运才使这个种族一次次逃过灭绝。它们取得地位，靠的是实践智能，而不是自我认识；在进化早期阶段，自我认识对一个必须不断战斗求生的物种来说，事实上往往更像障碍，而不是帮助。</p>
<p>随着长期安全与僵化社会状态的形成，它们在实际应变方面的灵活性逐渐衰退。终于，一场海底地质隆起把它们所在的岛屿变成半岛，这些温和狐猴的末日也随之到来。</p>
<p>一大群仍然半猿化的人类祖先涌进了这个小小天堂。</p>
<p>这些入侵者的感知远比当地居民粗糙，真正的智能也更低；可是，它们体形更大、更强壮、更好斗，而且完全没有怜悯。它们长着獠牙，浑身多毛，最终依靠数量和肌肉力量压倒了狐猴；也依靠狡诈，因为它们早已掌握一整套狐狸般的阴险手段。</p>
<p>狐猴只有一种武器：善意。而在当时那种情况下，善意毫无作用。</p>
<p>更糟的是，它们此时在情感上已经发展得过于精致，以至于看见鲜血就会昏厥。若其中一个被敌人抓住并杀死，旁边那些惊恐同伴会像成熟果实一样从树上掉下来，摔断脖子。</p>
<p>不到十年，整个种族便彻底灭绝。</p>
<p>当狐猴败给一个更强健、更外向的物种时，命运之书上便已经写下：迟早一定会出现一种像你们如今这样复杂的世界局势，并由此引发你们即将经历的这一整个世界大战时代。</p>
<p>从狐猴覆灭到你们这个时代之间，实践智能一直在缓慢前进，但从长远看，它取得了压倒性的胜利。智能的另一种用途——把智能转向内部生活——却始终只零星发展，往往只是行动智能增长时偶然附带的结果。</p>
<p>于是，在你们整个物种历史中，人类的动机几乎始终保持原始。</p>
<p>即使到了你们自己的时代，原始冲动在理智上已经被看出不足，你们也悲哀地努力走向更高的欲望；可你们的心仍然是猿的心，因此这种挣扎注定徒劳。</p>
<p>当然，从宇宙完美性的角度看，你们的挣扎一点也不徒劳。无论是英勇努力，还是命中注定的失败，你们都忠实完成了自己在存在这场戏剧中的角色。</p>
<p>但如果只从整个种族继续发展的角度看，这场挣扎确实是徒劳的，注定失败。你们如今已经隐约看见：人的意志必须经历一场极深刻的重组；可这场重组已经不可能及时完成，无法阻止灾难。</p>
<p>当世界仍然原始，而人控制物质自然的能力还很弱时，这种内在生活的贫乏虽说本身令人惋惜，却并不会威胁整个物种生存。</p>
<p>可到了你们的时代，人已经获得相当强大的力量。一方面，他把世界建设得如此复杂、如此具有爆炸性，以至于这个世界已经有摆脱其实际智能控制的危险；另一方面，也是更严重的一点，这个世界如今已经变成这样：只要那些古老而盲目的动机继续按照原样运作，就足以把它彻底毁掉。</p>
<p>唯一有可能拯救你们的，是一种远为彻底的自我认识能力。可这种能力在你们物种中只在极少数情况下开过花；而在你们这个发热般高速运转的时代，它还越来越受到工业社会日常惯例的挤压和扼杀。</p>
</section>

<h2>ii. THE PHILOSOPHICAL LEMURS</h2>
<p>YOUR HISTORIANS , WHEN they seek to trace the origin of your war, refer only to events which took place within the previous century. They single out such accidents as the murder of an archduke, the ambition of an emperor, the vendetta of two jealous nations, the thrusting growth of a new empire and the resistance of an old, the incompatibility of racial cultures, the debasing effects of materialism, or the inevitable clash of rival economic systems. Each of these factors was in some sense a cause of your war; but to assert that any of them or all of them together constituted the essential cause would be scarcely more profound than to say that the war was brought about by the movement of the pen that signed the first mobilization order. The Neptunian observer, though he duly notes these facts, seeks behind them all for the more general and more profound cause, by virtue of which these lesser causes were able to take effect. He looks further even than the birth of the nations of Europe many centuries before the war. He finds the explanation of your mutual slaughter by regarding it as a crucial incident in the long-drawn-out spiritual drama of your species.</p>
<p>Our observers have traced by direct inspection all the stages in the awakening of man out of his ape-like forerunner. Indeed, as our technique advances we are able to press back our exploration even along the generations of man’s pre-simian ancestors. Our most brilliant workers have actually succeeded in entering a few isolated individuals of a much more remote past, when the mammal had not yet emerged from the reptile. They have savoured the sluggish and hide-bound mentality that alone was possible to the cold-blooded forefathers of all men and beasts. They have also savoured by contrast th new warmth and lambent flicker of experience which was kindled when the first tentative mammal began living in a chronic fever. But for the understanding of your war it is unnecessary to go further into the past than the emergence of the ape from the pre-simian.</p>
<p>Even at that early age we observe two themes of mental growth which together constitute the vital motif of the career of the first human species, and the key to your present plight. These themes are the increasing awareness of the external world, and the more tardily increasing insight into the nature of the human spirit itself. The first theme is one of fluctuating but triumphant progress, since it was but the mental aspect of the intelligent mastery of physical nature which brought your species to dominance. The second theme depended on the application of intelligence to another sphere, namely the inner world of desires and fears. Unlike apprehension of the external, it had no survival value, and so its progress was halting. Because this inner wisdom had long ago been outstripped by the outer knowledge, there came at last your war and all its consequences.</p>
<p>Our observers have studied minutely all the crucial events of this age-long drama. Searching even among the dark pre-simian minds, we have singled out in every generation every individual that has been in advance of his kind in respect either of outward prowess and apprehension or of inward self-knowledge. As one may extract any fragments of iron from a heap of rubbish by passing a magnet over its surface, so we, with our minds set to a slightly higher degree of practical intelligence or of self-consciousness than that of the generation under study, have been able to single out from the mass those rare individuals whose minds were definitely in advance of their contemporaries. We find, of course, that of these geniuses some few have succeeded in handing on their achievement to the future, while many more have been defeated by adverse circumstance.</p>
<p>Roaming among the pre-simian minds, we discovered one surprising efflorescence of mentality whose tragic story is significant for the understanding of your own very different fate. Long before the apes appeared, there was a little great-eyed lemur, more developed than the minute tarsier which was ultimately to produce ape and man. Like the tarsier, it was arboreal, and its behaviour was dominated by its stereoscopic and analytic vision. Like the tarsier also, it was gifted with a fund of restless curiosity. But unlike the tarsier, it was prone to spells of quiescence and introversion. Fortune favoured this race. As the years passed in tens of thousands and hundreds of thousands it produced large individuals with much larger brains. It passed rapidly to the anthropoid level of intelligence, and then beyond. Among this race there was once born an individual who was a genius of his kind, remarkable both for practical intelligence and for introspection. When he reached maturity, he discovered how to use a stick to’ beat down fruit that was beyond his reach. So delighted was he with this innovation, that often, when he had performed the feat, and his less intelligent fellows were scrambling for the spoils, he would continue to wave his stick for very joy of the art. He savoured both the physical event and his own delight in it. Sometimes he would pinch himself, to get the sharp contrast between his new joy and a familiar pain. Then he would fall into a profound and excited meditation, if such I may call his untheorized tasting of his new mental processes. One evening, while he was thus absorbed, a tree-cat got him. Fortunately this early philosopher left descendants; and from them arose, in due course and by means of a series of happy mutations, a race of large-brained and non-simian creatures whose scanty remains your geologists have yet to unearth, and catalogue as an offshoot of the main line of evolution.</p>
<p>These beings have intrigued our explorers more than anything else in the whole terrestrial field. Their capacity for self-knowledge and mutual insight sprang from the vagaries of mutation, and was at first biologically useless. Yet, merely because in this species it was genetically linked to the practical intelligence which had such great survival value, it developed; until at last it justified itself in an event which from the human point of view seems almost miraculous. At an early stage the new race threatened to break up into a confusion of warring tribes, each jealously preserving its own fruit groves and raiding its neighbours. There was wholesale destruction of fruit. Starvation and mutual slaughter began to tell upon the nerves of this highly strung race. Population declined. Then it was that the miracle occurred. A certain remarkable female, the supreme genius of her race, organized a truce, and a concourse of all the tribes in the trees around a forest glade. Here, with the help of confederates and a heap of fruit, she performed an amazing pantomime, which might be called the forerunner of all sermons and of all propaganda plays. First, by means of dance and ululation, she put the spectators into an hypnoidal state of tense observation. She then evoked in them, by mere gesture and emotive sounds, a fury of tribal passion. At the moment when the companies in the tree-tops seemed about to fling themselves upon one another in battle, she suddenly changed the tone of her pantomime. By sheer histrionic ability she revealed to her bewildered but comprehending fellows her own agony of revulsion from hate, her own heart-searching and self-analysis, her own discovery of profound cravings which had hitherto been ignored. With no medium but the language of gesture and tone which she had in part to improvise, she praised love and beauty, she praised pure cognizance and spiritual peace. The enthralled spectators followed her every movement with unconscious mimicry. Each one of them recognized in his own heart that deeper nature which she was expressing. The result was something like a revivalist meeting, though shorn of the supernatural. In the midst of it the exhausted prophetess, overwhelmed by the emotional strain, succumbed to heart-failure, and thus sealed her gospel with death The whole incident was miraculous enough, but still more amazing was the issue. Seemingly at one stride, the race passed to a level of self-knowledge and mutual loyalty which the first human specie: was to seek in vain, and which therefore it is not possible for me to describe to you.</p>
<p>At their height these amiable lemurs attained also a crude material culture. They had wattle dwellings, set in the forks of trees. They used rough wooden tools, and earthenware vessels, which hung from the branches like fruit. They even learned to plant trees so as to extend their forest. They dried their fruits, and stored them in clay-lined baskets. But to our observers this practical ability was less interesting than the precocious inner life with which it was linked. Nature had favoured these animals by setting them in a great luxuriant island. Their sole enemies, the great cats, they trapped to extinction. Unlike their cousins on the mainland, the ancestors of ape and man, these lemurs were neither over-sexed nor over-aggressive. Consequently their population remained limited in numbers, and united in sentiment. They rivalled the ants in sociality; but theirs was an intelligent, self-conscious sociality; whose basis was not blind gregariousness but mutual insight. When they had attained a very modest standard of amenity and a stationary social order, their native genius for self-knowledge and mutual insight combined with their truly aesthetic relish of acrobatics and of visual perception to open up a whole world of refined adventures. They developed song and articulate speech, arboreal dance, and an abstract art based on wicker-work and clay. The flowers of personality and personal intercourse bloomed throughout the race. They began to generalize and speculate, in a naive but promising manner. By meditation and self-discipline they cultivated mystical experience. It was with amazement, almost with awe, that our earliest observers felt the minds of these tailed, soft-furred arboreals grope toward and even surpass the intuitions of your Socrates and your Jesus. It might be said that these, who flourished millions of years before the Christian era, were the only truly Christian race that ever existed. They were in fact a race of spiritual geniuses, a race which, if only it could have been preserved against the stresses of a savage world, might well have attained a far lovelier mentality than was ever to be attained by the First Men.</p>
<p>This could not be. Nothing but rare good fortune had saved them from extinction throughout the million years of their career. It was by practical intelligence alone that they had won their position, not by self-knowledge, which in the early stages of evolution is indeed rather a hindrance than a help to the battling species. With security and a stereotyped social condition, their practical versatility declined. When at last a submarine upheaval turned their island into a peninsula, the gentle lemurs met their doom. A swarm of the still half-simian ancestors of man invaded the little paradise. Far less percipient than the natives, and less truly intelligent, but larger, more powerful, quarrelsome, and entirely ruthless, these fanged and hirsute brutes overpowered the lemurs by numbers and weight of muscle; by cunning also, for they had a well-established repertoire of foxy tricks. The lemurs had but one weapon, kindliness; and this in the circumstances was ineffective. Moreover they were by now emotionally refined into such fastidiousness that at the sight of blood they fainted. If one of them was caught and slaughtered by the enemy, his horrified companions would drop like ripe fruit from the trees and break their necks. Within a decade the race was extinct.</p>
<p>With the defeat of the lemurs at the hands of a hardier and a more extravert species, it was written in the book of fate that, sooner or later, a world-situation such as your own must occur, and give rise to an epoch of world wars such as you are now to suffer. Between the downfall of the lemurs and your own age there has been a slow, but in the long run triumphant, advance of practical intelligence. That other application of intelligence, namely to the inner life, has developed but spasmodically, as an accidental consequence of the growth of intelligence in action. And so it has happened that man’s motives have remained throughout the career of your species almost invariably primitive. Even in your own day, when primitive impulses are intellectually seen to be inadequate, and you struggle pathetically toward loftier desires, your hearts remain the hearts of apes, and your struggle is vain. From the point of view of cosmical perfection, of course, your struggle is not vain at all. By your gallant effort and by your fated downfall also, you fulfil your part well and truly within the drama of existence. But from the point of view of the advancement of the race your struggle is indeed vain, doomed to failure. The profound reorganization of the human will, which you now see to be necessary, cannot be achieved in time to avert disaster. While the world remained primitive, and man’s power over physical nature feeble, this paucity of the inner life, though in itself deplorable, did not imperil the survival of the race. But in your day man has gained considerable power. Not only has he made his world so complex and so explosive that it threatens to break from the control of his practical intelligence but further, and more serious, it is now such that the operation of the old blind motives may destroy it. Nothing could save you but a far more radical exercise of those capacities of self-knowledge which in your species have flowered only in rare circumstances, and in your own feverish epoch are more and more crushed out by the routine of industrial society.</p>
