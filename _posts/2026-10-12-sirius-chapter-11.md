<h3>第十一章</h3>
<h3>人类暴君</h3>
<p>1939年夏天，战争的乌云已经在欧洲上空聚集。人人都生活在对未来的恐惧中，又或者怀着几乎不敢相信的希望，盼着风暴终究会因某种奇迹而没有真正爆发。天狼星过去从不怎么关心国际局势，可如今也同无数人一样，被迫关心起来。托马斯对战争的前景也主要只是厌烦。他想不受干扰地继续自己的工作，却担心战争会使这成为不可能。当然，真要打起来，我们就必须拼尽全力取胜；可要是那些愚蠢的政治家稍微聪明、诚实一点，事情本来根本不会发展到这个地步。天狼星的态度大体也如此，只不过除此之外，他对统治物种还生出一种越来越强的愤怒：他们已经获得如此巨大的力量和机会，却把自己的事务料理得如此狼狈不堪。</p>
<p>暑假里，特雷隆一家只在威尔士住了几个星期。这个假期始终蒙着阴影，因为谁也逃不开国际局势。托马斯满心苦涩，伊丽莎白则深深忧伤。坦茜几个月前已经结婚，这次在威尔士住的一周里，整天围着报纸和无线电忧心忡忡。莫里斯如今在剑桥任教，他同坦茜就希特勒究竟有多少胜算争论不休。贾尔斯变得很沉默，正在努力让自己接受一个事实：很快他就得去打仗。普拉克茜则彻底无视战争阴云，每次话题一转到这上面，她就离开房间。天狼星把主要精力都用来恢复在剑桥和伦敦生活后衰退的体能。</p>
<p>战争爆发时，天狼星正在坎伯兰一座牧羊农场学习湖区牧羊人的工作方式。他在坎伯兰的经历很有价值，却也十分痛苦。思韦茨绝不是湖区优秀牧民的典型，结果却成了一个严苛而不讲理的主人。他让天狼星第一次真正、持续地接触到人性中一个此前几乎没有亲身经验的方面。天狼星一开始就对思韦茨有所怀疑，因为思韦茨自己的边境牧羊犬罗伊总是能躲就躲，一听见主人叫它便畏缩起来。在思韦茨对待天狼星的态度里，似乎有某种同眼前事情毫无关系、甚至本人早已忘记的冲突找到了出口。他对这条狗产生了一种无法控制的厌恶，也许是因为他强烈怀疑，天狼星根本不是普通的超级牧羊犬，而正在暗中以极严厉的眼光判断自己的人格。无论原因是什么，思韦茨很快便开始以粗暴的苛刻态度对待天狼星。我很难替托马斯如此粗心地挑选这样一个人做天狼星的指导者辩护。托马斯对自己这场伟大实验的心理层面总是惊人地迟钝；或者倒不是迟钝，只是缺乏想象力。可这一回，他没能替天狼星确保起码体面的生活条件，失误实在明显到一种程度，使我几乎倾向于认为这是故意的。难道他有意让这条狗亲身体验人类更残暴的一面？如果真是这样，他的目的可谓实现得过于彻底。</p>
<p>总之，思韦茨不断拿天狼星出气：命令他用嘴叼沉重的东西，强迫他去做各种只有人的手才能有效完成的任务，让他没完没了地干那些别扭而显然毫无必要的重活，还在同邻居聊天时恶意嘲笑他。</p>
<p>起初，天狼星甚至颇高兴自己有机会同一个脾气残暴的人类长期接触。过去，他身边直接接触的人总体上都太友善，根本算不上人类的公平样本。他需要知道，人类在更严厉、更恶劣的形态下究竟是什么样。不久麻烦便出现了，因为思韦茨发号施令时，天狼星从不畏缩。他只是平静而有效率地完成任务。这反而刺激思韦茨，哪怕找得到的借口再牵强，他也会破口大骂；而每次如此，天狼星都会带着冷淡而故意夸张的惊讶盯着他。当然，这只会让情况更糟。时间一久，思韦茨那粗暴的嗓音和整个农场的气氛都开始折磨天狼星的神经。威尔士、剑桥和伦敦那些比较温和的人际经验逐渐从意识中褪去，他竟开始觉得，思韦茨才是典型的人类。他模模糊糊地把自己戏剧化成同类反抗暴君种族的斗士。思韦茨那双粗大而残酷的手，在他眼里象征着这个无情物种征服地球上所有其他生灵的整个过程。天狼星自己明明也是猎食者，一次又一次给别的动物带来痛苦和死亡，却毫无理性地对人类赤裸裸的残酷产生了一种自以为正义的愤怒。那些人类朋友亲手灌输给他的、对弱者的同情，此刻反过来使他站到了整个人类的对立面。</p>
<p>思韦茨已经好几次拿棍子威胁天狼星，却每次都在真正动手前改变主意，因为狗的体型太大，眼神里又总有一种危险意味。随着时间推移，这个人对狗那种毫无道理的恶意越来越重。不过，最终引发灾难的事件，攻击的并不是天狼星，而是罗伊。离托马斯来接天狼星只差几天时，罗伊把一群羊赶进院子，过程出了点问题。思韦茨猛地一棍打在牧羊犬后腿上。天狼星暴怒着扑向思韦茨，把他撞倒在地；可下一刻又恢复理智，退开一步，看着那人爬起来。罗伊立刻从现场消失。思韦茨有一条根深蒂固的原则：狗一旦反抗，就必须把它们打服；而所谓打服，在他看来差不多等于打到半死。他朝雇工喊：“安德森，这畜生发疯了！过来帮我教训它！”没有回答。安德森去了很远的地方。思韦茨不是胆小鬼，但也不愿单独攻击这头体型巨大而又机警的野兽。然而，不服从必须不惜代价地压下去。再说，如此危险的动物可能造成无法估量的伤害。最好立刻把它毁掉。他可以告诉特雷隆，这条狗突然发疯，只能开枪打死。于是他走进屋里。天狼星猜到，他出来时一定会带枪。因此，他迅速跑到房门口，伏在门的一侧。思韦茨跨过门槛，四下查看院子。天狼星猛扑上去，再次把他撞倒，并用牙咬住枪。两个敌手抱作一团，在地上不断翻滚。思韦茨挣扎着站起来，试图把枪口扭向狗的身体。一根枪管响了，却没有打中；紧接着另一根也响了。天狼星松开枪，一跃退开。思韦茨把手伸进口袋，掏出两颗子弹。天狼星再次扑上去，把他撞倒，咬住他的喉咙，以强壮双颚的全部力量死死夹紧气管。温热人血的味道和男人被压住的咕噜声，使一种狂喜而不顾一切的暴怒充满了他。通过这个象征性的行动，他仿佛不仅要杀死思韦茨，也要杀死整个暴君种族。从此以后，一切走兽飞禽都应该按照自然本性活着；地球的自然秩序，再也不能被这个后来居上的物种用阴谋诡计破坏。就在一人一犬扭作一团、相互扼住喉咙时，这些念头在天狼星脑中飞快闪过。</p>
<p>过了一会儿，男人的挣扎逐渐减弱，手上的力量也松了。与此同时，天狼星的心境开始改变。狂怒退去，取而代之的是一种更冷静、更疏离的观察。归根到底，这个生物不过是在表现宇宙塑造进他体内的本性；整个相同的人类种族也是如此。那自己为什么要怀着这种愚蠢的仇恨？刺鼻的人类气味忽然使他想起普拉克茜的芬芳。血的滋味令他恶心。牙齿之间那根被压碎的气管使他恐惧。他松开嘴，退到一边，站在那里看着自己那个非犬类兄弟微弱地抽动——而他，这个该隐，已经杀死了自己的兄弟。</p>
<p>实际问题随即摆到他面前。现在，人类之手真的会毫不留情地转向他了。二十亿人的手；除了自己少数几个朋友，整个物种都会与他为敌。一阵孤独的恐慌突然攫住了他。一个独自飞越敌方领土的飞行员，脚下全是敌人，头顶只有群星，也许偶尔会感到绝望般的孤独；可那种孤独仍比不上此刻压在天狼星身上的孤独：整个人类种族都反对他，而自己的物种根本无法理解他，世界上也没有任何一个群体能安慰他、接纳他的服务。</p>
<p>他走到院里的水槽边喝了水，又把嘴唇上的血舔干净。随后，他再次站着看思韦茨；那人现在已经一动不动，脖颈被撕裂，满是鲜血。思韦茨先前拼死扼住他的脖子，使他自己的颈部也僵硬疼痛。想到自己的牙齿必定给对方造成怎样的痛苦，他不禁畏缩。他重新走到尸体旁，嗅了嗅脖颈。已经出现一丝极淡的死亡气味。那么，也就没有必要冒着自己性命危险去找医生，试图救这个人了。出于一个突然而古怪的冲动，他飞快地伸出舌头，轻轻碰了一下被自己杀死的兄弟的额头。</p>
<p>远处传来脚步声！天狼星突然惊慌失措，转身就跑，跃过院门，朝山地狂奔而去。为了防止人们带猎犬追踪，他把狐狸会用的所有伎俩都使了出来：折返自己的踪迹，跳进溪流，等等。那天夜里，他睡在一条偏僻山谷的蕨丛下。第二天，饥饿迫使他出去打猎。他设法捉到一只兔子，叼回藏身处狼吞虎咽地吃掉。剩下整天，他都躲在那里，被自己的罪行纠缠；既受折磨，又奇异地感到亢奋。那确实是一桩罪行，可同时也是一次积极的自我宣告，使他永远从主宰种族的魔力中解放出来。从此以后，他不会仅仅因为一个人是人，就害怕他。又过了两个夜晚和夹在中间的一整天，他始终藏着。然后，他出发去截住托马斯；托马斯按计划将在当天下午到农场。他极其小心地沿山地折返回去，直到能俯瞰通往思韦茨农场的公路。他选择了公路上一处既有良好遮蔽物、又恰好形成急转发夹弯的地方。汽车在那里必须把速度降到近乎步行。他藏进一小片树林的灌木中等待。偶尔有行人经过，也偶尔有汽车。终于，远处传来托马斯那辆莫里斯十型车独一无二的声音。天狼星小心爬出藏身处，先确认附近看不见别的人。没有。他走上公路。托马斯停车下车，轻快地喊了一声：“哈喽！”天狼星夹着尾巴，只说了一句：“我杀了思韦茨。”托马斯惊叫：“上帝啊！”随后瞪着他，一句话也说不出来。狗敏锐的耳朵听见远处又有人走来，于是两人退进树林讨论。最后决定，托马斯装作对惨案一无所知，照常去农场；而天狼星继续藏起来。</p>
<p>托马斯具体怎样处理这件事，没有必要详细记述。自然，他没有告诉警察自己见过天狼星。他坚决否认自己的超级牧羊犬具有危险性，还提出证据支持这一点。他坚持认为，思韦茨一定曾极其恶劣地虐待天狼星；而且事实证明，这个人本来就以带有施虐倾向的脾气闻名。显然，他先用枪攻击了动物，而且很可能还打伤了它。狗是在自卫中杀死他的。那么狗现在在哪里？这头珍贵动物大概已经因为伤势，死在某处荒山上了。</p>
<p>托马斯当时只把这部分真相告诉了天狼星。直到很久以后，这名杀人者才知道剩下的情况。实际上，事情并没有托马斯后来声称的那么顺利。执法人员始终心存怀疑，并下令一旦发现那条狗，就必须将它处死。因此，为了保护自己独一无二的犬类杰作，托马斯决定使用诡计。隔上一段适当时间后，他会通知当局：那头杀过人的野兽终于自己找回了家，并且已经按规定处死。他会牺牲一条体型较大的阿尔萨斯超级牧羊犬，再把尸体冒充成天狼星。</p>
<p>直到验尸庭审当天很晚，托马斯才在那处发夹弯接上天狼星。尽管实行了灯火管制，他们仍连夜驱车回家；一轮满月多少帮了忙。</p>

<h3><span id="a578"></span>CHAPTER XI</h3>
<p><span class="t28">I</span><span class="t30">N </span><span class="t31">THE</span><span class="t30"> </span><span class="t31">SUMMER</span><span class="t30"> </span>of 1939 the clouds of war were already gathering over Europe. Everyone was living in dread of the future or hoping against hope that by some miracle the storm would after all not break. Sirius had never taken much interest in the international situation, but now, like so many others, he was forced to be interested. Thomas also was merely bored at the prospect of war. He wanted to carry on his work unhindered, and he feared that war would make this impossible. Of course, if the clash came, we should have to do our damnedest to win; but if only the fool politicians had been more intelligent and honest there would never have been any trouble. This was roughly Sirius’s attitude also, except that in addition he felt a rising fury against the dominant species, which had been given such power and such opportunity, and yet was making such a sorry mess of its affairs.</p>
<p>During the summer vac the Trelone family spent only a few weeks in Wales. It was a clouded holiday, for there was no escape from the international situation. Thomas was embittered, and Elizabeth desperately saddened. Tamsy, who had been married some months earlier, spent her week in Wales brooding over the newspapers and the wireless set. Maurice, now a don at Cambridge, argued a great deal with Tamsy about Hitler’s chances. Giles was very quiet, adjusting himself to the idea that he would soon have to fight. Plaxy ignored the war-clouds completely, and went out of the room whenever the subject was raised. Sirius concentrated on recovering physical fitness after Cambridge and London.</p>
<p>When war broke out Sirius was on a Cumberland sheep farm learning the ways of the Lakeland shepherds. Sirius’s Cumberland experiences were valuable, but painful. Thwaites, who was by no means typical of the fine Lakeland type, turned out to be a harsh and unreasonable master. He showed Sirius an aspect of humanity with which he had never before had personal contact to any appreciable extent. Sirius suspected Thwaites at the outset because his own dog, Roy, a Border Collie, avoided him whenever possible, and cringed when spoken to. In Thwaites’s relations with Sirius some quite irrelevant and probably forgotten conflict seems to have found expression. He conceived an uncontrollable dislike for the dog, perhaps because of a strong suspicion that Sirius was no ordinary super-sheep-dog, and was privately judging his character very ruthlessly. Whatever the cause, Thwaites soon began to treat Sirius with crude harshness. I find it difficult to excuse Thomas for his gross carelessness in choosing him as Sirius’s instructor. Thomas was always surprisingly insensitive about the psychological aspect of his great experiment; or perhaps not insensitive but unimaginative. But on this occasion his failure to secure decent conditions for Sirius was so flagrant that I am inclined to attribute it to deliberate purpose. Had he determined to afford the dog some first-hand experience of the more brutal aspect of humanity? If so, his purpose was only too well fulfilled.</p>
<p>Anyhow, Thwaites constantly indulged his spite against Sirius, ordering him to carry heavy loads in his mouth, forcing on him all sorts of tasks which only a human hand could perform efficiently, overworking him with awkward jobs that were obviously unnecessary, and laughing bitterly at him in conversation with his neighbours.</p>
<p>At first Sirius was rather pleased to have the experience of contact with a human being of brutal temper. Hitherto his immediate human environment had been on the whole too amiable to be a fair sample. He needed to know what man was like in his harsher modes. There was soon trouble, because Sirius did not cringe when Thwaites gave orders. Instead, he carried out his task with calm efficiency. This provoked the man to curse him on the flimsiest pretext; and when he did so, Sirius would gaze at him with cold and ostentatious surprise. This, of course, made matters worse. In time, Thwaites’s harsh voice and the whole atmosphere of the place began to get on Sirius’s nerves. The milder human contacts in Wales, Cambridge and London began to fade out of his consciousness, and he found himself feeling that Thwaites was the typical man. Obscurely he dramatized himself as the champion of his own kind against the tyrant race. Thwaites’s great cruel hands symbolized for him the process by which the ruthless species had mastered all the living creatures of the planet. Irrationally Sirius, though a hunter who had again and again inflicted agony and death, felt a self-righteous indignation against the sheer cruelty of man. Compassion for the weak; which had been inculcated in him by his own human friends, now turned him against humanity itself.</p>
<p>Thwaites had several times threatened Sirius with his stick, but had always thought better of it, sobered by the dog’s great size and a dangerous look in his eye. As time passed, the man’s irrational spite against the dog increased. But the incident which caused the final catastrophe was an attack not on Sirius but on Roy. A few days before Thomas was due to come and fetch Sirius away there was some difficulty with a bunch of sheep that Roy had brought into the yard. Thwaites caught the collie a sharp blow on the haunch. Sirius turned on Thwaites in fury and knocked him down; then, remembering himself, he withdrew and watched the man pick himself up. Roy promptly disappeared from the scene. It was a fixed principle with Thwaites that when dogs were rebellious one must thrash them into subjection, which meant in his view thrashing them almost to death. He called to his hired man, “Anderson, the brute’s gone savage, come and help me teach him.” There was no answer. Anderson was far afield. Thwaites was no coward, but he did not relish attacking the great cunning beast single-handed. However, insubordination must be crushed, at all costs. Besides, such a dangerous animal might do incalculable harm. Better destroy it at once. He could tell Trelone that the dog had gone mad and had to be shot. He went into the house. Sirius guessed that he would emerge with his gun. He therefore rushed to the house door and crouched at one side of it. Thwaites stepped over the threshold, looking round the yard. Sirius leapt at him, knocked him over again, and seized the gun with his teeth. Both antagonists rolled over and over. Thwaites struggled to his feet, and tried to turn the muzzle of the gun against the dog’s body. One barrel went off harmlessly, then the other. Sirius let go the gun and sprang away. Thwaites put his hand in his pocket and brought out a couple of cartridges. Sirius leapt at him, knocked him over again and seized his throat, gripping his windpipe with the full power of his strong jaws. The flavour of warm human blood and the sound of the man’s stifled gurgle filled him with an exultant, careless fury. In this symbolic act he would kill not only Thwaites but the whole tyrant race. Henceforth all beasts and birds should live naturally, and the planet’s natural order should never again be disturbed by the machinations of this upstart species. These thoughts flashed through his mind even as the couple floundered and crashed about, gripping each other’s throats.</p>
<p>Presently the man’s struggles slackened, his grip weakened. Then a change began to come over Sirius’s mind. Fury gave place to a more detached observation of the situation. After all, this creature was only expressing the nature that the universe had bred in him. And so was the whole human race. Why this silly hate? The human stink suddenly reminded him of Plaxy’s fragrance. The blood-taste nauseated him. The crushed windpipe between his teeth filled him with horror. He let go, moved away, and stood watching the feeble movements of his non-canine brother, whom he, Cain, had murdered.</p>
<p>Practical considerations presented themselves to him. The hand of man would now indeed be relentlessly turned against him. The hand of two thousand million human beings; all the race, save his own few friends. A panic of loneliness suddenly seized him. A solitary airman, flying over hostile territory, with nothing but enemies below and nothing but stars above, may sometimes feel desperately lonely; but that loneliness is nothing to the loneliness which now oppressed Sirius, with the whole human race against him, and his own species unable to comprehend him, and no pack anywhere to comfort him and accept his service.</p>
<p>He went over to the trough in the yard, drank, and licked his lips clean. Once more he stood watching Thwaites, who now lay still, with a torn and bloody neck. His own neck was stiff after Thwaites’s desperate effort. Imagining the pain that his teeth must have inflicted, he cringed. He returned to the body and sniffed the neck. Already there was a very faint odour of death. No need, then, to risk his own life fetching a doctor to save this human being. Obeying a sudden freakish impulse, he fleetingly touched the forehead of his slaughtered brother with his tongue.</p>
<p>Distant footsteps! He took to his heels in sudden panic, leapt the yard gate and raced away for the fells. Lest they should come after him with bloodhounds, he used every fox-trick to mislead them. He doubled on his track, he took to streams, and so on. That night he slept under the bracken in a remote ghyll. Next day hunger forced him to go hunting. He managed to secure a rabbit, and took it to his lair, where he wolfed it. He spent the rest of the day hidden, and haunted by his crime; haunted, yet strangely exultant. Though it was indeed a crime, it was a positive act of self-assertion which had emancipated him for ever from the spell of the master race. Henceforth he would fear no man simply as a man. Two more nights and the intervening day he spent in hiding. Then he set off to intercept Thomas, who was due at the farm in the afternoon. With great caution he worked his way back over the hills till he was looking down on the road to Thwaites’s farm. He went to a point on the road where there was good cover and a hairpin bend. Here the car would have to slow down almost to walking pace. He hid himself in the undergrowth of a little wood, and waited. An occasional foot-passenger passed, and an occasional car. At last there was the unmistakable sound of Thomas’s Morris Ten. Cautiously Sirius crept from his hiding, looking to see if any other human being was visible. There was no one. He stepped out into the road. Thomas stopped the car and got out with a cheery “Hello!” Sirius, with tucked-in tail, simply said, “I’ve killed Thwaites.” Thomas exclaimed, “God!” then gaped at him in silence. The dog’s keen ears heard distant footsteps, so they retired into the wood to discuss the situation. It was decided that Thomas should go up to the farm as though he knew nothing of the tragedy, while Sirius kept in hiding.</p>
<p>There is no need to record in detail how Thomas dealt with the problem. Naturally he did not tell the police that he had met Sirius. He strongly denied that his super-sheep-dogs were dangerous, and he produced evidence to that effect. He insisted that Thwaites must have treated Sirius very badly; and the man (it appeared) was known to be of a sadistic temper. Clearly he had attacked the animal with his gun, and had probably wounded it. In self-defence the dog had killed him. And where was the dog now? The valuable creature had probably died of wounds somewhere on the moors.</p>
<p>This much of the truth Thomas told Sirius, but not till long afterwards did the murderer learn the rest. Things had not in fact gone as well as Thomas had reported. The officers of the law remained suspicious, and ordered that if the dog was found it must be destroyed. Thomas therefore decided that in order to protect his unique canine masterpiece he must resort to trickery. After a suitable interval he would notify the authorities that the man-killing beast had at last found its way home, and that it had been duly destroyed. He would sacrifice a certain large Alsatian super-sheep-dog, and palm off his corpse as Sirius’s.</p>
<p>It was not until late on the day of the inquest that Thomas picked up Sirius at the hairpin bend. In spite of the black-out, they drove home through the night, helped by a full moon.</p>
