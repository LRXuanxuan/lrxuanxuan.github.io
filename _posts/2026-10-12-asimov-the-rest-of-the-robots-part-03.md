<h2>Part III</h2>
<p><em>Susan Calvin</em></p>
<p><em>The robot short stories that most interested me, however, were those that dealt with Dr Susan Calvin, robopsychologist extraordinary</em><em>. </em><em>A ‘robopsychologist</em>’ <em>is not a robot who is a psychologist, but a psychologist who is also a roboticist. It is an ambiguous word, unfortunately, but I am stuck with it.</em></p>
<p>As <em>time went on, I fell in love with Dr Calvin. She was a forbidding creature, to be sure – much more like the popular conception of a robot than were any of my positronic creations – but I loved her anyway.</em></p>
<p><em>She served as the central bond that knit together the stories of</em> I, Robot, <em>and in four of the stories she played a central role. What’s more, after</em> I, Robot <em>appeared</em> <em>(and despite the fact that the book contained an epilog briefly noting Dr Calvin’s death at an advanced age) I couldn’t help bringing her back. I wrote four more stories dealing with her.</em></p>
<p><em>In one of these, dear Susan appeared only glancingly. This was</em> ‘<em>Satisfaction Guaranteed,’ which appeared in the April 1951 issue of</em> Amazing Stories.</p>
<p><em>An interesting point about this story is the unusual quantity of mail from readers, almost all young ladies, and almost all speaking wistfully of Tony – as though I might know where he could be found.</em></p>
<p><em>I shall attempt to draw no morals (or immorals, either) from this.</em></p>
