<h2>i. 保罗与他的海王星寄生者</h2>
<p>我为什么选择保罗作为研究对象？为什么在我的全部标本中，偏偏打算比任何其他人都更多地向你们讲述他？我曾在不同时间里详尽考察过数量极其庞大的你们这一类人，不仅包括你们的同时代人，也包括许多比你们更早出生的人，以及许多在你们这个时代尚未出生的人。我熟悉希腊人、印度人、早期条顿人、古埃及人、中国各个历史阶段的人，以及尚未来到世上的美国人和巴塔哥尼亚人中若干选定个体的每一种性格、癖好，以及他们心灵中每一个细微片段。事实上，第一人类没有哪个种族没给我提供过样本；而随着我逐渐熟悉这些人，他们奇异而独特的个性竟如此吸引我，以至于我最后不再只把他们看作标本，而在某种意义上把他们视作朋友。这些存在向我暴露了他们本性中最隐秘的部分，其程度在正常的人际交往中根本不可能达到；可是他们从来没有同我交流过，其中绝大多数甚至从未怀疑过我的存在。</p>
<p>如果我愿意，我可以告诉你们许多著名人物的奇异秘密。比如，你们的伊丽莎白女王就是我的收藏之一。透过她的眼睛，我不仅看见过埃塞克斯俊美的脸，也看见过她自己身体上一处异常的构造；正是它使她如此孤独，使她如此执着地强调自己的女性身份，同时又如此具有君王气质。我品尝过她对命运的苦涩愤怒、贪婪的自爱，以及她对英格兰的爱。我也曾寄居在列奥纳多·达·芬奇身上。正是我，用比他的时代早了几个世纪的无数观念折磨过他；而我在他心中的影响那不可解释的谜，被他用颜料凝结成了一个微笑。阿肯那顿也受过我的摆布，在他妻子的脸上，我的影响又唤出了另一个微笑。在海王星生活的某些时刻，这些我所珍爱的标本会一齐涌入记忆，以他们无穷无尽的独特性把我淹没；那时我甚至会忘记自己的海王星人格，仿佛自己什么都不是，只是一座舞台，让这许许多多的人物在其中轮番登场。</p>
<p>如果你们觉得，一个人竟能拥有数以百万计如此亲密的对象，实在不可思议，那就请记住：最后人的心灵远比你们敏捷，也远比你们容量巨大；而且我已经为这项工作耗费了几千年。在绝大多数情况下，一整个地球人的一生，占去我的海王星生命不过一天而已。当然，即便我有数百万标本，也只能让我对你们这一物种获得局部的认识。但那些与我研究同一领域的同事，也以不逊于我的勤奋工作着。毫无疑问，我们所有人加起来所研究的，也仍只是你们物种极小的一部分；不过至少到如今，我们已经抽样调查过每一种文化、每一个地方种系、每一代人。而你们这个时代，因为正处在整段历史的转折点上，更被我们一遍又一遍、格外仔细地筛查过。</p>
<p>那么，在这样庞大的收藏中，我为什么偏偏选择保罗，作为我要向你们说明的一切的文本与缩影？因为无论他的性格、处境，还是他对我的影响所作出的反应，都集中体现了你们时代的精神危机，甚至体现了你们整个物种的命运。若说他是一个格外“平均”的同类，那当然不对。恰恰相反。保罗虽然在某种意义上典型而富有代表性，却绝不是平均人物；无论性格还是处境，他都颇为特殊。但他的典型性在于：他把你们物种内部的混乱本质，以及你们世界的失序，表现得格外清楚。在保罗的一生中，某些精神成长阶段以几乎幼稚的简明轮廓显现出来，却又被他那个时代——不，应该说被他整个物种——无处不在的病害所扭曲。从最早打制燧石的人，一直到巴塔哥尼亚最后的幸存者，你们所有人都把原始猿类本性与真正的人性——从你们的角度说，甚至可称“超人性”——之间的冲突当作生命的基本条件。在保罗身上，那旧的猿类本性毫无遮掩而且十分强硬；新的人的本性则界限鲜明、表达强烈。因此，两者之间的冲突格外清楚。</p>
<p>我多少有些为自己发现保罗这件事感到得意。我关于第一人类心态以及你们历史危机的终极研究，需要一个我早知极难找到的特殊标本。由于你们根本不了解我工作的原理，我很难清楚告诉你们究竟需要哪些条件；但简单说来，我必须挑选一个既典型又独一无二的人：他的智力、能力、意志强度都大致处于平均水平，但猿类本性与真正属于人的本性却都发育良好，而且彼此处在一种极微妙的平衡中。此外，他还必须拥有一种在你们物种中并不正常的自我意识程度。还有许多更细致的要求，我就不一一说明了。为了找到这个独特生物，我不得不探索的不只是你们自己的时代，还必须在许多更早的世代里搜寻有利于我要求的遗传系谱。</p>
<p>十七世纪有一个德国人家族曾经显得很有希望；但他们身上那些我需要的特征，又与一种高度音乐敏感性纠缠在一起，而音乐常常给家族中的许多人提供了逃离内在冲突的途径。不过我注意到，那些最接近我要求的家族成员，都有一种微妙而独特的笑声；或者更准确地说，尽管他们笑起来方式各异，每个人的笑里却都会短暂掠过一丝困惑的颤动。我拿着这条线索，再结合另一些只能由内省发现的迹象继续追踪，最后在阿尔弗雷德大王时代的英格兰，发现了一条类似、而且更加有希望的血统。此后我便跟着它穿过一代又一代，有时向这里，有时向那里分叉。其中最有希望的一支被黑死病彻底消灭；另一支因为与一条有缺陷的家系大量通婚而遭到不可挽回的污染；第三支则在十六世纪产生过女巫，在十七世纪产生过神秘主义者。十八世纪初，这条家系中的一名成员与我早已标记过的另一支血统联姻，随后产生了一批更具有科学气质的人。整个十八世纪和十九世纪初，这支家族的社会地位一路下降，并在英格兰北部工业区扩散成一大群贫困家庭。保罗的祖母便出生在其中一个家庭；幸运的是，她后来又嫁进了我选中的另一条血统。至于保罗的父亲，同样来自一支早已引起我注意的家系。</p>
<p>如今既然终于找到了这个独一无二的标本，我先匆匆扫了一眼他的整个人生轨迹，并高兴地发现：外部环境注定会把我打算研究的那些特征在他身上进一步强化，而且使它们显得更加清楚。在那第一次快速浏览中，我还看见了某种毫无疑问属于海王星影响的效果——显然正是我自己尚未施加、却将来必定会施加的影响。于是我接下来详细调查保罗祖先的每一条支系，好让我能够彻底了解他的天性。这项调查的结果足以写满许多卷书，但这里没有必要展开。顺便说，我甚至一路追溯了保罗外貌中每个特征的演化史：他的鼻子、眉毛、手，以及其他部位。他左眉有一小绺头发以一种奇怪方式扭转，这是一个隐性特征，近代祖先中从未显现过；我追踪到十四世纪一支法国血统才发现它的来源。颧骨上一处几乎不可察觉的突出，我追到八世纪一名丹麦入侵者，再由他经瑞典人和芬兰人追溯到中亚。另一条尤其有趣的祖先支系，一方面促成了保罗的求知欲，另一方面也影响了他拇指纹路的某些特征；我从十七世纪一名荷兰商人追到十三世纪一位莱茵河地区的男爵，又经若干瑞士牧民与意大利农夫来到叙拉古，继而进入古希腊。在那里，保罗的一位祖先有一次曾经亲手递给苏格拉底一只杯子。不过，除了十来条这类有趣的线索——当然，很多人的族谱里都有类似东西——保罗的祖先总体仍是通常那种错综复杂、彼此交叉的网络：其中有诺斯人、低地德意志人、古不列颠人、新石器时代人群，再往前，则汇入你们所有种族共同的祖先。再继续细查保罗更遥远的祖先便毫无意义，因为不会再发现任何真正只属于保罗本人的东西。在更久远的过去，那些最终形成保罗的基因，也同时属于他那个时代绝大多数人的祖先。这部分故事，我早已知道。</p>
<p>现在我必须告诉你们，我究竟准备拿保罗做什么，以及我怎样执行自己的计划。我打算在他身上进行一些极其精细的实验，并观察他的反应。本书某些读者也许会觉得，我不得不对保罗实施的这种精神活体解剖简直残酷。我想提醒他们三点：第一，这是完成一项极其崇高任务所必需的部分；第二，保罗本人在自己最好的时刻，也会为了那项伟大事业欣然接受我对他的一切折磨，甚至更多；第三，这些实验最终对作为一个具体人类个体的保罗而言，带来的其实是极其重大的精神丰富。</p>
<p>除非简单地说，我找到这个既极端典型、又格外具有自我意识的人以后，打算周期性地影响他，使他尽可能从海王星人的视角去观看自己的世界，否则很难让你们真正明白我的目的。然后，我就能够细致观察这种视野作用于他那原始本性时会产生什么效果。如果他属于英雄型人格，这种方法大概会把他变成某种先知或者怪人。但保罗的材料柔和得多，不会用那种方式把我的实验毁掉。事实上，我对他经验的间歇性操纵产生了一个后果：他越来越害怕自己表现得和别人不同。正因为他拥有一个完全属于自己的秘密世界，又经常在那个世界投来的光照之下，看见现实世界忽然变得滑稽或丑恶，他反倒越来越焦虑地想在现实世界里站稳脚跟，遵守一切惯常行为模式。毕竟，不管他偶尔获得怎样的海王星视野，他本质上仍是自己那个世界的产物。但随着我的影响越来越强，他不得不承认，自己体内存在某种与周围环境格格不入的东西，甚至连他自己也觉得陌生。有时他害怕自己快疯了；另一些时候，他又会极其自满地劝自己相信，自己其实得到了“启示”，甚至天命注定是个先知。但他根本没有承担这类英雄角色的能力，因此这种妄想很快又会破碎。有一段时间，他甚至面临分裂成两个人格的危险：一个是天然的保罗，另一个则是猛烈而间歇发作的理想主义者。不过我选人很仔细；他的整体人格整合得太好，不会真的用这种简单办法解决冲突。他没有解体，仍然保持着同一颗心，只是这颗心被撕扯得痛苦而困惑。</p>
<p>然而，随着时间推移，他逐渐发生了变化。最开始，他把自己天生的地球式心态看成真正的“自己”，而把海王星影响视作外来的、扰人的、不合逻辑的、近乎疯狂的东西。他受它吸引，却希望始终把它严密压制，让它无法实际起作用。后来，它反倒开始显得像一种神圣启迪：清醒、欢快，虽然仍旧不属于原本的自己，却珍贵无比。再后来，他终于开始尝试按照我的影响去生活，于是立刻遭到自己世界的嘲笑与敌视。惊恐之下，他又发誓抛弃那道曾把自己引入歧途的“内在之光”。他非常认真地努力对它视而不见，努力变得和身边所有人一样。但我的影响不肯让他安宁。它在他内部找到了一种能与之响应的东西；随着保罗渐渐成熟，那东西越来越大胆，最后竟完全把自己认同于海王星人的观点。他一点点开始把心中这项海王星因素看作真正的“自己”，而把正常的地球人成分视为“他者”，仿佛一匹不服管束的马，真正的自我必须设法驯服它、骑上它。接下来便是一段长期斗争，而斗争的结局是绝望，因为他终于明白，自己根本无法控制地球人的天性。事实上，在他看来，真正的自己像被捆住手脚、无能为力地绑在这头失控动物的背上；或者仿佛身体和日常智力仍照常在世界里四处活动，做着各种毫无目标的滑稽事情，而他的本质精神却被囚禁在脑袋某处，只能看着一切发生，却几乎毫无干预能力。当然，他称为“自己”的这一本质精神，并不只是我在他身上的影响；那是保罗自身最好、最属于人的那部分本性，而我的影响之所以能起作用，正是因为能够作用于它。但他这最好的部分，也不过是你们物种特有的、尚处萌芽阶段的“最好”；虽然我的影响给它灌注了极其丰富的想象财富，它大多数时候仍几乎完全无法付诸行动——你们这一类人大都如此。不过，保罗一生中也确实有几次成功作出了真正配得上“人”这个名称的行动，而在那些时刻，我的影响发挥了作用。这些危机，我很快就会讲到。</p>

<h2>i. PAUL AND HIS NEPTUNIAN PARASITE</h2>
<p>WHY DO I choose Paul for study? Why do I propose to tell you more of him than of anyone else in my collection? I have examined in detail at one time and another an immense number of specimens of your kind, not only your contemporaries but many others whose date is earlier than yours, and many who in your day are still to live. I have made myself familiar with every trait and whim, every minute episode in the minds of selected individuals from among the Greeks, the Indians, the early Teutons, the ancient Egyptians, the Chinese of all China’s phases, and also the still unborn Americans and Patagonians. In fact, there is no race of the First Men that has not yielded me samples; which, as I have grown intimate with them, have so captivated me with their fantastic personalities that I have come to regard them not merely as specimens but, in a manner, as friends. For these beings have yielded me the secrets of their nature to an extent impossible in normal intercourse; and yet they have never communicated with me, and for the most part have never suspected my existence.</p>
<p>If I chose, I could tell you many strange secrets about your famous characters, for instance about your Queen Elizabeth, who is among my collection. Through her eyes I have looked not only on the fair face of Essex, but also on that abnormal formation of her own body which caused her to be so lonely, so insistent on her womanhood, and withal so kingly. And I have tasted her bitter rage against fate, her greedy self-love, her love of England. Leonardo da Vinci also I have inhabited. It was I that tormented him with a thousand ideas in advance of his age. It was the enigma of my influence in him that he embodied in pigment as a smile. Aknahton also I swayed, and in his wife’s face my influence conjured another smile. There are moments in my life on Neptune when my beloved specimens crowd in upon my memory and overwhelm me with the infinity of their uniquenesses; when, forgetful of my own Neptunian personality, I seem to be nothing but the theatre in which these many play their parts.</p>
<p>If it is incredible to you that I should have millions of these intimates, remember, not only that the minds of the Last Men are far more agile and far more capacious than your minds, but also that I have spent many thousands of years upon the work, and that in most cases a whole terrestrial lifetime occupies me for no more than a day of my life on Neptune. Of course even my millions of specimens can afford only partial knowledge of your kind. But those of my colleagues who have been working in the same field have worked with no less industry. No doubt all of us together have studied only a minute fraction of your species; but at least we have by now sampled every culture, every local strain, every generation. And your own age, since it is the turning-point of your history, we have sifted again and again with special care.</p>
<p>Why then out of all this collection do I choose Paul as the text and the epitome of what I have to say to you? I choose him because he does epitomize in his character, his circumstances, and his reaction to my influence, the spiritual crisis of your age, and indeed the doom of your species. It would not be true to say that he was an exceptionally average member of his race. Far from it. Though in a sense typical and significant, Paul was not average. Indeed both in character and in circumstance he was rather unusual. Yet he is typical in that he illustrates very clearly the confused nature of his species, and the disorder of its world. In Paul’s life there appear with an almost naive simplicity of outline certain phases of spiritual growth, distorted by the all-pervading blight of his age, nay of his species. In all of you, from the earliest flint-worker, even to the last survivors of Patagonia, the conflict between the primitive simian nature and the genuinely human (or from your point of view the super-human) is present as a fundamental condition of life. In Paul the old simian nature was undisguised and insistent, while the new human nature had defined itself with precision and emphasis. The conflict therefore was clear.</p>
<p>I pride myself somewhat on the discovery of Paul. My crowning research upon the mentality of the First Men and on the crisis of their career demanded a specimen such as I knew would be hard to find. I cannot tell you at all clearly what was necessary, since you are ignorant of the principles of my work. But, to repeat, I had to select an individual both typical and unique, a being of average intelligence, average abilities, average strength of will, but one in whom the simian and the distinctively human were both well developed and delicately balanced. Further, he must be one capable of a degree of self-consciousness abnormal in your species. Many other more subtle requirements I must leave unmentioned. In order to discover this unique creature I had to explore not only your own age but many earlier generations in search of genetic strains favourable to my requirements.</p>
<p>In the Seventeenth Century there was a German family which promised what I wanted; but in them the desirable features were complicated by a musical sensitivity which in many individuals afforded escape from the conflict. I noticed, however, that those members of the clan who most nearly fulfilled my requirements had a subtly characteristic laugh; or rather that, though they laughed in many different styles, the laughter of each had a curious fleeting tremor of bewilderment. Armed with this clue, and certain others more introspective, I continued my search, until in the England of King Alfred I found a similar and even more promising strain. This I followed through the generations hither and thither. One branch of it, the most promising, was exterminated by the Black Death, another became hopelessly contaminated by interbreeding with a strain of defectives, a third produced witches in the Sixteenth Century and mystics in the Seventeenth. Early in the Eighteenth Century a member of this line married into another stock which I had already marked out, and produced a number of more scientifically-minded folk. Throughout the Eighteenth and early Nineteenth Centuries this line suffered social decline, and proliferated in a swarm of poverty-stricken households scattered over the industrial north of England. From one of these sprang Paul’s grandmother, who by good fortune married into yet another of my chosen lines. Paul’s father also was of a stock that had already interested me.</p>
<p>Having now discovered my unique specimen, I first took a hasty glance at his career, and found to my delight that circumstance was destined to intensify and clarify in him the characteristics which I proposed to study. In that first hasty glance I saw also the quite unmistakable effects of a Neptunian influence, doubtless my own influence which I had not yet exerted. I then proceeded to make a thorough investigation of every line of Paul’s ancestry, in order that I might know his nature through and through. Many volumes could be filled with the results of this inquiry, but here I need not elaborate. Incidentally I traced the evolution of Paul’s physical appearance, of his nose, eyebrows, hands, and so on. A curious twist of the hair of his left eyebrow, which was a recessive character, and had not appeared in any of his more recent ancestors, came in with a French strain in the Fourteenth Century. A certain scarcely noticeable prominence of the cheek-bones I traced to a Danish invader of the Eighth Century, and beyond him through Swedes and Finns into Central Asia. Another remarkable line of his ancestry, in part responsible for his intellectual curiosity and certain features of his thumb-print, took me via a Dutch merchant of the Seventeenth Century, a Rhenish baron of the Thirteenth, various Swiss herdsmen and Italian farmers to Syracuse, and so to ancient Greece, where one of Paul’s ancestors once handed a cup to Socrates. But apart from a dozen or so curious threads of this type, which of course occur in many family trees, Paul’s ancestry was the usual tangle of intercrossing lines; stretching back in manifold complexity among the Norse, the Low Germans, the Ancient British, and the Neolithic, and beyond these to the common forefathers of all your races. It would have been useless to trace Paul’s ancestry any further in detail, since nothing more that was distinctive of Paul himself remained to be discovered. In the more remote past the genes that were to produce Paul were ancestral also to the mass of his contemporaries. This story was already known to me.</p>
<p>I must now tell you what it was that I intended to do with Paul, and how I carried out my plan. I wished to perform certain delicate experiments on him and observe his reactions. To some of the readers of this book the mental vivisection which I had to practise upon Paul may seem merely brutal. I would remind them first that it was a necessary part of a very lofty task; second, that Paul himself in his best moments would have gladly accepted all and more of my torturing manipulation for the sake of that high enterprise; and lastly that the final result upon Paul, the individual human being, was a very great spiritual enrichment.</p>
<p>It is difficult to give you any clear idea of my aim beyond saying that, having found this uniquely typical yet also exceptionally self-conscious being, I intended to influence him periodically in such a manner that he should see his world as far as possible from the Neptunian point of view. I should then be able to observe in detail the effect of this vision on his primitive nature. Had he been of the heroic type, this policy would have turned him into some kind of prophet or crank. But he was made of milder stuff, and would never spoil my experiment in that style. Indeed, one effect of my intermittent manipulation of his experience was to inspire him with a dread of showing himself to be different from others. Just because he possessed a whole secret world of his own, and was prone to see the actual world turn farcical or hideous in the light cast by that other world, he became increasingly anxious to maintain a secure footing in the actual world and conform to all the customary modes of behaviour. For in spite of his Neptunian vision he remained essentially a product of his own world. But as my influence extended, he was forced to admit that there was something in him profoundly alien to his environment, something alien even to himself. At times he feared he was going mad; at other times he would persuade himself with immense complacency that he was ‘inspired’, and that he must indeed be a prophet; but as he was incapable of any such heroism, this delusion was soon shattered. There were moments when he threatened to break into two personalities, one the natural Paul, the other a violent and spasmodic idealist. But I had chosen him carefully; he was too well integrated actually to succumb to this easy solution of his conflict. Instead of disintegrating, he remained one mind, though torn and perplexed.</p>
<p>But as time passed a change came over him. At first he had regarded his native terrestrial mentality as himself, and the Neptunian influence as alien, disturbing, irrational, insane. He was fascinated by it, but he wished to keep it thoroughly suppressed and ineffective. Later, however, it came to seem a divine enlightenment, sane and joyous, still alien to himself but most precious. Then at last he began to try to live according to my influence, and incurred at once the ridicule and hostility of his own world. With horror he now foreswore his ‘inner light’ which had so misled him. He earnestly tried to become blind to it, and to be like his fellows. But my influence left him no peace. It found something responsive in him, which, as he matured, was emboldened to identify itself wholly with the Neptunian point of view. Little by little he came to think of this Neptunian factor in his mind as truly ‘himself’, and the normal Terrestrial as’ other’, something like an unruly horse which his true self must somehow break in and ride. There followed a period of struggle, which ended in despair; for it became clear that he could not master his Terrestrial nature. Indeed, it seemed to him that he himself was bound and helpless upon the back of this uncontrolled animal; or that while his body and his mundane intelligence went about the world performing all manner of aimless antics, his essential spirit was imprisoned somewhere in his head watching all, but almost wholly impotent. This essential spirit which he regarded as ‘himself’ was not, of course, merely my influence in him; it was his own best and most human nature, on which my influence was able to work. But this best of his was only the rudimentary best which was characteristic of his species; and though my influence had endowed it with an immense treasure of imagination, it remained almost wholly ineffective, as is usual with your kind. Yet there were times in his life when Paul did succeed in acting in a manner that was really human, and on these occasions my influence played a part. Of these crises I shall presently tell.</p>
