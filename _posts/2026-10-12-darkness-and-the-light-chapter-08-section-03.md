<h2>三、进步</h2>
<p>人类现在终于能够不受重大干扰地继续推进那些在世界统一刚刚实现时便已启动的事业。工业生产与分配必须获得充分发展，使两极之间的每一个人都能拥有安全、舒适的生活，以及身体与心智充分成长的条件。潮汐能与火山能资源必须得到最大限度的开发，因为人类不久后的需求将达到此前的一千倍。新的、更优良的合成材料必须被发明出来，一些旧材料也必须以远大于过去的规模生产。人类需要充足的新型建筑材料和标准化建筑构件，需要更耐用的日用品塑料、更好且数量更多的衣料、更好的食物、更好的交通，以及远为丰富的教育设备。</p>
<p>所有这些生产都必须以这样一种方式完成：一切‘低于人类应有水平’的劳动都只由机器承担。当然，只要人类平均能力仍维持当时的水平，就仍会有许多世界公民满足于从事较低级的工作；但绝不能再让任何人一生被迫从事低于自身能力的劳动，也绝不能让任何人被固定在一种与其特殊禀赋不相适合的职业里。职业心理学必须取得巨大进步，因此需要进行大量研究。</p>
<p>事实上，心理学此时终于迎来了自己的时代。科学时代的人类文化起初由物理学和化学支配，后来由生物学支配，而现在最终在很大程度上受到心理学影响。随着对人性的理解不断加深，教育方法取得重大进展。那些足以使人丧失正常生活能力的神经症逐渐消失。若能拍摄一张全人类的合成肖像，那张脸上会呈现出坦率、自信与友善；在更早的时代，这种神情只有那些在遗传与成长环境方面格外幸运的人才会拥有。</p>
<p>到这时，人们已经普遍认识到，充实的生活虽然离不开充分的物质条件，却不能简单以奢侈程度衡量，更应以身体健康以及身体与心智能力的较高发展为尺度。新秩序因而相当明确地区分了纯粹的奢侈品，以及身体或心智发展所需要的用品。工业规划让前者较难获得，让后者容易获得。奢侈本身绝未受到谴责，但世界社会制造奢侈品的无限能力受到有意限制。这样，每个人都可以用自己的‘奢侈配额’获得一定数量的纯奢侈享受，却不能把大量珍品集中到自己手里，超过自身使用或欣赏它们的能力。因此，最华丽的服装虽然没有遭禁，却只生产很少数量；较朴素的材料和样式则种类丰富、供应充足。基本食品随处可以大量取得，奢侈食品和更珍贵的葡萄酒却很难买到。实用汽车和飞机每个公民都可使用，豪华汽车和飞机则只有那种愿意在其他一切方面省吃俭用的狂热者才弄得到。上等珠宝几乎无法获得，主要用于公共陈列而非个人炫耀；但由熟悉地方传统或尝试新形式的工匠手工制作的简单饰物，却可供任何想要的人购买。总的目标，是利用人类巨大的机械资源去简化生活，而不是使生活复杂化，并把一切真正需要的东西置于所有人伸手可及之处。机器要被充分利用，却又必须确保机器不成为主宰。旧时代里，普通人的需要只是私人逐利企业在经营过程中偶然照顾到的副产品；结果，商业不断迎合人类更原始、更强迫性的冲动，严重败坏人的感受力与正直。如今，公共需要成了第一要求，就必须判断公众真正需要什么，以及哪些需要最值得培养，再依此规划工业。</p>
<p>此时开始形成的世界，与旧世界属于完全不同的类型。虽然几乎所有人都以某种方式劳动，‘工人阶级’却正在迅速消失。大多数人不再长时间工作却收入不足，不再生活于不同程度的肮脏贫困之中，也不再连维持心理健康所必需的那一点自我表达机会都得不到。过去普遍存在的挫败感与苦难曾塑造出一种典型心态，如今这种心态已经消失。例如在政治上，受挫情绪常表现为一种啃噬人心的报复欲，后来的人看来简直愚蠢。在政治光谱两端，乃至很大程度上整个政治领域中，恐惧、嫉妒、仇恨，以及受压抑后急于炫耀自我的冲动，都曾是主要动机，只不过它们往往披着‘义愤’的外衣。这便滋生了法西斯主义、纳粹主义以及较卑劣的共产主义。当然，到这时法西斯主义与纳粹主义早已消失。共产主义曾经对思想、情感和制度作出重大贡献，如今却不再是一种狂热信条。从某种意义上说，一切神志健全的人都是共产主义者，因为他们都接受了马克思社会分析中的许多内容；但好斗的共产党早已不复存在，而马克思主义那种试图取消爱与智慧这两种根本价值首要地位的倾向，也被看作机器时代有毒社会空气造成的一种偏执。</p>
<p>取代‘工人阶级’的，是越来越多生活水准可被我们称作‘舒适中产阶级’的人。不过，他们的心态与我们时代的中产阶级非常不同，因为他们不再被商业斗兽场中那种非得设法压过邻人的绝望需要所塑造。大多数人如今领取薪金，为世界国家、某个民族国家、地方机构或职业机构服务。只有三类人不领工资，而在需要时领取每个公民都有权获得的优厚生活津贴。第一类，是人数很少而又颇为特殊的私人资本家。他们的作用，是通过大胆的私人工业开拓，为社会提供私人进取的益处。他们依靠利润生活，但奢侈限制法和税收阻止他们超过社会所容许的富裕程度。他们的雇员是各类熟练工人，这些人被比国家部门略高的工资、略短的工时，以及小规模共同事业所带来的冒险感吸引。大多数雇员都曾节省自己的奢侈配额，把积蓄投入工厂设备，因此他们本人也成了资本家。创办企业的原始资本家或资本家集团，总是以建立一个合作、自我管理的共同体为目标，在其中所有成员都以某种程度成为资本所有者。</p>
<p>第二类不领固定工资的人是艺术家和作家。他们开始职业生涯时，完全依靠生活津贴，以及父母朋友所能提供的额外帮助。他们也可能获得国家颁发的‘才华补贴’；但总体而言，他们仍希望靠出售作品生活，因为在这个新世界里，人们对书籍、绘画、音乐演出等文化产品的需求，远远超过我们这个时代。</p>
<p>第三类无薪者由天生的懒散者和流浪汉组成。他们只占很小的少数，有的人偶尔干一天活来补贴生活津贴，有的人则干脆完全依赖‘救济金’。这些人中的绝大多数对社会毫无用处，但世界社会完全养得起他们；之所以容忍这种闲散，是为了不漏掉偶尔会从这一群体中出现的极具创造力或批判能力的少数头脑。许多顽固流浪者带有强烈反社会冲动，把一切社会组织都当成讨厌的累赘，也当成掠夺型游荡者理所当然可以下手的‘猎物’。他们竟然仍被容忍，甚至得到保护，这本身就说明新世界领导者具有何等稳定性和智慧。这些无赖人数极少，因为改良教育已经大幅减少了单纯因人格扭曲而反社会的人。这些人看起来并未扭曲，只是天生个人主义。有些人的个人主义甚至发展到拒绝领取救济，几乎完全靠小偷小摸生活，偶尔还会大胆拦路抢劫。起初，我觉得这种事情竟然会在一个近乎乌托邦的社会中被允许，简直不可思议。但这些‘法外之徒’只占个人主义者中的极微小一部分，而且任何遭其侵害的人都可向国家索赔，所以社会并不试图把他们彻底消灭。即使被抓住，只要没有对受害者造成身体伤害，他们受到的处罚也很轻。</p>
<p>在同议会制度并行的职业代表制度中，资本家、作家、艺术家和流浪汉都拥有自己的选举团，与工程师、教师等领取薪金的职业群体并列。不过，流浪汉与法外之徒很少真正行使投票权。</p>
<p>领取薪金者的生活当然差别极大。制度的目标，是让枯燥工作对应较短工时，让有趣工作可以有较长工时。少数情况下，某些单调工作不得不维持较长工时，那么便会挑选那类最适合单调劳动、并能一边工作一边做白日梦的心理类型去承担。反过来，一些极其迷人的工作反而被限制为短工时，因为它们造成的精神压力太大。</p>
<p>一个很引人注目的制度最先在北美试行，随后立刻被中国仿效，不久便推广到全世界，这就是‘应急队’。应急队从几乎所有职业中挑选那些适应性强、富于进取精神的人员，让他们持续训练并领取全额薪金，以便根据需要随时调往任何地方。这样，如果出于某种原因需要改道一条河流、移走一座山、排干一片海域，便能调动成千上万名土木工程师，而无需打乱正在进行的常规事业。应急队在功能上相当于旧资本主义制度中的失业人口，但其精神状态完全不同。</p>
<p>世界政府公开宣称的目标，是在专业化与全面发展之间取得恰当平衡。因此，一个人的职业越专门化，就越受鼓励去从事职业之外的活动。当然，每个人首先都被教育成一个发展充分的人格与负责任的公民。他会获得世界历史和现代世界文化的概要知识，也会被有意识地培养出广泛的同情心与理解力。无论其特殊才能是什么，他都要接受训练，对其他人的活动形成一定程度的理解。社会不断提醒每个人，他的首要责任具有两重性：既要发展自己的特殊禀赋，也要尽可能理解并促进他人的特殊禀赋。</p>
<p>世界政府非常谨慎地行使监督各国教育体系的权利，确保新世界关于公民教育的基本原则不被违背，而且真正得到积极实践。教育的目标不仅是传授一幅清晰的人类历史轮廓，并辅以本国和本省历史的较详细知识，还要培养两种至关重要的人类冲动：共同体意志与求知意志。不仅个人之间的专业化受到限制，民族之间的专业化也受到谨慎控制。起初，一些国家不可避免地主要从事工业，另一些主要从事农业，但制度有意确保这种专业分工建立在基本自给自足的基础上。这令我吃惊，因为民族之间发生战争的危险到这时已经消失。既然如此，为什么还如此坚持自给自足？部分原因在于，自给自足本就是经济自然发展的结果。随着物理和化学技术巨大进步，工业对地域的依赖大为降低。无论食品还是打字机，如今几乎都能在任何地区生产，因为最基本的原料不过是植物组织和十分常见的矿物。</p>
<p>不过，提高自给自足程度还有另一个原因。乍一看，这个理由似乎会导向相反方向。世界政府的目标是发展整个世界，而不是某一个民族。因此，地方文化差异必须受到保护，因为人们已经认识到，精神多样性正是生活的呼吸。照此推论，似乎应该鼓励每个国家进行经济专业化，因为经济差异理应产生精神差异。然而，人们现在认识到，过度的心理专业化非常危险。过去那种高度专业化的工厂工人不过是真正人的漫画；只懂萝卜的农业工人同样受到严重限制。一个民族要形成有意义的文化变体，其内部就必须包含极为多样的活动。各行各业的人必须持续受到其他职业、因而具有不同心态者的直接影响。一个高度专业化的国民经济会培育出畸形偏斜的精神文化。在由高度专业化国家构成的世界里，可以通过强制或鼓励国际旅行来部分避免这种危险，但效果仍有限：旅行要么只是度假活动，虽有价值却影响不深；要么成为一种生活方式，而那样一来，旅行者在精神上便会失去同本土文化的根。</p>
<p>新世界领导人的目标，不仅是高度的国家自给自足，也是高度的地方自给自足。例如在英国，经济组织的中心位于苏格兰西海岸的潮汐发电设施，但工业并不被允许集中在那个地区。改良后的输电技术使电力能够送往整个岛屿，于是明亮的新工厂和工人住宅分散在各个农业区。另一方面，兰开夏、约克郡和中部地区过去拥挤的工业带，其中很大一部分重新成为农业地区。结果，不仅苏格兰和威尔士，连英格兰那些‘新而古老’的省份——威塞克斯、东盎格利亚、诺森布里亚、麦西亚等等——也各自形成有限却有活力的自治，并分别对英格兰文化作出贡献。英格兰本身也比过去更加自给自足。农业进步和人口减少使三个不列颠民族能够自行养活自己，虽然仍会从国外大量进口奢侈食品。英国很早以前就不再是‘世界工厂’，因为此时每个国家基本上都是自己的工厂；不过，英国仍以出口某些高品质机械与织物来‘支付’进口，而这些特种产品正在成为英国人的名牌。事实上，贸易越来越不再交换生活必需品，而是交换由地方天才生产、用来丰富和装点全世界生活的产品。每个民族都力求基本自给，同时又为世界经济生产某一类凭借本地传统与技能能够格外成功制造的特殊商品。每个民族既以自身的世界主义文化为荣，也以民族文化为荣；既以理解人类共同传统为荣，也以自己对那不断开枝散叶的文化所作出的独特贡献为荣。</p>
<p>于是，英国人虽然从严格的智识意义上说从来不是一个高度文化化的民族，却颇有理由声称，他们仍可通过自己的政治生活向世界提供范例：那套制度虽然反常，却十分有效，其精神温和而克制。尽管英国普通人口对文学仍比较迟钝，英国作家、尤其英国诗人，却面向全世界写作，而且比任何其他国家的作家都更广泛地被世界阅读。这部分是历史偶然赋予英语巨大地位的结果。此时，英语已经成为所有其他民族的‘第二语言’，而且不断从其他语言中大量借词，丰富到这样的程度：那个时代的英国人看我们这个时代的英语，就像我们看乔叟的英语一样古老。</p>
<p>德国人仍向世界提供伟大的音乐、宏大的哲学著作——而且越来越常用英语写成——以及对科学一丝不苟的实际应用。他们的组织才能也在全球显现：在世界卫生、邮政、无线电、交通等委员会的管理层中，德国人占据极大比例。甚至有人低声议论，说德国人终于实现了自己的世界帝国梦想。俄罗斯人摆脱了帝国主义迷梦之后，凭借洞察人格的能力和同志式精神，理所当然地赢得世界赞赏。西藏人因曾经通过精神纪律对黑暗力量取得辉煌胜利而始终受到尊敬，被普遍视为精神最坚固的堡垒。不过，更微妙、更富多样性的印度人正在成为向世界其他地区解释精神经验的主要民族。北美人此时是工业发明的首要开拓者，也领先于人类不断扩展的天文学探索；除此之外，他们还声称自己在一项重要任务上处于领先地位，即消化、协调其他文化。中国人则凭借庞大人口和文明的连续性，在塑造新世界文化方面发挥巨大作用；他们确保普通人在自身能力范围内也真正成为有文化的人，并提供一套细腻而人道的个人行为范式。因此，在乌托邦式发展阶段刚开始时，各民族之间存在巨大的文化多样性。当然，一个人在任何文化方向上要取得卓越成就，并不必须属于最擅长该领域的民族。事实上，在每一个文化领域，任何民族的个人都可能作出杰出贡献；有些文化活动本来就比另一些更加国际化，例如大多数自然科学都同样依赖许多民族。不过，从总体与长期来看，每个民族都形成了某种特殊声誉；而一个人若想在某领域达到最高水平，最好先吸收在该领域贡献最大的民族所积累的成果。民族才能当然不会永远固定不变。旧声誉可能丧失，新声誉也可能建立。每一个民族都可能在此前从未尝试的方向上以成就震惊世界。很少有人会料到，俄罗斯人在经历一个狂热唯物主义时代后，会发展出对神秘经验的特殊敏感；更少有人会想到，设得兰群岛上那一点点饱受风暴侵袭的人口竟会在哲学上取得如此成就，以至于新建立的勒威克小大学能在这一领域同德国和印度的著名学府竞争。</p>
<p>虽然社会尽一切可能鼓励每个民族发展自己的特殊能力，但所有国家都必须保证若干基本原则，即那些被认为对全人类福祉以及人类能力进一步发展所必需的习俗、制度和价值。比如教育方面，每个民族以及民族内部较大的少数群体，都可以按照自身特殊需要与传统安排课程和学校、学院的风格，但所有教育都必须符合新世界的根本原则：培养人格、培养世界公民，并使人的潜能得到充分表达。法律方面也是如此。虽然各国大体保留原有法律体系，但在公民自由、健康、防止经济剥削等关键问题上，都必须达到一些基本要求。如果某国法律在任何方面低于人类共同标准，那么不论改革多么剧烈，都必须实施。事实上，在法律问题上已经出现强烈趋势：取消各国一切无关紧要的怪异差别，逐步建立一套统一的世界法体系。</p>
<p>新世界秩序既已稳固，世界政府最主要的关切就是细致组织人类事务，以确保未来各代拥有尽可能优越的条件。在经济领域，目标是在生产资料与消费资料之间取得一种平衡：当代人的生活条件必须足以保障身体与心理健康，但未来的生活条件还要远胜于现在。为此，世界经济发展委员会必须进行大量研究，并作出大胆规划。与此同时，世界卫生部也得以组织协调严密的疾病攻势，确保正在成长的一代比前辈更健康。</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" class="stapledon-body" lang="en" hidden>
<h2>iii. PROGRESS</h2>
<p>THE HUMAN RACE was now able to carryon without distraction enterprises that had been started as soon as world unity had been attained. Industrial production and distribution had to be fully developed in such a way as to afford security, comfort, and full growth of body and mind to every human being between the poles. Resources of tidal and volcanic power had to be exploited to their full extent. For the needs of the race would soon be a thousandfold what they had been. New and better synthetic materials must be invented, and some old materials must be produced in far larger quantities. There must be new and plentiful building materials and standardized parts of buildings, more durable plastics for articles of domestic use, better and far more plentiful fabrics for clothing, better food, better transport, far more lavish educational equipment.</p>
<p>All this production must be done in such a way that ‘sub-human’ work could be carried on solely by machines. Of course, so long as the standard of human capacity remained what it was, many world-citizens would be content with low-grade work; but no human being must ever have to spend his life on work below his capacity, and none must ever be tied to a kind of work for which his special aptitudes were unsuited. There must be a great advance in vocational psychology, and therefore much research.</p>
<p>Psychology, indeed, now came into its own. Human culture in the scientific age had at first been dominated by physics and chemistry, then by biology; and now finally it was largely influenced by psychology. As the understanding of human nature increased, great advances were made in educational method. Crippling neuroses gradually disappeared. A composite photograph of mankind would have shown an expression of frankness, confidence, and friendliness such as in an earlier age was to be seen only in those who were outstandingly fortunate in their genes and their nurture.</p>
<p>By now it was universally realized that fullness of life, though it involved ample material means, was not to be measured simply in terms of luxury, but rather in terms of bodily well-being and the higher ranges of bodily and mental skill. A rather sharp distinction was made in the new order between articles of mere luxury and articles needed for the development of body or mind. Industry was planned so as to make the former difficult to procure, the latter easy. Luxury was by no means condemned, but the unlimited power of the world-society to produce luxury articles was deliberately restricted, so that though every one could procure a certain amount of pure luxury with his ‘luxury allowance’, no one could gather to himself masses of choice articles which it was beyond his power to use or appreciate. Thus the more flamboyant kinds of clothing, though not banned, were produced in very small quantities; while simpler materials and patterns were plentiful and various. Essential foods were obtainable everywhere in lavish amounts. Luxury foods and the more precious kinds of wine were difficult to come by. Serviceable motor cars and aeroplanes were available for every citizen. Luxury cars and planes were to be obtained only by the fanatic who was willing to stint himself in all other respects. Choice jewellery was almost unobtainable, and was used mainly for communal rather than individual display, but simple trinkets, hand-made by craftsmen steeped in some local tradition or venturing upon new forms, were available for all who wanted them. In general the aim was to use the vast mechanical resources of the race not to complicate but to simplify life, and to bring all that was needed within the reach of all. Full use was to be made of machinery while ensuring that machinery should not dominate. In the old days the needs of ordinary people were catered for incidentally by enterprises undertaken for private profit. The result was a constant appeal to the more primitive and more insistent impulses of men, and a gross degradation of sensibility and integrity. But now that public need was the first claim it was necessary to decide what the public need really was, and which needs were most to be fostered. Industry had to be planned accordingly.</p>
<p>The world which now began to emerge was of very different type from the old one. While nearly everyone was in some style a worker, the ‘working class’ was rapidly vanishing. No longer did the bulk of the population work for long hours and for insufficient pay, living more or less in squalor, and failing to secure that small amount of self- expression without which mental health is impossible. The general frustration and misery of the past had produced a characteristic mentality, now vanished. In politics, for instance, frustration had expressed itself in a gnawing vindictiveness which later on seemed merely silly. At each end of the political spectrum, and indeed to a great extent throughout it, fear, jealousy, hatred, and a frustrated itch for self-display, were dominant motives, though often appearing under the guise of righteous indignation. Hence Fascism, Nazism, and the baser sort of Communism. By now, Fascism and Nazism had of course long ago vanished. Communism, which at one time had made so great a contribution to thought and feeling and institutions, was no longer a fanatical creed. In a sense all sane men were communists, since all accepted much of the Marxin social analysis; but the militant Communist Party had long since vanished, and the Marxian attempt to do without the primacy of the fundamental values, love and wisdom, was recognized as a perversity due to the poisonous atmosphere of the machine age.</p>
<p>Instead of the ‘working class’ there were increasing millions of people whose standard of life we should call ‘comfortable middle- class’, but whose minds were very different from our middle-class minds, since they were no longer moulded by the desperate necessity of trying to get the better of their neighbours in the commercial dog- fight. Most men were now salaried servants of the world state or some national state or local or vocational authority. Three classes alone received no salary, but drew, when necessary, the liberal maintenance allowance to which every citizen was entitled when he needed it. The small and curious class of private capitalists, whose function it was to provide society with the benefits of daring private enterprise in industrial pioneering, lived on profits, but were prevented by sumptuary laws and taxation from attaining more than the tolerated degree of affluence. Their employees were skilled workers of all kinds, attracted by the possibility of somewhat higher pay and shorter hours than were allowed in state service, and by a sense of adventure in a small common enterprise. Most of them were persons who had saved up their luxury allowances to contribute to the equipping of the factory. Thus they themselves were capitalists. The aim of the original capitalist or group of capitalists who founded the concern was always to build up a co-operative and self-governing society in which all the members were in some degree capitalists.</p>
<p>The second class of unsalaried persons were the artists and writers who started professional life in complete dependence on the maintenance allowance and such extra help as they could obtain from their parents or friends. They might also gain state ‘subsidies of merit’; but in the main they hoped to live on the sale of their works, since in the new world the demand for books, pictures, musical performances, and so on, was far greater than in our own day.</p>
<p>The third unsalaried class was made up of the born idlers and tramps. These, a small minority, either supplemented their maintenance allowance with an occasional day’s labour, or frankly depended wholly on the ‘dole’. Although the great majority of these people were socially quite useless, the world society could easily afford to keep them in idleness for the sake of the few outstandingly creative or critical minds that now and then emerged from among them. Many of these inveterate tramps were people with strong anti-social impulses. They regarded all social organization as a nuisance and as ‘fair game’ for the predatory wanderer. The fact that they were nevertheless tolerated and even fostered is a measure of the stability and the wisdom of the leaders of the new world. These ne’er-do-weels were very few, for improved education had greatly reduced the number of merely warped minds. These were apparently not warped but innately individualistic. Some were individualistic to such an extent that they refused to avail themselves of the dole, and lived almost entirely by pilfering, sometimes by audacious highway robbery. To me it seemed at first incredible that this sort of thing should be permitted in this almost Utopian society. But these ‘outlaws’ were a minute section of the individualist class, and anyone who suffered from their attentions could claim compensation from the state. There was therefore no attempt to eradicate them. When they were caught they were very leniently punished, except when they had done bodily hurt to their victims.</p>
<p>In the vocational representative system which ran parallel to the parliamentary system, the capitalists, writers, artists, and tramps had their own voting colleges, along with the salaried occupations, such as engineers and teachers. The tramps and outlaws, however, very seldom exercised their right to vote.</p>
<p>The lives of salaried persons of course varied very much. The aim was to provide that in boring occupations hours should be short, and in interesting work long. Exceptionally, some monotonous work involved rather long hours, but in such cases the workers were chosen from the psychological class who thrive best on monotonous occupations in which they can day-dream. On the other hand some enthralling work was restricted to short hours because of the strain which it involved.</p>
<p>One striking institution, first tried out in North America, but immediately copied in China and soon adopted throughout the world, was the Corps of Emergency. This consisted of workers from almost every occupation chosen for their versatility and enterprise, and kept in training and on full pay, to be moved hither and thither as occasion required. Thus, if for some reason a river had to be deflected, a mountain removed, a sea drained, thousands of civil engineers were available without disturbance to existing enterprises. The Corps fulfilled the function of the unemployed in the old capitalist system, but with a very different temper.</p>
<p>The professed aim of the World Government was to secure a right balance of specialization and all-roundness. Thus the more specialized a man’s trade, the more he was encouraged to take up outside activities. Every individual, of course, was educated primarily to be a developed personality and a responsible citizen. He was given an outline of world-history and of the modern world culture. He was also deliberately educated for breadth of sympathy and understanding. Whatever his special capacities, he was trained to some degree of insight into the activities of others. It was constantly urged upon him that his prime duty was twofold, both to develop his own special aptitude and to comprehend and foster so far as possible the special aptitudes of others.</p>
<p>The World Government jealously exercised its right to supervise all national educational systems so as to ensure that the essential principles of education for citizenship in the new world should not be violated; should in fact be vigorously practised. The aim was, not only to impart a clear outline of man’s story, along with some detail of national and provincial history, but also to foster the two supremely important human impulses, the will for community and the will for intelligence. Not only as between individuals but also as between peoples specialization was carefully restricted. Inevitably at first some countries were predominantly industrial, others agricultural, but it was deliberately designed that this specialization should be based on an underlying self-sufficiency. This surprised me, for the danger of war between the peoples had by now vanished. Why, then, this insistence on self-sufficiency? Partly, self-sufficiency was a result of natural economic development. With the great advance of physical and chemical technique, industry had become far less dependent on locality. Anything from food to typewriters could now be produced in almost any district, for the primary raw materials were vegetable tissues and the very common minerals.</p>
<p>But there was another reason for increasing self-sufficiency. At first sight it seemed a reason pointing in the opposite direction. The aim of the world government was the development of the world as a whole, not of any one people. Local cultural differences were therefore to be fostered, since it was realized that mental diversity was the breath of life. This, it might seem, would involve fostering economic specialization in each country, since economic diversity should produce mental diversity. But extreme psychological specialization was now recognized to be very dangerous. The highly specialized factory worker of the past had been but the caricature of a real man. The agricultural worker who knew of nothing but turnips had been equally limited. For a people to be capable of significant cultural variation it must have within its range a great diversity of activities. Persons in each walk of life must be open to the direct and constant influence of persons whose occupations, and therefore their mentalities, are different. A highly specialized national economy breeds a lop-sided mental culture. In a world of highly specialized nations this danger can be partly avoided by the insistence on foreign travel; but not effectively; for travel is either a holiday occupation, in which case its effect though valuable, is not far-reaching; or a way of life, in which case the traveller is mentally uprooted from his native culture.</p>
<p>The aim of the leaders of the new world was a high degree not only of national but of provincial self-sufficiency. Thus in Britain, where economic organization centred on the tidal generators of the west coast of Scotland, industry was not allowed to concentrate in that district. Improved transmission made it possible to take the electric current into every part of the island, and to scatter the new bright factories and workers’ dwellings throughout the agricultural regions. On the other hand much of the former congested industrial area of Lancashire and Yorkshire and the Midlands was once more largely agricultural. In consequence, not only the Scottish and Welsh nations but the new-old English provinces of Wessex, East Anglia, Northumbria, Mercia, and so on, developed each its own limited but vigorous autonomy, and made its own contribution to the English culture. England in turn was becoming far more self-sufficient than of old. Improved agriculture and reduced population made it possible for the three British peoples to feed themselves, though there was always a large import of luxury foods from abroad. Britain had long ago ceased to be ‘the workshop of the world’, since every country was in the main its Own workshop, but Britain’s imports were ‘paid for’ by the export of the special lines of high-quality machinery and fabrics for which the British were becoming famous. Trade, in fact, was becoming more and more an exchange not of necessities, but of products which local genius produced for the amplification and embellishment of life throughout the world. Each people aimed at being basically self- sufficient but also at producing for the world economy some special class of goods which could be produced with unique success by its local tradition and skill. Each also prided itself both on its cosmopolitan and on its national culture, both on its insight into the common human tradition and on its peculiar contribution to that ever- exfoliating culture.</p>
<p>Thus the British, never a highly cultured race in the intellectual sense, claimed with some justice that they could still teach the world through the example of their political life, with its anomalous but effective institutions and its temperate and forbearing spirit. And though the population of Britain remained relatively unresponsive to literature, English writers, and particularly English poets, wrote for the world and were read by the world more than the writers of any other land. This was partly due to the importance which the luck of history had given to the English language, for at this time it had become the ‘second language’ of all other peoples, and was being constantly enriched by extensive borrowings from other languages, to such an extent that the Englishman of that age considered the English of our day as archaic as Chaucer’s English.</p>
<p>The Germans still gave the world great music, monumental works of philosophy (increasingly often written in English) and meticulous applications of science. Their organizing ability expressed itself throughout the world in the great preponderance of Germans in the control of cosmopolitan institutions such as the World Commissions for Health, Postage, Radio, Transport. Indeed there were those who murmured that the Germans had at last achieved their dream of world empire. The Russians, freed from their delusion of imperialism, rightly claimed the world’s admiration for their powers of insight into personality and their spirit of comradeship. The Tibetans, ever- respected for the glorious victory that they had won against the forces of darkness through their spiritual discipline, were universally regarded as the main fastness of the spirit. The more subtle and more diverse Indians, however, were becoming the main interpreters of spiritual experience to the rest of the world. The North Americans, now the leading pioneers in industrial invention, and also in man’s ever-increasing astronomical exploration, claimed in addition that they were leaders in the important task of digesting and co-ordinating the other cultures. The Chinese, who in virtue of sheer numbers and the continuity of their civilization played an immense part in forming the culture of the new world, ensured that the ordinary man should indeed within his powers be a cultured man, and provided him with a subtle and humane pattern of personal conduct. Thus at the outset of the phase of Utopian development there was great cultural diversity among the peoples. Of course, to excel in any one cultural direction an individual had not necessarily to belong to the people which was its chief exponent. Indeed, in every cultural sphere outstanding contributions might be made by individuals of any nation. Moreover, some cultural activities were far more international than others. Most of the natural sciences, for instance, depended on many peoples equally. But on the whole, and in the long run, each people gained its special reputation, and to excel in any sphere a man must if possible start by absorbing the contribution of the people that had done most in that sphere. Not that the talent of a people remained fixed for ever. Reputations might be lost, and new ones made. Indeed each people was capable of surprising the world with achievement in directions hitherto unattempted by it. Few would have expected that the Russians, after an age of fanatical materialism, would develop a special aptitude for mystical experience; still fewer that the minute and storm-racked population of the Shetland Isles would come to excel in philosophy to such an extent that the new little university of Lerwick vied with the great German and Indian seats of learning in this respect.</p>
<p>Though everything possible was done to encourage each people to develop its special capacities, certain essential principles were ensured in all states, namely those customs, institutions, and values which were deemed necessary for the welfare of mankind as a whole and the further development of human capacity. Thus in education, while each people and each large minority within a people was permitted to arrange curricula and the temper of its schools and colleges in accord with its peculiar needs and tradition, all must conform to the fundamental principles of the new world, educating for personality and world-citizenship, and the full expression of the potentiality of man. Similarly in respect of law, though each country preserved its legal system mainly intact, all must in respect of such vital matters as civil liberty, health, the prevention of economic exploitation, fulfil certain essential requirements. If in any respect its national legal system fell short of the common standard of mankind, changes, however drastic, had to be made. But indeed, in respect of law there was a strong tendency to abolish all national oddities and to work out a single uniform system of world law.</p>
<p>Now that the new world order was firmly established the main concern of the World Government was the detailed organization of human affairs so as to secure that future’ generations should have the best possible conditions. In the economic field the aim was to strike such a balance between producer’s goods and consumer’s goods that, though present conditions should be as favourable as was necessary for physical and mental health, future conditions should be far better. This involved a great deal of research and bold planning by the World Economic Development Commission. At the same time the World Health Ministry was able to organize a well-co-ordinated attack on disease, and to secure that the rising generation should be more healthy than their predecessors.</p>
