<h2>第二章 现代时代</h2>
<p>在一个对于本书读者而言远在未来的时刻，我忽然意识到：自己已经恍惚地观看人类事件的流动很久了。我向自己死后的记忆深处窥视，仿佛窥视第二个童年，只寻得一些碎片；它们必定来自一个漫长而动荡的时代。读者称作“现代”的时期，必然位于其中——或者说，必将位于其中——不过是一个更漫长阶段中的瞬间；而在那个更长的阶段里，光明与黑暗的斗争始终没有结果。</p>
<p>一边，是那种迟钝、爬虫般的意志：只求安逸、睡眠和死亡，有时又会突然转化为主动的仇恨和毁灭欲；另一边，则是仍蒙着眼、仍跌跌撞撞，却执意追求清醒而协调精神生活的意志。每一代人似乎都怀着勇气和希望上路，也确实具有某种走向爱与智慧生活的真实禀赋；然而他们同时承受人性致命的脆弱，又生活在不利于精神充分成长的环境里。于是，每一代人最终都在无数孤独而短暂的斗争之后走入中年：或者幻灭，或者狂热；或者麻木不仁，或者病态地贪求个人权力。</p>
<p>这个世界像一只蛹，但蛹已经受损。在科学与机械化的压力下，旧秩序早已衰败，旧有的生活模式已经无法健康地延续；可是新的秩序与新的心态又迟迟不能出生。无数心灵按旧模式塑成的人类生灵，被从安全抛进不安与迷惘之中。那些原本由环境专门训练、能够把自己嵌入既存社会结构的人，眼看那结构正在瓦解，自己也被抛入可怕的混乱；他们的才能失去用途，思想已经过时，价值尺度遭到证伪。于是，他们像失去蜂王的蜂群一样跌回原始状态。他们变成四处劫掠的匪徒，或者大声要求一种新的、强硬的、冷酷而野蛮的部族秩序，好让自己重新嵌进去。在文明的这个最低点，在这种席卷社会、迷恋野蛮与赤裸强力的渴望里，在这场精神之夜中，以往最卑贱、最受鄙视的人类类型反而掌握了权力：流氓和枪手。他们除个人支配之外不承认任何价值；他们怀着复仇心，立志践踏那个曾经鄙弃自己的文明，只为抢掠本身而统治一个新的匪帮社会。</p>
<p>因此，凡是旧秩序崩解得足够彻底的地方，新的秩序确实开始出现——残酷、野蛮，却掌握科学，而且经过精密组织以服务战争。在那个时代，战争虽然并非永不停止，却从未真正远离。地球总有某个区域几乎始终在打仗；一场战争刚刚结束，另一处就会立刻开战。即使没有实际战争的地方，也始终笼罩着下一场战争即将到来的恐惧。</p>
<p>这个尚未完成的人类物种，一半仍是动物，却又潜藏着成为真正“人”的可能；它的根本难题始终是：人类一次又一次试图意愿出真正的共同体，却始终没有成功。所谓真正的共同体，乃是独立精神之间真实而完整的结合；其中每个人都有自己的个性、彼此有差异，却能够互相理解、互相珍爱。然而，那种摸索着走向共同体的冲动，总会因为人们无法区分“真正的共同体”与“兽群式的野蛮一致”而受挫；另一方面，人们同样无法区分对内在精神的忠诚与更隐蔽的自负，也无法区分爱与单纯的占有欲。</p>
<p>如今，在光明与黑暗最终势均力敌的时刻，人类刚刚得到的阿拉丁神灯——科学——赋予他们如此巨大的行善与作恶能力，以至于他们必须迅速突破到真正的共同体，否则便会踏上一条越来越陡、最终通往毁灭的斜坡。在人与人的直接交往中，在城市、地区、国家与社会阶级的事务中，以及更进一步，在如何安排整个行星这件最新、也最危险的任务上，一种新的精神必须开始显出哪怕一点微光；否则，人类一旦在这场大考中失败，就会滑入一种新的、不可逆转的野蛮。而在一个被科学紧密联结起来的世界中，野蛮所带来的就是死亡。</p>
<p>在火车、轮船、飞机与无线电把各地联成一体的新世界里，只容得下一个社会。然而一个覆盖全球的社会，必然必须在每个细节上经过规划和组织，否则根本无法保障所有个人的自由与实现。那种旧日的偶然秩序——尤其有利于幸运而狡猾的自利者——正到处消失。人们的生活必然越来越多地接受权威调节。但是什么样的权威？又由什么样的精神来支配它？一个没有真正共同体洞察力而建立的庞大计划国家，必然走向暴政；一旦再把科学用于镇压和宣传，它就必然摧毁公民的人性。只有真正共同体的洞察与意志，才有可能正当地运用一个国家的权力，更不用说运用整个世界的权力。</p>
<p>而在光明与黑暗彼此平衡的那个时代，各国恰恰缺乏这种洞察与意志，因此它们都沉重地压在自己的公民身上，也彼此压迫。为了国家安全，个人行动越来越多地受到国家控制，思想也越来越被塑造成国家要求的标准样式。所有人都接受纪律化和标准化。每个人在这部防御与进攻的庞大共同机器中，都有官方指定的位置和任务。任何抗议者，任何不够热烈的人，都必须被消灭。国家永远处在危险之中，每一根神经也永远绷紧。又因为各国都刻意在敌国公民中播撒叛乱，没有谁能够信任自己的邻居。丈夫怀疑妻子，妻子怀疑丈夫；孩子以告发父母为荣。即使只是和平时期生活的压力，也足以损伤所有人的心灵。疯狂像瘟疫一样蔓延。那些最“正常”的人虽然自以为判断未受扭曲，实际上也已经成了被恐惧折磨的神经症患者。于是，这整个种族尽管仍被一种模糊的精神幻象所撩拨，尽管对爱与理性仍保留脆弱的忠诚，最终却还是大体把自己交给了更低贱的天性。</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" class="stapledon-body" lang="en" hidden>
<h2>2. THE MODERN AGE</h2>
<p>AT SOME DATE which to readers of this book is far off in the future I became aware that I had long been dreamily witnessing a flux of human events. Peering back into my post-mortal memory as though into a second infancy, I came upon fragments of what must have been a long age of turmoil. Within that age must have lain, or must lie, the period that readers of this book call modern, a moment within a longer period during which the struggle between the light and the darkness remained inconclusive.</p>
<p>On the one side was the sluggish reptilian will for ease and sleep and death, rising sometimes to active hate and destructiveness; on the other side the still blindfold and blundering will for the lucid and coherent spirit. Each generation, it seemed, set out with courage and hope, and with some real aptitude for the life of love and wisdom, but also with the fatal human frailty, and in circumstances hostile to the generous development of the spirit. Each in turn, in the upshot of innumerable solitary ephemeral struggles, sank into middle age, disillusioned or fanatical, inert or obsessively greedy for personal power.</p>
<p>The world was a chrysalis world, but the chrysalis was damaged. Under the stress of science and mechanization the old order had become effete, the old patterns of life could no longer be healthily lived; yet the new order and the new mentality could not be born. The swarms of human creatures whose minds had been moulded to the old patterns were plunged from security into insecurity and bewilderment. Creatures specialized by circumstance to knit themselves into the existing but disintegrating social texture found themselves adrift in dreadful chaos, their talents useless, their minds out-moded, their values falsified. And so, like bees in a queenless hive, they floundered into primitive ways. They became marauding gangsters, or clamoured for some new, strong, ruthless and barbaric tribal order, into which they might once more themselves. In this nadir of civilization, this wide- craving for the savage and the stark, this night of spirit, there rose to power the basest and hitherto t despised of human types, the hooligan and the gun-man, who recognized no values but personal dominance, whose vengeful aim was to trample the civilization that spurned them, and to rule for brigandage alone a new gangster society.</p>
<p>Thus, wherever the breakdown of the old order was far gone, a new order did indeed begin to emerge, ruthless, barbaric, but armed with science and intricately fashioned for war. And war in that age, though not perpetual, was never far away. In one region or another of the planet there was nearly always war. No sooner had one war ended than another began elsewhere. And where there was no actual war, there was the constant fear of wars to come.</p>
<p>The crux for this unfinished human species, half animal but potentially humane, had always been the inconclusive effort to will true community, true and integrated union of individual spirits, personal, diverse, but mutually comprehending and mutually cherishing. And always the groping impulse for community had been frustrated by the failure to distinguish between true community and the savage unity of the pack; and on the other hand between a man’s duty to the innermost spirit and mere subtle self-pride, and again between love and mere possessiveness.</p>
<p>And now, in this final balance of the strife between light and darkness, the newly won Aladdin’s lamp, science, had given men such power for good and evil that they inevitably must either win speedily through to true community or set foot upon a steepening slope leading to annihilation. In the immediate contacts of man with man, and in the affairs of cities, provinces, slates and social classes, and further (newest and most dangerous necessity) in the ordering of the planet as a whole, there must now begin some glimmer of a new spirit; or else, failing in the great test, man must slide into a new and irrevocable savagery. And in a world close-knit by science savagery brings death.</p>
<p>In the new world, made one by trains, ships, aeroplanes and radio there was room for one society only. But a world-wide society must inevitably be planned and organized in every detail. Not otherwise can freedom and fulfilment be secured for all individuals. The old haphazard order so favourable to the fortunate and cunning self- seeker, was everywhere vanishing. Inevitably men’s lives w bound to be more and more regulated by authority. But what authority, and in what spirit? A great planned state, controlled without insight into true community, must turn to tyranny. And, armed with science for oppression and propaganda, it must inevitably destroy the humanity of its citizens. Only the insight and the will of true community can wield rightly a state’s authority, let alone a world’s.</p>
<p>Lacking that insight and that will, the states of theworld in the age of balanced light and darkness bore very heavily on their citizens and on one another. For national safety men’s actions were increasingly controlled by the state, their minds increasingly moulded to the formal pattern that the state required of them. All men were disciplined and standardized. Everyone had an official place and task in the huge common work of defence and attack. Anyone who protested or was lukewarm must be destroyed. The state was always in danger, and every nerve was constantly at strain. And because each state carefully sowed treason among the citizens of other states, no man could trust his neighbour. Husbands and wives suspected one another. Children proudly informed against their parents. Under the strain even of peace-time life all minds were damaged. Lunacy spread like a plague. The most sane, though in their own view their judgment was unwarped, were in fact fear-tortured neurotics. And so the race, as a whole, teased by its obscure vision of the spirit, its frail loyalty to love and reason, surrendered itself in the main to its baser nature.</p>
