<h3>第九章</h3>
<h3>天狼星与宗教</h3>
<p>同普拉克茜共度那一天之后，天狼星步行回实验室。一路上，他反复想着人类的缺陷、自己的孤独以及宇宙的冷漠，渐渐又滑进了狼性的情绪。挫败感总容易在他身上造成这种结果，而此刻他正感到绝望地受挫。他渴望表达自我，却找不到任何真正能做到这一点的途径。还是幼犬时，他曾经决定将来要当一名将军，用超人的技巧调度自己的人类军队，带着他们冲向超人的胜利。多么可笑、又多么不可能的梦想！后来，他又曾决定去探索西伯利亚苔原或者大草原——在他想象中，那样的地方很适合自己的能力。可一条狗怎么可能带着必要装备远行，而又不引起沿途人类居民的骚动？又或者，澳大利亚的牧羊生活适合他？再不然，到加拿大北部从事某种狩猎职业？不。到现在，一切已经再清楚不过：没有任何生活真正适合他。除了做一条超级哈巴狗兼超级实验动物之外，什么都不可能。</p>
<p>可他体内始终有某种奇怪而顽固的“东西”在催促：“快去做！你有独一无二的能力。世界上只有一个你，而你的存在就是为了对世界作出你自己的贡献。找到你的使命。对你来说这当然困难，可你必须找到，否则你就完了。”有时，那声音还会说：“对你而言，人类就是你的群体。你不是他们中的一员，可他们创造了你，你也理应服务于他们。正因为你不同，你才能让他们看见某种凭自己永远看不到的东西。”那么，自己毕竟有没有可能通过音乐来完成这项使命？一些宏大得近乎荒唐的幻想开始袭击他：“天狼星，独一无二的犬类作曲家，不仅彻底改变了人类音乐的面貌，把狗更加精细的听觉感受带进其中；还在自己无与伦比的创作中，表达了所有精神——无论犬类、人类还是超人类——在差异之下的根本同一性。”</p>
<p>可不！不可能。人类根本不会听他的。而他又凭什么相信，自己真有足够的天才，能让音乐刺进人类那深不可测的心灵深处？</p>
<p>回实验室的路上，那熟悉而烦人的声音又出现了，催促他把自己身上的“精神”表达出来。他在心里冲它低吼。自己还能怎么办？什么也办不了。他是个不合规格的东西，是个错误。他根本不该出现。</p>
<p>他越来越强烈地想在街上发狂。这样的生命对他有什么用？为什么不干脆扔掉？为什么不尽可能多咬死一些这些打扮得滑稽、又自命不凡的猿猴，直到他们终于设法把自己杀死？“我不会，我不会。”他一遍遍告诉自己，“就算他们是猿，或者长着分叉肢体的虫子，他们归根结底还是和我由同一种东西构成。”仿佛是在逃离自己，他从小跑加速到快跑，又变成真正的狂奔，只想尽快躲回自己的房间。到了那里，他来来回回踱了好几个小时，一直到深夜。这几个小时成了他一生中的关键转折点，因此我将引用他第二天亲手写下的记录。文字相当臃肿，却很能说明他当时那种不健康的精神状态。</p>
<p>“我不停走来走去。每次转身，肩膀都会故意重重擦过墙壁，疼得厉害；经过窗帘时，我还会一口咬过去。这其实是在做作——我把自己演成一头关在笼子里的野兽。各学院和教堂的钟声每刻钟响一次。随着夜越来越深，街上汽车经过的声音也渐渐消失。我一遍遍愤怒地想起普拉克茜的气味——既亲爱又令人厌恶；想起我上一只母狗的香气，那么甜，却又是假的，因为它仿佛承诺着一个根本不存在的可爱灵魂。接着，又突然闻见记忆里伊德瓦尔那友好的气味，还有一群被雾打湿的羊。普格的味道，带着汗和兴奋。霜的气味，夏日的气味，从海上吹来的风，还有风向从西转东时的变化。兔子的踪迹，野兔的踪迹。猫那令人暴怒的臭味。狐狸——浓郁而微妙。动物园。氯仿，以及那两个袭击我的恶棍。还有实验室某个我从未进去过的区域，偶尔会飘来一股极淡、却让喉咙发紧的痛苦气味。</p>
<p>“在这一整片气味洪流下面，还有声音组成的暗流：人的声调，狗的声音；羊和羔羊的叫声；风，有时呜咽，有时暴怒；零碎的人类音乐，还有我自己唱过的旋律主题。</p>
<p>“我的整个人生仿佛都通过气味和声音挤进脑子里；还有触觉——我感觉到普拉克茜的手放在我脖子上，感觉到骨头在我牙齿之间碎裂，也感觉到很久以前在费斯蒂尼奥格爱过的一只年轻塞特犬柔软的腰胁。</p>
<p>“视觉形象也出现了，只是很暗淡，而且不稳定。有时我瞥见托马斯抿着嘴，若有所思地打量我；有时看见普拉克茜微笑。</p>
<p>“这些记忆不断出现时，各种念头也在我脑子里飞奔、碰撞。最多的是恐惧和愤恨：人类对我拥有怎样的权力，而我又怎样没能成为自己命运的主人。我怎么才能救自己，不让已经开始的崩溃继续下去？哪里有东西能帮助我？托马斯其实并不真正理解自己造出的这个生物。伊丽莎白随时愿意听我诉苦、安慰我；可不知为什么，她总会把我的困境变成一个孩子的烦恼。普拉克茜如今又离得那么远。我们以前说过，真正重要的是‘精神’；我们‘在精神上’永远结合在一起。可现在呢？我们说‘精神’这个词时，究竟真的指向了什么实在的东西吗？我开始怀疑。也许说到底，我们都不过是有一点智力的动物，而且是不同物种的动物，注定永远不能真正合而为一，永远处在不协调中，如今又不可避免地越漂越远。</p>
<p>“为什么？为什么所有东西在承诺中都那么甜美，一旦实现却总是苦涩？</p>
<p>“可过了一阵，当我还在那间小屋里来回踱步时，一件奇怪的事发生了。仿佛我那漫游不定的想象忽然碰上了一种新的性质，一种我从未体验过的东西；可它同时又比普拉克茜在爱意中散发的气味更熟悉、更亲密，比母狗更加尖锐地甜美，也比狐狸的踪迹更值得追猎。</p>
<p>“不，我不能把它浪漫化。这是一份科学报告。实际上并没有什么新的感官性质真的进入我的感觉。只是我的心里发生了某件事，而除了这样描述，我找不到别的办法。假如那东西真能叫作一种芳香，那么它就是爱、智慧与创造本身的芳香——这些东西仅仅因为自身就值得追求，无论最后有没有成功、有没有幸福。正是这种芳香，以一种我前所未有地强烈感到的鲜明方式来到我这里，所以对我而言几乎成了全新的东西。那芳香仿佛拖着踪迹穿过整个宇宙，在所有深渊和缝隙之间蜿蜒穿行；过去它其实已经无数次诱惑过我，只是现在，在这种亢奋状态中，它呈现得如此鲜明，我不得不在心里把它戏剧化成一种全新的性质——既不是气味，不是声音，也不是可见形状；但若一定要比喻，它最像一种必须循迹追逐的气味。</p>
<p>“于是我真的追了下去。我停止踱步，趴下来，把两条前腿向前伸直，下巴搁在上面。我不再理会其他所有记忆中的气味，只用内在注意力那双飞快的脚，追逐这条陌生的新踪迹。越往前追，踪迹就越强、越清楚、越精妙。有时它会从我这里逃掉，我只好折返回去重新搜索，再把它找回来。有时我的力量衰竭，速度慢下来，那气味也会跟着变淡。可我又会重新集中自己，继续追猎；随着我一点点缩短与猎物之间的距离，那气味也越来越清晰，越来越不可抗拒。</p>
<p>“最后，发生了一件可怕的事。随着我不断接近，那天国猎物的性质仿佛开始改变。原先那种精妙的甜美仍在，仍引诱我向前；但里面混入了一种新的、刺激性的味道——辛辣、刺痛、令人窒息、苦涩，同时又美妙而可怕的芳香。它里面有某种东西令我的心智摇晃，就像氯仿曾经做过的那样；还有某种猛烈的东西，仿佛老虎和狮子强烈的气味，却带着任何尘世气味都没有的冷酷。我没法放弃追猎。即使心智踉跄，我仍死死咬住那条踪迹。我追逐的东西，必定是一切宇宙芳香的源头，同时也是一切恐怖的源头。而我对它饥渴到了极点。我必须、必须追到它；虽然到了最后，肯定不会是我吞掉它，而是它吞掉我。这个我疯狂追逐的东西，一定正是人类所称的上帝——亲爱的、美丽的、又令人战栗的东西。</p>
<p>“最后，仿佛猎物忽然转身迎战，把我彻底压倒。如今再回忆，我已经无法重新抓住那一瞬间的痛苦与狂喜：我的自我仿佛被杀死，那是痛苦；而我体内的精神仿佛被释放，那是狂喜。就好像——我该怎么说？——最初许诺给我一块最鲜美猎物、后来又变成最可怕却又摄人心魄之敌的那条踪迹，最终带我到的并不是一头宇宙之虎，而是一位宇宙之主；一位超越人类的主人，而我的超犬类本性竟是如此绝望地需要这样一位主人来占有我，用他对绝对忠诚与服务的要求把我稳定下来。</p>
<p>“那个最高的时刻过去了。我只记得，它过去之后，我获得了一种此前从未有过的安宁，而且我相信自己再也不会完全失去它。整个宇宙忽然带上一种新的性质，仿佛我原本只能看见单色的视野，突然获得了色彩的辉煌。但我看见的颜色并不属于感觉，而是精神之眼看见的颜色。此前我在日常生活那平淡灰色里见过的一切事物和一切人，现在都被这种我暂且称为‘色彩’的新性质以极丰富的方式充满，于是获得了新的意义，就像声音在语言或音乐里会获得新的意义一样。我看见所有东西呈现出自己真正的颜色，同时又被整体的音乐所浸透。甚至现在，在那个荣耀时刻已经过去一天之后——它本身已经消失，只在我心里留下余辉——我仍看见一切都被精神的光染上颜色。”</p>
<p>后面还有一段附记。</p>
<p>“上面这些，是在那次异象——如果它真能叫异象——发生后的第二天写下的。现在又过了一天。我重新读了一遍，发现这些文字根本没有描述出真正发生在我身上的事情。它只是一堆伤感的辞藻。它不仅没把那次经验重新带回我面前，反而把它弄模糊了。但我确定，确实有某件重大的事情发生在我身上。证据将体现在我的生活里。我要接管自己的生命。我不再漂流。我依旧会忠于科学，但也会忠于自己新获得的光。我会怀疑一切，只有一件事除外；那件事一旦被真正看清，就根本容不得怀疑：尽可能让精神真正醒来，是一件确实重要的事；而自己的生活，也应该用来帮助一切地方的精神获得这种醒来。事实上，我要成为精神的猎犬。就我？这个懒惰、总替自己找借口的我？这不是个好笑话吗！要是用科学式的冷静眼光看，我敢肯定，还没到周末我就又会开始漂流。好吧，就算真那样，前天夜里发生的事情也一定会造成某种差别。可如果是在那东西的光照下来看——不，以上帝之名！我绝不会再漂流了！至少从根本上不会。”</p>
<p>天狼星怀着极大的不安，还是老老实实把这份文字交给托马斯。他会觉得好笑吗？会恼火吗？还是会以那种疏离的科学冷静，只把它当作一项心理学资料？天狼星始终没弄清托马斯真正怎么看这件事。那位大生理学家的态度很尊重，甚至有点拘谨。他只问天狼星介不介意把这份文字打成三份，“一份留作实验室记录，另外两份——如果你不介意——给我的几个朋友看看。”</p>
<p>这次看似神秘主义的经验，使天狼星对宗教产生了新的兴趣。通过托马斯的一位客人，他偶然接触到神秘主义文学，很快便花大量时间阅读锡耶纳的圣加大利纳、十字若望、雅各布·伯墨、《吠檀多》等等。替他弄来这些书的人当然是托马斯；这项差事让托马斯身上的气味变得辛辣而带有明显的不赞成，虽然在言语和行动上，他依然表现得很理解。</p>
<p>天狼星现在非常渴望同一个真正虔诚、而且正统的宗教人士讨论宗教问题。可在托马斯那些值得信赖、可以知道天狼星秘密的朋友中，似乎没有这样的人。他们要么在狭义意义上完全是科学主义者，要么只会说：“人骨子里总觉得宗教应该有点什么东西，可到底是什么，上帝才知道。”同这些人接触，只会进一步刺激天狼星追究宗教问题的欲望，却给不了他什么帮助。</p>
<p>有时，他会在小礼拜堂和教堂门口徘徊，看着会众进出；或者竖起自己过分敏锐的耳朵，努力捕捉从建筑深处传出的音乐、祈祷、经文朗读和讲道的回声。自己仅仅因为是一条狗，就不被允许进入神圣建筑；这个事实进一步加深了他的流亡感和低人一等的感觉，也使他越来越容易相信：尽管那些批评宗教的人说了许多话，人类最高层次的经验也许真的就发生在这些墙壁之内。</p>
<p>有一次，他对真理的饥渴强烈到了让自己做出一件极其愚蠢的事。那时正值夏天，遇上一场热浪。他一直看着信徒走进一间小小的卫理公会礼拜堂。不同于平时，礼拜开始以后大门并没有关。充满情绪的祈祷和有力的歌唱从里面涌出来。以天狼星精细的感受力来看，那音乐粗糙，演唱也俗气；但恰恰是这些缺陷，反而让他更强烈地觉得：这里的音乐不过是在匆忙表达某种更深层经验的符号。诗就算写得仓促，也可能是真诚的。那粗野的声音让他不舒服，却又吸引着他，于是他一步一步挪进门廊，又跨过里面那道门槛。进去时，人们正在祈祷。牧师虔敬地闭着眼睛，声音油滑，又带着一种自鸣得意的奴顺。他用悔罪和崇拜的惯常语调，虽然内心其实根本没有真正体验到这些情绪，却宣称整个人类种族都有罪；随后又充满信心、近乎奉承地请求自己的上帝宽恕自己和这群羊，并赐予他们永恒福乐。低头祈祷的会众从长椅上方露出一排排后背，看起来像羊圈里羊的脊背。只不过在那个炎热的日子，他们身上的气味实在太人类了。</p>
<p>祈祷结束，牧师睁开眼睛，看见那条大狗正站在过道中央。他戏剧性地伸手指向天狼星，大喊：“谁把那只动物带进上帝的殿堂？把它赶出去！”几件黑外套和条纹裤子立刻朝天狼星移动过去。他们以为他会自动后退，可天狼星寸步不让，头和尾巴都高高竖起，背上的毛根根炸开。一声很轻的低吼，像远处雷声，让准备驱赶他的人犹豫起来。天狼星环视整间礼拜堂。所有眼睛都盯着他，有的愤怒，有的觉得好笑。他慢慢转身，准备原路离开。那些赶他出去的人谨慎地向前跟进。一个人说：“好狗！回家去！”另一个却拿雨伞赶他，还愚蠢地在他后腿上敲了一下。天狼星猛地转身一声狂吠，声音在空荡的礼拜堂里回响，把追赶者吓得齐齐退了一步。他站在那里看了他们几秒，觉得这种轻易取得的小胜利很有趣。脊背上的毛也慢慢落下。他含糊地摇了一下尾巴，再次朝门口走去。就在这时，一个恶作剧的念头突然占据了他。走到门口，他又转过身面向全体会众，用清晰、准确、虽然没有歌词的声音，把自己进门之前人们唱过的那首赞美诗副歌完整唱了一遍。等他转身离开时，一个女人尖叫起来。牧师用有些紧绷的声音说：“各位朋友，我想我们最好再一起祷告一次。”</p>
<p>另一次，他跟在救世军的鼓队旁边行进，有时甚至忘乎所以，跟着小号一起发声。后来他告诉托马斯，那种露天礼拜给了自己一种毫无理性的“得救感”。最吸引他的是其中一首大家唱得极有劲头的赞美诗，主题是“在羔羊的血中洗净”。他忍不住也低声加入歌唱。他完全看不出这种意象怎样能同爱的宗教协调起来，可它偏偏对他有一种奇异力量。他模糊而毫无理性地觉得，这首歌把自己生命里全部的温柔，同体内全部的狼性统一到了一起。他仿佛又闻见自己杀死的那只公羊和那匹小马身上诱人的血腥气。那种长期纠缠他的怜悯与嗜血之间的冲突，仿佛突然被解决了。他的罪责被洗净。没有任何合理理由，他就是这么感觉。他和这些人类动物仿佛一起把自己的罪卸给了羔羊，于是从彼此相连、所有人共同相连之中，获得了一种粗糙的狂喜。他们把自己贬低、消融进这个群体人格化的精神。陶醉的心智放弃了一切清晰思考和精确感受，屈服于共同心态；而那共同心态又仿佛是普遍的、宇宙性的，是所有世界中一切个体精神“共同在场”的人格化。这就是粗野旋律浸透他头脑时，他所体验到的东西。可在心灵另一部分看来，小号的爆鸣、鼓声的轰响以及人类充满力量的歌唱，又遥远得像丛林里另一个陌生物种的嚎叫。不是这样——他心里反抗的部分说——不是为了获得“大家在一起”的温暖，就放弃清晰思想和精确感受；真正同他和这些人类都同一的那种根本精神，不会在这里被找到。只有在对自我和他者最清楚、最准确、最充分表达的意识里，那东西才可能出现。比如，他同普拉克茜偶尔达到精神和谐的那些罕见时刻；他们正是通过彼此的差异和各自鲜明的独立，才发现底层的同一性。是的，他有时同托马斯也以另一种方式找到过它：两个人的理智沿某个论证陡峭的山路一同向上攀登，托马斯始终在前面引路，直到两人一起抵达某个峰顶；从那里望去，仿佛整个宇宙都可以被收入眼底。</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" lang="en" hidden>
<h3><a id="a576"></a>CHAPTER IX</h3>
<p><span class="t28">A</span><span class="t30">S S</span><span class="t31">IRIUS</span><span class="t30"> </span><span class="t31">WALKED</span><span class="t30"> </span>home to the Laboratory after his day with Plaxy, brooding on the shortcomings of man, and his own loneliness, and the indifference of the universe, he began to slip into the wolf-mood. Frustration always tended to have this effect on him, and he was feeling desperately frustrated. He longed for self-expression, and could find no means of attaining it. When he was a puppy he had decided that he would be a general, deploying his human troops with superhuman skill, charging with them to superhuman victory. Ludicrous, impossible dream! Later he had determined to be an explorer of the Siberian Tundra or prairies (a country that he thought suited to his powers); but how could a dog take the necessary gear with him without causing excitement among the human inhabitants? Or perhaps Australian sheep-farming would suit him, or some kind of hunting career in the north of Canada. No, by now it was all too clear that nothing would suit him, nothing was possible but to be a super-lap-dog-cum-super-laboratory-animal.</p>
<p>Yet always there was a strange nagging “something” within him which said, “Get on with it! You have unique powers. There is only one of you, and you exist to make your contribution to the world. Find your calling. It is difficult for you, no doubt, but you must find it or be damned.” Sometimes the voice said, “For you, humanity is the pack. You are not one of them, but they made you and you are for them. And because you are different you can give them a vision which they can never win for themselves.” Could he, after all, fulfil his task, perhaps, through music? Grandiose fantasies assailed him. “Sirius, the unique canine composer, not only changed the whole character of human music, importing into it something of the dog’s finer auditory sensibility; he also, in his own incomparable creations, expressed the fundamental identity-in-diversity of all spirits, of whatever species, canine, human or superhuman.”</p>
<p>But no! It could not be. Man would never listen to him. And what reason had he to suppose that he had the genius to strike his music into the deep incomprehensible heart of man?</p>
<p>On the way back to the Laboratory Sirius heard the familiar nagging voice, calling him to express the “spirit” in him. He greeted it with an inward snarl. What could he possibly do about it? Nothing. He was a misfit, a mistake. He ought never to have occurred.</p>
<p>He felt an increasing impulse to run amok in the street. Life was no good to him. Why not throw it away, why not kill as many as he could of these ridiculously bedecked, swelled-headed apes, until they managed to destroy him? “I won’t, I won’t,” he kept saying to himself. “Even if they are apes, or forked worms, they are the same stuff really as I am.” Fleeing from himself, he broke into a trot, a canter, a real gallop, needing the seclusion of his own room. There, he paced up and down for many hours, far into the night. These hours form a crucial point in his life, so I shall quote from the account which he himself wrote down on the following day; turgid stuff, but significant of his unwholesome state.</p>
<p>“I walked and walked, rubbing my shoulder painfully against the wall every time I turned, snapping at the curtain as I passed it. This was affectation; I was dramatizing myself as a caged beast. The colleges and churches chimed, quarter by quarter. The noise of passing cars died down as the night advanced. I kept remembering with fury the smell of Plaxy, dear and loathsome; and the scent of my last bitch, so sweet, but false, promising a lovely spirit that did not exist. Then the sudden presence of Idwal’s friendly smell, and of a flock of sheep, drenched with mist. The smell of Pugh, sweaty and excited. Of frost, of a summer day, of wind from the sea, of the change of wind from west to east. Trails of rabbit and hare. The infuriating stink of cat. Fox, rich and subtle. The menagerie. Chloroform, and the two toughs that had attacked me. The faint, throat-tightening smell of suffering which sometimes seems to come from the part of the Lab where I have never been.</p>
<p>“Below all this flood of smells there was an undercurrent of sounds; tones of human voices, and dog voices; bleating of sheep and lambs; the wind, whimpering or furious; snatches of human music, and themes of my own singing.</p>
<p>“My whole life seemed to crowd in on me in smell and sound; and touch also, for I felt Plaxy’s hand on my neck, and the cracking of bones between my teeth, and the soft flanks of a young setter that I had loved long ago in Ffestiniog.</p>
<p>“Visual shapes came too, but dimly, unsteadily. Sometimes I glimpsed Thomas with pursed lips, considering me; sometimes Plaxy smiling.</p>
<p>“While these memories presented themselves to me, thoughts also kept racing and jostling one another through my mind, chiefly terrified and resentful thoughts about man’s power over me, and my own failure to be master of my fate. How could I ever save myself from the breakdown that had already begun in me? What help was there anywhere for me? Thomas did not really understand the creature he had made. Elizabeth was always ready to hear my troubles and comfort me; but somehow she turned them all into child’s troubles. And Plaxy was now so far away. It was ‘the spirit,’ we had said, that mattered. It was ‘in the spirit’ that we were eternally together. But now? Had we meant anything real at all by ‘the spirit’? I wondered. After all we were just animals, with some degree of intelligence; animals of different species, doomed never really to be at one with each other, always in discord, and now drifting inevitably apart.</p>
<p>“Why, why was everything so sweet in promise and yet always in realization bitter?</p>
<p>“But presently, as I paced up and down the little room, a queer thing happened. It was as though my wandering imagination came upon a new quality, different from all that I had ever known; yet one which was also more familiar and intimate than the smell of Plaxy in the mood of love, more piercing sweet than bitches, more hunt-worthy than the trail of a fox.</p>
<p>“No, I must not romanticize. This is a scientific report. No new sensory quality really came to me. But something happened in my mind which I can’t describe in any other way. If it was a fragrance at all, it was the fragrance of love and wisdom and creating, of these for their own sake, whether crowned with success and happiness or not. It was this fragrance, which somehow came to me with such a fresh poignancy that it was something entirely new to me. It was this fragrance, trailed across the universe, winding in and out of all its chasms and interstices, that had so often enticed me; but now in my excited state it presented itself to me so vividly that I had to dramatize it to myself as a new quality, neither odour nor sound nor visible form, but most like an odour to be pursued.</p>
<p>“And I did pursue it. I stopped pacing, and lay down with my forelegs stretched before me; and I laid my chin along them. Ignoring all the other remembered scents, I pursued this strange new trail, with the flying feet of inner attention. And as I followed, the trail became stronger, clearer, more exquisite. Sometimes it escaped me, but casting back I recovered it. Sometimes my strength failed, and as I flagged the trail grew fainter. But I gathered myself together again for the chase, and as I pursued, lessening the distance between myself and the quarry, the scent grew clearer and more compelling.</p>
<p>“At last a terrible thing happened. As I drew nearer, the quality of the heavenly quarry seemed to change. Though its exquisite sweetness remained, drawing me on, a new, pungent tang, a stinging, choking, bitter, exquisite and terrifying perfume, was mixed with it. There was something in it that made my mind reel, as the chloroform had done; and something fierce, like the mighty smells of tiger and lion, but with a grimness that no earthly smell ever had. I could not give up the chase. With staggering mind I still clung to the trail. The thing that I was hunting must surely be the source of all fragrance in the universe, and all horror also. And I was famished for the thing. I must, must reach it; though in the end surely not I should devour it, but it would devour me. Surely the thing that I was crazily hunting must be the very thing that men called God, the dear and beautiful and dread.</p>
<p>“At last it was as though the quarry turned at bay and overwhelmed me. Remembering, I cannot recapture that moment of agony and bliss, the agony of my slaughtered self, the bliss of the freed spirit in me. It was as though — how can I put it? — as though the trail which had first promised the most succulent prey, and then the most formidable but spell-binding enemy, had led after all not to the universal Tiger but to the universal Master, the superhuman master whom my super-canine nature so desperately needed to take possession of me and steady me with his claim for absolute loyalty and service.</p>
<p>“That supreme moment passed. I can remember only that when it passed I found peace such as I had not known before and shall never, I believe, quite lose. The whole universe now presented itself with a new quality, as though my monochrome vision had suddenly gained the glory of colour. But the colours that I saw were not of sensation. They were the colours that are seen by the eye of the spirit. All the things and people that I had seen hitherto in the plain greys of ordinary life were now enriched with a great diversity of the new quality which I am calling colour, so that they gained a new meaning, much as sounds gain a new meaning in speech or music. I saw them all in their own true colours and suffused with the music of the whole. And even now, on the day after my glorious moment, which is lost save for its afterglow in my mind, I still see everything coloured by the light of the spirit.”</p>
<p>There followed a postscript.</p>
<p>“All this was written on the day after my vision, if vision it was. And now another day has passed. I have read it over, and I see that it does not describe at all the thing that happened to me. It is sentimental verbiage. It does not recapture the experience for me, it blurs it. But I am certain that something big really did happen to me. And the proof shall be shown in my life. I will take charge of my life. I will drift no more. I will still be true to science, but I will be true to my new light also. I will be a sceptic about everything but one thing, which does not admit of scepticism (once one has clearly seen it), namely that it does indeed matter to be as quickened a spirit as possible, and to live for the quickening of the spirit everywhere. In fact I am going to be the hound of the spirit. Me? Lazy, excuse-finding me? That’s a good joke, isn’t it! Looking at the matter with scientific detachment, I am sure I shall be adrift again before the week is out. Well, even if I am, the thing that happened the night before last will make a difference. And looking at it all in the light of the thing — no, by God! I shall never be adrift again! Not fundamentally.”</p>
<p>With much misgiving Sirius dutifully offered this document to Thomas. Would he be amused, or annoyed? Or would he take it with all his aloof scientific detachment as a psychological datum? Sirius never discovered what Thomas really felt about it. The great physiologist was respectful, almost diffident; and hoped Sirius did not object to having the document typed in triplicate, “for the Laboratory records, and to show to a few of my friends, if you don’t mind.”</p>
<p>This seemingly mystical experience awakened in Sirius a new interest in religion. Through one of Thomas’s guests he stumbled on the literature of mysticism, and was soon devoting a great deal of his time to St. Catherine of Siena, St. John of the Cross, Jacob Boehme, the Vedanta, and so on. It was Thomas who had to procure these works for him; and the task made Thomas smell acrid and disapproving, even though in word and deed he remained sympathetic.</p>
<p>Sirius now conceived a great desire to discuss religion with some sincere and orthodox religious person. No such person, it seemed, was among Thomas’s circle of trusted friends who might be admitted into the secret of Sirius’s intelligence. They were all either strictly scientific in the narrow sense or inclined to say “One feels in one’s bones that there must be something in religion, but God knows what.” Contact with these people merely increased Sirius’s desire to pursue the matter, without helping him.</p>
<p>Sometimes he would hang about the doors of chapels and churches, to watch the congregation enter or leave the building, or to strain his hypersensitive ears to catch reverberations of the music, the prayers, the lessons and the sermon. The fact that as a mere dog he was not allowed into the sacred building increased his sense of exile and inferiority, and his readiness to believe that in spite of the critics it was within those walls that man attained his highest range of experience.</p>
<p>On one occasion his hunger for the truth was so great that he could not restrain himself from a very foolish act. It was summer time, and there was a heat wave. He had been watching the worshippers entering a little Methodist Chapel. Contrary to custom, the doors were not shut before the service began. Emotional prayer and vigorous singing flooded out upon him. To his refined sensibility the music was crude and the execution vulgar, but these very imperfections increased for him the feeling that music was here only a hastily executed symbol of some ulterior experience. A poem might be sincere no matter how hastily it had been scribbled. Jarred by the barbaric sound, yet fascinated, Sirius drifted step by step into the porch and across the inner threshold. He had entered during a prayer. The minister’s eyes were reverently closed. His tone of voice was unctuous and complacently servile. With the conventional intonation of penitence and worship, but without any inner experience of them, he affirmed the sinfulness of the whole human race, and confidently, flatteringly, asked his God for forgiveness and eternal bliss for himself and his flock. The backs of the bowed congregation appeared above the pews like the backs of sheep in a pen. But their smell on that hot day was all too human.</p>
<p>When the prayer was ended the minister opened his eyes. He saw the great dog standing in the aisle. Pointing dramatically at Sirius, he exclaimed, “Who has brought that animal into God’s House? Put it out!” Several black coats and striped trousers moved towards Sirius. They expected him to retire before them, but he stood his ground, his head and tail erect, his back bristling. A faint growl, rather like distant thunder, made the assailants hesitate. Sirius looked round the building. All eyes were turned on him, some outraged, some amused. He turned slowly to retrace his steps. The ejectors cautiously advanced. One of them said, “Good dog! Go home!” but another began to chivvy him with an umbrella, and rashly tapped his haunch. Sirius leapt round with a bark that echoed through the bare chapel, and his pursuers retreated a pace. He stood looking at them for a few moments, amused at his easy triumph. The hair on his spine subsided. He vaguely waved his tail, and turned towards the door. Then a mischievous idea took possession of him. At the door he once more faced the congregation, and in a clear, accurate, though wordless, voice he sang the refrain of the hymn that had been sung before he entered. As he turned to leave the building a woman screamed. The minister in a rather strained voice said, “Friends, I think we had better join once more in prayer.”</p>
<p>On another occasion he marched beside the drums of the Salvation Army, sometimes forgetting himself so far as to add his voice to the trumpets. The open air service gave him, he told Thomas, an irrational sense of salvation. What appealed to him most was one of the hymns, sung with immense gusto. “Washed in the blood of the Lamb,” was its theme. He could not resist joining in the singing, though softly. He could not see how the imagery of the hymn agreed with the religion of love, but somehow it had a strange power over him. He vaguely and quite irrationally felt it as unifying all the tenderness of his life with all the wolf in him. He scented again the seductive reek of his killed ram and his killed pony. Somehow the haunting conflict between pity and blood-lust seemed to be resolved. His guilt was washed away. There was no sound reason for this; he just felt it. He and these human animals somehow unloaded their sins upon the Lamb, and found a crude ecstasy of community one with another, and all together. They abased themselves into the personified spirit of the group. The intoxicated minds gave up all attempt to think clearly and feel precisely, and yielded to the common mentality; which somehow seemed to be universal, cosmical, the personified “togetherness” of all individual spirits in all the worlds. Thus he felt, as the barbaric tune soaked through his brain. Yet to another part of his mind the blasting of the trumpets, the thundering of the drums and the lusty human singing seemed as remote as the howling of an alien species in the jungle. Not in this way, said the protesting part of his mind, not in the remission of clear thought and feeling for the sake of the mere warmth of togetherness, could one find the essential spirit, identical in himself and in these humans. Only in the most articulate, precise self- and other-consciousness was the thing to be found; for instance on those rare occasions of spiritual accord with Plaxy, when through their very difference and distinctness they discovered their underlying identity. Yes, and in another manner he had sometimes found that thing, with Thomas, when their two intellects had moved together up the steep path of some argument, Thomas always leading, till they had reached together some pinnacle from which, it seemed, they could view the whole universe.</p>
