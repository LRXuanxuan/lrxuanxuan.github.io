<h2>序言</h2>
<p>我从前一本书的一位评论者曾说，他很难理解为什么会有人写出那样一本书。从他的立场来看，这句话倒也不无道理，因为那本书的目的恰好落在他意识的探照灯之外。尽管如此，一想到绝大多数书本来就不该被写出来，任何作者都应当稍稍停笔自省。如今纸张短缺，战时工作又迫在眉睫，“一本书究竟值不值得写”这个问题——更不用说值不值得出版——比任何时候都更切要。至于本书是否具有足以证明其问世合理性的意义，只能留待读者和评论者判断；不过，倘若我先作几句说明，想来他们也不会见怪。</p>
<p>当然，本书无意被当作预言。这里为人类设想的两种未来，没有哪一种哪怕稍微有可能照原样发生。历史预测注定总要失败。最老练的社会学家，更不用说小说家，在预言未来这件事上也未必比“老摩尔”更可靠。至于我本人，连法西斯主义的兴起都全然未能预见，自然更没有资格声称自己能够描绘欧洲下一阶段的变迁。</p>
<p>但本书关心的并不是预言。它只是试图以象征性的方式，表现当今世界彼此冲突的两种倾向。找不到更好的词，我把它们称作“黑暗意志”与“光明意志”。我以具体形式呈现摆在人类面前的两类可能性，但与其说是精确的照片，不如说是夸张的漫画。这样一本书是否值得写，取决于三个问题的答案：这样的冲突是否真的存在？它是否重要？而我为它画出的这幅漫画，是否足够准确，足以澄清头脑并触动心灵？</p>
<p>奥拉夫·斯特普尔顿 1941年10月</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" class="stapledon-body" lang="en" hidden>
<h2>PREFACE</h2>
<p>AREVIEWER OF an earlier book of mine said that it was difficult to see why such a book should ever have been written. From his point of view the remark was reasonable enough, for the aim of the book happened to fall outside the spot-light of his consciousness. All the same, the fact that the great majority of books ought never to have been written must give the writer pause. To-day, what with the paper shortage and the urgency of war work, the question whether a book is worth writing, let alone publishing, is more pertinent than ever. Whether this book has enough significance to justify its appearance must be left to the judgment of readers and reviewers; but perhaps they will not take it amiss if I offer a word of explanation.</p>
<p>This book is, of course, not meant to be regarded as prophecy. Neither of the two futures which I here imagine for mankind is in the least likely to happen. Historical prediction is doomed always to fail. The most sophisticated sociologist, let alone a writer of fiction, is scarcely a more trustworthy prophet than Old Moore. Certainly I, who entirely failed to foresee the advent of Fascism, cannot lay claim to describe the next phase of European change.</p>
<p>But this book is not concerned to prophesy. It seeks merely to give a symbolic expression to two dispositions now in conflict in the world. For lack of better words I call them the will for darkness and the will for the light. I present in concrete form, but rather as caricature than with photographic accuracy, two kinds of possibility that lie before the human race. The justification for writing such a book depends on the answers to three questions. Is there such a conflict? Is it important? Is the caricature that I have drawn of it well enough drawn to clear the mind and stir the heart?</p>
<p>OLAF STAPLEDON October 1941</p>
