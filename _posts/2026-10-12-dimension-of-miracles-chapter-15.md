<h2>第十五章</h2>
<p>“喂，等一下，这到底是怎么回事？”卡莫迪跟着那两个穿宇航服的人穿过森林时问道。“你们是谁？为什么会在这里？”</p>
<p>“天哪！”女孩脸一红，显得很不好意思。“我们刚才一直忙着东奔西跑，居然连自我介绍都忘了！卡莫迪先生，您一定把我们当成一群大傻瓜了！”</p>
<p>“完全没有。”卡莫迪很有礼貌地说。“不过我确实很想知道——呃，想知道一些事情，你明白我的意思吧。”</p>
<p>“当然明白。”女孩说。“我叫阿维瓦·克里斯蒂安森，这是我父亲，拉尔斯·克里斯蒂安森教授。”</p>
<p>“‘教授’那套就免了。”克里斯蒂安森粗声粗气地说。“叫我拉尔斯、克里斯，或者你脑子里随便蹦出来什么都行。”</p>
<p>“好的，爸爸。”阿维瓦故意装出一种亲昵的娇嗔。“总之，卡莫迪先生——”</p>
<p>“叫我汤姆就行。”</p>
<p>“那就汤姆。”阿维瓦说，脸颊漂亮地泛起红晕。“我刚才说到哪儿了？哦，对。爸爸和我都隶属于‘地球星际救援协会’——Terran Interstellar Rescue Association，简称TIRA，总部设在斯德哥尔摩、日内瓦和华盛顿特区。”</p>
<p>“恐怕我从来没听说过你们这个组织。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“这不奇怪。”阿维瓦说。“地球才刚刚跨到星际探索的门槛上。即使就在现在，遍布地球的实验室里，也正在试验各种全新的能源，它们的威力远远超过你所熟悉的那些粗糙原子装置。很快，由地球人驾驶的宇宙飞船就会探索银河最遥远的角落。而这当然会给我们那颗疲惫的老行星开启一个国际和平与合作的新时代。”</p>
<p>“会吗？”卡莫迪问。“为什么？”</p>
<p>“因为到时候就再也没有什么真正值得争夺的东西了。”阿维瓦说。三个人正在矮灌木丛里一路小跑，因此她说得有些上气不接下气。“外面有数不清的世界，这一点你大概已经注意到了。各种社会实验、冒险，以及你能想象的一切东西，都有足够空间去实现。因此，人类的精力会向外释放，而不是向内耗散，变成灾难性的自相残杀战争。”</p>
<p>“这丫头告诉你的全是真货。”拉尔斯·克里斯蒂安森用他那低沉、粗哑、友善而绝不废话的嗓音说。“她听起来也许像个没头脑的小姑娘，可她后面有大概七十七打博士学位和博士头衔给她这些话撑腰。”</p>
<p>“我爸听起来也许像个粗人，”阿维瓦立刻回敬，“可他的行李箱里塞着三枚诺贝尔奖！”</p>
<p>父女俩交换了一个眼神，那神情莫名其妙地既像威胁，又像亲昵。</p>
<p>“总之，”阿维瓦继续说，“事情现在就是这样——或者应该说，再过几年就会变成这样。不过多亏马多克斯博士，我们提前起跑了。你很快就会见到他。”阿维瓦犹豫了一下，压低声音说道，“我想告诉你这一点应该不算泄密：马多克斯博士是一个——一个——突变人。”</p>
<p>“呸，这个词有什么好紧张的。”拉尔斯·克里斯蒂安森咆哮道。“突变人完全可以和我们一样好。而马多克斯博士这种情况，甚至能比我们好上一千倍！”</p>
<p>“真正把这个计划送上轨道的人就是马多克斯博士。”阿维瓦接着说。“你看，他对未来进行了一次投影预测——至于他怎么做到的，我完全不知道！——于是发现，随着廉价、无限、而且安全便携的能源即将被发现，很快到处都会有宇宙飞船！然后会有很多人连合适的装备、导航仪器之类都没带齐，就一股脑冲进太空——”</p>
<p>“一大群半吊子蠢货。”克里斯蒂安森干巴巴地评论。</p>
<p>“爸爸！总之，这些人到时候会需要帮助。但在87.238874年之内，都不会出现一个有组织的‘银河救援巡逻队’——这个数字是博士极其仔细算出来的。你明白了吗？”</p>
<p>“我想明白了。”卡莫迪说。“你们三个发现了这个问题，然后——然后你们自己站出来填补空缺。”</p>
<p>“是的。”她简简单单地说。“我们站出来了。爸爸非常热衷于为别人服务，尽管光听他那副暴躁说话的样子，你永远也猜不出来。对我爸爸来说够好的事情，对我来说也够好。至于马多克斯博士——这个嘛，他简直是我认识的所有人里，把人类潜能实现到最极限、最顶格的那个。”</p>
<p>“对，他就是这么回事，而且还得再加倍、再翻倍。”拉尔斯·克里斯蒂安森低声说。“这个人的家族史相当不简单。你知道，突变通常都具有负面价值。一千个里面最多只有一两个挖出来的是黄金，而不是黄铁矿。但马多克斯博士家族的情况不同：他们有一整段大规模突变的家族史，其中大多数突变都是有利的，而且全部无法解释。”</p>
<p>“我们怀疑，是某种善意的外星干预。”阿维瓦几乎用耳语说道。“马多克斯家族的历史只能追溯到两百年前。故事很奇怪。马多克斯博士的曾祖父埃利尔·马多克斯，是个威尔士煤矿工人。他在臭名昭著的老格林吉煤矿干了将近二十年，却是少数一直保持健康的工人之一。那是1739年。最近老格林吉矿重新开采时，人们在旁边发现了惊人的‘散哀’铀矿床。”</p>
<p>“一切大概就是从那里开始的。”克里斯蒂安森说。“接下来我们在1801年的墨西哥瓦哈卡再次找到这个家族的踪迹。托马斯·马多克斯——他当时就是这么称呼自己的——娶了美丽而专横的特蕾西塔·德·瓦尔德斯，阿拉贡伯爵夫人，墨西哥南部最好庄园的主人。1801年4月6日早晨，托马斯正在外面骑马放牧，‘死亡红星’——La Estrella Roja de Muerto，后来证实是一颗体积很大、放射性极强的陨石——落在离牧场不到两英里的地方。托马斯和特蕾西塔是极少数幸存者之一。”</p>
<p>“然后就到了20世纪30年代。”阿维瓦接过故事。“下一代马多克斯已经没有以前那么富有，搬到了洛杉矶。博士的祖父欧内斯特·马多克斯当时正在向医生和牙医推销一种刚出现的新奇装置，叫作‘X射线机’。至少十年里，马多克斯每周都要演示两次这种机器，而且总拿自己当被照射对象。尽管长期遭受巨量硬辐射——也许恰恰因为如此——他还是活到了相当体面的年纪。”</p>
<p>“他的儿子，”拉尔斯说，“不知道受到什么冲动驱使，于1935年前往日本，做了一名禅宗僧人。整个战争期间，他一直住在一个tsuktsuri里——也就是废弃地下室的一个角落——一次也没开口说过话。当地人以为他是个性情古怪的巴基斯坦人，于是一直没人打扰他。马多克斯住的地下室就在广岛，距离1945年原子弹爆炸中心只有7.9英里。爆炸以后，他立刻离开日本，前往惠申寺。那座寺院位于西藏北部最难接近的一座山峰上。据一个当时恰巧在那里旅行的英国游客所说，那些喇嘛居然早就在等他！他在那里定居下来，专门研究某些密续经典。他娶了一名拥有克什米尔王室血统的女子，两人生了一个儿子：也就是我们的欧文博士。中国红色军队发动入侵前一个星期，全家离开西藏，前往美国。欧文先后在哈佛、耶鲁、加州大学洛杉矶分校、牛津、剑桥、索邦和海德堡受教育。他究竟是怎么找到我们的，本身也是个相当离奇的故事，等到更合适的时候你会听到。眼下我们已经到飞船了，我觉得不能再浪费时间闲扯。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪看见那艘飞船停在一小片林间空地上，庄严宏伟，像一座摩天大楼直冲天空。船身布满翼片、喷口、舱门，以及许多其他凸起。飞船前面，一个年过中年不久的男人坐在折叠椅上，脸庞慈祥，布满深深皱纹。一眼就能看出来，这个人正是“突变人马多克斯”：他每只手都有七根手指，额头也巨大地向外隆起，为后面那颗额外增大的脑子腾出空间。</p>
<p>马多克斯不紧不慢地站起来——用他的五条腿——然后点头欢迎他们。“你们真的是千钧一发才赶到。”他说。“敌对力线几乎已经抵达交汇点。大家快进飞船！我们必须立刻架起力场护盾。”</p>
<p>拉尔斯·克里斯蒂安森大步向前走，自尊心使他不肯跑。阿维瓦挽住卡莫迪的胳膊；卡莫迪感觉到她在发抖，同时也发现，那身毫无形状的灰色宇航服并不能遮住这个美丽姑娘柔韧优美的身体线条，尽管她自己似乎毫无察觉。</p>
<p>“局面很糟。”马多克斯一边嘟囔，一边收起帆布折叠椅，把它塞进飞船。“我的计算当然包含这种节点出现的可能性，可无穷组合的本质决定了，我们不可能事先预测节点的具体构型。总之，我们只能尽力。”</p>
<p>走到宽阔的入口舱门前，卡莫迪犹豫了。“我觉得我应该先跟莫兹利先生告个别。”他对马多克斯说。“也许我甚至应该问问他的意见。他一直很帮忙，而且正在想办法把我送回地球。”</p>
<p>“莫兹利！”马多克斯叫道，同时和克里斯蒂安森意味深长地交换了一个眼神。“我就怀疑背后是他！”</p>
<p>“看起来就是他那套该死的手艺。”克里斯蒂安森咬牙说道。</p>
<p>“什么意思？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“我的意思是，”马多克斯说，“你已经成了一场巨大阴谋中的受害者和棋子。这场阴谋牵涉不少于十七个恒星系统。现在我没法把全部情况解释给你听；但你一定要相信我，危险的不只是你的命和我们的命，还有几十亿类人生物的生命——其中大多数都有蓝眼睛和浅色皮肤！”</p>
<p>“哦，汤姆，快，快点！”阿维瓦拉着他的胳膊叫道。</p>
<p>“好吧，好吧。”卡莫迪说。“不过我之后要一个完整而令人满意的解释。”</p>
<p>“你会得到的。”卡莫迪跨进舱门时，马多克斯说。“现在就给你。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪猛然转身。他从马多克斯的嗓音里听出了一丝威胁。他死死盯着这个突变人，突然感到一阵震惊。随后，他又看向三个救援者——这一次，才真正第一次看清他们。</p>
<p>人类心智非常擅长构造完形。几条曲线就足以让人看见一座山，六七条断裂的线段也能画出一朵还算像样的浪花。如今，在卡莫迪逐项细看的凝视下，那个完形开始崩溃。他发现，阿维瓦漂亮的眼睛只是风格化、暗示性的图案，并不具备视觉功能——就像飞蛾翅膀上画出来的眼斑。拉尔斯脸部下三分之一处有一个暗红色椭圆，中间被一道更深的线分开；那东西本来是在冒充嘴巴。至于马多克斯的手指——七根全都在——其实只是画在他大腿高度的身体表面。</p>
<p>整个完形彻底瓦解了。</p>
<p>卡莫迪看见一条细细的黑线，像地板上的裂缝一样，把他们每一个都连接到飞船上。他僵在原地，看着三个东西朝自己移动。它们没有可以举起的手，没有可以行走的脚，没有用于观看的眼睛，也没有能够解释的嘴。事实上，它们只是顶部圆钝、表面毫无特征的圆柱体，被技巧高超却又仅仅停留在表面的伪装装扮成人类。它们没有用于执行功能的部件；它们本身就是部件，而现在正在执行自己唯一的功能。它们恰好就像一只巨人手上的三根可怕手指。三个圆柱体柔软、无骨地向前逼近；很明显，它们想把卡莫迪赶进飞船更深处那张黑色巨口。</p>
<p>飞船？</p>
<p>卡莫迪猛地绕过三个东西，朝自己进来的方向冲去。可舱门上下忽然伸出尖锐牙齿，先张得更大了一点，随后开始闭合。他怎么会以为这东西是金属？飞船黝黑发亮的两侧此刻像皮肉一样起伏，并开始收缩。他的双脚陷进海绵般柔软、黏糊糊的甲板，而那三根“手指”正绕到他周围，切断他和那块越来越小的方形日光之间的道路。</p>
<p>卡莫迪像一只落进蜘蛛网的苍蝇一样绝望挣扎。（这个比喻完全准确，只可惜领悟来得太晚。）他疯狂搏斗，却毫无作用。那块方形日光已经变得圆润而湿漉漉，并缩小到棒球那么大。三个圆柱体此刻把他牢牢夹住，卡莫迪已经分不清哪个是哪个。</p>
<p>这就是最后的恐怖；除此之外，飞船——或者随便这是什么东西——的墙壁和天花板已经变成湿润而青紫的暗红色，正在向内收拢，把他整个吞没。</p>
<p>无路可逃。卡莫迪彻底无能为力，既不能动，也喊不出声，什么都做不了——除了失去意识。</p>
</section>

<p><b>CHAPTER 15</b></p>
<p>‘Hey, wait a minute, what is all this?’ Carmody asked as he followed the space-suited people through the forest. ‘Who are you people? What are you doing here?’</p>
<p>‘Criminy!’ the girl said, flushing with embarrassment. ‘We’ve been rushing around so, we haven’t even introduced ourselves! A fine lot of fools you must take us for, Mr Carmody!’</p>
<p>‘Not at all,’ Carmody said courteously. ‘But I would like to know – well, to <i>know,</i> if you know what I mean.’</p>
<p>‘Of course, I know what you mean,’ the girl said. ‘I am Aviva Christiansen, and this is my father, Professor Lars Christiansen.’</p>
<p>‘You can knock off that “Professor” stuff,’ Christiansen said gruffly. ‘Just call me Lars, or Chris, or anything else that comes to mind.’</p>
<p>‘All right, Dad,’ Aviva said, with a mock show of fond petulance. ‘Anyhow, Mr Carmody –’</p>
<p>‘The name’s Tom.’</p>
<p>‘Tom, then,’ Aviva said, colouring prettily. ‘Where was I? Oh yes, Dad and I are connected with the Terran Interstellar Rescue Association (TIRA), which has its offices in Stockholm, Geneva, and Washington, DC.’</p>
<p>‘I’m afraid I’ve never heard of your organization,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘There’s nothing surprising about that,’ Aviva said. ‘Earth has just entered upon the threshold of interstellar exploration. Even now, in laboratories all over the earth, new sources of power far exceeding the crude atomic devices to which you have been accustomed are now in the experimental stage. And very soon indeed, spacecraft piloted by men of Earth will probe to the farthest corners of the galaxy. And this will, of course, usher in a new period of international peace and co-operation upon our tired old planet.’</p>
<p>‘It will?’ Carmody asked. ‘Why?’</p>
<p>‘Because there will no longer be anything significant to fight about,’ Aviva said, somewhat breathlessly since all three were trotting through low underbrush. ‘There are countless worlds out there, as you may have noticed,’ she continued, ‘and there is room for all kinds of social experiments, and adventures, and everything you could possibly imagine. So man’s energies will be directed outward instead of dissipated inward in the form of disastrous internecine warfare.’</p>
<p>‘The kid’s giving you the straight dope,’ said Lars Christiansen, in his deep, gruff, friendly, no-nonsense voice. ‘She may sound like a scatterbrain, but she’s got about eleventy-seven Ph.Ds and Doctorates to back up her line of gab.’</p>
<p>‘And my pop may sound like a roughneck,’ Aviva flashed back, ‘but he’s got three Nobel prizes in his footlocker!’</p>
<p>Father and daughter exchanged looks which were somehow threatening and affectionate at the same time. ‘So anyhow,’ Aviva said, ‘that’s how it is, or I should say, how it’s <i>going to be</i> in a couple of years. But we got a head start on it all due to Dr Maddox, whom you will meet shortly.’ Aviva hesitated a moment, then said in a lower voice, ‘I don’t think I’ll be betraying a confidence if I tell you that Dr Maddox is a – a – mutant.’</p>
<p>‘Rats, there’s no need to be nervous about the word,’ Lars Christiansen growled. ‘A mutant can be every bit as good as we are. And in the case of Dr Maddox, he can be about a thousand times better!’</p>
<p>‘It was Dr Maddox who really put this project into orbit,’ Aviva went on. ‘You see, he made a projection of the future (how he does it I don’t know!) and he realized that soon, with the imminent discovery of cheap, unlimited power in a safe, portable form, there were going to be spaceships all over the place! And a lot of people would just rush into space without proper equipment or navigational instruments or stuff –’</p>
<p>‘A lot of half-baked fools,’ Christiansen commented drily.</p>
<p>‘Dad! Anyhow, these people were going to need help. But there would be no organized Galactic Rescue Patrol (he computed this figure very carefully) for 87·238874 years. Do you see?’</p>
<p>‘I think so,’ Carmody said. ‘You three saw the problem and – and you stepped in.’</p>
<p>‘Yes,’ she said simply. ‘We stepped in. Dad’s very keen on serving others, though you could never tell it from the grouchy way he talks. And what’s good enough for my dad is good enough for me. And as for Dr Maddox – well, he’s just the utter maximum top realized-potential of any human being of my acquaintance.’</p>
<p>‘Yeah, he’s that all right, in spades, doubled,’ Lars Christiansen said quietly. ‘The man has had quite a history. Mutations are usually of negative value, you know. Just one or two out of a thousand pan out gold instead of pyrites. But in Dr Maddox’s case, there is a family history of massive mutation, most of it favourable, all of it inexplicable.’</p>
<p>‘We suspect benevolent alien intervention,’ Aviva said, almost in a whisper. ‘The Maddox family can only be traced back for two hundred years. It’s a strange story. Aelill Madoxxe, Maddox’s great-grandfather, was a Welsh coal miner. He worked for nearly twenty years in the notorious Auld Gringie Mine, and was one of the few labourers to retain his health. That was in 1739. Recently, when Auld Gringie was reopened, the fabulous Scatter-wail uranium deposits were found adjacent to it.’</p>
<p>‘It must have begun there,’ Christiansen said. ‘We pick up the family next in 1801, in Oaxaca, Mexico. Thomas Madoxxe (as he styled himself) had married the beautiful and imperious Teresita de Valdez, Contessa de Aragon, owner of the finest hacienda in southern Mexico. Thomas was out riding herd on the morning of April 6th, 1801, when La Estrella Roja de Muerto – Red Star of Death (subsequently identified as a large, highly radioactive meteorite) – fell within two miles of the ranch. Thomas and Teresita were among the few survivors.’</p>
<p>‘Next we come to the 1930s,’ Aviva said, picking up the tale. ‘The next Maddox generation, much reduced in wealth, moved to Los Angeles. Ernest Maddox, the doctor’s grandfather, was selling a new-fangled contraption, to doctors and dentists. It was called an “X-ray machine.” Maddox demonstrated the machine twice weekly for at least ten years. He used himself as subject. Despite the massive overdose of hard radiation, <i>or perhaps because of it,</i> he lived to a very respectable age.’</p>
<p>‘His son,’ Lars said, ‘moved by we know not what compulsion, travelled to Japan in 1935 and became a Zen monk. He lived in a <i>tsuktsuri,</i> or corner of an abandoned basement, throughout the war years, never once uttering a word. The local people left him alone, thinking he was an eccentric Pakistani. Maddox’s basement was in Hiroshima, just 7·9 miles from the epicentre of the atomic explosion of 1945. <i>Immediately after the explosion,</i> Maddox left Japan and went to the Hui-Shen monastery, situated on the most inaccessible peak in northern Tibet. According to the story, of an English tourist who was there at the time, <i>the lamas had been expecting him!</i> He settled there, devoting himself to the study of certain Tantras. He married a woman of royal Kashmiri blood, by whom he had one son: that was Owen, our doctor. The family left Tibet for the United States one week before the Red Chinese launched their invasion. Owen was educated at Harvard, Yale, UCLA, Oxford, Cambridge, the Sorbonne, and Heidelberg. How he found us is quite a strange story in itself, which you shall hear upon a more propitious occasion. For now we have reached the ship, and I think we dare not waste any more time in palaver.’</p>
<p>Carmody saw it in a little clearing, a majestic spaceship which rose upwards like a skyscraper. It possessed vanes, jets, hatches, and many other protuberances. In front of it, seated in a folding chair, was a man somewhat above middle age, with a benevolent, deeply creased face. It was immediately apparent that this man was Maddox the Mutant, for he had seven fingers on each hand, and his forehead bulged hugely to make room for the extra brain behind it.</p>
<p>Maddox rose in a leisurely manner (on five legs!) and nodded in welcome. ‘You have come only just in time,’ he said. ‘Lines of inimical force have very nearly reached the intersection point. Come into the ship quickly, all of you! We must erect the force-shield without delay.’</p>
<p>Lars Christiansen marched forward, too proud to run. Aviva took Carmody’s arm, and Carmody perceived that she was trembling, and that the shapeless grey cloth of her suit could not conceal the lissome lines of the beautiful girl, though she seemed unaware of it.</p>
<p>‘It’s a nasty situation,’ Maddox muttered, folding his canvas chair and putting it within the ship. ‘My calculations allow for this sort of nodal point, of course, but by the very nature of interminable combination one cannot predict their configuration. Still, we do our best.’</p>
<p>At the wide entrance hatch, Carmody hesitated. ‘I really think I should say goodbye to Mr Maudsley,’ he told Maddox. ‘Perhaps I should even ask his advice. He’s been very helpful, and he’s working on a way to get me back to Earth.’</p>
<p>‘Maudsley!’ Maddox cried, exchanging significant glances with Christiansen. ‘I suspected he was behind this!’</p>
<p>‘It looked like his damned handiwork,’ Christiansen grated.</p>
<p>‘What do you mean?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘I mean,’ Maddox said, ‘that you have been victim and pawn in a vast conspiracy involving no fewer than seventeen star-systems. I cannot explain it all now; but believe me, not only your life and ours is at stake, but also the life of several dozen billion humanoids, most of them blue-eyed and fair-skinned!’</p>
<p>‘Oh Tom, hurry, hurry!’ Aviva cried, pulling at his arm.</p>
<p>‘Well, all right,’ Carmody said. ‘But I shall want a complete and satisfactory explanation.’</p>
<p>‘And you shall have it,’ Maddox said as Carmody stepped through the hatch. ‘You shall have it right now.’</p>
<p>Carmody turned quickly, detecting the note of menace in Maddox’s voice. He looked at the mutant intently and experienced a wave of shock. He looked again, at his three rescuers, and really saw them for the first time.</p>
<p>The human mind is adept at constructing gestalts. A few curves suffice for a mountain, and half a dozen broken lines can produce a passable wave. The gestalt was breaking down now under Carmody’s particularizing gaze. He saw that Aviva’s lovely eyes were stylized and suggestive rather than functional – like the design of eyes on the wings of a moth. Lars had a dark red oval in the lower third of his face, divided by a darker line; this was supposed to be a mouth. Maddox’s fingers, all seven of them, were painted on his body at thigh level.</p>
<p>The gestalt broke down completely, Carmody saw the thin black line, like a crack in the floor, that connected each of them to the ship. He stood, frozen, and watched them move towards him. They had no hands to raise, no feet to move, no eyes to see with, no mouth to explain with. They were in fact round-topped and featureless cylinders, artfully but superficially disguised as human. They had no parts with which to function; they were themselves parts, and they were now performing their sole function. They were the exact and terrible counterpart of three fingers on a giant’s hand. They advanced with supple bonelessness; they evidently wanted to drive him deeper into the black maw of the ship.</p>
<p>The ship? Carmody darted around the three and raced back the way he had come in. But the hatch extruded pointed teeth from top and bottom, opened a little wider, and then began to close. How could he have thought it was metal? The dark shiny sides of the ship rippled now and began to contract. His feet were caught in the spongy, sticky deck, and the three fingers were moving around him, blocking him off from the diminishing square of daylight.</p>
<p>Carmody struggled with the desperation of a fly caught in a spider’s web. (the simile was exact, but the insight had come too late). He fought with a frenzy and with no effect. The square of daylight had become round and wet, and shrunk to the size of a baseball. The three cylinders were holding him now, and Carmody could not tell one from the other.</p>
<p>That was the final horror; that, and the fact that the walls and ceiling of the spaceship (or whatever it was) had turned a moist and livid red, and were closing in and engulfing him.</p>
<p>There was no escape. Carmody was helpless, unable to move or shout, unable to do anything but lose consciousness.</p>
