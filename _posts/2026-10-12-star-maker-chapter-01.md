<h3>1　起点</h3>
<p>有一天夜里，我尝尽苦涩之后，独自走上山坡。黑暗中的石南绊住我的脚。下方，郊区街灯排成行列，像在行进。拉上窗帘的窗户宛如闭起的眼睛，向内凝视着梦中的生活。越过海面平坦的黑暗，一座灯塔有节律地闪烁。头顶，一片晦暗。</p>
<p>我辨认出自己的家——世界汹涌而苦涩的潮流中，我们的一座小岛。十五年来，我们两人秉性迥异，却在错综复杂的共生关系中，一点点彼此长入，为对方提供支撑与养分。我们每天在那里筹划各自的事务，讲述一天中发生的古怪小事与烦恼。那里总堆着等回的信，等补的袜子。孩子们也在那里出生——那些突然闯入的新生命。在那屋顶之下，我们两个人的生命虽然有时彼此抗拒，却始终令人感激地合为一体，成为一种比任何一方单独存在都更广大、更有意识的生命。</p>
<p>这一切当然是好的。然而，苦涩依然存在。而且苦涩不仅从外部世界侵入我们，也从我们自己的魔法圆环内部涌出。正因为恐惧我们的徒劳、恐惧我们自身的不真实——并且也恐惧这个世界的癫狂——我才被逼上了山。</p>
<p>我们总是在一个小小的紧急任务与另一个之间奔忙，可最终的结果却空洞得近乎没有实质。也许，我们从根本上就误解了自己的整个存在？我们是否仿佛是从错误的前提出发生活？尤其是我们这段伴侣关系——这个看起来如此稳固、仿佛足以成为我们参与世界活动的支点——到头来会不会只是一股自满而内向的家庭生活小漩涡，在巨大洪流的表面徒劳旋转，本身既没有存在的深度，也没有意义？难道我们终究只是欺骗了自己？在那些凝神般的窗户背后，我们是否也像无数人一样，其实只活在一场梦里？在一个病态的世界里，就连健康的人也是病人。而我们两人，大部分时候只是按习惯纺织着自己的小小生活，很少真正清醒地认识，很少以坚定的意志行动——我们本就是一个病态世界的产物。</p>
<p>然而，我们的生活也并非全是空洞贫瘠的幻想。它难道不是用现实真正的纤维纺成的吗？那些纤维来自我们门前的一切来往，来自我们与郊区、与城市、与更遥远城市乃至世界尽头的一切联系。我们难道不是也在共同纺织一种对自身本性的真实表达吗？我们的生活难道不是每天都吐出或坚韧或脆弱的行动之线，把自己织进那张不断成长的网，织进人类那错综复杂、永不停息地繁衍扩展的图案中吗？</p>
<p>我怀着安静的兴趣，又带一点觉得好笑的敬畏，端详起“我们”来。我该怎样向自己描述我们的关系，才能既不贬低它，又不拿廉价的感伤装饰去羞辱它？因为我们这种依赖与独立之间微妙的平衡，这种冷静挑剔、机敏嘲弄却又彼此相爱的接触，确实像是真正共同体的一个微缩模型；以它那朴素的方式说，它终究是世界所追求的那种崇高目标一个真实而鲜活的实例。</p>
<p>整个世界？整个宇宙？头顶的晦暗揭开了一颗星。一支颤抖的光箭——多少千年前射出的？——此刻刺入我的神经，化作视觉，又刺入我的心，化作恐惧。因为在这样的宇宙里，我们偶然、脆弱而转瞬即逝的共同体，又能有什么意义？</p>
<p>可就在这时，我毫无道理地被一种奇异的崇拜攫住了。所崇拜的当然不是那颗星——那不过是一座熔炉，只因距离遥远才被虚假地神圣化——而是另一种东西，是那颗星与我们之间骇人的反差在心灵中所象征的某种东西。可是，那究竟象征什么？理智越过星辰向外窥望，并没有发现什么造星主，只有黑暗；没有爱，甚至没有力量，只有虚无。然而，心灵仍在赞颂。</p>
<p>我不耐烦地甩掉这份愚蠢，从不可参透之物退回熟悉而具体的事物。我把崇拜、恐惧和苦涩一并推开，决心更冷静地考察这个非同寻常的“我们”——这个出人意料地重要的事实。对我们自己而言，它仍然是宇宙的根基；可同群星相比，它却显得如此微不足道。</p>
<p>即使完全不去考虑那令我们显得渺小的宇宙背景，我们终究也无足轻重，甚至有些可笑。我们太常见，太老套，也太体面了。我们只是一对已婚夫妇，设法在不过度互相折磨的前提下一起生活。在我们这个时代，婚姻本就遭人怀疑。而我们的婚姻又起源于琐碎的浪漫，因此更显可疑。</p>
<p>我们初次相遇时，她还是个孩子。我们的目光相遇了。她安静而专注地看了我片刻；我甚至曾浪漫地想象，那目光中有一种隐晦、深藏的认出。至少我自己从那目光里认出了命运——青春期发烧般的激情曾这样说服我。是啊！我们的结合当时看起来多么像命中注定！可如今回头看，又是多么偶然！当然，作为一对结婚多年的夫妻，我们确实相当合拍，像两棵靠得很近的树，树干一起向上生长，渐渐长成一根共同的柱干；它们彼此扭曲，也彼此支撑。此刻我冷静地估量她，只把她视作我个人生活中一个很有用、却也经常令人恼火的附属。总体上，我们算是理智的伴侣。我们给彼此留出一定自由，因此才得以忍受长久的亲近。</p>
<p>这就是我们的关系。若只这样陈述，它对于理解宇宙似乎并没有什么重要意义。然而在内心深处，我知道它确实重要。即便冰冷的群星，即便整个宇宙那一切空洞无意义的广大，也无法让我相信：我们珍视的这粒共同体原子，虽然不完美，虽然注定短命，却没有意义。</p>
<p>可我们这种不可言说的结合，真的可能具有超出自身之外的意义吗？譬如，它是否证明所有人的根本天性都是爱，而不是憎恨与恐惧？它是否证明，世界各地所有男人与女人尽管常被环境阻碍，本质上都能够支撑一个以爱联结的世界共同体？更进一步，它既然也是宇宙的产物，是否就证明爱在某种意义上也是宇宙本身的根基？它那种由自身内在卓越而被直接感受到的价值，是否能够保证我们两个脆弱的承载者在某种意义上拥有永生？归根到底，它是否证明爱就是上帝，而上帝正在天堂等待我们？</p>
<p>不！我们这朴素、友善、恼人、令人发笑、毫无装饰却又最受珍视的精神共同体，并不能证明这些事中的任何一件。它不能确切保证任何东西，除了保证它自身那种虽不完美却真实存在的恰当。它不过是存在诸多潜能之一的一个极微小、极明亮的缩影。我想起那些无知无觉的群星。我想起充斥人类世界的憎恨、恐惧与苦涩的喧腾。我也想起我们自己并不罕见的不和。于是我提醒自己：我们很快也会像微风掠过静水时激起的一阵细纹一样消失。</p>
<p>群星与我们之间那种奇异的对照再次向我显现。宇宙不可估量的威力，以一种神秘方式增强了我们这短暂共同体火花的正当，也增强了人类那短暂而不确定的尝试的正当；而反过来，这些东西又使宇宙本身鲜活起来。</p>
<p>我在石南丛中坐下。头顶的晦暗此刻已经全面退却。在它退去的后方，天空中获释的居民一个接一个从藏身处跃出——一颗星，又一颗星。</p>
<p>四面八方，朦胧的山丘，或只能凭猜测知道存在、没有任何特征的海面，一直延伸到视野之外。然而想象力如鹰一般飞翔，追随它们弯曲下沉，越过地平线。我意识到，自己正待在一粒圆圆的岩石与金属上，外面覆着一层薄薄的水与空气，在阳光与黑暗中旋转。而就在这粒小小尘粒的表皮上，一代又一代、成群结队的人类在劳作与蒙昧中生活，间歇地享受欢乐，也间歇地获得精神上的清醒。他们全部的历史——民族迁徙、帝国兴亡、哲学、引以为傲的科学、社会革命，以及对共同体日益增长的渴望——都不过是恒星生命中某一天的一次闪烁。</p>
<p>如果能够知道，在那闪耀的群星之间，是否也零零散散存在着别的、由精神生命栖居的岩石与金属尘粒；如果能够知道，人类笨拙地追寻智慧与爱的历程，究竟只是一次孤独而无足轻重的震颤，还是某种普遍运动的一部分！</p>
<h3>2　群星之间的地球</h3>
<p>头顶的晦暗已经消失。从一个地平线到另一个地平线，天空成了一整片没有中断的星海。两颗行星一眨不眨地凝视着。那些最醒目的星座纷纷显出自己的个性。猎户座方正的双肩与双足、他的腰带与佩剑，大熊座的北斗，仙后座的曲折线条，亲密簇拥的昴宿星团，都在黑暗中清楚地勾勒出来。银河是一道朦胧的光环，横跨天空。</p>
<p>想象力补全了单凭肉眼无法完成的事情。我向下望去，仿佛能够透过一颗透明的行星：穿过石南与坚硬岩石，穿过埋藏着消亡物种的古老墓地，向下穿过熔融玄武岩的流层，进入地球那由铁组成的核心；随后仍像是在继续向下，穿过南半球的地层，来到南方的海洋与陆地，越过桉树的根、越过那些倒悬在我下方的对跖居民的双脚，穿过他们头顶那片蓝色、被阳光刺透的白昼天幕，最后飞出到永恒的黑夜之中——在那里，太阳与群星同时存在。因为就在那令人眩晕的遥远下方，像湖水深处的鱼群一样，躺着另一半天空的星座。上下两个天穹此刻融合成一个中空球体，布满群星，漆黑一片，即使在耀眼的太阳旁也是如此。新月只是一弯白炽的金属丝。银河完整的光环围住了整个宇宙。</p>
<p>一种奇异的眩晕袭来，我转眼去寻找自家窗户那一点点发光的安慰。它们仍在那里；整个郊区也还在那里，山丘也在。然而群星却穿透这一切而闪耀。仿佛地上的万物都是玻璃制成，或者由某种更清澈、更空灵的玻璃质构成。远处，教堂钟声朦胧地敲响午夜。那声音暗淡而后退，敲下第一响。</p>
<p>想象力此刻又被激发出一种新的、陌生的感知方式。当我的目光从一颗星转向另一颗星时，我看到的天空不再是一块上下镶满珠宝的穹顶，而是一重又一重向纵深展开的太阳世界。虽然绝大多数巨大而熟悉的亮星仍像是我们的近邻，但有些光芒耀眼的星其实遥远而巨大，有些昏暗的小灯则仅仅因为距离很近才得以看见。四面八方的中距离空间里，挤满了成群、成流的星辰。然而现在，就连这些也仿佛近在咫尺；因为银河已经退向一个无可比拟地更加遥远的距离。透过银河较近部分的缝隙，又显现出一重接一重的发光云雾远景，以及一层层恒星族群的深邃透视。</p>
<p>命运把我安置其中的这个宇宙，并不是一间镶满星光的房间，而是一个可见的、由恒星流组成的巨大旋涡。不！它还不止如此。我从群星之间向外部黑暗窥去，还看见其他这样的旋涡、这样的星系，不过只是些微小的亮斑与光点，稀疏地散布在虚空中，一层深过一层，遥远到即使想象之眼也找不到这个宇宙性、包罗万有的“星系之星系”的边界。此刻的宇宙在我眼中仿佛一片虚空，其中漂浮着稀少的雪花；每一片雪花，都是一个宇宙。</p>
<p>我凝视所有宇宙中最暗淡、最遥远的那些，仿佛凭借一种超望远镜式的想象力，看见其中一个其实也是太阳的群体；其中某颗太阳附近有一颗行星，那行星背向太阳的一面有一座山，而山上正坐着我自己。因为我们的天文学家告诉我们：在这个被称为宇宙的、无边而有限的整体中，光的直线并不会通向无穷，而会最终回到它的源头。随后我想到，假如我的视野依赖的是物理的光，而不是想象之光，那么这些“绕着”整个宇宙来到我这里的光线所显示的，就不会是现在的我，而是早在地球形成之前，甚至也许在太阳形成之前就已经结束的事件。</p>
<p>可我又一次避开这些巨大尺度，重新寻找自家拉着窗帘的窗户。虽然群星仿佛能穿透它，但对我来说，那仍比所有星系更加真实。然而家已经不见了；整个郊区不见了，山丘也不见了，连海也消失了。我刚才坐着的地面本身都已经不存在。取而代之的是下方遥远处一团没有实体的昏暗。而我自己仿佛也失去了身体，因为我既看不见，也触不到自己的肉体。我想让四肢活动，却什么也没发生。我已经没有四肢。那些熟悉的、来自身体内部的感觉，以及从早晨起一直折磨我的头痛，都被一种模糊的轻盈与振奋取代。</p>
<p>当我真正意识到自己身上发生了什么变化时，我开始怀疑自己是否已经死去，正在进入某种完全出乎意料的新存在。这种如此老套的可能性起初令我恼火。随后，我忽然惊恐地明白：如果我真的死了，我就再也回不到自己珍视的、具体而微的共同体原子中。我的痛苦如此猛烈，连我自己都震惊。但很快，我又用另一个念头安慰自己：毕竟我大概并没有死，只是陷入了某种恍惚状态，随时都可能醒来。因此我决定，不要因为这个神秘变化而过分惊慌。我要以科学的兴趣观察接下来发生的一切。</p>
<p>我注意到，取代地面的那团昏暗正在缩小、凝结。下方的群星已经无法再透过它看见。不久以后，地球在我下方变得像一张巨大的圆桌桌面——一块宽阔的黑暗圆盘，四周环绕着星辰。我显然正以难以置信的速度飞离自己的母星。先前还能凭想象在下方天穹中看见的太阳，此刻又一次在物理意义上被地球遮住。虽然这时我必定已经来到地面数百英里之上，我却丝毫没有受到缺氧与气压消失的困扰。我感到的只有越来越强的振奋，以及思想令人愉悦的沸腾。群星异常明亮，使我兴奋不已。无论这是因为没有空气遮蔽，还是因为我自己的感知变得更加敏锐，抑或两者兼有，天空都呈现出我从未见过的面貌。每一颗星都像是突然提高了几个星等。整个天穹熊熊发亮。主要亮星仿佛远方汽车的前灯。银河不再被黑暗冲淡，而成了一条环绕四周、粒粒分明的光之河。</p>
<p>不久，在远远位于我下方的行星东缘出现了一条微弱的亮线；随着我继续上升，其中一些地方渐渐变成橙色和红色。显然，我不仅在向上飞，也在向东移动，正绕进白昼的一面。很快，太阳猛然跃入视野，它的辉煌一口吞没了巨大的晨曦月牙。但随着我继续疾驰，太阳与行星看起来逐渐分开，而黎明那条细线则增厚成一大片朦胧的日光。它不断扩展，仿佛肉眼可见地盈起来的月亮，直到半个行星被照亮。在夜与昼之间，一条带着暖色调、宽如次大陆的阴影带标示出黎明区域。我继续升高并向东飞行，看见陆地与白昼一道向西旋转，直到我来到太平洋上空，正值正午。</p>
<p>这时，地球已经成为一颗巨大的明亮球体，比满月大上数百倍。球体中央有一块刺眼的亮斑，是海洋反射出的太阳影像。行星的边缘不是清晰的线，而是一大片发光的薄雾，向四周太空的黑暗逐渐淡去。略微向我倾斜的北半球很大一片都被积雪和云顶覆盖。我能辨认日本与中国轮廓的某些部分，它们模糊的棕色与绿色切入海洋模糊的蓝灰色。靠近赤道之处，空气更加清澈，海洋显得很暗。一小团耀眼的云旋也许是某场飓风的顶部。菲律宾和新几内亚的轮廓精确得像绘在地图上一样。澳大利亚则逐渐消失在南侧边缘的薄雾里。</p>
<p>眼前的景象以一种奇异的方式打动了我。个人的焦虑被惊叹与赞美一扫而空；因为我们这颗行星纯粹的美丽竟令我如此意外。它是一颗镶嵌在星点斑斓的乌木上的巨大珍珠。它有珍珠母的光泽，又像一块蛋白石。不，它远比任何宝石都更美。它那带有纹理的色彩更加微妙、更加空灵。它具有活物才有的细腻与明亮、复杂与和谐。奇怪的是，正因为离得如此遥远，我反而第一次如此真切地感到地球那种生命般的存在：仿佛它真是一头活着的生物，只是陷入恍惚，朦胧地渴望醒来。</p>
<p>我想到，这颗天上的活宝石所有肉眼可见的特征，没有一处泄露出人类存在的痕迹。就在我眼前、却完全不可见的地方，分布着一些人口最稠密的人类聚居中心。下方有庞大的工业区域，正用烟尘熏黑空气。然而所有这些拥挤的生命、所有对人类而言意义重大的事业，在行星的外表上竟没有留下任何痕迹。从这个高处望去，地球与人类黎明之前相比根本不会有什么不同。任何来访的天使，或来自另一颗行星的探险者，都无法猜到这个温和的球体上竟爬满了害虫般的生灵——这些正在征服世界、折磨自身，又刚刚显出几分天使可能性的野兽。</p>

<h3>1. THE STARTING POINT</h3>
<p>ONE NIGHT WHEN I had tasted bitterness I went out on to the hill. Dark heather checked my feet. Below marched the suburban street lamps. Windows, their curtains drawn, were shut eyes, inwardly watching the lives of dreams. Beyond the sea’s level darkness a lighthouse pulsed. Overhead, obscurity.</p>
<p>I distinguished our own house, our islet in the tumultuous and bitter currents of the world. There, for a decade and a half, we two, so different in quality, had grown in and in to one another, for mutual support and nourishment, in intricate symbiosis. There daily we planned our several undertakings, and recounted the day’s oddities and vexations. There letters piled up to be answered, socks to be darned. There the children were born, those sudden new lives. There, under that roof, our own two lives, recalcitrant sometimes to one another, were all the while thankfully one, one larger, more conscious life than either alone.</p>
<p>All this, surely, was good. Yet there was bitterness. And bitterness not only invaded us from the world; it welled up also within our own magic circle. For horror at our futility, at our own unreality, and not only at the world’s delirium, had driven me out on to the hill.</p>
<p>We were always hurrying from one little urgent task to another, but the upshot was insubstantial. Had we, perhaps, misconceived our whole existence? Were we, as it were, living from false premises? And in particular, this partnership of ours, this seemingly so well-based fulcrum for activity in the world, was it after all nothing but a little eddy of complacent and ingrown domesticity, ineffectively whirling on the surface of the great flux, having in itself no depth of being, and no significance? Had we perhaps after all deceived ourselves? Behind those rapt windows did we, like so many others, indeed live only a dream? In a sick world even the hale are sick. And we two, spinning our little life mostly by rote, seldom with clear cognizance, seldom with firm intent, were products of a sick world.</p>
<p>Yet this life of ours was not all sheer and barren fantasy. Was it not spun from the actual fibres of reality, which we gathered in with all the comings and goings through our door, all our traffic with the suburb and the city and with remoter cities, and with the ends of the earth? And were we not spinning together an authentic expression of our own nature? Did not our life issue daily as more or less firm threads of active living, and mesh itself into the growing web, the intricate, ever-proliferating pattern of mankind?</p>
<p>I considered ‘us’ with quiet interest and a kind of amused awe. How could I describe our relationship even to myself without either disparaging it or insulting it with the tawdry decoration of sentimentality? For this our delicate balance of dependence and independence, this coolly critical, shrewdly ridiculing, but loving mutual contact, was surely a microcosm of true community, was after all in its simple style an actual and living example of that high goal which the world seeks.</p>
<p>The whole world? The whole universe? Overhead, obscurity unveiled a star. One tremulous arrow of light, projected how many thousands of years ago, now stung my nerves with vision, and my heart with fear. For in such a universe as this what significance could there be in our fortuitous, our frail, our evanescent community?</p>
<p>But now irrationally I was seized with a strange worship, not, surely of the star, that mere furnace which mere distance falsely sanctified, but of something other, which the dire contrast of the star and us signified to the heart. Yet what, what could thus be signified? Intellect, peering beyond the star, discovered no Star Maker, but only darkness; no Love, no Power even, but only Nothing. And yet the heart praised.</p>
<p>Impatiently I shook off this folly, and reverted from the inscrutable to the familiar and the concrete. Thrusting aside worship, and fear also and bitterness, I determined to examine more coldly this remarkable ‘us’, this surprisingly impressive datum, which to ourselves remained basic to the universe, though in relation to the stars it appeared so slight a thing.</p>
<p>Considered even without reference to our belittling cosmical background, we were after all insignificant, perhaps ridiculous. We were such a commonplace occurrence, so trite, so respectable. We were just a married couple, making shift to live together without undue strain. Marriage in our time was suspect. And ours, with its trivial romantic origin, was doubly suspect.</p>
<p>We had first met when she was a child. Our eyes encountered. She looked at me for a moment with quiet attention; even, I had romantically imagined, with obscure, deep-lying recognition. I, at any rate, recognized in that look (so I persuaded myself in my fever of adolescence) my destiny. Yes! How predestinate had seemed our union! Yet now, in retrospect, how accidental! True, of course, that as a long-married couple we fitted rather neatly, like two close trees whose trunks have grown upwards together as a single shaft, mutually distorting, but mutually supporting. Coldly I now assessed her as merely a useful, but often infuriating adjunct to my personal life. We were on the whole sensible companions. We left one another a certain freedom, and so we were able to endure our proximity.</p>
<p>Such was our relationship. Stated thus it did not seem very significant for the understanding of the universe. Yet in my heart I knew that it was so. Even the cold stars, even the whole cosmos with all its inane immensities could not convince me that this our prized atom of community, imperfect as it was, short-lived as it must be, was not significant.</p>
<p>But could this indescribable union of ours really have any significance at all beyond itself? Did it, for instance, prove that the essential nature of all human beings was to love, rather than to hate and fear? Was it evidence that all men and women the world over, though circumstance might prevent them, were at heart capable of supporting a world-wide, love-knit community? And further, did it, being itself a product of the cosmos, prove that love was in some way basic to the cosmos itself? And did it afford, through its own felt intrinsic excellence, some guarantee that we two, its frail supporters, must in some sense have eternal life? Did it, in fact, prove that love was God, and God awaiting us in his heaven?</p>
<p>No! Our homely, friendly, exasperating, laughter-making, undecorated though most prized community of spirit proved none of these things. It was no certain guarantee of anything but its own imperfect rightness. It was nothing but a very minute, very bright epitome of one out of the many potentialities of existence. I remembered the swarms of the unseeing stars. I remembered the tumult of hate and fear and bitterness which is man’s world. I remembered, too, our own not infrequent discordancy. And I reminded myself that we should very soon vanish like the flurry that a breeze has made on still water.</p>
<p>Once more there came to me a perception of the strange contrast of the stars and us. The incalculable potency of the cosmos mysteriously enhanced the rightness of our brief spark of community, and of mankind’s brief, uncertain venture. And these in turn quickened the cosmos.</p>
<p>I sat down on the heather. Overhead obscurity was now in full retreat. In its rear the freed population of the sky sprang out of hiding, star by star.</p>
<p>On every side the shadowy hills or the guessed, featureless sea extended beyond sight. But the hawk-flight of imagination followed them as they curved downward below the horizon. I perceived that I was on a little round grain of rock and metal, filmed with water and with air, whirling in sunlight and darkness. And on the skin of that little grain all the swarms of men, generation by generation, had lived in labour and blindness, with intermittent joy and intermittent lucidity of spirit. And all their history, with its folk-wanderings, its empires, its philosophies, its proud sciences, its social revolutions, its increasing hunger for community, was but a flicker in one day of the lives of stars.</p>
<p>If one could know whether among that glittering host there were here and there other spirit-inhabited grains of rock and metal, whether man’s blundering search for wisdom and for love was a sole and insignificant tremor, or part of a universal movement!</p>
<h3>2. EARTH AMONG THE STARS</h3>
<p>OVERHEAD OBSCURITY WAS gone. From horizon to horizon the sky was an unbroken spread of stars. Two planets stared, unwinking. The more obtrusive of the constellations asserted their individuality. Orion’s foursquare shoulders and feet, his belt and sword, the Plough, the zig-zag of Cassiopeia, the intimate Pleiades, all were duly patterned on the dark. The Milky Way, a vague hoop of light, spanned the sky.</p>
<p>Imagination completed what mere sight could not achieve. Looking down, I seemed to see through a transparent planet, through heather and solid rock, through the buried grave-yards of vanished species, down through the molten flow of basalt, and on into the Earth’s core of iron; then on again, still seemingly downwards, through the southern strata to the southern ocean and lands, past the roots of gum trees and the feet of the inverted antipodeans, through their blue, sun-pierced awning of day, and out into the eternal night, where sun and stars are together. For there, dizzyingly far below me, like fishes in the depth of a lake, lay the nether constellations. The two domes of the sky were fused into one hollow sphere, star-peopled, black, even beside the blinding sun. The young moon was a curve of incandescent wire. The completed hoop of the Milky Way encircled the universe.</p>
<p>In a strange vertigo, I looked for reassurance at the little glowing windows of our home. There they still were; and the whole suburb, and the hills. But stars shone through all. It was as though all terrestrial things were made of glass, or of some more limpid, more ethereal vitreosity. Faintly the church clock chimed for midnight. Dimly, receding, it tolled the first stroke.</p>
<p>Imagination was now stimulated to a new, strange mode of perception. Looking from star to star, I saw the heaven no longer as a jewelled ceiling and floor, but as depth beyond flashing depth of suns. And though for the most part the great and familiar lights of the sky stood forth as our near neighbours, some brilliant stars were seen to be in fact remote and mighty, while some dim lamps were visible only because they were so near. On every side the middle distance was crowded with swarms and streams of stars. But even these now seemed near; for the Milky Way had receded into an incomparably greater distance. And through gaps in its nearer parts appeared vista beyond vista of luminous mists, and deep perspectives of stellar populations.</p>
<p>The universe in which fate had set me was no spangled chamber, but a perceived vortex of star-streams. No! It was more. Peering between the stars into the outer darkness, I saw also, as mere flecks and points of light, other such vortices, such galaxies, sparsely scattered in the void, depth beyond depth, so far afield that even the eye of imagination could find no limits to the cosmical, the all-embracing galaxy of galaxies. The universe now appeared to me as a void wherein floated rare flakes of snow, each flake a universe.</p>
<p>Gazing at the faintest and remotest of all the swarm of universes, I seemed, by hypertelescopic imagination, to see it as a population of suns; and near one of those suns was a planet, and on that planet’s dark side a hill, and on that hill myself. For our astronomers assure us that in this boundless finitude which we call the cosmos the straight lines of light lead not to infinity but to their source. Then I remembered that, had my vision depended on physical light, and not on the light of imagination, the rays coming thus to me ‘round’ the cosmos would have revealed, not myself, but events that had ceased long before the Earth, or perhaps even the Sun, was formed.</p>
<p>But now, once more shunning these immensities, I looked again for the curtained windows of our home, which, though star-pierced, was still more real to me than all the galaxies. But our home had vanished, with the whole suburb, and the hills too, and the sea. The very ground on which I had been sitting was gone. Instead there lay far below me an insubstantial gloom. And I myself was seemingly disembodied, for I could neither see nor touch my own flesh. And when I willed to move my limbs, nothing happened. I had no limbs. The familiar inner perceptions of my body, and the headache which had oppressed me since morning, had given way to a vague lightness and exhilaration.</p>
<p>When I realized fully the change that had come over me, I wondered if I had died, and was entering some wholly unexpected new existence. Such a banal possibility at first exasperated me. Then with sudden dismay I understood that if indeed I had died I should not return to my prized, concrete atom of community. The violence of my distress shocked me. But soon I comforted myself with the thought that after all I was probably not dead, but in some sort of trance, from which I might wake at any minute. I resolved, therefore, not to be unduly alarmed by this mysterious change. With scientific interest I would observe all that happened to me.</p>
<p>I noticed that the obscurity which had taken the place of the ground was shrinking and condensing. The nether stars were no longer visible through it. Soon the earth below me was like a huge circular table-top, a broad disc of darkness surrounded by stars. I was apparently soaring away from my native planet at incredible speed. The sun, formerly visible to imagination in the nether heaven, was once more physically eclipsed by the Earth. Though by now I must have been hundreds of miles above the ground, I was not troubled by the absence of oxygen and atmospheric pressure. I experienced only an increasing exhilaration and a delightful effervescence of thought. The extraordinary brilliance of the stars excited me. For, whether through the absence of obscuring air, or through my own increased sensitivity, or both, the sky had taken on an unfamiliar aspect. Every star had seemingly flared up into higher magnitude. The heavens blazed. The major stars were like the headlights of a distant car. The Milky Way, no longer watered down with darkness, was an encircling, granular river of light.</p>
<p>Presently, along the Planet’s eastern limb, now far below me, there appeared a faint line of luminosity; which, as I continued to soar, warmed here and there to orange and red. Evidently I was travelling not only upwards but eastwards, and swinging round into the day. Soon the sun leapt into view, devouring the huge crescent of dawn with his brilliance. But as I sped on, sun and planet were seen to drift apart, while the thread of dawn thickened into a misty breadth of sunlight. This increased, like a visibly waxing moon, till half the planet was illuminated. Between the areas of night and day, a belt of shade, warm-tinted, broad as a sub-continent, now marked the area of dawn. As I continued to rise and travel eastwards, I saw the lands swing westward along with the day, till I was over the Pacific and high noon.</p>
<p>The Earth appeared now as a great bright orb hundreds of times larger than the full moon. In its centre a dazzling patch of light was the sun’s image reflected in the ocean. The planet’s circumference was an indefinite breadth of luminous haze, fading into the surrounding blackness of space. Much of the northern hemisphere, tilted somewhat toward me, was an expanse of snow and cloud-tops. I could trace parts of the outlines of Japan and China, their vague browns and greens indenting the vague blues and greys of the ocean. Toward the equator, where the air was clearer, the ocean was dark. A little whirl of brilliant cloud was perhaps the upper surface of a hurricane. The Philippines and New Guinea were precisely mapped. Australia faded into the hazy southern limb.</p>
<p>The spectacle before me was strangely moving. Personal anxiety was blotted out by wonder and admiration; for the sheer beauty of our planet surprised me. It was a huge pearl, set in spangled ebony. It was nacrous, it was an opal. No, it was far more lovely than any jewel. Its patterned colouring was more subtle, more ethereal. It displayed the delicacy and brilliance, the intricacy and harmony of a live thing. Strange that in my remoteness I seemed to feel, as never before, the vital presence of Earth as of a creature alive but tranced and obscurely yearning to wake.</p>
<p>I reflected that not one of the visible features of this celestial and living gem revealed the presence of man. Displayed before me, though invisible, were some of the most congested centres of human population. There below me lay huge industrial regions, blackening the air with smoke. Yet all this thronging life and humanly momentous enterprise had made no mark whatever on the features of the planet. From this high look-out the Earth would have appeared no different before the dawn of man. No visiting angel, or explorer from another planet, could have guessed that this bland orb teemed with vermin, with world-mastering, self-torturing, incipiently angelic beasts.</p>
