<h2>第二章</h2>
<p>这趟旅程很短，只持续了“瞬时”再加上一微秒的平方；而且一路无事，因为在如此纤薄的时间切片中，不可能产生任何有意义的体验。因此，几乎谈不上有什么过渡，卡莫迪便发现自己已经置身于银河中心宽阔的广场和奇形怪状的建筑之间。</p>
<p>他一动不动地站着，四下观看，尤其注意到头顶三颗暗淡的矮星正彼此环绕运行。他看见树木对栖息在枝头的绿色羽毛鸟儿含含糊糊地发出威胁。他还注意到另外一些东西；但由于缺乏可供类比的指涉物，那些东西根本没能在他的意识里登记下来。</p>
<p>“哇。”他终于说道。</p>
<p>“抱歉？”使者问。</p>
<p>“我说‘哇’。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“哦。我还以为你说的是‘哎哟’。”</p>
<p>“不，我说的是‘哇’。”</p>
<p>“现在我明白了，”使者略显不耐烦地说。“你觉得我们的银河中心怎么样？”</p>
<p>“非常壮观。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“我想是吧。”使者漫不经心地说。“它本来就是专门为了显得壮观才建的。当然，我个人觉得它和任何别的银河中心也差不多。建筑风格你应该看得出来，基本就是你能预料到的那种——新独眼巨人式，典型的政府风格，没有任何审美上的内在要求，唯一目的就是震慑选民。”</p>
<p>“那些漂在空中的楼梯可真够厉害。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“舞台效果而已。”使者评论道。</p>
<p>“还有那些巨大的建筑——”</p>
<p>“是啊，设计师把复合反向曲线配合过渡消失点用得挺巧，”使者很内行地说道。“他还采用了时间边缘畸变来激发敬畏感。算是挺漂亮吧，不过手法太露骨。你可能会有兴趣知道，那边那组建筑的设计，是从你们星球上的一次通用汽车展览整个照搬来的。它被认为是‘原始准现代主义’的杰出范例；古雅和温馨是它的主要优点。中景那栋‘漂移多层摩天楼’中央闪烁的灯光，则是纯粹的银河巴洛克。它们没有任何实用功能。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪无法一次把整组建筑尽收眼底。每当他盯住其中一栋，其他建筑似乎就会改变形状。他使劲眨眼，可视野边缘里的楼宇仍旧不断融化、变形。（“周边转化，”使者告诉他。“这些人为了效果，真是什么都干得出来。”）</p>
<p>“我到哪里领取奖品？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“这边走。”使者说着，领他穿过两座高耸入云的幻想产物，来到一栋小小的长方形建筑前。那建筑几乎完全藏在一座倒置的喷泉后面。</p>
<p>“我们真正办事的地方就在这里，”使者说。“最近的研究表明，直线构成的形状能安抚许多生物的突触。说起来，我对这栋楼还颇为自豪。你看，长方形就是我发明的。”</p>
<p>“扯淡，”卡莫迪说。“我们用了几百年了。”</p>
<p>“那你以为最早是谁把它带给你们的？”使者轻蔑地反问。</p>
<p>“这也不像什么了不起的发明。”</p>
<p>“不像吗？”使者说。“这只能说明你知道得太少。你把复杂当成了创造性的自我表达。你知不知道，自然界从来不会产生一个完美的长方形？正方形嘛，我承认，够显而易见；对没研究过这个问题的人来说，长方形也许看起来像正方形的自然延伸。可它不是！事实上，从正方形演化出去的形态应该是圆。”</p>
<p>使者的眼睛湿润起来。他用一种安静而悠远的嗓音说：“我很多年前就知道，以正方形为起点，还存在另一条发展道路。我盯着它看了很久。那种令人发疯的千篇一律既困扰我，又吸引我。边长相等，角也相等。有一阵子，我试着改变角度。最初的平行四边形是我做出来的，不过我并不觉得那算什么大成就。我继续研究正方形。规律性固然悦目，却不能过度。怎样才能改变那种摧毁心智的同一性，同时又保留一种可识别的周期性？后来有一天，我突然想通了！我只要改变两条平行边相对于另外两条边的长度就行了。如此简单，却又如此艰难！我浑身颤抖着试了一次。它成功的时候，我得承认，我进入了躁狂状态。接下来的日日夜夜里，我不停建造长方形，各种尺寸，各种形状，既有规律，又富于变化。简直像一只源源不绝吐出长方形的丰饶角！那真是令人激动的岁月。”</p>
<p>“我想是吧，”卡莫迪说。“后来你的工作终于被接受时——”</p>
<p>“那也很激动人心，”使者说。“不过足足过了几个世纪，才有人肯认真对待我的长方形。‘挺有意思，’他们会说，‘可新鲜劲儿一过，你还剩什么？你剩下的不就是一个不完美的正方形！’我争辩说，我推导出的是一种全新而独立的形态，一种像正方形一样具有必然性的形态。我遭受过辱骂。但最后，我的远见胜利了。到目前为止，银河里有略多于七百亿座长方形建筑。它们每一座，都源自我最初的那个长方形。”</p>
<p>“这个嘛……”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“总之，我们到了，”使者说道。“你从那里进去。把他们需要的数据交给他们，然后领取你的奖品。”</p>
<p>“谢谢。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>他走进房间。就在那一瞬间，钢箍啪地合拢，扣住他的手臂、双腿、腰和脖子。一个高个子、肤色黝黑的人朝卡莫迪走来；那人长着鹰钩鼻，左脸颊有一道疤，看他的神情只能形容为：杀气腾腾的欣喜与油腔滑调的悲悯混合而成。</p>
</section>
<section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="en" lang="en" aria-label="English original" hidden>
<p><b>CHAPTER 2</b></p>
<p>The trip was brief, lasting no more than Instantaneity plus one microsecond squared; and it was uneventful, since no meaningful experience was possible in so thin a slice of duration. Therefore, after no transition to speak of, Carmody found himself among the broad plazas and outlandish buildings of Galactic Centre.</p>
<p>He stood very still and looked, taking particular note of the three dim dwarf suns that circled each other overhead. He observed the trees, which muttered vague threats to the green-plumaged birds in their branches. And he noticed other things, which, for lack of analogizing referents, failed to register.</p>
<p>‘Wow,’ he said at last.</p>
<p>‘Beg pardon?’ the Messenger asked.</p>
<p>‘I said “wow,”’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Oh. I thought you said “ow.”’</p>
<p>‘No, I said “wow.”’</p>
<p>‘I understand that now,’ the Messenger said, somewhat testily. ‘How do you like our Galactic Centre?’</p>
<p>‘It’s very impressive,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘I suppose so,’ the Messenger said carelessly. ‘It was built specifically to <i>be</i> impressive, of course. Personally, I think that it looks very much like any other Galactic Centre. The architecture, you will note, is just about what you would expect – Neo-Cyclopean, a typical government style, lacking any aesthetic imperatives, designed solely to impress the constituents.’</p>
<p>‘Those floating staircases are certainly something,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Stagey,’ the Messenger commented.</p>
<p>‘And those immense buildings –’</p>
<p>‘Yes, the designer made a rather neat use of compound reverse curves with transitional vanishing points,’ the Messenger said knowledgeably. ‘He also utilized temporal edge distortion to evoke awe. Rather pretty, I suppose, in an obvious sort of way. The design for that cluster of buildings over there, you will be interested to know, was lifted bodily from a General Motors Exhibition from your own planet. It was judged an outstanding example of Primitive Quasi-Modernism; quaintness and cosiness are its main virtues. Those flashing lights in the middle foreground of the Drifting Multiscraper are pure Galactic Baroque. They serve no useful purpose.’</p>
<p>Carmody could not grasp the entire group of structures at one time. Whenever he looked at one, the others seemed to change shape. He blinked hard, but the buildings continued to melt and change out of the corners of his eyes. (‘Peripheral transmutation,’ the Messenger told him. ‘These people will quite literally stop at nothing.’)</p>
<p>‘Where do I get my Prize?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Right this way,’ the Messenger said, and led him between two towering fantasies to a small rectangular building nearly concealed behind an inverted fountain.</p>
<p>‘This is where we actually conduct business,’ the Messenger said. ‘Recent researches have shown that a rectilinear form is soothing to the synapses of many organisms. I am rather proud of this building, as a matter of fact. You see, I invented the rectangle.’</p>
<p>‘The hell you did,’ Carmody said. ‘We’ve had it for centuries.’</p>
<p>‘And who do you think brought it to you in the first place?’ the Messenger asked scathingly.</p>
<p>‘Well, it doesn’t seem like much of an invention.’</p>
<p>‘Does it not?’ the Messenger asked. ‘That shows how little you know. You mistake complication for creative self-expression. Are you aware that nature never produces a perfect rectangle? The square is obvious enough, I’ll grant you; and to one who has not studied the problem, perhaps the rectangle appears to be a natural outgrowth of the square. But it is not! The circle is the evolutionary development from the square, as a matter of fact.’</p>
<p>The Messenger’s eyes grew misty. In a quiet, faraway voice, he said, ‘I knew for years that some other development was possible, starting from the square. I looked at it for a very long time. Its maddening sameness baffled and intrigued me. Equal sides, equal angles. For a while I experimented with varying the angles. The primal parallelogram was mine, but I do not consider it any great accomplishment. I studied the square. Regularity is pleasing, but not to excess. How to vary that mind-shattering sameness, yet still preserve a recognizable periodicity! Then it came to me one day! All I had to do, I saw in a sudden flash of insight, was to vary the lengths of two parallel sides in relationship to the other two sides. So simple, and yet so very difficult! Trembling, I tried it. When it worked, I confess, I went into a state of mania. For days and weeks I constructed rectangles, of all sizes and shapes, regular yet varied. I was a veritable cornucopia of rectangles! Those were thrilling days.’</p>
<p>‘I suppose they were,’ Carmody said. ‘And later, when your work was accepted –’</p>
<p>‘That was also thrilling,’ the Messenger said. ‘But it took centuries before anyone would take my rectangle seriously. “It’s amusing,” they would say, “but once the novelty wears off, what have you got? You’ve got an imperfect square, that’s what you’ve got!” I argued that I had deduced an entirely new and discrete form, a form as inevitable as that of the square. I suffered abuse. But at last, my vision prevailed. To date, there are slightly more than seventy billion rectangular structures in the galaxy. Each one of them derives from my primal rectangle.’</p>
<p>‘Well,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Anyhow, here we are,’ the Messenger said. ‘You walk in right there. Give them the data they require and collect your Prize.’</p>
<p>‘Thank you,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>He entered the room. Immediately, steel bands snapped shut around his arms, legs, waist and neck. A tall, dark individual with a hawk nose and a scar down his left cheek approached Carmody and looked at him with an expression that could only be described as a compound of murderous glee and unctuous sorrow.</p>
</section>
