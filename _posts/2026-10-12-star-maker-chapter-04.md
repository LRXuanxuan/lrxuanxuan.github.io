<section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="zh" lang="zh-CN" aria-label="中文译文">
<p>我一定在另一个地球上度过了好几年，远远超过最初在田野里遇见那个跋涉而行的农民时所预想的停留时间。我常常渴望回家。我总是痛苦而焦虑地猜想，故乡那些亲爱的人如今过得怎样；假如我终有一天能够返回，又会发现怎样的变化。令我自己也惊讶的是，尽管在另一个地球上经历的一切如此新奇而密集，关于家的念头仍顽固地萦绕不去。仿佛就在片刻之前，我还坐在山坡上望着郊区的灯火。然而事实上，几年已经过去。孩子们大概已经变得几乎认不出来了。他们的母亲呢？她如今又会是什么样子？</p>
<p>我在另一个地球上停留如此之久，部分原因在布瓦尔图。他坚持认为，在我们双方都真正理解了对方的世界之前，我绝不能离开。我不断刺激他的想象力，让他尽可能清楚地设想我那颗行星上的生活；而他也在其中发现了与我在他的世界里所见相似的东西——壮丽与荒诞纠缠在一起。事实上，他完全不同意我认为他的世界总体上更加怪诞的看法。</p>
<p>把信息传给彼此，并不是把我和布瓦尔图联系在一起的唯一原因。我已经对他产生了极深的友谊。刚开始共同生活时，我们之间有时也会出现紧张。尽管我们都是文明人，也总努力以礼貌和宽厚待人，但那种极端亲密偶尔仍会使彼此疲惫。例如，我常常受不了他对本世界“味觉艺术”的狂热。他可以连续几个小时坐在那里，用敏感的手指抚过浸渍过味道的细绳，从中捕捉那些在他看来极其微妙、又富于形式与象征意味的味觉序列。起初我觉得新奇，后来甚至在审美上也受到触动；可即使有他耐心帮助，在这个早期阶段，我仍始终无法完整而自发地进入味觉艺术的审美世界。迟早我总会疲倦，或者感到无聊。再者，我也会不耐烦于他周期性的睡眠需求。由于我没有肉身，自己完全不需要睡觉。我当然可以暂时脱离布瓦尔图，独自在世界上游荡；可是，仅仅为了让宿主的身体恢复体力，就不得不中断一天中那些有趣的经历，常使我十分恼火。至于布瓦尔图，至少在我们合作的早期，他多少有些厌恶我能够旁观他的梦。因为清醒时，他还能把某些思想从我的观察中收回；一旦睡着，就毫无防备。当然，我很快学会了克制，不去使用这种能力；而随着我们的亲密逐渐变成相互尊重，他也不再那么严守这块隐私。久而久之，我们都开始觉得，若不能与对方共同品尝生活的滋味，便会错过其中一半的丰富与微妙。没有另一个人在场、以毫不留情却又友善的方式批评时，谁也不能完全信任自己的判断或动机。</p>
<p>我们终于想出一个办法，既能满足彼此的友谊，也能满足他对我的世界的兴趣，以及我自己思乡的渴望。既然我能从自己的行星来到这里，为什么不能设法和他一起返回那里？我们可以先在我的世界停留一段时间，再一同开始那场更宏大的远征。</p>
<p>为此，我们必须攻克两个性质完全不同的问题。第一，我此前只是出于偶然、用极其碰运气的方式完成了星际旅行，如今必须真正掌握这种技术；第二，我们还得想办法在异世人的天文图上定位我的故乡行星系统。</p>
<p>这个地理学——更准确说，是宇宙地理学——的问题最终证明无法解决。无论怎样努力，我都提供不了足够的数据来确定方向。不过，这次尝试却使我们获得了一个惊人、而对我来说又十分恐怖的发现：我不仅穿越了空间，还穿越了时间。首先，异世人的天文学已经极其发达，可在他们的观测中，像异世太阳以及我的太阳那样成熟的恒星却很少见；然而在地球天文学里，这类恒星却被认为是银河系中最常见的类型。为什么会这样？随后我又发现了另一件令人困惑的事：异世天文学家所认识的银河系，和我记忆中我们天文学家描述的银河系显著不同。在异世人看来，这个巨大的恒星系统远没有我们所见的那样扁平。我们的天文学家告诉我们，银河系像一块圆饼，直径大约是厚度的五倍；在他们看来，它倒更像一只面包。我自己也早就注意到，异世地球夜空中的银河带格外宽阔而边界模糊。还有一件事也曾令我惊讶：异世天文学家相信，银河系中还存在大量尚未凝聚成恒星的气体物质；而在我们的天文学中，银河系几乎完全由恒星组成。</p>
<p>难道我在不知不觉间旅行得比自己原先以为的远得多，甚至已经闯进另一个更加年轻的星系？也许在我那段黑暗飞行中——那时天空中的红宝石、紫水晶和钻石全都消失——我其实已经横跨了星系际空间。起初，这似乎是唯一可能的解释；但某些事实迫使我们抛弃了它，转而接受一个更加离奇的答案。</p>
<p>把异世人的天文学同我残缺的地球天文学记忆相比之后，我确信：他们所认识的整个星系宇宙，与我们所认识的整个星系宇宙都不相同。对他们而言，星系的平均形状更圆、更富含气体——也就是说，整个宇宙显得比我们时代原始得多。</p>
<p>此外，在异世地球的天空中，有几个星系近得惊人，即便裸眼也能看见显眼的朦胧光斑。天文学家已经证明，这些所谓的“宇宙”中，有许多距离本地“宇宙”的距离，比我们天文学中已知最近的外星系还要近得多。</p>
<p>这时，布瓦尔图和我同时意识到的真相实在令人眩晕。一切都指向同一件事：不知通过什么方式，我竟逆着时间之河而上，落到了遥远的过去，那时绝大多数恒星还很年轻。异世天文学中那么多星系彼此距离如此之近，也可以用“膨胀宇宙”理论来解释。我很清楚，这个戏剧性的理论仍只是假说，远未令人满意；但眼前至少又出现了一条相当醒目的证据，说明它在某种意义上很可能是正确的。在宇宙早期，星系自然应该拥挤在一起。毫无疑问，我被送到了一个这样的世界：当我的故乡行星甚至还没有从太阳的子宫中诞生出来时，这里就已经达到了人类文明阶段。</p>
<p>当我真正意识到自己在时间上距离故乡何等遥远时，一个事实——或者至少是一种极大的可能——突然重新回到心头；奇怪的是，我此前很久竟把它忘了。照理说，我已经死了。我忽然疯狂地渴望回家。家始终那么鲜明，那么近；即使相隔的是以秒差距计算的空间和以亿万年计算的岁月，它在感觉上仍一直就在身旁。我只要醒过来，难道不会重新发现自己正站在我们的山坡上吗？可我无论怎样都醒不过来。透过布瓦尔图的眼睛，我仍在查看星图和满是异样文字的书页。他抬起头时，我看到我们对面站着一个近乎人类讽刺画般的生物：一张蛙一样、几乎不能算作脸的面孔，胸膛像鼓胀的嗉囊鸽；除了一层淡绿色绒毛，她几乎赤裸。细长的双腿套着绿色丝袜，上面顶着一条红色丝质短裤。对地球人的眼睛来说，这生物完全就是怪物；可在另一个地球上，她却被认为是年轻而美丽的女子。而我自己，通过布瓦尔图那双善意的眼睛观察她时，竟也承认她确实美丽。对于已经习惯异世地球的人来说，她的五官和每一个动作都透露着聪慧与机敏。显然，既然我已经能够欣赏这样的女人，我自己也必定改变了。</p>
<p>若要详细讲述我们通过怎样一系列实验，逐渐掌握并完善可控星际飞行的技术，未免太过冗长。</p>
<p>只需说，经历许多冒险之后，我们终于学会了随时从行星上升起，仅凭意志行动就在群星之间自由改变航向。我们共同飞行时，无论灵活性还是准确性，都明显强于任何一个人单独进入太空。我们的心灵共同体似乎连空间运动的能力都得到了增强。</p>
<p>置身深空，被黑暗和群星包围，却始终与一个看不见的同伴保持着紧密的个人联系，这是一种极其奇异的体验。天国那些耀眼的灯火从身旁疾驰而过时，我们会在思想中彼此交谈，讨论经历，争论下一步计划，或者共享两个故乡世界的记忆。有时我们用我的语言，有时用他的语言；有时根本不需要词语，只需让两个心灵中的意象之流直接彼此相通。</p>
<p>在群星间进行这种脱离肉身的飞行，大概是所有“体育运动”中最令人振奋的一种。它并非没有危险；不过我们很快发现，危险来自心理，而不是身体。既然没有肉身，撞上天体也无关紧要。冒险初期，我们有时会因为失误，一头扎进一颗恒星。恒星内部当然热得无法想象，可我们所感受到的，不过是无比强烈的光辉。</p>
<p>这种运动真正严重的是心理危险。我们很快发现，灰心、精神疲劳和恐惧都会削弱我们的移动能力。不止一次，我们竟完全僵在太空中，像海面上一艘失去动力的弃船；而这种困境本身又会激起如此强烈的恐惧，以至于根本无法重新移动。我们只能把绝望的一整套滋味全都尝遍，随后经过麻木，最终进入一种哲学式的平静，行动能力才逐渐恢复。</p>
<p>还有一种更加严重的危险，不过只把我们困住过一次，那就是心灵冲突。我们对未来计划发生了一次严重分歧，这不但使我们完全无法移动，还引发了可怕的精神紊乱。知觉变得混乱，幻觉不断欺骗我们，连贯思考的能力也消失了。经过一段充满“即将彻底湮灭”感觉的谵妄之后，我们发现自己竟又回到了另一个地球：布瓦尔图重新躺在自己的身体里，仍在离开时那张床上；而我则再次成为一个无形的观察点，漂浮在行星表面某处。我们两人都陷入近乎疯狂的恐惧，很久才恢复。此后又过了几个月，我们才重新建立起心灵伙伴关系，再次开始冒险。</p>
<p>很久以后，我们才理解这次痛苦事件的原因。看来，我们彼此之间已经形成了极深的心灵协调，以至于一旦发生冲突，它更像同一个心灵内部出现了人格解离，而不是两个独立个体之间发生意见分歧。因此，后果才会如此严重。</p>
<p>随着脱离肉身飞行的技巧越来越熟练，我们从在群星之间任意穿梭中获得了强烈的快乐。那同时兼有滑冰与飞翔的快感。我们一次又一次纯粹为了取乐，在一对“双星”的两颗恒星之间和周围划出巨大“8”字。有时，我们会长时间静止不动，近距离观看一颗变星逐渐增亮、又逐渐黯淡。我们也常一头闯入拥挤的星团，在无数太阳之间滑行，仿佛汽车穿行在城市灯火之中。我们常贴着起伏、微微发光的气体表面掠过，在羽毛般的气体碎片和日珥之间穿梭；有时一头扎入星云，四周顿时只剩一片没有任何特征的黎明般微光。还有些时候，毫无预警的黑暗尘埃大陆会把我们吞没，整个宇宙都被遮去。有一次，我们正在穿越一片恒星密集的天区，一颗恒星忽然爆发出异常强烈的光芒，成为一颗“新星”。它周围似乎包着一层本身不发光的气体，因此我们甚至亲眼看见恒星爆发释放的光形成一个不断膨胀的球面。那道光以光速向外传播，又被周围气体反射，于是看起来就像一个不断鼓大的光球，越膨胀越暗淡。</p>
<p>这些不过是我们在异世太阳附近学习星际飞行技术时，像长着燕翼一样轻盈地来回滑翔所见到的少数恒星奇景。等到技艺成熟，我们便飞向更远的地方，并学会以极高速度运动，快到和我此前那次不由自主的飞行一样：前方的恒星与后方的恒星先后染上颜色，随后一切都陷入黑暗。不仅如此，我们也再次进入了我早先航程中曾经历过的那种更具精神性的视觉状态，在那里，物质光线造成的种种怪异效果都被超越。</p>
<p>有一次，我们向银河系边缘飞去，最终进入外面的虚空。附近的恒星已经越来越少。身后的半个天空挤满微弱光点，而前方则是一片无星黑暗，只有少数孤立闪烁的光斑打破虚无——那是几块脱离银河主体的碎片，或者说行星般的“次级星系”。除此之外，黑暗几乎毫无特征；只有大约半打模糊斑点，我们知道那是距离最近的几个外星系。</p>
<p>眼前景象令我们敬畏，于是我们在虚空中静止了很久。把整个一个“宇宙”摊开在眼前，实在令人心潮激荡：其中包含十亿颗恒星，也许还有数千个有生命居住的世界；而黑暗天空中每一颗微小斑点，本身又是另一个这样的“宇宙”，除此之外还有数百万个，只因距离极端遥远而无法看见。</p>
<p>这般物质上的巨大与复杂，究竟有什么意义？如果只看它本身，显然不过是赤裸裸的徒劳和荒凉。可我们又怀着敬畏和希望告诉自己：这样的物质规模，必然预示着心灵世界中更加宏大的复杂、精微与多样。只有这点，才足以为它辩护。然而，这个令人生畏的许诺固然鼓舞人心，同时也令人恐惧。</p>
<p>就像一只雏鸟第一次从巢沿探头，看见巨大世界后又缩回自己小小的家中，我们也曾越过那座小小星巢的边界——长期以来，人类错误地把它称作“宇宙”。而现在，我们又向内退去，重新把自己埋进故乡银河系那片亲切的疆域。</p>
<p>我们的经历提出了许多理论问题，不进一步研究天文学就无法解决，因此决定先返回另一个地球。可经过漫长而徒劳的搜寻之后，我们终于意识到，自己已经彻底迷失方向。群星彼此太相似了；而且在这个宇宙早期时代，很少有恒星能像异世太阳那样古老而温和。我们高速随机搜寻，却既找不到布瓦尔图的行星，也找不到我的行星，甚至没有找到任何熟悉的太阳系。无可奈何之下，我们再次停在虚空中思索自己的处境。四面八方都是乌木般的天空，上面嵌着钻石似的群星，仿佛向我们提出一道谜题：这片星尘中究竟哪一点才是异世太阳？这个早期宇宙的天空中照例到处都能看见星云物质的条带，可它们的形状全都陌生，无法用来定向。</p>
<p>迷失在群星之间并没有让我们沮丧。相反，我们仍因冒险而兴奋，而彼此本身又都成为对方保持好心情的原因。最近的经历使我们的精神生活更加活跃，也进一步把两个心灵组织成一个整体。大多数时候，我们仍能意识到对方和自己是两个独立存在；可记忆与性情的共享、融合已经发展到这样的程度：我们常常忘记这种区别。两个没有肉身的心灵占据同一个视觉位置，拥有同一批记忆和愿望，又常常同时完成同样的心理活动，这样的存在几乎无法再设想为真正彼此独立。然而奇怪的是，这种日益加深的同一性，却同时伴随着越来越强烈的相互认识与同志感。</p>
<p>彼此深入对方心灵所带来的，不只是精神财富的相加，而更像是相乘；因为我们每个人从内部认识的，不仅有自己和对方，还包括两个人彼此对应时形成的复调式和谐。事实上，以一种我无法精确描述的方式，我们的心灵结合还生成了第三个心灵。它此时仍只是间歇出现，却比正常状态下的任何一个人都更精微、更有意识。我们两人——或者更准确地说，两人共同组成的存在——会不时“醒来”，成为这个更高层次的精神。每一个人的全部经历，都在另一个人的照耀下获得新的意义；两个心灵结合成一个新的、更有洞察力、也更具自我意识的心灵。在这种清醒程度提升的状态中，我们——或者说那个新的“我”——开始有意识地探索其他类型生命和智慧世界可能拥有的心理形态。我以全新的穿透力，在自己和布瓦尔图身上区分出哪些属性是精神本身不可缺少的，哪些只不过是各自世界强加给我们的偶然特征。很快，这种想象性的尝试便证明自己是一种方法，而且是一种极其有力的宇宙学研究方法。</p>
<p>我们这时开始更清楚地认识到一个早已隐约怀疑的事实。在此前那次把我带到另一个地球的星际航行中，我无意间其实使用了两种不同的旅行方式。一种是脱离肉身、穿越空间的飞行；另一种，我不妨称为“心灵吸引”。它并不是沿空间连续飞行，而是把心灵通过某种感应直接投射到一个异族世界中。那个世界也许在时间和空间上都极其遥远，但在出发的那一刻，它与探索者自己的心灵恰好“同调”。显然，真正把我引向另一个地球的，主要正是这种方法。我们两个种族之间惊人的相似性造成了一种强烈的“心灵吸引”，它比我所有漫无目的的星际漂流都强大得多。从现在开始，布瓦尔图和我就要有意识地练习并完善这种方法。</p>
<p>不久，我们注意到自己已经不再静止，而是在缓慢漂移。同时又产生了一种奇怪感觉：表面看来，我们孤零零地处在一片广阔的恒星和星云荒漠中，可实际上，某些看不见的智慧存在仿佛就在心灵上离我们很近。我们集中注意这种“在场感”后，漂移开始加速；而且无论怎样以强烈意志强行改变方向，只要意志稍一放松，我们就会不可避免地重新转回原来的航线。很快，漂移变成了飞驰。又一次，前方群星变成紫色，后方变成红色；又一次，一切都消失了。</p>
<p>在绝对的黑暗与寂静中，我们讨论自己的处境。显然，我们此刻正以超过光速的速度穿越空间。也许，我们还以某种无法理解的方式同时穿越着时间。与此同时，那种其他生命就在附近的感觉越来越强烈，虽然仍同样混乱。随后，群星再次出现。它们像飞散的火花从身旁掠过，却不再带着异常颜色，而是保持正常色泽。正前方有一点格外明亮的光。它不断增强，变成令人目眩的光辉，随后显出一个圆盘。我们努力用意志降低速度，小心翼翼地绕着这颗太阳转动，开始搜寻。令我们欣喜的是，它周围果然伴随着几个也许能够孕育生命的小小颗粒。凭借那种明确无误的心灵存在感，我们选中了其中一颗行星，缓缓向它降落。</p>
</section>

<h3>IV. I TRAVEL AGAIN</h3>
<p>I MUST HAVE spent several years on the Other Earth, a period far longer than I intended when I first encountered one of its peasants trudging through the fields. Often I longed to be at home again. I used to wonder with painful anxiety how those dear to me were faring, and what changes I should discover if I were ever to return. It was surprising to me that in spite of my novel and crowded experiences on the Other Earth thoughts of home should continue to be so insistent. It seemed but a moment since I was sitting on the hill looking at the lights of our suburb. Yet several years had passed. The children would be altered almost beyond recognition. Their mother? How would she have fared?</p>
<p>Bvalltu was partly responsible for my long spell on the Other Earth. He would not hear of my leaving till we had each attained a real understanding of the other’s world. I constantly stimulated his imagination to picture as clearly as possible the life of my own planet, and he had discovered in it much the same medley of the splendid and the ironical as I had discovered in his. In fact he was far from agreeing with me that his world was on the whole the more grotesque.</p>
<p>The call to impart information was not the only consideration that bound me to Bvalltu. I had come to feel a very strong friendship for him. In the early days of our partnership there had sometimes been strains. Though we were both civilized human beings, who tried always to behave with courtesy and generosity, our extreme intimacy did sometimes fatigue us. I used, for instance, to find his passion for the gustatory fine art of his world very wearisome. He would sit by the hour passing his sensitive fingers over the impregnated cords to seize the taste sequences that had for him such great subtlety of form and symbolism. I was at first intrigued, then aesthetically stirred; but in spite of his patient help I was never at this early stage able to enter fully and spontaneously into the aesthetic of taste. Sooner or later I was fatigued or bored. Then again, I was impatient of his periodic need for sleep. Since I was disembodied, I myself felt no such need. I could, of course, disengage myself from Bvalltu and roam the world alone; but I was often exasperated by the necessity of breaking off the day’s interesting experiences merely in order to afford my host’s body time to recuperate. Bvalltu, for his part, at least in the early days of our partnership, was inclined to resent my power of watching his dreams. For though, while awake, he could withdraw his thoughts from my observation, asleep he was helpless. Naturally I very soon learned to refrain from exercising this power; and he, on his side, as our intimacy developed into mutual respect, no longer cherished this privacy so strictly.</p>
<p>In time each of us came to feel that to taste the flavour of life in isolation from the other was to miss half its richness and subtlety. Neither could entirely trust his own judgement or his own motives unless the other were present to offer relentless though friendly criticism.</p>
<p>We hit upon a plan for satisfying at once our friendship, his interest in my world, and my own longing for home. Why should we not somehow contrive to visit my planet together? I had travelled thence; why should we not both travel thither? After a spell on my planet, we could proceed upon the larger venture, again together.</p>
<p>For this end we had to attack two very different tasks. The technique of interstellar travel, which I had achieved only by accident and in a very haphazard manner, must now be thoroughly mastered. Also we must somehow locate my native planetary system in the astronomical maps of the Other Men.</p>
<p>This geographical, or rather cosmographical, problem proved insoluble. Do what I would, I could provide no data for the orientation. The attempt, however, led us to an amazing, and for me a terrifying discovery. I had travelled not only through space but through time itself. In the first place, it appeared that, in the very advanced astronomy of the Other Men, stars as mature as the Other Sun and as my own Sun were rare. Yet in terrestrial astronomy this type of star was known to be the commonest of all throughout the galaxy. How could this be? Then I made another perplexing discovery. The galaxy as known to the Other Astronomers proved to be strikingly different from my recollection of the galaxy as known to our own astronomers. According to the Other Men the great star-system was much less flattened than we observe it to be. Our astronomers tell us that it is like a circular biscuit five times as wide as it is thick. In their view it was more like a bun. I myself had often been struck by the width and indefiniteness of the Milky Way in the sky of the Other Earth. I had been surprised, too, that the Other Astronomers believed the galaxy to contain much gaseous matter not yet condensed into stars. To our astronomers it seemed to be almost wholly stellar.</p>
<p>Had I then travelled unwittingly much further than I had supposed, and actually entered some other and younger galaxy? Perhaps in my period of darkness, when the rubies and amethysts and diamonds of the sky had all vanished, I had actually sped across intergalactic space. This seemed at first the only explanation, but certain facts forced us to discard it in favour of one even stranger.</p>
<p>Comparison of the astronomy of the Other Men with my fragmentary recollections of our own astronomy convinced me that the whole cosmos of galaxies known to them differed from the whole cosmos of galaxies known to us. The average form of the galaxies was much more rotund and much more gaseous, in fact much more primitive, for them than for us.</p>
<p>Moreover, in the sky of the Other Earth several galaxies were so near as to be prominent smudges of light even for the naked eye. And astronomers had shown that many of these so-called ‘universes’ were much closer to the home ‘universe’ than the nearest known in our astronomy.</p>
<p>The truth that now flashed on Bvalltu and me was indeed bewildering. Everything pointed to the fact that I had somehow travelled up the river of time and landed myself at a date in the remote past, when the great majority of the stars were still young. The startling nearness of so many galaxies in the astronomy of the Other Men could be explained on the theory of the ‘expanding universe’. Well I knew that this dramatic theory was but tentative and very far from satisfactory; but at least here was one more striking bit of evidence to suggest that it must be in some sense true. In early epochs the galaxies would of course be congested together. There could be no doubt that I had been transported to a world which had reached the human stage very long before my native planet had been plucked from the sun’s womb.</p>
<p>The full realization of my temporal remoteness from my home reminded me of a fact, or at least a probability, which, oddly enough, I had long ago forgotten. Presumably I was dead. I now desperately craved to be home again. Home was all the while so vivid, so near. Even though its distance was to be counted in parsecs and in aeons, it was always at hand. Surely, if I could only wake, I should find myself there on our hill-top again. But there was no waking. Through Bvalltu’s eyes I was studying star-maps and pages of outlandish script. When he looked up, I saw standing opposite us a caricature of a human being, with a frog-like face that was scarcely a face at all, and with the thorax of a pouter-pigeon, naked save for greenish down. Red silk knickers crowned the spindle shanks that were enclosed in green silk stockings. This creature, which, to the terrestrial eye, was simply a monster, passed on the Other Earth as a young and beautiful woman. And I myself, observing her through Bvalltu’s benevolent eyes, recognized her as indeed beautiful. To a mind habituated to the Other Earth her features and her every gesture spoke of intelligence and wit. Clearly, if I could admire such a woman, I myself must have changed.</p>
<p>It would be tedious to tell of the experiments by which we acquired and perfected the art of controlled flight through interstellar space. Suffice it that, after many adventures, we learned to soar up from the planet whenever we wished, and to direct our course, by mere acts of volition, hither and thither among the stars. We seemed to have much greater facility and accuracy when we worked together than when either ventured into space by himself. Our community of mind seemed to strengthen us even for spatial locomotion.</p>
<p>It was a very strange experience to find oneself in the depth of space, surrounded only by darkness and the stars, yet to be all the while in close personal contact with an unseen companion. As the dazzling lamps of heaven flashed past us, we would think to one another about our experiences, or debate our plans, or share our memories of our native worlds. Sometimes we used my language, sometimes his. Sometimes we needed no words at all, but merely shared the flow of imagery in our two minds.</p>
<p>The sport of disembodied flight among the stars must surely be the most exhilarating of all athletic exercises. It was not without danger; but its danger, as we soon discovered, was psychological, not physical. In our bodiless state, collision with celestial objects mattered little. Sometimes, in the early stages of our adventure, we plunged by accident headlong into a star. Its interior would, of course, be inconceivably hot, but we experienced merely brilliance.</p>
<p>The psychological dangers of the sport were grave. We soon discovered that disheartenment, mental fatigue, fear, all tended to reduce our powers of movement. More than once we found ourselves immobile in space, like a derelict ship on the ocean; and such was the fear roused by this plight that there was no possibility of moving till, having experienced the whole gamut of despair, we passed through indifference and on into philosophic calm.</p>
<p>A still graver danger, but one which trapped us only once, was mental conflict. A serious discord of purpose over our future plans reduced us not only to immobility but to terrifying mental disorder. Our perceptions became confused. Hallucinations tricked us. The power of coherent thought vanished. After a spell of delirium, filled with an overwhelming sense of impending annihilation, we found ourselves back on the Other Earth; Bvalltu in his own body, lying in bed as he had left it, I once more a disembodied view-point floating somewhere over the planet’s surface. Both were in a state of insane terror, from which we took long to recover. Months passed before we renewed our partnership and our adventure.</p>
<p>Long afterwards we learned the explanation of this painful incident. Seemingly we had attained such a deep mental accord that, when conflict arose, it was more like dissociation within a single mind than discord between two separate individuals. Hence its serious consequences.</p>
<p>As our skill in disembodied flight increased, we found intense pleasure in sweeping hither and thither among the stars. We tasted the delights at once of skating and of flight. Time after time, for sheer joy, we traced huge figures-of-eight in and out around the two partners of a ‘double star’. Sometimes we stayed motionless for long periods to watch at close quarters the waxing and waning of a variable. Often we plunged into a congested cluster, and slid amongst its suns like a car gliding among the lights of a city. Often we skimmed over billowy and palely luminous surfaces of gas, or among feathery shreds and prominences; or plunged into mist, to find ourselves in a world of featureless dawn light. Sometimes, without warning, dark continents of dust engulfed us, blotting out the universe. Once, as we were traversing a populous region of the heaven, a star suddenly blazed into exaggerated splendour, becoming a ‘nova’. As it was apparently surrounded by a cloud of non-luminous gas, we actually saw the expanding sphere of light which was radiated by the star’s explosion. Travelling outward at light’s speed, it was visible by reflection from the surrounding gas, so that it appeared like a swelling balloon of light, fading as it spread.</p>
<p>These were but a few of the stellar spectacles that delighted us while we easefully skated, as on swallow wings, hither and thither among the neighbours of the Other Sun. This was during our period of apprenticeship to the craft of interstellar flight. When we had become proficient we passed further afield, and learned to travel so fast that, as on my own earlier and involuntary flight, the forward and the hinder stars took colour, and presently all was dark. Not only so, but we reached to that more spiritual vision, also experienced on my earlier voyage, in which these vagaries of physical light are overcome.</p>
<p>On one occasion our flight took us outwards toward the limits of the galaxy, and into the emptiness beyond. For some time the near stars had become fewer and fewer. The hinder hemisphere of sky was now crowded with faint lights, while in front of us lay starless blackness, unrelieved save by a few isolated patches of scintillation, a few detached fragments of the galaxy, or planetary ‘sub-galaxies’. Apart from these the dark was featureless, save for half a dozen of the vague flecks which we knew to be the nearest of the alien galaxies.</p>
<p>Awed by this spectacle, we stayed long motionless in the void. It was indeed a stirring experience to see spread out before us a whole ‘universe’, containing a billion stars and perhaps thousands of inhabited worlds; and to know that each tiny fleck in the black sky was itself another such ‘universe’, and that millions more of them were invisible only because of their extreme remoteness.</p>
<p>What was the significance of this physical immensity and complexity? By itself, plainly, it constituted nothing but sheer futility and desolation. But with awe and hope we told ourselves that it promised an even greater complexity and subtlety and diversity of the psychical. This alone could justify it. But this formidable promise, though inspiring, was also terrifying.</p>
<p>Like a nestling that peers over the nest’s rim for the first time, and then shrinks back from the great world into its tiny home, we had emerged beyond the confines of that little nest of stars which for so long, but falsely, men called ‘the universe’. And now we sank back to bury ourselves once more in the genial precincts of our native galaxy.</p>
<p>As our experiences had raised many theoretical problems which we could not solve without further study of astronomy, we now decided to return to the Other Earth; but after long and fruitless search we realized that we had completely lost our bearings. The stars were all much alike, save that few in this early epoch were as old and temperate as the Other Sun. Searching at random, but at high speed, we found neither Bvalltu’s planet nor mine, nor any other solar system. Frustrated, we came to rest once more in the void to consider our plight. On every side the ebony of the sky, patterned with diamonds, confronted us with an enigma. Which spark of all this star-dust was the Other Sun? As was usual in the sky of this early epoch, streaks of nebular matter were visible in all directions; but their shapes were unfamiliar, and useless for orientation.</p>
<p>The fact that we were lost among the stars did not distress us. We were exhilarated by our adventure, and each was a cause of good spirits in the other. Our recent experiences had quickened our mental life, still further organizing our two minds together. Each was still at most times conscious of the other and of himself as separate beings; but the pooling or integration of our memories and of our temperaments had now gone so far that our distinctness was often forgotten. Two disembodied minds, occupying the same visual position, possessing the same memories and desires, and often performing the same mental acts at the same time, can scarcely be conceived as distinct beings. Yet, strangely enough, this growing identity was complicated by an increasingly intense mutual realization and comradeship.</p>
<p>Our penetration of one another’s minds brought to each not merely addition but multiplication of mental riches; for each knew inwardly not only himself and the other but also the contrapuntal harmony of each in relation to the other. Indeed, in some sense which I cannot precisely describe, our union of minds brought into being a third mind, as yet intermittent, but more subtly conscious than either of us in the normal state. Each of us, or rather both of us together, ‘woke up’ now and then to be this superior spirit. All the experiences of each took on a new significance in the light of the other; and our two minds together became a new, more penetrating, and more self-conscious mind. In this state of heightened lucidity we, or rather the new I, began deliberately to explore the psychological possibilities of other types of beings and intelligent worlds. With new penetration I distinguished in myself and in Bvalltu those attributes which were essential to the spirit and those mere accidents imposed on each by his peculiar world. This imaginative venture was soon to prove itself a method, and a very potent method, of cosmological research.</p>
<p>We now began to realize more clearly a fact that we had long suspected. In my previous interstellar voyage, which brought me to the Other Earth, I had unwittingly employed two distinct methods of travel, the method of disembodied flight through space and a method which I shall call ‘psychical attraction’. This consisted of telepathic projection of the mind directly into some alien world, remote perhaps in time and space, but mentally ‘in tune’ with the explorer’s own mind at the time of the venture. Evidently it was this method that had really played the chief part in directing me to the Other Earth. The remarkable similarities of our two races had set up a strong ‘psychical attraction’ which had been far more potent than all my random interstellar wanderings. It was this method that Bvalltu and I were now to practise and perfect.</p>
<p>Presently we noticed that we were no longer at rest but slowly drifting. We had also a queer sense that, though we were seemingly isolated in a vast desert of stars and nebulae, we were in fact in some kind of mental proximity with unseen intelligences. Concentrating on this sense of presence, we found that our drift accelerated; and that, if we tried by a violent act of volition to change its course, we inevitably swung back into the original direction as soon as our effort ceased. Soon our drift became a headlong flight. Once more the forward stars turned violet, the hinder red. Once more all vanished.</p>
<p>In absolute darkness and silence we debated our situation. Clearly we were now passing through space more quickly than light itself. Perhaps we were also, in some incomprehensible manner, traversing time. Meanwhile that sense of the proximity of other beings became more and more insistent, though no less confused.</p>
<p>Then once again the stars appeared. Though they streamed past us like flying sparks, they were colourless and normal. One brilliant light lay right ahead of us. It waxed, became a dazzling splendour, then visibly a disc. With an effort of will we decreased our speed, then cautiously we swung round this sun, searching. To our delight, it proved to be attended by several of the grains that may harbour life. Guided by our unmistakable sense of mental presence, we selected one of these planets, and slowly descended toward it.</p>
