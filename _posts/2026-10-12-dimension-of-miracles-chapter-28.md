<h2>第二十八章</h2>
<p>卡莫迪身在纽约市，站在河滨大道与九十九街交口。西边，新泽西岸线上方，太阳正落到地平线大厦背后；右边，Spry 的广告牌也已经华灯齐放。河滨公园的树木披着绿色与煤灰，在西侧公路排出的废气中轻轻沙沙作响。周围不时传来受挫、神经紧绷的孩子们的尖叫，其间偶尔还夹杂着他们同样受挫、同样神经紧绷的父母发出的怒吼。</p>
<p>“这是你的家吗？”奖品问。</p>
<p>卡莫迪低头一看，发现奖品又变了形，这次成了一块《迪克·特雷西》手表，里面暗藏立体声扬声器。</p>
<p>“看起来像。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“似乎是个挺有意思的地方。”奖品说。“很有活力。我喜欢。”</p>
<p>“是啊。”卡莫迪勉强说道。他自己到底对这个家有什么感觉，连他也完全拿不准。</p>
<p>他开始沿街向北走。河滨公园里的灯已经亮了。推着婴儿车的母亲们正在离开，再过一阵，公园就会只剩警车和抢劫犯。</p>
<p>四周的烟雾像踩着小猫的脚步一样悄然涌来。透过烟霾，可以隐约看见一栋栋大楼，像迷了路的巨人。道路两侧，下水道欢快地流进哈得孙河，与此同时，哈得孙河也欢快地流进下水道。</p>
<p>“嘿，卡莫迪！”</p>
<p>卡莫迪停步转身。</p>
<p>一个男人快步朝他走来。那人穿着商务西装，脚上却套着运动鞋，头戴圆顶礼帽，脖子上系着白帆布领巾。卡莫迪认出他是乔治·马伦迪，一个自己认识的穷困艺术家。</p>
<p>“嘿，伙计。”马伦迪走上前来，和他握手。</p>
<p>“嘿，嘿。”卡莫迪笑得像个共犯。</p>
<p>“怎么样啊，伙计，最近混得如何？”马伦迪问。</p>
<p>“哦，你懂的。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“岂止懂，简直不能更懂！”马伦迪说。“海伦一直问起你。”</p>
<p>“真的？”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“千真万确。迪基·泰特下周六办派对。你来不来？”</p>
<p>“当然。”卡莫迪说。“泰特怎么样？”</p>
<p>“这个嘛，伙计，你懂的。”</p>
<p>“当然懂。”卡莫迪用极其同情的语气说。“还是那样，嗯？”</p>
<p>“不然你还指望怎样？”马伦迪反问。</p>
<p>卡莫迪耸耸肩。</p>
<p>“就没人打算介绍一下我吗？”奖品问。</p>
<p>“闭嘴！”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“嘿，伙计，你手腕上那是什么？”马伦迪弯腰凑近卡莫迪的手腕。“小录音机，嗯？太棒了，宝贝，真他妈太棒了。你给它编好程序了吗？”</p>
<p>“我没有被编程。”奖品说。“我是自主的。”</p>
<p>“嘿，这可太漂亮了！”马伦迪说。“我的意思是，真的漂亮。喂，米老鼠，你还会说什么？”</p>
<p>“滚去操你自己。”奖品说。</p>
<p>“住嘴！”卡莫迪急切地低声说。</p>
<p>“哎呀，”马伦迪咧嘴笑道，“这小家伙脾气还挺大，是不是，卡米？”</p>
<p>“确实。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“哪儿弄来的？”</p>
<p>“我得到它——嗯，是我离开期间得到的。”</p>
<p>“你出过远门？难怪这几个月一直没看见你。”</p>
<p>“大概就是这个原因。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“你到底去了哪儿？”马伦迪问。</p>
<p>卡莫迪差点脱口说“迈阿密”。</p>
<p>可不知为何，他忽然灵机一动，说道：“我去了宇宙，去了宇宙本身。在那里，我经历了一些经过挑选的短片，从今以后，我们将把它们统称为现实。”</p>
<p>马伦迪理解地点点头。“你去了一趟‘旅程’，对吧，伙计？”</p>
<p>“确实。”</p>
<p>“而且在那趟旅程中，你感知到了万事万物在分子层面的合一性，并倾听了你肉体中的能量，是不是？”</p>
<p>“不完全是。”卡莫迪说。“在我那一趟特殊的旅程中，我尤其观察到了其他造物能够自主支配的能量，而且从个人—分子层面超越到了外部—原子层面。也就是说，我的旅程使我确信：除了我自己之外，其他生物不仅具有现实性，而且确实存在。”</p>
<p>“听起来像特别猛的 LSD。”马伦迪说。“哪儿能弄到？”</p>
<p>“经验之酸，是从实践这株乏味杂草中蒸馏出来的。”卡莫迪说。“许多人渴望客观存在，但真正得到它的人寥寥无几。”</p>
<p>“你不肯透露，是吧？”马伦迪说。“算了，宝贝。你能去的 Trip，我都能去得更好。”</p>
<p>“我很怀疑。”</p>
<p>“你会怀疑这一点，我一点都不怀疑。不过算了。你去不去开幕式？”</p>
<p>“什么开幕式？”</p>
<p>马伦迪惊讶地看着他。“伙计，你不光是离开了，而且彻底跟时代脱节了。今天开幕的，毫无疑问是我们这个时代最重要的艺术展览——甚至可能是所有时代里最重要的一次。”</p>
<p>“这件审美典范到底是什么？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“我正要去。”马伦迪说。“跟我来。”</p>
<p>尽管奖品一路嘟嘟囔囔，卡莫迪还是和朋友并肩向北走去。</p>
<p>一路上，马伦迪给他讲最新八卦：众议院非美活动委员会被判犯有“非美罪”，不过最后只判了缓刑；Pepperidge Farm 新推出的“冻个人”计划大获成功；昨天，美国五个空中骑兵师成功击毙了五名越共游击队员；NBC 电视台又推出一部火爆新剧，名叫《自由放任资本主义历险记》。</p>
<p>他还听说，通用汽车公司出于一种前所未有的爱国姿态，派出了一整团文职志愿人员，由一位副总裁率领，前往柬埔寨边境附近的贤卡。</p>
<p>两人就这样一路聊着，最终来到一百零六街。这里原本的几栋建筑已经被拆掉，原址上竖起一座新建筑。</p>
<p>这东西看起来像一座城堡，可又是卡莫迪从未见过的那一种城堡。</p>
<p>于是他向精神亢奋的同伴马伦迪询问解释。</p>
<p>“你眼前这座庞然大物，”马伦迪说，“由建筑师德尔瓦努伊设计。他还规划过著名的纽约收费公路‘死亡陷阱 66 号’——至今没有任何人能够从起点一路开到终点而不发生事故。你也许还记得，同一个德尔瓦努伊还设计了芝加哥最新的贫民窟‘闪点塔楼’。那是世界上唯一真正做到形式服从功能的贫民窟；也就是说，它是第一座骄傲地、公开地以<strong>贫民窟</strong>为设计目标建造的贫民窟，而且已经得到‘总统城市美术肇事委员会’认证，正式列为‘不可更新建筑’。”</p>
<p>“这确实是项非凡成就。”卡莫迪说。“那这栋建筑叫什么？”</p>
<p>“这是他的 opus magnum——最高杰作。”马伦迪说。“朋友，这就是——<strong>垃圾城堡</strong>。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪注意到，通往城堡的道路设计得极为巧妙，由蛋壳、橙皮、牛油果核和蛤蜊壳铺成。道路一直通向一道巨大的门，两侧门柱由生锈的床垫弹簧构成。门楣上方，用涂过清漆的鱼头拼出一句格言：</p>
<p>“<strong>以浪费捍卫奢侈绝非恶行；在传播过剩时保持节制绝非美德。</strong>”</p>
<p>他们走进去，穿过一条条由压制纸板构成的走廊，最后来到一座露天庭院，中央一股凝固汽油喷泉正欢快燃烧。</p>
<p>他们从旁经过，走进一间由铝、钢、聚乙烯、福米卡塑料、苯乙烯、胶木、混凝土、仿胡桃木、腈纶和乙烯基材料建成的房间。房间另一端，还有更多走廊向各个方向分岔。</p>
<p>“喜欢吗？”马伦迪问。</p>
<p>“还不知道。”卡莫迪说。“这到底是什么东西？”</p>
<p>“博物馆。”马伦迪告诉他。“人类历史上第一座<strong>废弃物博物馆</strong>。”</p>
<p>“明白了。”卡莫迪说。“公众评价怎么样？”</p>
<p>“热烈得让我都惊讶。我的意思是，我们艺术家和知识分子当然知道它好，可没想到广大群众这么快就能理解。不过他们确实理解了。在这件事情上，公众表现出了天生的好品味，并且认识到：这才是我们这个时代唯一真正的艺术。”</p>
<p>“是吗？就我个人来说，这一切有点让人难以接受。”</p>
<p>马伦迪悲伤地看着他。“我真没想到，偏偏你会成为审美上的反动派。你想要什么？希腊雕像？还是拜占庭圣像？”</p>
<p>“当然不是。可为什么偏偏是这些？”</p>
<p>“因为这些，卡莫迪，才是真正的<strong>当下</strong>；真正的艺术必须建立在当下之上。我们消费，所以我们存在！可人类一直不愿正视这个关键事实。他们转过脸去，不肯看垃圾——我们一切享乐不可再还原的残余物。</p>
<p>“可你想想：废物是什么？难道它不是我们需求的纪念碑吗？‘勤俭持家，免于匮乏’——那是古代肛门焦虑式的劝诫。可如今，这条虚假的公理已经改变了。为什么要谈垃圾？确实！那为什么又要谈性、谈美德，或者谈任何其他重要东西？”</p>
<p>“让你这么一说，好像还挺有道理。”卡莫迪说。“不过还是……”</p>
<p>“跟我来。观察，学习。”马伦迪说。“这个概念会慢慢在你身上生长起来——和垃圾本身差不多。”</p>
<p>他们走进“多余噪声厅”。</p>
<p>卡莫迪在那里听见：厕所永不停歇的冲水声；交通噪声组成的音乐盛典；事故发生时激动人心的尖啸；暴民从喉咙深处发出的咆哮。</p>
<p>这些声音中还混入了一组“回顾性声音”：活塞式飞机的嗡鸣，铆钉枪的哒哒声，风镐沉重有力的撞击声。再往前就是“音爆厅”。卡莫迪刚进去就赶紧退了出来。</p>
<p>“完全正确。”马伦迪说。“那里很危险。不过很多人专程来，而且有些人会在那间屋里待上五六个小时。”</p>
<p>“嗯。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“也许吧。”马伦迪说。“现在，这边就是整个展览的核心声音：垃圾车咀嚼垃圾时那亲切可爱的咆哮。好听吧？从这里过去，是一组空品脱酒瓶展。那边还有一座地铁复制品。它被设计成能完整表现每一次摇晃和颠簸，车厢空气环境则由西屋公司提供烟雾调节。”</p>
<p>“那边有人在喊什么？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“一盘英雄之声录音带。”马伦迪说。“第一个是埃德·布伦，绿湾包装工队的全明星四分卫。接下来那个尖细的哀鸣，是纽约最近一任市长的声音肖像。再然后——”</p>
<p>“我们继续走吧。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“当然。右边是涂鸦馆。左边是一座按照旧式建筑法规建造的廉租公寓精确复制品——依我看，那东西多少带了点虚假的浪漫主义。正前方则是我们的电视天线收藏。这一件是英国型号，大约 1960 年。请注意它的严峻与克制。再把它和那件 1959 年的柬埔寨作品比较。看见东方型号上那些丰饶、流动的线条了吗？这就是民间艺术以一种真正可生存的形式表达自身。”</p>
<p>马伦迪转向卡莫迪，认真说道：“看见，并且相信，我的朋友。这就是未来的浪潮。曾经，人类抗拒现实所蕴含的意义。那个时代已经结束。如今我们知道，艺术就是事物本身，再加上它向冗余领域的延伸。</p>
<p>“我得赶紧说明，我说的不是波普艺术——波普艺术会嘲笑，会夸张。这是<strong>大众艺术</strong>，它只是存在。这是一个我们无条件接受不可接受之物的时代，并由此宣告：我们的人工性本身就是自然的。”</p>
<p>“我不喜欢！”卡莫迪说。“西斯赖特！”</p>
<p>“你喊什么？”马伦迪问。</p>
<p>“西斯赖特！西斯赖特！快他妈把我弄出去！”</p>
<p>“他疯了。”马伦迪说。“这里有医生吗？”</p>
<p>立刻，一个矮个、肤色黝黑的男人出现了。他穿着连体工作服，手里拎着一只小黑包。黑包上钉着一块银牌，上面写着：</p>
<p>“<strong>小黑包</strong>”。</p>
<p>“我是医生。”医生说。“让我看看他。”</p>
<p>“西斯赖特！你他妈到底在哪儿？”</p>
<p>“嗯——我明白了。”医生说。“此人表现出典型的急性幻觉匮乏症。嗯。对，我触诊他的头部，发现一团坚硬的大块增生物。这个倒属于正常现象。但是进一步看……嗯，惊人。这个可怜人简直已经被<strong>缺乏幻觉</strong>饿坏了。”</p>
<p>“医生，你能救他吗？”马伦迪问。</p>
<p>“你叫我来得正是时候。”医生说。“这种症状可以逆转。我这里有神圣的万能药。”</p>
<p>“西斯赖特！”</p>
<p>医生从“小黑包”里拿出一个盒子，组装起一支闪闪发亮的皮下注射器。</p>
<p>“这是标准加强针。”他对卡莫迪说。“完全不用担心，小孩打了都不会疼。里面是一种非常令人愉快的混合物：LSD、巴比妥类药物、安非他命、镇静剂、精神提升剂、情绪兴奋剂，还有各种其他好东西。另外加了一丁点砒霜，让你的头发更有光泽。现在别动……”</p>
<p>“该死的西斯赖特！快把我弄出去！”</p>
<p>“只有疼痛存在的时候，它才会疼。”医生安慰他说。</p>
<p>他举起注射器，猛地扎了下去。</p>
<p>就在同一时刻——或者几乎同一时刻——卡莫迪消失了。</p>
<p>垃圾城堡里一片惊慌和混乱，直到每个人都给自己来了一剂之后，事情才算解决。随后，众人以奥林匹斯诸神般的平静把这一切抛在脑后。</p>
<p>至于卡莫迪，一名祭司高声念道：</p>
<p>“多余之人啊，你如今前往天空中那伟大的冗余之境；在那里，一切不必要之物都有自己的位置。”</p>
<p>而卡莫迪本人，则在忠实的西斯赖特推动下，继续一头扎进无穷无尽的世界。</p>
<p>他沿着一个最适合被称为“向下”的方向移动，穿过地球数不胜数的潜在可能，进入成簇的低概率世界，最后又坠进排列森严的人造不可能世界群峰之中。</p>
<p>奖品责备他说：“卡莫迪，你刚才抛弃的就是你自己的世界！你知道吗？”</p>
<p>“我知道。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“而且现在再也回不去了。”</p>
<p>“这个我也知道。”</p>
<p>“我猜你是以为，前面的世界里会有某种花里胡哨的乌托邦？”奖品明显带着讥讽说道。</p>
<p>“不，也不完全是。”</p>
<p>“那你到底图什么？”</p>
<p>卡莫迪摇摇头，不肯回答。</p>
<p>“不管你图什么，都可以忘了。”奖品苦涩地说。“你的捕食者已经紧跟在身后，而且它注定会要你的命。”</p>
<p>“我毫不怀疑。”卡莫迪说，忽然进入一种奇怪的平静。“不过从长期规划来看，我本来也从没指望自己能活着离开这个宇宙。”</p>
<p>“这话毫无意义。”奖品说。“事实就是，你已经失去了一切。”</p>
<p>“我不同意。”卡莫迪说。“请允许我指出，我眼下仍然活着。”</p>
<p>“同意。不过只活在这一刻。”</p>
<p>“我从来都只活在这一刻。”卡莫迪说。“我从来不能指望得到更多。错的是我以前竟然期待更多。我相信，这一点适用于我所有可能和潜在的处境。”</p>
<p>“那你打算用这一刻获得什么？”</p>
<p>“什么也不获得。”卡莫迪说。“获得一切。”</p>
<p>“我已经听不懂你了。”奖品说。</p>
<p>“你身上有什么东西变了，卡莫迪。是什么？”</p>
<p>“一件小事。”卡莫迪告诉它。“我只是放弃了一种我本来就从未拥有过的长寿。我不再参加诸神在天国游艺场里经营的那场骗局了。永生这颗豌豆到底藏在哪只杯子下面，我已经不在乎。我不需要它。我有属于我的这一刻，而这一刻完全够用了。”</p>
<p>“圣徒卡莫迪！”奖品用最深的讥讽口气说道。“你和死亡之间只隔着一道影子那么薄的距离！那你现在打算拿你这可怜的一刻做什么？”</p>
<p>“继续把它活下去。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“时刻本来就是拿来活的。”</p>
</section>
<section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="en" lang="en" aria-label="English original" hidden>
<p><b>CHAPTER 28</b></p>
<p>Carmody was in New York City, on Riverside Drive and Ninety-ninth Street. To the west, above the Jersey shore, the sun was dropping down behind Horizon House, and, to the right, the Spry sign had come on in all its glory. The trees of Riverside Park, clad in green and soot, rustled faintly in the exhaust fumes from the West Side Drive. Around him he could hear the screams of frustrated, highly-strung children, punctuated by an occasional bellow from their equally frustrated and highly- strung parents.</p>
<p>‘Is this your home?’ asked the Prize.</p>
<p>Carmody looked down and saw that the Prize had metamorphosed again, appearing now as a Dick Tracy watch with hidden stereo speaker.</p>
<p>‘It looks like it,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Seems like rather an interesting spot,’ the Prize said. ‘Lively. I like that.’</p>
<p>‘Yeah,’ Carmody said reluctantly, not at all sure how he felt about his home.</p>
<p>He began to walk uptown. The lights had come on in Riverside Park. Mothers with baby carriages were leaving, and soon the park would be left alone to police cars and muggers. All around him the smog rolled in on little cat feet. Buildings could be glimpsed through it like giants who had lost their way. To either side, the sewers ran merrily into the Hudson, while at the same time the Hudson ran merrily into the sewers.</p>
<p>‘Hey, Carmody!’</p>
<p>Carmody stopped and turned. A man was walking briskly towards him. The man wore a business suit, sneakers, a bowler, and a white canvas ascot. Carmody recognized him as George Marundi, an indigent artist of his acquaintance.</p>
<p>‘Hey, man,’ said Marundi, coming up and shaking hands.</p>
<p>‘Hey, hey,’ said Carmody, smiling like an accomplice.</p>
<p>‘Well, man, how you <i>been?</i>’ Marundi asked.</p>
<p>‘Oh, you know,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Indeed, do I <i>not</i> know!’ Marundi said. ‘Helen’s been asking about you.’</p>
<p>‘That a fact?’Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Most assuredly. Dicky Tait’s throwing a party next Saturday. You wanna come?’</p>
<p>‘Sure,’ Carmody said. ‘How is Tait?’</p>
<p>‘Well, man, you know.’</p>
<p>‘Sure, I know,’ Carmody said, in a tone of deep compassion. ‘Still, eh?’</p>
<p>‘What would you expect?’ Marundi asked.</p>
<p>Carmody shrugged.</p>
<p>‘Isn’t anyone going to introduce me?’ the Prize asked.</p>
<p>‘Shut up!’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Hey, man, what’s that you got there?’ Marundi bent down and peered at Carmody’s wrist. ‘Little tape recorder, huh? That’s the greatest, baby, the greatest. You got it programmed?’</p>
<p>‘I am not programmed,’ the Prize said. ‘I am autonomous.’</p>
<p>‘Hey, that’s beautiful!’ Marundi said. ‘I mean, it really is. Hey there, Mickey Mouse, what else you got to say?’</p>
<p>‘Go screw yourself,’ the Prize said.</p>
<p>‘Stop it!’ Carmody whispered urgently.</p>
<p>‘Well now,’ Marundi said, grinning, ‘little fellow’s got a lot of spunk, eh Carmie?’</p>
<p>‘That he has,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Where’d you get it?’</p>
<p>‘I got it – well, I got it while I was away.’</p>
<p>‘You’ve been away? I guess that’s why I haven’t seen you around for these last several months.’</p>
<p>‘That must be it,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Where away have you been?’ Marundi asked.</p>
<p>It was on the tip of Carmody’s tongue to say that had been in Miami. But instead he was inspired to say, ‘I have been out in the Universe, the Cosmos itself, wherein I have passed through certain selected short subjects which shall henceforward be known as reality.’</p>
<p>Marundi nodded with understanding. ‘You been on a Trip, yes, man?’</p>
<p>‘Indeed I have.’</p>
<p>‘And on that Trip you have perceived the molecular all-in-oneness of all things and have listened to the energies of your body, <i>nicht wahr?</i>’</p>
<p>‘Not exactly,’ Carmody said. ‘Upon my particular Trip, I observed most particularly the discretionary energies of other creations and went beyond the personal-molecular into the external-atomic. That is to say, my Trip convinced me of the reality, to say nothing of the existence, of creatures other than myself.’</p>
<p>‘That sounds like powerful acid,’ Marundi said. ‘Where might it be obtained?’</p>
<p>‘The Acid of Experience is distilled from the dull weed of Practice,’ Carmody said. ‘Objective existence is desired by many but obtained by few.’</p>
<p>‘You won’t talk, huh?’ Marundi said. ‘Never mind, baby, any Trip you can make I can make better.’</p>
<p>‘I doubt that.’</p>
<p>‘I doubt not that you doubt that. But never mind. Are you coming to the Opening?’</p>
<p>‘What Opening?’</p>
<p>Marundi looked at him with amazement. ‘Man, you have not only been away, you have been out of touch besides. Today is the opening of what is past a doubt the most important art exhibition of our times and perhaps of any times.’</p>
<p>‘What is this paragon of aesthetics?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘I am going there,’ Marundi said. ‘Accompany me.’</p>
<p>Despite the mumblings of the Prize, Carmody fell into step beside his friend. They walked uptown, and Marundi told the latest gossip: how the House Un-American Activities Committee had been found guilty of Un-Americanism but had got off with a suspended sentence; the success of Pepperidge Farm’s new Freez-a-Man Plan; how five US Air Cavalry divisions had yesterday succeeded in killing five Vietcong guerrillas; how NBC-TV had begun a wildly successful new series entitled <i>Adventures in Laissez-Faire Capitalism.</i> And he also learned that General Motors, in a gesture of unprecendented patriotism, had sent a regiment of clerical volunteers led by a vice- president to Xien Ka near the Cambodian border.</p>
<p>Thus they conversed, and at length they came to 106th Street, where several buildings had been razed and a new structure erected to stand in their stead. This structure appeared to be a castle, but such a one as Carmody had never before seen. And he addressed his companion, the high-spirited Marundi, asking for an explanation.</p>
<p>‘This massy building that you see before you,’ Marundi said, ‘was designed by the architect Delvanuey, who also planned Death Trap 66, the famous New York toll road which no one has succeeded in driving from start to finish without accident. This same Delvanuey, you may recall, drew up the plans for Flash-Point Towers, Chicago’s newest slum, the only slum in the world in which form follows function; that is to say, the first slum which is proudly and avowedly designed <i>as</i> a slum, and which has been certified “unrenewable” by The President’s Commission on the Perpetration of Fine Arts in Urbanamerica.’</p>
<p>‘That is a singular accomplishment,’ Carmody said. ‘What does he call this particular structure?’</p>
<p>‘This is his opus magnus,’ Marundi said. ‘This, my friend, is The Castle of Garbage.’</p>
<p>The roadway to the Castle, Carmody perceived, was cunningly constructed of egg shells, orange peels, avocado stones and clam shells. It led to a great doorway whose two sides were made of rusty bedsprings. Above the gate, in letters formed by varnished fishheads, was the motto: ‘Wastefulness in the defence of luxury is no vice; moderation in the dissemination of excess is no virtue.’</p>
<p>They entered and walked through hallways of pressed cardboard, coming at last to an open courtyard in which a fountain of napalm blazed merrily away. They went past it into a room made of aluminium, steel, polyethylene, formica, styrene, bakelite, concrete, simulated walnut, acrilan and vinyl. Beyond that, other corridors branched out.</p>
<p>‘Do you like it?’ Marundi asked.</p>
<p>‘I don’t know yet,’ Carmody said. ‘What on earth is it?’</p>
<p>‘It is a museum,’ Marundi told him. ‘It is the first museum of human waste.’</p>
<p>‘I see,’ Carmody said. ‘How has it been received?’</p>
<p>‘With great enthusiasm, to my amazement. I mean, we artists and intellectuals knew it was good, but we didn’t think the public at large would catch on so fast. But they have. In this regard they have displayed innate good taste and have recognized this is the only true art of our times.’</p>
<p>‘Do they? I, personally, find all of this a little hard to take.’</p>
<p>Marundi looked at him with sorrow. ‘I had not thought that you of all people would be an aesthetic reactionary. What would you like? Greek statuary or Byzantine icons, perhaps?’</p>
<p>‘Certainly not. But why this?’</p>
<p>‘Because this, Carmody, is the real present, upon which true art must be constructed. We consume, therefore we are! But men have been unwilling to face this vital fact. They have turned away from Garbage, that irreducible residue of our pleasures. Yet consider – what is waste? Is it not a memorial to our needs? Waste not, want not: this was the ancient counsel of anal anxiety. But now the false axiom has been changed. Why talk about waste? Indeed! Why talk about sex, or virtue, or any other important thing?’</p>
<p>‘It sounds reasonable when you put it that way,’ Carmody said. ‘But still …’</p>
<p>‘Come with me, observe, learn,’ Marundi said. ‘The concept grows on you, very much like waste itself.’</p>
<p>They walked into the Extraneous Noises Room. Here Carmody listened to the sound of a continually flushing toilet, the musical pageant of traffic noises, the thrilling screech of an accident, the deep-throated roar of a mob. Mingled with this were Retrospective Sounds: the burr of a piston aircraft, the chatter of a riveting gun, the strong thud of a jackhammer. Past that was the Sonic Boom Room, which Carmody hastily backed out of.</p>
<p>‘Quite right,’ Marundi said. ‘It <i>is</i> dangerous. But a lot of people come here, and some stay in this room for five or six hours.’</p>
<p>‘Huh,’ said Carmody.</p>
<p>‘Perhaps,’ Marundi said. ‘Now, right over here is the keynote sound of our exhibition: the beloved bellow of a rubbish truck chewing up rubbish. Nice, eh? And right through here is an exhibition of empty pint wine bottles. Over there is a replica of a subway. It is built to convey every lurch, and its aerial environment is smoke-conditioned by Westinghouse.’</p>
<p>‘What’s that shouting?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘A tape of heroic voices,’ Marundi said. ‘That first one is Ed Brun, all-pro quarterback for the Green Bay Packers. The next, a high-pitched whine, is a sound-portrait of New York’s most recent mayor. And after that –’</p>
<p>‘Let’s go on,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Certainly. To the right is the Graffiti wing. To the left is an exact replica of an old-law tenement (a spurious bit of romanticism, to my way of thinking). Straight ahead you can see our collection of television antennas. This one is a British model, circa 1960. Note the severity, the restraint. Compare it to that 1959 Cambodian job. Do you see the luxuriant flowing of lines on the Oriental model? That is popular art expressing itself in a viable form.’</p>
<p>Marundi turned to Carmody and said earnestly, ‘See and believe, my friend. This is the wave of the future. Once upon a time men resisted the implications of actuality. That day is gone. We know now that art is the thing itself together with its extensions into superfluity. Not pop art, I hasten to say, which sneers and exaggerates. This is <i>popular</i> art, which simply exists. This is the age in which we unconditionally accept the unacceptable, and thus proclaim the naturalness of our artificiality.’</p>
<p>‘I don’t like it!’ Carmody said. ‘Seethwright!’</p>
<p>‘What are you shouting for?’ Marundi asked him.</p>
<p>‘Seethwright! Seethwright! Get me the hell out of here!’</p>
<p>‘He’s flipped,’ Marundi said. ‘Is there a doctor in the house?’</p>
<p>Immediately a short swarthy man in a one-piece jumpsuit appeared. The man was carrying a little black bag with a silver plaque on it, upon which was written, ‘Little Black Bag.’</p>
<p>‘I am a physician,’ the physician said. ‘Let me see him.’</p>
<p>‘Seethwright! Where in hell are you?’</p>
<p>‘Hmmmmmm, I see,’ the doctor said. ‘This man shows every sign of acute hallucinatory deprivation. Hmm. Yes, I palpate the head and find a hard massy growth. That much is normal. But going beyond that … hmm, amazing. The poor man is literally starved for illusion.’</p>
<p>‘Doc, can you help him?’ Marundi asked.</p>
<p>‘You called me just in time,’ the doctor said. ‘The condition is reversible. I have here the divine panacea.’</p>
<p>‘Seethwright!’</p>
<p>The doctor drew a case out of his Little Black Bag and fitted together a glittering hypodermic. ‘This is the standard booster,’ he said to Carmody. ‘Nothing to worry about, it wouldn’t hurt a child. It contains a highly pleasing mixture of LSD, barbiturates, amphetamines, tranquillizers, psychic lifts, mood stimulators, and various other good things. And just a touch of arsenic to make your hair glossy. Hold still now …’</p>
<p>‘Damn you, Seethwright! Get me out of this!’</p>
<p>‘It only hurts while the pain is present,’ the doctor assured him, poised the hypodermic and thrust home.</p>
<p>At the same moment, or nearly the same moment, Carmody disappeared.</p>
<p>There was consternation and confusion in the Castle, which was not resolved until everyone had fixed. Then it was passed over with Olympian calm. As for Carmody, a priest intoned the words: ‘Superfluous man, goest thou now to that great realm of the Extraneous in the sky, where there is place for all unnecessary things.’</p>
<p>But Carmody himself, propelled by the faithful Seeth-wright, plunged onward through the endless worlds. He moved in a direction best characterized as ‘down,’ through the myriad potentialities of Earth, and into the clustered improbabilities, and finally into the serried ranges of the constructed impossibilities.</p>
<p>The Prize chided him, saying, ‘That was your own world that you abandoned, Carmody! Are you aware of that?’</p>
<p>‘Yes, I am aware of it,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘And now there can be no return.’</p>
<p>‘I am aware of that, too.’</p>
<p>‘I suppose you thought you’d find some gaudy utopia in the worlds ahead?’ the Prize said; with a marked sneer.</p>
<p>‘No, not exactly.’</p>
<p>‘What then?’</p>
<p>Carmody shook his head and refused to answer.</p>
<p>‘Whatever it was, you can forget about it,’ the Prize said bitterly. ‘Your predator is close behind you and will infallibly be your death.’</p>
<p>‘I don’t doubt it,’ Carmody said, in a moment of strange calm. ‘But in terms of long-range planning, I never did expect to get out of this Universe alive.’</p>
<p>‘That is meaningless,’ the Prize said. ‘The fact is, you have lost everything.’</p>
<p>‘I don’t agree,’ Carmody said. ‘Permit me to point out that I am presently still alive.’</p>
<p>‘Agreed. But only for the moment.’</p>
<p>‘I have always been alive only for the moment,’ Carmody said. ‘I could never count on more. It was my error to expect more. That holds true, I believe, for all of my possible and potential circumstances.’</p>
<p>‘Then what do you hope to achieve with your moment?’</p>
<p>‘Nothing,’ Carmody said. ‘Everything.’</p>
<p>‘I don’t understand you any longer,’ the Prize said.</p>
<p>‘Something about you has changed, Carmody. What is it?’</p>
<p>‘A minor thing,’ Carmody told him. ‘I have simply given up a longevity which I never possessed anyhow. I have turned away from the con game which the Gods run in their heavenly sideshow. I no longer care under which shell the pea of immortality might be found. I don’t need it. I have my moment, which is quite enough.’</p>
<p>‘Saint Carmody!’ the Prize said, in tones of deepest sarcasm. ‘No more than a shadow’s breadth separates you and death! What will you do now with your pitiable moment?’</p>
<p>‘I shall continue to live it,’ Carmody said. ‘That is what moments are for.’</p>
