<h2>ii. 众人与人类</h2>
<p>每当我们试图唤起“种族心灵”时，行星上的所有成年人都会前往指定地点，聚集在遍布各大陆的若干巨塔之一附近。首先是准备阶段：个人之间，以及此时开始形成的各级群体心灵之间，会进行大量心灵感应交流。随后便到达那个至高时刻。若一切顺利，世界上这无数人群中的每一个男人和女人都会仿佛从个体状态中醒来，成为同一个“种族心灵”；这个单一心灵拥有世上所有人的数百万具身体，就像一个人的心灵拥有自己肉体中无数细胞一样。</p>
<p>如果那天早晨你有幸站在那根巨大石质尖塔的顶端俯瞰，而我和许多人正聚集在塔下，你会看见，在这座高达二十英里的建筑峭壁脚下，一片没有任何特征的灰色平原向四面八方一直铺到地平线，看上去简直像一片荒漠。若再举起一架强力望远镜，你就会发现，那片平原其实被极其细密的棕色微点布满，点与点之间夹着几乎看不见的绿色细痕。若换成更强大的望远镜，你就会惊讶地发现，每一个棕点其实都是一群人。于是，整片“荒漠”会显露出真相：那是数量惊人的男人和女人，他们组成一个个小团体，团体之间露出绿色草地。整片大地的纹理几乎像显微镜下的植物组织，又像利维坦由细胞构成的血肉。每个“细胞”内部，你都可以数出九十六个颗粒——九十六个微小的亚细胞器，实际上也就是九十六张男女的面孔。我在别处已经谈过这些构成我们社会基础的小型婚姻群体，这里不再赘述。你只需知道：你看到的每一个小点都不是一张脸，而是一组面孔。放眼各处，所有面孔都朝向高塔，像沙粒一样一动不动。然而，这样一支大军不过是数千支同类队伍中的一支，它们散布在所有大陆之上。</p>
<p>再强一些的望远镜会让你看见：我们全都站着，每个人都把脱下的飞行服搭在手臂或肩头。你的镜筒向这里那里移动时，会发现我们之间存在极大的差异。我们虽然都属于同一个物种，但这个物种内部的形态差异，甚至比你们家犬之间的差异还大；而我们的心灵，同样不比身体更趋一致。你会注意到，我们几乎全都赤裸着；但也有少数人浑身覆盖着自己的天鹅绒般毛皮。绝大多数最后人类却没有体毛，皮肤可能是棕色、金色、黑色或灰色，也可能带条纹、斑点。你固然一眼便能认出我们的身体确实属于“人形”，却同时会被那千差万别的外观，以及它们偏离你心目中“真正人类形态”的各种方式吓一跳。你会在我们的脸和四肢轮廓上看出强烈的动物暗示：仿佛这里是一匹马，那里是一只虎，再远处又是一头远古爬行动物，它们被灌入了人的心灵；虽然如今确凿无疑是男人或女人，身体上却仍保留着动物祖先的回声。我们的这种动物性会令你厌恶。然而，我们的人性已经稳固得无可动摇，因此无需害怕自己同时也是动物。你们则不同：你们的人性尚且脆弱，所以出于恐惧与羞耻，你们总想杀死自己体内的动物。而这种屠杀本身，反过来毒害了你们。</p>
<p>随着望远镜继续扫过人群，你会发现我们所有人都处在人生的暮春或盛夏。婴儿这一天被留在自动托儿所里；儿童则在自己的住宅中自由活动；青年男女则住在“青年之地”，过着他们狂野而浪漫的生活。虽然聚集在你脚下这支人群的平均年龄已经有数千岁，却不会有任何一个成员在你眼里显得衰老。我们之中没有老年衰退这回事。</p>
<p>如果只把我们当作一个整体来看，你很难相信，脚下每一个微小的人类原子其实都是一个独特而高度发展的个体；他看待你们这个物种尚未成熟的人格，大概就像你们看待自己那些半人类祖先一样。然而，只要仔细调好望远镜，你便会在每一张脸上看到强烈而独一无二的意识之光。因为这支庞大人群中的每一个成员，都有自己独特而细腻的性格、丰富的记忆、所爱之物与人生目标，也都在我们不可思议地复杂的社会中贡献着一份只有自己能做的工作。农夫、园丁、工程师、建筑师、化学家、亚原子物理学家、生物学家、心理学家和优生学家，与各种创造性艺术家并肩站在一起；还有天文学家、哲学家、历史学家、过去探索者、教师、职业母亲，以及许多在你们世界中根本找不到对应职业的人。在海王星那些较为固定的居民之外，人群中还会零星出现一些以太航行员——他们的飞船碰巧停靠在母星；甚至还有少数来自天王星和冥王星聚居地的拓荒者。往返行星之间的以太航行员，是我们唯一的一类运输从业者。在母星上，人员交通完全依靠私人飞行艇或飞行服；货物则由地下管道自动运送，其中有些管道经过适当制冷，几乎沿直线穿过行星内部。</p>
<p>至于你们所理解的“工业”和“商业”，在我们社会中根本找不到对应物，因为我们的世界没有类似你们的工业制度。我们的工业里既没有操作工人，也没有工业巨头。制造若只是例行程序，就由机器完成，人类除了按一下按钮之外无需介入；制造若涉及创新，便属于科学家和工程师的工作；若涉及组织，则由职业组织者负责。至于商业，我们根本没有这种东西，因为不存在买卖。生产和分配都由组织者调节，他们接受“最高统一学院”的指导。严格说来，这绝不是一个政府机关，因为它只提供建议；然而，由于我们始终保持心灵感应交流，它提出的建议总是理智而有说服力。因此，它虽然没有任何强制权，却仍是整个共同生活最重要的协调中心。在你脚下的人群里，会有不少最高统一学院的成员；但他们身上没有任何标志能把他们与其他同样受尊敬的职业区分开来。</p>
<p>如果你观察得足够久，会在数小时的静止之后，看见灰色平原上发生一种闪动般的变化——整片人海同时骚动起来。那一刻，种族心灵正在整个行星人口中苏醒。望远镜会让你看到，原本平静的所有面孔忽然被一种高度紧张、又仿佛凯旋般的神情照亮。因为此刻，我们每一个人终于都在脱离较低层次的自我，进入一种更高的自我存在，并发现自己竟成为了一个世界唯一而包容一切的心灵。</p>
<p>就在那一刻，我——“人类”——所感知的，不再只是行星表面所有男人和女人各自拥有的千万种感知，而是这些感知汇聚成的单一意义。我通过所有个体的双脚把握着自己的行星，就像一个人把一只球握在掌中。我拥有所有男人和女人的全部记忆；可那些记忆对我而言并不仅仅是“记忆”，而仿佛都是当下正在直接发生的经验。我看见自己这一代、甚至整个物种的完整传记，就像你们听见一段旋律：它固然在流动，可全部又同时存在于“现在”。我看见行星绕太阳旋转，甚至看见恒星在天空中像昆虫一样缓慢爬动；但若我愿意，也能看见原子内部的运动，数清光波的脉动。我同样拥有所有男女的全部情感与欲望。我从内部观察这些情感，就像一个人内省自己的欢愉与悲痛；然而我又能不带偏私地观察它们。我存在于所有恋人的相爱之中，也存在于所有敢于冒险者的冒险之中。我品味所有胜利与失败、所有想象与推理。</p>
<p>然而，对于这些由我无数微小成员提供的、喧腾繁复的经验，我始终保持着超然。我把注意力转向一个远离它们的领域；在那里，我——“人类”——经历着属于自己的庄重欲望与恐惧，进行属于自己的高层推理与沉思。至于当时究竟是什么占据着“种族心灵”——也就是我的注意，我这个此刻正在与你们通讯的微小个体根本不可能给出任何明确说明，因为那类经验超出了任何单个个体的理解能力。不过至少可以说一点：当我苏醒进入这种崇高经验，并像一个人看着自己肉体的细胞那样俯视我的个体成员时，真正令我震撼的并不是自己的伟大，而是自己的渺小。因为相对于万物整体，我看见自己不过是一个极其微小、简单而无助的存在；我注定很快会被某个恒星事件摧毁，而即使站在这种新而崇高的理解层次上，我仍无法理解那个事件的意义。</p>
<p>不过，就连此刻正与你们通讯的这个小小个体，也还记得：当我——“人类”——面对这一命运时，我没有丝毫沮丧。我反而带着昂扬之情接受它，把它看作整体那种巨大美之中一个显而易见的美丽部分。因为我的全部经验，都被一种对那包罗万有而又令人战栗的美的感知所贯穿、所照亮。即使在我存在的那个更高层面，我也不过瞥见了它一角；但仅仅这一瞥，已经让我以低层个体状态绝无可能达到的洞见与狂喜去凝视它。</p>
<p>如果你一直留在塔顶，直到第二天早晨，那么日出后不久，你会看见整片平原再次活动起来。我们正在准备离去。不一会儿，在你眼中仿佛连行星表面本身都开始脱离地面、向上升腾：像风扫过道路时扬起的尘土，像热水上冒出的蒸汽，又像从山顶俯瞰时一团正在谷中翻腾上升的云。它越升越高，朝你涌来，很快即使不用望远镜，也能分辨出那其实是由无数男女组成的巨大烟云。片刻之后，你会看见他们布满你的四周乃至头顶，一个个张开双臂在空中游动、盘旋、攀升，把天空都遮暗。经历短暂的、毫无秩序却近乎狂喜的飞行之后，他们又会被看见向四面八方成流散去，最后只在地平线上留下淡淡薄雾。再向下看，原本灰色的平原已经变成绿色，远方则渐渐融入蓝色与紫色。</p>
<p>这些聚会的解散，并不意味着种族经验就此结束。在随后长短不定的数月乃至数年中，每个个体虽然各自离开、过自己的生活、履行自己在共同体中的特殊职能，却仍继续被种族心灵占据。每个人都以个体身份去感知、思考、努力；可与此同时，他也是“人类”，以整个种族的尺度感知，并以较低存在状态下完全不可能实现的方式思考。就像大脑中的每个细胞都过着自己的生命，却又参与整个大脑的经验一样，我们也是如此。只是过了一段时间以后，那个巨大存在终究会再次沉睡。</p>
<p>我刚才描述的这次觉醒中，种族心灵持续了许多年；然而有一类人却不得不停止参与，那就是即将从事过去探索的人。出于我稍后会解释的原因，这项工作要求探索者切断与同类的一切心灵感应交流，因而也必须离开种族心灵赖以存在的心灵感应网络。可是，这一次种族觉醒恰恰就是为了帮助我们的工作而特意安排的，它的目的正是给我们的冒险增添力量。就我个人而言，当我先赶回家，继而驾驶飞行艇穿过高空、奔向北极时，我觉得自己仿佛已经被一团永不熄灭的火焰点燃。虽然从此以后，我必须被放逐在种族心灵那种崇高经验之外，却获得了一种新的热情与更澄澈的视野；有了它们，我在再次观察你们的世界时，便能够拥有比以往几次访问更精细的洞见。</p>
</section>

<h2>ii. MEN AND MAN</h2>
<p>WHEN AN ATTEMPT is to be made to call the Race Mind into being, all adult persons on the planet seek their allotted places near one or other of a number of great towers which are scattered throughout the continents. After a preparatory phase, in which much telepathic intercourse occurs between individuals and between the group minds of various orders which now begin to emerge, there comes a supreme moment, when, if all goes well, every man and woman in the world-wide multitude of multitudes awakes, as it were, to become the single Mind of the Race, possessing the million bodies of all men and women as a man’s mind possesses the multitude of his body’s cells.</p>
<p>Had you been privileged to watch from the summit of the great needle of masonry round which I and so many others had gathered on that morning, you would have seen far below, at the foot of the twenty-mile high architectural precipice, and stretching to the horizon in every direction, a featureless grey plain, apparently a desert. Had you then used a powerful field-glass, you would have discovered that the plain was in fact minutely stippled with microscopic dots of brown, separated by almost invisible traces of green. Had you then availed yourself of a much more powerful telescope, you would have found, with amazement, that each of these brown dots was in fact a group of persons. The whole plain would have been revealed as no mere desert, but a prodigious host of men and women, gathered into little companies between which the green grass showed. The whole texture of the earth would have appeared almost like some vegetable tissue seen through the microscope, or the cellular flesh of Leviathan. Within each cell you might have counted ninety-six granules, ninety-six minute sub-cellular organs, in fact ninety-six faces of men and women. Of these small marriage groups which are the basis of our society, I have spoken elsewhere, and shall say no more now. Suffice it that you would have found each dot to be not a face, but a group of faces. Everywhere you would have seen faces turned towards the tower, and motionless as grains of sand. Yet this host was one of thousands such, scattered over all the continents.</p>
<p>A still more powerful telescope would have shown that we were all standing, each with a doffed flying-suit hanging over an arm or shoulder. As you moved the telescope hither and thither you would have discovered our great diversity. For though we are all of one species, it is a species far more variable even than your domestic dog; and our minds are no less diverse than our bodies. Nearly all of us, you would have noted, were unclad; but a few were covered with their own velvet fur. The great majority of the Last Men, however, are hairless, their skins brown, or gold, or black, or grey, or striped, or mottled. Though you would recognize our bodies as definitely human, you would at the same time be startled by the diversity of their forms, and by their manifold aberrations from what you regard as the true human type. You would notice in our faces and in the moulding of our limbs a strong suggestion of animal forms; as though here a horse, there a tiger, and there some ancient reptile, had been inspired with a human mind in such a manner that, though now definitely man or woman, it remained reminiscent of its animal past. You would be revolted by this animal character of ours. But we, who are so securely human, need not shrink from being animal too. In you, humanity is precarious; and so, in dread and in shame, you kill the animal in you. And its slaughter poisons you.</p>
<p>Your wandering telescope would have revealed us as one and all in the late spring or full summer of life. Infants had been left behind for the day in the automatic creches; children were at large in their own houses; the youths and girls were living their wild romantic lives in the Land of the Young. And though the average age of the host gathered below you was many thousands of years, not one of its members would have appeared to you aged. Senility is unknown among us.</p>
<p>Regarding us in the mass, you would have found it hard to believe that each human atom beneath you was in reality a unique and highly developed person, who would judge the immature personality of your Own species as you judge your half-human progenitors. Careful use of the telescope, however, would have revealed the light of intense and unique consciousness in every face. For every member of this great host had his peculiar and subtle character, his rich memories, his loves and aims, and his special contribution to our incredibly complex society. Farmers and gardeners, engineers and architects, chemists and sub-atomic physicists, biologists, psychologists and eugenists, stood together with creative artists of many kinds, with astronomers, philosophers, historians, explorers of the past, teachers, professional mothers, and many more whose work has no counterpart in your world. There would be also, beside the more permanent denizens of Neptune, a sprinkling of ethereal navigators, whose vessels happened to be on the home-planet, and even a few pioneers from the settlements on Uranus and Pluto. The ethereal navigators who ply between the planets are our only transport-workers. Passenger traffic on the home-planet takes place entirely in private air-boats or flying-suits. Freight travels automatically in subterranean tubes, some of which, suitably refrigerated, take direct routes almost through the heart of the planet. Industry and commerce, such as you know, have no representatives among us, for in our world there is nothing like your industrial system. Our industry has neither operatives nor magnates. In so far as manufacture is a routine process, it is performed by machinery which needs no human influence but the pressing of a button. In so far as it involves innovation, it is the work of scientists and engineers. In so far as it involves organization, it is controlled by the professional organizers. As for commerce, we have no such thing, because we have no buying and no selling. Both production and distribution are regulated by the organizers, working under the Supreme College of Unity. This is in no sense a governing body, since its work is purely advisory; but owing to our constant telepathic intercourse, its recommendations are always sane, and always persuasive. Consequently, though it has no powers of compulsion, it is the great co-ordinator of our whole communal life. In the crowd beneath you many members of this Supreme College would be present; but nothing would distinguish them from members of the other and equally honourable professions.</p>
<p>If you had watched us for long enough, you would have noticed, after some hours of stillness, a shimmering change in the grey plain, a universal stirring, which occurred at the moment of the awakening of the Racial Mind throughout the whole population of the planet. The telescope would have revealed that all the faces, formerly placid, were suddenly illuminated with an expression of tense concentration and triumph. For now at last each one of us was in the act of emerging into the higher self-hood, to find himself the single and all-embracing mind of a world.</p>
<p>At that moment, I, Man, perceived, not merely the multitudinous several perceptions of all men and women on the face of the planet, but the single significance of all those perceptions. Through the feet of all individuals I grasped my planet, as a man may hold a ball in his hand. Possessing all the memories of all men and women, not merely as memory but as direct experience of the present. I perceived the whole biography of my generation, nay, of my species, as you perceive a melody, in flux yet all of it ‘now’. I perceived the planets whirling round the sun, and even the fixed stars creeping about the sky like insects. But also at will I perceived movements within the atoms, and counted the pulsations of light Waves. I possessed also all the emotions and desires of all men and women. I observed them inwardly, as a man may introspect his own delighting and grieving; but I observed them dispassionately. I was present in the loving of all lovers, and the adventuring of all who dare. I savoured all victories and defeats, all imaginings and reasonings. But from all these teeming experiences of my tiny members I held myself in detachment, turning my attention to a sphere remote from these, where I, Man, experienced my own grave desires and fears, and pursued my own high reasoning and contemplation of what it was that then occupied me, the Mind of the Race, it is impossible for me, the little individual who is now communicating with you, to say anything definite. For these experiences lie beyond the understanding of any individual. But this at least may be said. Waking into this lofty experience, and looking down upon my individual members as a man may regard the cells of his flesh, I was impressed far more by my littleness than by my greatness. For in relation to the whole of things I saw myself to be a very minute, simple, and helpless being, doomed to swift destruction by the operation of a stellar event whose meaning remained unintelligible to me, even upon my new and lofty plain of understanding. But even the little individual who is now communicating with you can remember that, when I, Man, faced this doom, I was in no manner dismayed by it. I accepted it with exultation, as an evident beauty within the great beauty of the whole. For my whole experience was transfused and glorified by the perception of that all-embracing and terrible beauty. Even on that loftier plane of my being I did but glimpse it; but what I glimpsed I contemplated with an insight and a rapture impossible in my lowlier mode of being.</p>
<p>Had you remained upon the tower until the following morning, you would have seen, shortly after sunrise, the whole plain stir again. We were preparing to go. Presently it would have seemed to you that the surface of the planet was detaching itself and rising, like dust on a wind-swept road, or steam from hot water, or like a valley cloud seen from a mountain-top, and visibly boiling upwards. Higher and higher it would have risen toward you, presently to resolve itself even for the naked eye into a vast smoke of individual men and women. Soon you would have seen them all around you and above you, swimming in the air with outspread arms, circling, soaring, darkening the sky. After a brief spell of random and ecstatic flight, they would have been observed streaming away in all directions to become a mere haze along the horizon. Looking down, you would now have seen that the grey plain had turned to green, fading in the distance into blues and purples.</p>
<p>The dispersal of the gatherings does not put an end to the racial experience. For an indefinite period of months or years each individual, though he goes his own way, living his own life and fulfilling his special function in the community, remains none the less possessed by the race mind. Each perceives, thinks, strives as an individual; but also he is Man, perceiving racially, and thinking in manners wholly impossible in the humbler mode of being. As each cell in a brain lives its own life, yet participates in the experience of the whole brain, so we. But after a while the great being sleeps again.</p>
<p>After the awakening which I have just described the racial mentality endured for many years; but one class of individuals had perforce to refrain from any further participation in it, namely those who were to engage upon exploration of the past. For this work it is necessary, for reasons which I shall explain later, to cut off all telepathic communication with one’s fellows, and consequently to leave the telepathic system in which the Race Mind inheres. Yet it was expressly to further our work that this particular awakening had been ordained. It was to strengthen us for our adventures. For my part, as I hastened first to my home and thence through the upper air in my flying-boat toward the Arctic, I felt that I had been kindled with an inextinguishable flame; and that though I must henceforth be exiled from the lofty experience of the Race Mind, I had acquired a new fervour and clarity of vision which would enable me to observe your world with finer insight than on my earlier visits.</p>
