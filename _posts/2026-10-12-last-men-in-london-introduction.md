<h2>导言：未来为何关切过去</h2>
<p>地球上的男人和女人们！短暂的地球人啊——你们属于那样一个瞬间：第一人类物种仿佛一朵浪，正悬在自身成就的浪峰上，下一刻便要倾覆。我是最后人类物种的一员，来自你们之后二十亿年的时代；对于你们而言，那个时代的遥远，正如地球的起源在过去中同样遥远。现在，我第二次向你们讲话。</p>
<p>在上一次通讯中，我讲述了横亘在你们的时代与我的时代之间，那浩瀚无比的事件洪流。我讲过许许多多人类种族的兴起与衰亡，讲过精神漫长的荒芜与短暂的辉煌。我讲过，人类如何一次又一次在延续亿万年的沉睡之后醒来，朦胧地看见自己本应成为什么、应当做什么；又如何据此努力掌握自己的世界、掌握自己的本性；然而每一次，不是环境，就是他自身的无知与无能，又把他抛回黑暗。我讲过他怎样与入侵者斗争，怎样被从一颗行星驱赶到另一颗行星，并为了适应每一个新世界而重新塑造自己。我不但讲了他的巨大兴衰，也讲了他在不同时代所具有的种种不同心灵形态。我讲过，最后，在幸运与娴熟控制的共同作用下，一个更加辉煌的人类终于被塑造出来——第十八人类物种，也就是我自己的物种。我尽我所能，略略暗示了人类精神这最后一次表现所具有的巨大丰富性与精微性，那种近乎完美的和谐与幸福。我还讲过，我们发现自己的美丽行星连同太阳的一切子嗣，很快就都将毁灭；也讲过我们甚至以昂扬的心情接受了这一命运。最后，我讲过迫近的终局怎样迫使我们投入那些最后的事业。</p>
<p>在这第二次通讯里，我很少会谈到我自己的世界，更少谈及横亘在我们之间的漫长年代。相反，我主要要说的是你们的世界，以及你们自己。我要试着让你们通过最后人类的眼睛，看见你们自己。我也会谈到我和我的同事，但主要只是把我们当作连接你们世界与我世界的纽带：我们是进入你们世界的先驱探险者，也是秘密居住在你们心灵中的访客。我要讲述，我们对那些在我们看来已经属于过去的时代进行这种奇异探索时所遭遇的困难与危险，还要讲我们以更加奇异的方式影响过去心灵的经历。但我最主要要谈的，仍然是生活在你们所谓基督纪元二十世纪欧洲的男人和女人，以及我们在你们世界里观察到的一场巨大危机——一个宏大的机会，而你们却悲剧性地未能抓住它。</p>
<p>若与我在上一次通讯中展开的那部长篇历史剧相比，你们小小世界里哪怕最紧迫、影响最深远的事件，似乎也都微不足道。那些足以撼动世界的人物的兴衰，你们各个民族的盛开与凋零，以及它们如植物般盲目进行的一切生存竞争；你们社会缓慢的变化和突如其来的剧烈震荡；宗教派别古老的激情，以及时髦思想转瞬即逝的更替——若放在那亿万年的历史尺度上看，这一切不过像人类这条大河上漂浮的碎屑所作的徒劳回旋。河流真正的方向，并不是由这些表面运动决定的，而是由它自身巨大水体的推动，以及河床地形的形状所决定。</p>
<p>在群星的光芒下，一支军队的失败、一场政治争论的结果、一本书的成功或失败、一场足球赛的胜负，这些渺小事件又有什么意义？在那冷峻的星光下，甚至一个物种的覆亡也算不上多么重要。而人类在踉跄犯错、朝不保夕地度过二十亿年之后迎来的最终灭绝，也不过是宇宙宏大乐章中一段短促而颤动的主题停止了奏鸣。</p>
<p>然而，渺小的事件有时会造成惊人的后果。在我从前讲述的那部宏大历史中，这一点曾一次又一次地显现。现在我要描述的一些事件，若放在人类全部历程中看，不过是转瞬即逝的细小波动；可对于你们自己来说，它们却漫长而沉重，孕育着命运。正因为这些离你们如此之近、却又如此晦暗的瞬间事件，人类的整个历程才注定成为我上一次所描述的那种令人疲惫的循环：灾难接踵而来，胜利却总是不完整。</p>
<p>不过，对这些事件的叙述虽然在某种意义上构成了本书的主要题材，却并不是本书唯一的目的，甚至也不是最重要的目的。我会说许多关于你们的卑劣，也会说许多关于你们的徒劳。但如果我说得足够好，如果被我选作传声筒的那个心灵也能充分为我服务，那么我的一切话语都应该被一种感受所点亮：尽管你们有那么多愚蠢与背叛，你们仍然拥有一种独属于你们的美。你们这个物种的全部历程是如此混乱、如此贫瘠；若以一个又一个物种的兴衰、一个又一个世界的毁灭为背景，你们任何一个个体——即使最辉煌者——的一生似乎都完全无效而无足轻重。然而，只要眼睛足够敏锐，就会看见：你们或任何其他物种中哪怕最微小的一个成员，也都具有一种独特的美；这美不只属于他的物种，也只属于他这一个个体。</p>
<p>对我们而言，人类的黎明本身便弥足珍贵。即使你们取得最高成就时，我们看待你们，也仍把你们看作黎明中的生物。对我们来说，早期人类的本性，以及每一个原始的人类个体，都有一种我们自己尽管赢得了那么多成就却已经不再拥有的美：那是生命第一次困惑地试着乘上精神之翼时的美，是孩童连同其天真、兽性与残酷共同具有的美。我们对过去的理解，比过去对自身的理解更深；我们对过去的爱，也比过去能够给予自身的爱更深。因为我们把过去放在万物的关系中去看，所以能够看见它真正的样子；因此，就连它的愚蠢与背叛，我们也能够怀着敬意观察，因为我们知道：如果我们也被安置在同样的位置，并被塑造成同样的样子，我们也会如此行事。对于过去的一切成就，无论它们多么脆弱、多么转瞬即逝，我们都怀着尊敬致意，因为我们深知，在那样的环境与那样的本性之下，哪怕取得任何一点真正的成就，都需要一种在当时近乎奇迹的信念与坚忍。于是，一种对祖先的孝敬之情驱使着我们，以尽可能精确的方式观察过去所有的人类种族，如果可能，也观察每一个个体的一生；这样，在我们自身毁灭之前，我们至少能够为这些种族加冕：承认他们虽然在成就上远不及我们，却在荣耀上与我们平等。如此，我们将为宇宙增添一种原本不会存在的美——也就是我们对你们怀有的那种既批判、又赞叹的爱。</p>
<p>然而，我们这些生活在人类黄昏中的存在，并不只是以观察者的身份关切你们这些黎明的孩子。在我从前的信息中，我讲过未来如何可能真正影响过去；讲过像我的同时代人这样的存在，因为在某种程度上获得了永恒的自由，便可能从自己在永恒中的立足点伸入过去的心灵，为那些心灵的经验添上一份力量。凡是真正永恒之物，在一切时代中都同样在场；因此，只要我们能够分享永恒，我们也就是存在于你们时代中的一种影响。我说过，我们会找出过去历史上的那些节点——在那里，为了让过去自身的本性得到实现，我们的帮助是不可缺少的——而这种“启发过去”的工作，如今已经成为我们的主要事业之一。它究竟如何可能，我会在后文更充分地解释。确实，这是一件奇异的事：我们一方面如此专注于整个种族经验的伟大冒险，又如此专注于迎接本世界即将到来的毁灭，并研究如何把生命的种子播撒到银河系遥远的区域；另一方面，我们竟然仍发现自己对那已经消逝、已经无法改变的过去负有责任。</p>
<p>我们的任何影响都不可能拯救你们这个物种免于毁灭。除非你们自身的本性发生一种深刻的变化，否则没有任何东西能够救你们；而这样的变化不可能发生。当我们行走在你们之中时，始终预先知道，你们自身的不完善会把怎样的厄运强加在你们身上。即使我们能够改变这一命运，我们也不会这样做；因为在那奇异的天体音乐中，这同样是一个必需的主题。</p>
</section>

<h2>INTRODUCTION. THE FUTURE’S CONCERN WITH THE PAST</h2>
<p>MEN AND WOMEN of Earth! Brief Terrestrials, of that moment when the First Human Species hung in the crest of its attainment, wavelike, poised for downfall, I a member of the last Human Species, address you for a second time from an age two thousand million years after your day, from an age as remotely future to you as the Earth’s beginning is remotely past.</p>
<p>In my earlier communication I told of the huge flux of events between your day and mine. I told of the rise and fall of many mankinds, of the spirit’s long desolations and brief splendours. I told how, again and again, after age-long sleep, man woke to see dimly what he should be doing with himself; how he strove accordingly to master his world and his own nature; and how, each time, circumstances or his own ignorance and impotence flung him back into darkness. I told how he struggled with invaders, and how he was driven from planet to planet, refashioning himself for each new world. I told, not only of his great vicissitudes, but also of the many and diverse modes of mind which he assumed in different epochs. I told how at length, through good fortune and skilled control, there was fashioned a more glorious mankind, the Eighteenth Human Species, my own. I hinted as best I might at the great richness and subtlety, the perfect harmony and felicity, of this last expression of the human spirit. I told of our discovery that our own fair planet must soon be destroyed with all the sun’s offspring; and of our exultant acceptance even of this doom. I told of the final endeavours which the coming end imposes on us.</p>
<p>In this my second communication I shall say little of my own world, and less of the ages that lie between us. Instead I shall speak mostly of your world and of yourselves. I shall try to show you yourselves through the eyes of the Last Men. Of myself and my fellow-workers, I shall speak, but chiefly as the link between your world and mine, as pioneering explorers in your world, and secret dwellers in your minds. I shall tell of the difficulties and dangers of our strange exploration of ages that to us are past, and of our still stranger influence upon past minds. But mostly I shall speak of men and women living in Europe in your twentieth Christian century, and of a great crisis that we observe in your world, a great opportunity which you tragically fail to grasp.</p>
<p>In relation to the long drama which I unfolded in my earlier communication it might well seem that even the most urgent and the most far-reaching events of your little sphere are utterly trivial. The rise and fall of your world-moving individuals, the flowering and withering of your nations, and all their blind, plant-like struggle for existence, the slow changes and sudden upheavals of your society, the archaic passions of your religious sects, and quick-changes of your fashionable thought, all seem, in relation to those aeons of history, no more than the ineffective gyrations of flotsam of the great river of humanity, whose direction is determined, not by any such superficial movements, but by the thrust of its own mass and the configuration of the terrain.</p>
<p>In the light of the stars what significance is there in such minute events as the defeat of an army, the issue of a political controversy, the success or failure of a book, the result of a football match? In that cold light even the downfall of a species is a matter of little importance. And the final extinction of man, after his two thousand million years of precarious blundering, is but the cessation of one brief tremulous theme in the great music of the cosmos.</p>
<p>Yet minute events have sometimes remarkable consequences. Again and again this was evident in the great story that I told. And now I am to describe events some of which, though momentary and minute in relation to the whole career of man, are yet in relation to yourselves long-drawn-out and big with destiny. In consequence of these momentary happenings, so near you, yet so obscure, man’s career is fated to he the Weary succession of disasters and incomplete victories which I described on an earlier occasion.</p>
<p>But the account of these events, though it is in some sense the main theme of this book, is not its sole, not even its chief purpose. I shall say much of your baseness, much of your futility. But all that I say, if I say it well, and if the mind that I have chosen for my mouthpiece serves me adequately, shall be kindled with a sense of that beauty which, in spite of all your follies and treasons, is yours uniquely. For though the whole career of your species is so confused and barren, and though, against the background of the rise and fall of species after species and the destruction of world after world, the life of any individual among you, even the most glorious, seems so completely ineffective and insignificant, yet, in the least member of your or any other species, there lies for the discerning eye a beauty peculiar not only to that one species but to that one individual.</p>
<p>To us the human dawn is precious for its own sake. And it is as creatures of the dawn that we regard you, even in your highest achievement. To us the early human natures and every primitive human individual have a beauty which we ourselves, in spite of all our triumphs, have not; the beauty namely of life’s first bewildered venturing upon the wings of the spirit, the beauty of the child with all its innocent brutishness and cruelty. We understand the past better than it can understand itself, and love it better than it can love itself. Seeing it in relation to all things, we see it as it is; and so we can observe even its follies and treasons with reverence, knowing that we ourselves would have behaved so, had we been so placed and so fashioned. The achievements of the past, however precarious and evanescent, we salute with respect, knowing well that to achieve anything at all in such circumstances and with such a nature entailed a faith and fortitude which in those days were miracles. We are therefore moved by filial piety to observe all the past races of men, and if possible every single individual life, with careful precision, so that, before we are destroyed, we may crown those races our equals in glory though not in achievement. Thus we shall contribute to the cosmos a beauty which it would otherwise lack, namely the critical yet admiring love which we bear toward you.</p>
<p>But it is not only as observers that we, who are of man’s evening, are concerned with you, children of the dawn. In my earlier message I told how the future might actually influence the past, how beings such as my contemporaries, who have in some degree the freedom of eternity, may from their footing in eternity, reach into past minds and contribute to their experience. For whatever is truly eternal is present equally in all times; and so we, in so far as we are capable of eternity, are influences present in your age. I said that we seek out all those points in past history where our help is entailed for the fulfilment of the past’s own nature, and that this work of inspiration has become one of our main tasks. How this can be, I shall explain more fully later. Strange it is indeed that we, who are so closely occupied with the great adventure of racial experience, so closely also with preparations to face the impending ruin of our world, and with research for dissemination of a seed of life in remote regions of the galaxy, should yet also find ourselves under obligation toward the vanished and unalterable past.</p>
<p>No influence of ours can save your species from destruction. Nothing could save it but a profound change in your own nature; and that cannot be. Wandering among you, we move always with fore-knowledge of the doom which your own imperfection imposes on you. Even if we could, we would not change it; for it is a theme required in the strange music of the spheres.</p>
