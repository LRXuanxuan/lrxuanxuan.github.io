<h2>三、世界帝国的解体</h2>
<p>终于有一天，那些神圣习俗连表面上的维持都做不到了。社会已经既缺乏劳动力，也缺乏足够的活力和智力，无法继续执行那套被神圣化、完全定型的社会职能。第一次真正严重的崩溃发生在火山能源方面。每当大型火山爆发，原本用于驯服并利用那头潜伏在地下的“泰坦”的机械系统，就很可能失灵甚至彻底毁坏。等骚乱平息之后，当地能源系统便必须重新建造，而且往往是在已经完全改变的新条件下重建。大规模喷发虽然罕见，但只要以世纪计，在每一座活火山上终究都会发生。</p>
<p>只要人类智力仍然强健，损坏就能很快修复。甚至在真正自由的智力已经灭绝很久以后，那种只能在正统观念与价值体系内部活动、受到限制的“束缚智力”，仍具有相当强的实际创造能力。当一座火山发电站被摧毁、整个火山地形也完全改变时，即便这种束缚智力仍能够重新设计发电系统。然而，当人类与生俱来的智力能力本身也开始严重衰退，当现存最优秀的头脑不但受到束缚，而且本身也十分孱弱时，这类大型工程难题便越来越少能够成功解决。最终，它们彻底变得不可解决。于是，一座又一座大型火山发电站不可避免地被废弃，世界能源供应稳步下降。</p>
<p>如果只是这样，问题或许还不致命，因为人口本身也在减少，对能源的需要同样缩小。真正灾难性的后果出现在交通与通信方面。过了一段时间，全球运输体系已经无法维持。先是各大陆之间，后来甚至同一大陆内部的不同地区之间，都逐渐无法继续按照正统制度进行传统贸易往来。这种神圣体系显而易见的崩溃，不仅造成严重经济混乱，也在人们心中引发了强烈的心理震荡。</p>
<p>顺便必须说明，到这时，用无线电控制思想与意志的体系也已经完全崩溃。那种精细的外科手术和复杂装置，早已超出晚期人类能够掌握的范围。摆脱这种暴政之后，人们重新成为独立个体——至少理论上本该如此；可群体情绪与暗示的暴政仍在支配他们。大多数时候，群体情绪和暗示仍然偏向政府；但官方叙述与人们亲眼看到的现实之间差距越来越大，有时甚至连这些已经退化的晚期群众也开始产生批判态度。因为到了最后，就连最迟钝的普通人也无法再否认一个事实：那个从小教育他们必须为之牺牲肉体与灵魂的“世界天朝帝国”，正在眼前解体。这种认识引发了一种近乎宗教性的恐惧。人们仿佛觉得，正在崩塌的并不只是国家，而是整个宇宙本身。</p>
<p>这场解体过程大概至少持续了几个世纪。在各省彼此完全隔绝、过去时代的一切清晰记忆也彻底消失以前，社会经历了一段剧烈动荡时期。这个种族一度仿佛即将从长期束缚它的神经症式迷 trance 中醒来，随时可能要求革命性的改变。然而旧文化的力量太强，而革命者又太愚蠢、太缺乏目标，最终这场危机还是被熬了过去。这个种族没有真正醒来、恢复清醒，反而像梦游者一样重新适应了新环境，同时丝毫不肯放弃自己珍爱的幻觉。</p>
<p>从一个极其复杂、联系紧密的全球经济体系，退化为一大堆彼此孤立的社会，这一转变极能说明人类当时的状态。只要地区之间还残留哪怕一点通信，人们就不可能不知道外国仍然存在，也不可能不因为帝国正在失败而感到不安。机械交通彻底崩溃后，人们还曾试图依靠帆船与商队维持联系。但这两类活动都需要早已被遗弃的技术，而那些近乎弱智的人口根本无力有效恢复它们。</p>
<p>无线电倒还在一段时间内维持了不同民族之间的联系，因为这种技术虽然相当复杂，却被传统保存了下来。然而，对各省政府而言，与外国进行无线电通信越来越令人厌恶；当然，各省全部无线电系统都掌握在政府手里。广播中的国外消息不断提醒民众：世界上还存在一个更大的外部世界，而从政府角度看，人们最好把它彻底忘掉，因为这种记忆只会激起不安和麻烦的追问。于是，各地政府一个接一个切断了同外国的一切无线电通信。从此以后，任何试图通过无线电同那些“想象出来的其他国度”取得联系的人，都会被判处死刑。这种状态持续到智力进一步恶化，最终连无线电技术本身也完全失传。</p>
<p>当同外部世界的联系彻底中断后，每个孤立民族终于可以在心理上重新调整：他们接受了这样一个虚构——自己其实就是全人类，而自己的国家便是那个世界帝国。那些关于生产与消费的神圣公式，当然已经不可能照字面执行，于是便被“象征性地解释”，被说成具有一种完全不同于原意、却能够适应缩小后的“世界帝国”生活的新含义。观察这种重新解释一步一步如何确立起来，是件颇有意思的事。</p>
<p>通信体系的缓慢崩溃，当然意味着神圣国际贸易公式遭到越来越严重的破坏。在帝国鼎盛时代，各省都高度专业化，分别负责特定的农业、采矿和制造业。早期世界政府有意鼓励这种专业化，不仅让地区专业化，也让社会阶层乃至个人专业化。凡是能够增加所有人对帝国组织和政府依赖的事情，都应该去做。任何地区都不应自给自足，任何个人都不应获得全面发展。每个人都只能是庞大机器中的一个齿轮，或巨大政治躯体中的一枚专门化细胞。</p>
<p>然而现在，通信失败迫使各民族要么彻底改变自己的经济体系，要么灭亡。这样的巨大转型当然既没有真正规划，即使偶尔有规划，也往往方向错误。智力资源的贫乏，加上传统经济制度的神圣性，使有意识的整体规划根本不可能。每个地区都不得不自行长出新的产业；可是创造能力和组织才能都极度缺乏，而且整个社会又普遍谴责新事物，因此那些先驱只能一边蹒跚摸索，一边给自己的工作披上一层厚重的伪装与自欺外衣。各省的生活水平不可避免地不断恶化。过去由机器大规模生产、像洪水般涌出的工业品，逐渐只剩下一股可怜的细流：地方工匠用手工制造最粗劣的应急替代品。这些工匠不仅天生迟钝，而且没有任何传统技术可继承，更始终被一种使人失去活力的罪恶感折磨，因为他们知道自己正在从事“不该存在”的新行业。</p>
<p>在农业地区，食物一度仍然充足，但一切舒适生活都消失了。不久以后，由于无法获得新的农业机械，耕作本身也退化成一种近乎弱智的“原始农业模仿”。在制造业地区，某些商品曾短暂出现巨量过剩，而另一些生活必需品却完全不存在；与此同时，食物变得越来越难取得。人口在长期饥饿中缓慢减少，数量出现灾难性萎缩。残余人口一代又一代，越来越多地转向极其低效的耕作。</p>
<p>在旧工业地区，工业主义的神圣传统则继续作为一种同实际生活完全脱节的宗教崇拜存在。昔日大工厂的废墟被当作神庙。每隔七天，以及在数量众多、已经神圣化的“银行假日”里，全体居民都会前往这些废墟，执行一套仪式；这些仪式其实只是古代工业技术在彻底遗忘后留下的畸变残片。人们相信，如果不一丝不苟地完成这些仪式，田地就不会结果。</p>
<p>平日里，人们带着罪恶感，用自己制作的石器或骨器抓挠土地表面；这些工具粗劣得连真正石器时代的人都会觉得羞耻。到了安息日，全体人口便共同祈求工业诸神原谅人类亵渎神圣法则的行为，不要降灾摧毁庄稼。在少数旧工业地区，仍有一两台大型机器被一个由“工程师祭司”组成的世袭阶层成功维持，并在圣日里启动。如果还能找到合适的原料，这些机器便会断断续续地生产出少量古代商品。但这些产品被视为过于神圣，绝不允许真正使用。</p>
<p>由于过去当地工业产品主要用来出口，现在这些仪式性产品只要条件允许，就会由庞大的信徒队伍抬到海边。随后，人们把它们装上一艘神圣船只，驶出海岸，直到越过地平线，再在那里举行仪式，把整船货物沉入海中。</p>
</section>

<h2>iii. DISINTEGRATION OF THE WORLD EMPIRE</h2>
<p>PRESENTLY THERE CAME a time when the sacred customs could no longer be even superficially maintained. There was neither the labour nor the degree of vigour and intelligence to maintain the sacred stereotyped functions of society. The first serious breakdown was connected with volcanic power. Whenever great volcanic eruptions occurred, the machinery for harnessing and using the submerged titan was likely to be thrown out of gear or destroyed. When the tumult had subsided the local system had to be reconstructed, probably in new conditions. Great eruptions are rare, but over the centuries they occur in every active volcano. So long as intelligence was strong, the damage was quickly repaired. Long after the extinction of the fully free intelligence the limited, bound intelligence which functioned only within the orthodox system of ideas and values was still capable of great practical inventiveness. When a volcanic power station was destroyed and the volcano changed its whole configuration, even the bound intelligence was able to reconstruct the generating system. But when the actual innate capacity for intelligence had seriously declined, when even the best surviving intelligence was not only bound but feeble, such great problems of engineering could seldom be successfully tackled. In due season they became completely insoluble. Inevitably the great volcanic power stations fell one by one into disuse. The world’s supply of power steadily diminished. Since the needs of the declining population were also shrinking, this might not have mattered, had it not been for the effect on communications. After a while it became impossible to maintain the world’s transport system. Little by little the continents, and then the regions within a single continent, failed to maintain the orthodox trade-intercourse with one another. This obvious breakdown in the sacred system caused not only grave economic disorder but also a severe psychological disturbance in men’s minds. It should be mentioned that radio-control of thought and volition had by now broken down completely. The delicate surgical operation and the delicate mechanism which it involved were far beyond the compass of latter-day man. Relieved of this tyranny, men were once more independent individuals; or at least they would have been, had not the tyranny of mob-feeling and suggestion still controlled them. Generally mob-feeling and suggestion favoured the government; but the increasing gap between the official version of events and the state of affairs that men perceived around them sometimes inclined even the degenerate latter-day mobs to criticism. For at last it became impossible even for the average dullard of the race not to recognize that the Celestial World Empire, for which he had been taught to sacrifice himself body and soul, was disintegrating. This knowledge produced a kind of religious terror. The very universe, it seemed, was crumbling about men’s heads.</p>
<p>The process of disintegration must have lasted for several centuries at least. During this period, until the isolation of the provinces was complete and all clear memory of the past age had been lost, there was a phase of violent social unrest. The race, it seemed, was on the verge of waking from the neurotic trance which had so long gripped it. It might at any moment insist on revolutionary changes. But such was the strength of the old culture, and such the stupidity and aimlessness of the revolutionaries, that the crisis was weathered. Instead of waking into sanity the rare somnambulistically adjusted itself to its new circumstances without sacrificing its cherished delusions.</p>
<p>The transition from a very complex and close-knit world-economy to a medley of isolated societies was very significant of the condition of the species. So long as some meagre communication persisted, it was impossible for people not to realize that foreign countries existed, and to be perturbed by the failure of the empire. When mechanical transport had collapsed altogether, attempts were made to maintain contact by sailing-ships and caravans. But both these occupations depended on techniques long since abandoned. The half-wit populations could not effectively recover them. The radio still for a while maintained contact between peoples, for this technique, though fairly complex, was preserved in the tradition. Radio communication with foreign lands, however, came to seem very objectionable to the provincial governments, which, of course, controlled the whole of each provincial radio system. Radio news kept reminding people of the existence of a world which, from the government’s point of view, they should forget; since the recollection of it filled them with restlessness and awkward questioning. One by one the governments therefore broke off all radio communication with foreign countries. Any attempt to make contact by radio with ‘imaginary other lands’ was henceforth punished by death. This state of affairs lasted until the final loss of radio through the further deterioration of intelligence.</p>
<p>When contact with the outside world had been completely severed, each isolated people was able to readjust itself mentally by accepting the fiction that it was in fact the whole of mankind and that its state was the world empire. The sacred formulae for production and consumption could not, of course, any longer be literally applied; but they were ‘symbolically interpreted’ to mean something very different from their original intention, something adapted to the reduced life of the ‘world empire’. It was interesting to observe the stages by which this reinterpretation established itself.</p>
<p>The slow breakdown of communications had, of course, involved a constantly increasing infringement of the sacred formulae for international trade. In the heyday of the empire the provinces had been highly specialized for particular forms of agriculture, mining, and manufacture. Specialization had been encouraged by the early world-governments, for individuals, social classes, and peoples. Everything must be done to increase dependence on the imperial organization and the government. No region must be self-sufficient, no individual a person of all-round development. No one must ever be more than a cog in the great machine or a specialized cell in the great body politic. But now the failure of communications forced the peoples to change their whole economy or be extinguished. The great change was of course unplanned or misguided. The paucity of intelligence and the sanctity of the traditional economy made conscious planning impossible. New industries had to sprout in every region; but lack of inventiveness and organizing talent, and the universal condemnation of novelty, forced the pioneers to flounder along under a heavy cloak of subterfuge and self-deception. Inevitably the standard of living in each province deteriorated. Little by little the flood of mass- produced machine-made goods gave place to a miserable trickle of the crudest hand-made makeshifts produced by local craftsmen who were hampered not only by innate obtuseness, but by lack of all traditional technique, and also by the enervating sense that their occupation was sinful.</p>
<p>In agricultural regions, though food was for a while plentiful, comfort vanished; and presently, through the failure to procure new agricultural machines, tillage itself degenerated into a kind of half- wit caricature of primitive methods. In manufacturing regions there was for a while a huge surplus of certain goods and a complete absence of others, while food became ever more difficult to obtain. Populations were slowly starved, their numbers shrinking, catastrophically. The remnant, generation by generation, turned more and more to tillage of a wretchedly inefficient type.</p>
<p>In the old industrial regions the sacred tradition of industrialism remained as a cult wholly divorced from practical life. The ruins of the great factories were treated as temples, where, once every seven days and on the many sanctified ‘bankolidays’, everyone repaired to carry out rituals which were corruptions of the forgotten techniques of the ancient industry. The fields would not bear, it was believed, unless these rituals were meticulously performed. Throughout the week men guiltily scratched the surface of the earth with home-made implements of stone or bone, implements which the ancient Stone Age men would nave been ashamed to use. On the sabbath the whole population implored the gods of industrialism to forgive men their impious infringement of the sacred law, and to refrain from blasting the fields. One or two of the great machines in some of the former industrial regions were successfully maintained by a caste of priestly engineers, and put in action on holy days. When possible, appropriate raw materials were procured for them, so that they were able to produce a small and erratic stream of the ancient goods. These were considered far too sacred to use. Since in the old days the products of the local industry had mainly been exported, these ritual products were, if possible, carried to the sea by a great procession of the faithful. They were then loaded into a sacred ship which was taken out to sea and over the horizon, there to be ceremonially sunk.</p>
