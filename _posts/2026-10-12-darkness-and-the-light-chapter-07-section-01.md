<h2>一、回到西藏革命</h2>
<p>读者，我们已经把那条悲惨故事一路追到了尽头。我们看见历史的两股巨大洪流之一，最终消失在苦难与卑贱兽性的沼泽之中。现在，我们可以回到那个我第一次发现自己关于人类历史的体验出现矛盾的节点——事实上，也正是在那里，历史洪流已经开始分叉。那就是“青年喇嘛”成功发动西藏革命的时刻。在他们领导下，新的西藏国家已经成为一个成就辉煌、而前景更加辉煌的社会。</p>
<p>我此前已经注意到，西藏人身上存在两种非常不同的精神气质。有时一种占上风，有时另一种占上风。在第一种情绪里，新社会的领导者以冷静的坚忍面对自己的任务。他们十分清楚，面对两个大帝国的敌意，除非发生奇迹，否则新的秩序几乎不可能保存下来。而在另一种情绪中，同样这些领导者虽然完全明白困难和危险，却被一种看似非理性、甚至近乎喧腾的希望托举着——不仅是希望，简直是对胜利的确定无疑。</p>
<p>他们承认，只有奇迹才能拯救西藏，也许甚至拯救整个物种；但只要这种昂扬状态还支配着他们，他们又同样知道：奇迹其实已经在他们自身内部发生了，而且也能够让它发生在整个西藏民族身上。此时，西藏人民已经对自己的领导者抱有绝对信任。甚至那些完全无法清楚理解新社会目标的迟钝者，也模糊感觉到，自己正参与一项光荣事业。</p>
<p>我经验中的第一处不一致，是一种奇怪感觉：这种近乎奇迹般的希望，仿佛既支配着整个民族的生活，又仿佛并没有支配它。随后，外部事件本身也开始出现相互矛盾的版本。我的意识被一点一点撕开，被迫同时生活在两个彼此排斥的世界里。</p>
<p>这种精神气质上的双重性，很快又发展成外部事件的双重性，并越过西藏边境显现出来。各国进步少数派都既受到、又没有受到一种崭新、反抗而欢快的信心支配。两种精神状态分别在各地造成相应行动；但希望最先真正取得胜利的地方还是西藏，而正是西藏近乎奇迹的成功鼓舞了世界其他地区。</p>
<p>西藏人取得的第一次重要胜利，同俄罗斯和中国所宣传的“合成信仰”有关。前面已经说过，这种精心设计、专门迎合人类卑劣本性的信仰，是帝国政府为了击败西藏传教士而制造出来的。在我已经讲过的那条故事线里，它成功了；而在我现在要讲的这条故事线里，它失败了。</p>
<p>处于明朗自信状态中的西藏传教士，用笑声把这场新运动笑进了失败，使帝国政府措手不及。因为一种伪造出来的信仰无法承受嘲弄。关于“痛苦宗教”的符号与口号，在每一面墙上都遭到讽刺和戏仿。苦行托钵僧组织的集会，则通过巧妙的起哄被一步一步推向滑稽戏。</p>
<p>然而事情还不止于此。许多传教士在酷刑中始终不屈，以自己的行为为信仰作证。起初，各国政府非常热衷于把他们“公开示众”，直到当局发现，每一次公开殉道反而只会传播西藏信仰。传教士既接受精神纪律训练，也接受宣传技术训练。他们的象征和口号出现在所有公共场所，而且常常直接覆盖在合成信仰的标志上。</p>
<p>苦行僧组织的宣传大会，经常会被听众中某个不起眼的人打乱。这个人会当场挑战演讲者，同他比赛谁更能经受酷刑。前面已经说过，按照合成信仰的教义，人在酷刑中理应体验到最高狂喜。挑战者会建议苦行僧，两个人现在就当众给自己造成剧烈痛苦，或者共同接受第三人的刑讯。很多时候，仅仅提出这种挑战，就足以暴露对方是骗子。</p>
<p>当然，也有一些苦行僧是特意挑选出来、并得到丰厚报酬的，因为他们特别能够忍受疼痛。当这类人真正接受酷刑来证明信仰时，很快便显出一个事实：西藏传教士似乎能够从某种雇佣殉道者无法接触的源泉中获得力量。传教士能够让自己的皮肉遭受远比对手严重的撕裂和挤压，而且从不虚假声称自己享受痛苦。他会说，自己因为有机会通过痛苦为信仰作证而感到欣慰，却绝不以痛苦本身为乐。</p>
<p>苦行僧则完全不同。他会在剧痛中夸张地宣称自己正体验狂喜，直到突然间——而且通常比对手更早——再也坚持不住，高喊要求停止。帝国政府确实一度扳回局面：它们派出一些特殊准备过的苦行僧，事先把一条手臂永久麻醉，用来应付这种必然出现的酷刑挑战。但这种把戏很快也被揭穿。</p>
<p>帝国主义者下一步，则是组织所谓“自发”的私刑，专门处死那些胆敢挑战苦行僧的人。然而这一政策同样失败了。一部分原因在于传教士的勇气，另一部分原因则在于一批受过高度训练的群众引导者。他们能够通过非常机敏的插话与干预，时常把暴民情绪从施虐冲动扭转到善意一边。</p>
<p>传教士勇气的根源，当然是他们对“精神”的信仰。但如果这种勇气没有同一种狡黠、友善的讥讽结合起来，合成信仰恐怕不会如此迅速、如此彻底地败下阵来。传教士采用的方法本身并不新鲜；然而过去从来没有人以如此大的规模、又带着如此专业的心理学理解使用这些方法。更何况，此前也从未有过这样一种情况：运用这些手段的人，是一个已经建立起来的乌托邦社会派出的使者，而这个社会正准备为自己的生存而战。</p>
<p>传教士的成功当然远不只是因为他们能够忍受痛苦。他们组成了一支庞大的“第五纵队”，散布在两个帝国领土各处，秘密把危险的政治与社会思想灌入民众心中。最初来自西藏的传教士，很快又得到各国本土传教士组成的大军增援。总人数达到数百万，每个人都像带着新火种四处行走的一颗火星。</p>
<p>在这种影响下，人们早已干枯的心灵简直像火绒一样容易点燃。大多数传教士都在社会中下层或中层从事普通职业，并刻意凭借工作效率、正直品格与忠诚的伙伴精神，赢得同事的尊敬和爱戴。依靠这种个人声望，他们能够争取所有尚未彻底堕落者的忠诚，点燃他们的想象力，并在每个国家一点一点建立起庞大的“光明仆从”群体。</p>
<p>他们的方法，结合了宗教传教士与社会革命者的两种传统。一方面，他们不强迫任何人接受特定形而上学教条，却宣讲“内在之光”，并用行动让它显现出来。另一方面，他们虽然避免过去许多革命者惯用的那种精密马基雅维利式阴谋，却会直接介入政治争论，并且往往以生命为代价宣布：现在已经到了不再把“属于恺撒的东西”交给恺撒的时候。</p>
<p>他们说，对于整个宇宙，人类所知几乎为零；但我们在自己心中能够发现，在正确的人际关系中，人可以实现自身。爱与智慧是绝对正确的；彼此尊重的个体组成的真正共同体，以及无所畏惧、自由运作的智力与想象力，也同样绝对正确。而且，我们所有人其实都知道这一点。</p>
<p>他们说，真正内在的善只有一种，也只有这一种：觉醒的生命，也就是爱与智慧的生命。这是神圣之物。宇宙中一切充分发展的存在，只要没有被蒙蔽，就必然会意欲这种生命。他们还说，在一切有意识存在的事务中，这种精神从长远来看终究是全能的；只是这个“长远”可能非常非常漫长。至于这种精神在整个存在总体中究竟占有什么地位，则没有人能够知道，也没有必要知道。</p>
<p>如果传教士不能指向西藏已经实际取得的成就，这场遍及全球的传教运动效果必然会差得多。他们会说：“在西藏，警察人数很少，而且不携带武器。在西藏，没有人需要给门上锁。在西藏，没有人需要靠纵情残酷来获得满足。我们既没有富人，也没有穷人。我们的监狱已经被拆毁，或者改造成实验室和美术馆。我们知道该怎样生活，而且我们也拥有这样生活所需的手段。”</p>
<p>来自国外的访问者在西藏受到欢迎，可以亲眼确认这些说法都是真的。终于，帝国政府采取了最严厉的措施。当局意识到，“世界屋脊”已经变成一座麦加式圣地，一切煽动者都前往那里学习并策划革命，于是禁止所有人前往西藏，并发动大规模行动，试图搜捕、消灭所有传教士。</p>
<p>然而，西藏与外界之间的往来并没有因此停止。无论限制多严，无数大胆的热情者仍设法偷偷进入这个“幸运之国”，在那里获得思想和精神上的补给，再悄悄离开，把福音传播出去。而西藏本土传教士外出的规模之所以减少，也并不是因为帝国政府成功切断了这条通道，而是因为国内此时越来越需要人手，必须组织一场绝望的军事防御。</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" class="stapledon-body" lang="en" hidden>
<h2>i. HARKING BACK TO THE TIBETAN REVOLUTION</h2>
<p>READER, WE HAVE followed the sorry tale through to its end. We have seen one of the two great streams of history lose itself in a swamp of misery and abject brutishness. We may now return to that point where I first realized an inconsistency in my experience of man’s career, where, in fact, the torrent of history was already dividing. This was the point at which the Tibetan revolution had been successfully brought off by the Young Lamas. Under their guidance the new Tibetan state was already becoming a thing of splendid achievement and more splendid promise.</p>
<p>I had already noticed among the Tibetans two very different tempers. Sometimes the one had dominated, sometimes the other. In the one mood the leaders of the new society faced their task with sober fortitude and a clear understanding that only by a miracle could they preserve the new order against the hostility of the two great empires. In the other mood these same leaders, though they fully realized the difficulties and dangers, were buoyed up by a seemingly irrational and almost boisterous hopefulness, nay a certainty of victory. Though they recognized that only a miracle could save Tibet and perhaps the whole species, they also knew, so long as the mood of exaltation was on them, that the miracle had already happened in themselves, and that it could be made to happen in the whole Tibetan people. By now the Tibetan people had supreme confidence in their leaders. Even the dullards, who could not appreciate at all clearly the aim of the new society, felt vaguely that they were sharing in a glorious enterprise.</p>
<p>The first sign of inconsistency in my experience was a strange sense that this miraculous hopefulness both dominated and did not dominate the whole life of the people. Then inconsistencies of external events began to appear, so that little by little my torn mind was forced to live in two mutually exclusive worlds.</p>
<p>This duality of temper, followed by a duality of external events, soon made itself evident beyond the frontiers of Tibet. The progressive minority in all lands was dominated and was not dominated by a new, defiant, and gay confidence. Each mood produced everywhere its effect on action; but it was in Tibet that hope first triumphed, and it was Tibet’s miraculous success that inspired the rest of the world.</p>
<p>It was in connection with the synthetic faith propagated in Russia and China that the Tibetans gained their first important success. The calculated appeal to man’s baser nature, it will be remembered, had been propagated in order to defeat the Tibetan missionaries. In the story that I have already told it succeeded; in the story that I shall now tell it failed. The Tibetan missionaries in their mood of bright confidence disconcerted the imperial governments by laughing the new movement into frustration. For a sham faith cannot stand ridicule. The symbols and slogans of the religion of pain were ridiculed and parodied on every wall. By skilful heckling the meetings organized by the dervishes were given a tilt towards farce. But this was not all. Many a missionary bore witness to his own faith by unflinching behaviour under torture. For the governments were at first eager to ‘make an exhibition’ of them, until it was clear that every public martyrdom merely spread the Tibetan faith. The missionaries were trained both in spiritual discipline and in the technique of advertisement. The symbols and slogans of their faith were made to appear in every public place, often superimposed on the emblems of the synthetic faith. The propaganda meetings organized by the dervishes were often frustrated by some obscure member of the audience who challenged the speaker to compete with him in an ordeal by torture. According to the synthetic faith, it will be remembered, the supreme ecstasy was to be experienced under torture. The challenger would suggest to the dervish that they should both, in public and at once, inflict severe pain on themselves, or be tortured by a third party. The mere challenge was often enough to expose the impostor. But when dervishes who had been specially chosen and handsomely paid for their ability to endure pain undertook to prove their faith under torture, it soon appeared that the missionaries could draw upon some source of strength inaccessible to hired martyrs. The missionary could allow his flesh to be torn or crushed to a far greater extent, and in doing so he made no false claim that he enjoyed it. Though he rejoiced in the opportunity to bear witness to his faith through pain, he took no delight, he said, in pain itself. The dervish, on the other hand, would make agonized protestations of delight, until suddenly, and sooner than his rival, he called out for release. The governments did, indeed, gain a temporary success by sending out dervishes who had been specially prepared for the inevitable ordeal by having an arm permanently anaesthetized. But it was not long before the trick was exposed. The next move by the imperialists was to organize ‘spontaneous’ lynchings of those who dared to challenge the dervishes. But this policy also was defeated, partly by the courage of the missionaries, partly by highly trained crowd-controllers who by shrewd interjections often succeeded in turning the temper of the mob from sadism to kindliness.</p>
<p>The source of the courage of the missionaries was, of course, their faith in the spirit. But courage alone might not have achieved so swift and complete a discomfiture of the synthetic faith had it not been reinforced by a sly and friendly ridicule. There was nothing new in the method of the missionaries; but never before had it been used on such a scale and with such expert psychological understanding. And never before had those who used these methods been the emissaries of an established Utopian society preparing to fight for its life.</p>
<p>The success of the missionaries certainly did not depend wholly on their powers of enduring pain. They constituted a great army of ‘fifth-columnists’ disseminated throughout the imperial territories, secretly inspiring the people with dangerous political and social thoughts. The original Tibetan missionaries were reinforced by a great company of native missionaries in every country. Altogether there were millions of them, and each one was a travelling spark of the new fire. Under this influence men’s desiccated hearts were tinder. Most of the missionaries worked at some trade in the lower or middle reaches of society, and were at pains to earn the respect and love of their fellow workers for their efficiency, integrity, and loyal comradeship. Armed with this personal prestige, they were able to capture the allegiance and fire the imagination of all who were not yet hopelessly perverted; and to build up little by little a great body of servants of the light in every land. Their method combined that of the religious missionary with that of the social revolutionary. On the one hand, though they showed no insistence on any metaphysical doctrine, they preached the inner light, and manifested it in action. On the other, though they avoided the subtle Machiavellian intrigues which had been used by so many revolutionaries in the past, they entered into political disputes and declared, often at the cost of their lives, that the time had come to withhold from Caesar the things that were Caesar’s. Of the universe, as a whole, they said, man knows next to nothing; but in our hearts we find that in right personal relation man fulfils himself. Love, they said, and wisdom are right absolutely. True community of mutually respecting individuals, and also fearless free intelligence and imagination, are right absolutely. And we all knew it. There is one intrinsic good, they said, and one only, the awakened life, the life of love and wisdom. This is the sacred thing which all developed beings throughout the universe cannot but will, unless they have been blinded. This spirit, they said, is in the long run all-powerful in the affairs of conscious beings. But the run may be very long. And what the scope of that spirit is in the whole of things no man can know, nor needs to know.</p>
<p>The world-wide missionary effort would have been far less effective if the missionaries had not been able to point to the example of Tibet’s actual achievement. ‘In Tibet the police are few and unarmed,’ they said. ‘In Tibet no doors need be locked. In Tibet no one feels any need of the debauch of cruelty. We have neither rich nor poor. Our prisons have been destroyed or turned into laboratories and art galleries. We know how to live, and we have the means.’ Visitors to Tibet were welcomed and could see for themselves that these claims were true. At last the imperial governments adopted drastic measures. Realizing that ‘the roof of the world’ was becoming a Mecca where the seditious gathered to study and plan revolution, they forbade all travel to Tibet, and made a great effort to round up and destroy all the missionaries. But intercourse with Tibet continued. In spite of all restrictions, hosts of daring enthusiasts managed to slip through into ‘the fortunate country’ for mental and spiritual fortification; and to slip out again to spread the gospel. And the stream of native Tibetan missionaries was restricted not by the imperial attempt to put an end to it but by the needs of the home country to organize a desperate military defence.</p>
