<h3>四、让人适应行星</h3>
<p>现在该从另一个角度看整个问题了。如果山不来就穆罕默德，那穆罕默德就去找山。如果其他行星无法适应人类现有的形态，也许人类可以改变自己，以适应这些陌生世界的异质环境。或者更准确地说，把改造环境与改造人类结合起来，也许能使我们最充分地利用这些世界。事实上，只要生物学知识和优生技术足够先进，就可能培育出新的人类类型，让他们居住在其他行星上。</p>
<p>再次看来，火星最有希望。要培育一种能够承受经改善后火星环境严酷条件的智人变种，应该相对容易。也许最适合作为起点的人类种群是西藏人，因为他们习惯寒冷、干燥的气候和稀薄空气。不过，除非火星大气能大幅增厚、地表温度也能显著提高，否则这种专门化的“火星人类”大概会缺乏建立高度文明所需的生命活力。只有当自然以某种丰饶程度繁盛起来时，人类精神本身才容易繁盛。不过，通过环境改造和优生改造相结合，长远看来也许能够在火星建立一个充满活力的人口。</p>
<p>在金星上，只要解决氧气和水的问题，人类的生化与优生技术也许可以制造出适应当地环境的人类变种，甚至一种新的人类物种。由于金星人必须承受高温，试验也许可以从我们物种中某些赤道地区的人群开始。想必在那个炎热世界里，深色皮肤会很有用；除非整个行星都长期笼罩在云层形成的阴影中。毫无疑问，那里相当大比例的水会倾向于永久悬浮在大气里。</p>
<p>在外行星上，优生学恐怕必须发挥主要作用。即使大气与极端低温的问题能够解决，仍然会面对巨大困难。为了应付过强重力，必须创造一种身材极矮小、经过专门适应的人类物种。但这很可能导致大脑皮层体积严重缩小，从而降低智力。因为智力似乎取决于神经系统中高级细胞的实际数量；或者说，取决于“电话交换机”的复杂程度。因此，一个体型极小的哺乳动物不可能获得人类级智力，除非能找到一种办法，在不损害效率的前提下大幅缩小单个细胞以及神经纤维的粗细。还有另一种可以设想的出路：对现有人体进行极其激进的优生改造，使现有人脑在超强重力下仍能得到支撑。可以让人重新采取四足姿势，大幅强化四条腿，同时把头部向后移，使其重量在前后肢之间均匀分布。但手怎么办？前肢会完全用于承重，无法操作工具。我唯一能提出的建议，是把鼻子大幅延长成象鼻，并配上类似手指的精细抓握器官。最好有两条，甚至三条象鼻。顺便说一句，眼睛还得从向后移的大脑颅腔明显向前突出，否则这种“木星人”（Homo jovianus）连自己踩在哪里都看不见。</p>
<p>以上已经足以说明：如果地球人继续通过原子能发展对物理环境的控制，同时获得足够的生物学知识和优生技术，能够培育或以其他方式构造适应陌生环境的人类或准人类种族，那么至少某些行星的殖民，终有一天可能成为实际可行之事。</p>
<p>不过必须提醒一句。任何这类优生实验，在我们尚未彻底了解每一种拟议改变可能产生的间接后果之前，都绝不应该实施。例如，如果只追求极矮身材，却不设法避免智力下降，那将是一场灾难。此外，我们还必须非常清楚：哪些人类特征无关紧要，可以安全牺牲；哪些则不可缺少，绝不能冒险损害。良好视觉、高智力、合作能力和手工操作能力都是不可缺少的，如果可能还应该增强；牙齿和头发则在必要时可以牺牲。若轻率地在人性上“乱动手脚”，结果可能是人类在心理与精神上的毁灭。</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" lang="en" hidden>
<h3><span id="a1086"></span>IV. Adapting Man to the Planets</h3>
<p><span class="t28">I</span><span class="t31">T</span><span class="t30"> </span><span class="t31">IS</span><span class="t30"> </span><span class="t31">TIME</span><span class="t30"> </span>to approach the whole matter from another angle. If the mountain will not come to Mohamet, Mohamet must go to the mountain. If the planets are unadaptable to man in his present form, perhaps man might adapt himself to the alien environments of those strange worlds. Or rather, perhaps a combination of the two processes might enable man to make the best possible use of those worlds. In fact, given sufficient biological knowledge and eugenical technique, it might be possible to breed new human types of men to people the planets.</p>
<p>Once more, Mars seems to offer the best opportunity. It should be fairly easy to produce a variety of <span class="t24">homo sapiens</span> capable of surviving the rigours of an improved Martian environment. Perhaps the best human stock from which to start would be the Tibetans, who are used to a cold, arid climate and a rarified atmosphere. But unless the Martian atmosphere could be augmented quite a lot, and the surface temperature greatly raised, the specialized human Martians would probably lack the vital energy for any kind of highly developed civilization. Only where nature blossoms with a certain luxuriance can the human spirit itself blossom. However, by a combination of environmental and eugenical alteration, it might perhaps be possible in the long run to establish a vigorous population on Mars.</p>
<p>On Venus, given oxygen and water, man’s biochemical and eugenical technique might perhaps produce a well-adapted human variety or a new human species. Since Venerian man would have to stand great heat, the work might start with experiments on some Equatorial varieties of our species. Presumably in that hot world a dark skin would be useful; unless, indeed, permanent shade was maintained by a cloud-blanket over the whole planet. There would certainly be a tendency for a large proportion of the planet’s water to remain permanently suspended in the atmosphere.</p>
<p>On the outer planets, eugenics would have to play a major part. Even if the problems of the atmosphere and the extreme cold could be solved, there would be very great difficulties to face. It would be necessary to create a specially adapted human species of very small stature to cope with the excess of gravitation. This might well involve a serious reduction in the size of the cerebral cortices, with a consequent reduction of intelligence. For intelligence seems to depend on the actual number of top-level cells in the nervous system; or, as it were, on the complexity of the telephone exchange. Thus the attainment of human intelligence in a very small mammal would be impossible, unless some way could be found for greatly reducing the size of the individual cells and the thickness of the fibres without impairing efficiency. There is one other conceivable way out of the difficulty. By very drastic eugenical operation on the existing human form, it might be possible to enable the present human brain to be supported, in spite of excessive gravitation, by throwing man into the quadruped position, greatly strengthening the four legs, and at the same time pushing the head far backwards so as to distribute its weight evenly between the fore and hind legs. But what of the problem of providing hands? The fore-limbs would be fully occupied and unavailable for manipulation. My only suggestion is that the nose might be greatly elongated into a trunk, equipped with delicate grasping instruments like fingers. It would probably be desirable to have two trunks, if not three. The eyes, by the way, would have to be projected well forward from the thrown-back brain-pan, otherwise <span class="t24">Homo Jovianus</span> would not be able to see where he was stepping.</p>
<p>Enough has been said to suggest that the colonization of some of the planets may in time become practicable, if terrestrial man continues to develop his control of the physical environment through atomic power, and if he attains sufficient biological knowledge and eugenical art to breed, or otherwise construct, human or quasi-human races adapted to strange environments.</p>
<p>But a word of caution is necessary. It is extremely important that none of these eugenical ventures should be attempted without thorough knowledge of the probable indirect results of each proposed change. For instance, it would be disastrous to aim at very small stature without doing something to avoid reduction in intelligence. Further, it is necessary to have very clear ideas as to which human characteristics are unimportant and might be safely sacrificed, and which are indispensable and should never be endangered. Thus good vision, high intelligence, co-operativeness and manual dexterity are indispensable and should if possible be increased; while teeth and cranial hair could if necessary be sacrificed. The result of thoughtless “messing about” with human nature might be the psychological and spiritual ruin of man.</p>
