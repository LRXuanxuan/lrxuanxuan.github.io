<h2>第二十四章</h2>
<p>呼——！咔嚓！砰！</p>
<p>现在我们到了某个地方，可究竟是哪里、何时、哪一个？卡莫迪当然不知道。他发现自己身处一座极有说服力的城市，怎么看都很像纽约。</p>
<p>很像。</p>
<p>可真的是吗？</p>
<p>“这是纽约吗？”卡莫迪自言自语。</p>
<p>“我他妈怎么知道？”一个声音立刻回答。</p>
<p>“那是个反问句。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“这个我当然知道；不过，我有正式修辞学资格证，所以还是回答了。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪四下看了看，发现声音来自自己左手握着的一把黑色大雨伞。</p>
<p>他问：“你是我的奖品？”</p>
<p>“那当然。”奖品说。“我看起来总不至于像一匹设得兰矮种马吧？”</p>
<p>“我刚才在那座模范城市的时候，你跑哪儿去了？”</p>
<p>“我去休了一个短暂而且完全应得的假期。”奖品说。“你抱怨也没用。银河联合奖品工会和获奖者联盟之间的合同里，明确规定了休假时间。”</p>
<p>“我没抱怨。”卡莫迪说。“我只是……算了。这里确实很像我的地球。事实上，很像纽约。”</p>
<p>他身处一座城市。人流和车流都十分密集。剧院很多，热狗摊很多，人更多。许多商店都宣布自己即将倒闭，因此不计成本清仓出售全部库存。霓虹灯到处闪烁。餐馆也很多，其中最显眼的几家分别叫“西部人”“南部人”“东部人”和“北部人”，每家都推出牛排配细薯条的特价套餐。此外还有“东北风人”“西南风人”“东偏东北人”和“西偏西北人”。</p>
<p>街对面一家电影院正在上映《次经》——宣传语是“比《圣经》更宏大、更离奇”——演员阵容号称数千人。旁边是“世界肚脐”迪斯科舞厅，里面有一支自称“狗屎乐队”的民谣摇滚组合正在制造震耳欲聋的音乐，一群尚未成熟的处女穿着露出腰腹的裙子随之狂舞。</p>
<p>“够热闹。”卡莫迪舔了舔嘴唇。</p>
<p>“我只听见收银机叮当作响。”奖品用一种道德优越感极强的口气说。</p>
<p>“别这么古板。”卡莫迪说。“我觉得我到家了。”</p>
<p>“但愿不是。”奖品说。“这地方让我神经紧张。请你仔细看看，确认一下。记住，相似并不等于完全相同。”</p>
<p>他面前正好有一个 IRT 地铁入口。卡莫迪看见自己位于第五十街与百老汇交口。</p>
<p>没错，他回家了。</p>
<p>他快步走向地铁，沿台阶下去。眼前的一切既熟悉、又令人兴奋，同时还带着一种奇怪的悲伤。大理石墙壁上湿淋淋地渗着黏液，一列闪闪发亮的单轨列车从一条隧道驶出来，又消失进另一条隧道……</p>
<p>“哦。”卡莫迪低声说。</p>
<p>“怎么了？”奖品问。</p>
<p>“没什么。”卡莫迪说。“我转念一想，还是在街上散散步吧。”</p>
<p>他开始原路返回，悄悄朝入口处那块被框出的蓝天走去。可一群人已经聚在那里，挡住了去路。卡莫迪往前挤，人群却把他推了回来。</p>
<p>地铁潮湿的墙壁开始颤动，随后有节奏地抽搐。那条闪亮的单轨列车从支架上挣脱下来，像一条黄铜做成的舌头一样向后卷曲，猛地朝他弹来。</p>
<p>卡莫迪转身狂奔，把挡路的人一个个撞翻。他隐约注意到，那些人一倒地就立刻重新弹回站姿，仿佛脚底装了加重底座。</p>
<p>脚下的大理石地面开始变软，变得像糖浆一样黏稠。他的脚越陷越深。那些人形东西已经围到身边，而单轨列车则悬在他头顶上方。</p>
<p>卡莫迪大喊：“西斯赖特！把我弄出去！”</p>
<p>“我也要！”奖品大喊。</p>
<p>“我也要！”狡猾的捕食者尖叫道——因为这正是它本人，绝无其他。它巧妙地伪装成了一座地铁站，而卡莫迪已经自己走进了它的嘴里。</p>
<p>什么也没发生。</p>
<p>卡莫迪突然产生一种极其可怕的念头：也许西斯赖特正好出去吃午饭了，或者正在上厕所，又或者在接电话。</p>
<p>出口处那块蓝色天空越来越小，因为出口正在自行封闭。周围的人影逐渐失去人类外形。墙壁变成紫红色，开始鼓动、颤抖，随后向内收缩。细长的单轨列车饥饿地缠上卡莫迪的双脚。</p>
<p>捕食者体内先响起巨大的呜鸣，随后是一阵持续不断的流涎声。</p>
<p>（众所周知，以卡莫迪为食的动物都非常贪吃，而且毫无餐桌礼仪。）</p>
<p>“救命！”卡莫迪尖叫。消化液已经开始腐蚀他的鞋底。“西斯赖特，救我！”</p>
<p>“救救他，救救他！”奖品抽泣着说。“或者，如果救他实在太困难，就救我！把我弄出去，我一定在各大报纸刊登广告，召集委员会，组织行动团体，上街举牌，确保卡莫迪绝不会白白牺牲！而且我进一步郑重承诺——”</p>
<p>“别胡扯了。”一个声音说道。</p>
<p>卡莫迪认出那是西斯赖特。</p>
<p>“太丢脸了。至于您，卡莫迪先生，以后请务必在踏进捕食者嘴里<strong>之前</strong>就作出判断。我的办公室并不是专门用来进行千钧一发式救援的。”</p>
<p>“可这一次您还是会救我，对吧？”卡莫迪哀求。“会吧？会吧？”</p>
<p>“已经救完了。”西斯赖特说。</p>
<p>卡莫迪环顾四周，发现事实确实如此。</p>
</section>
<section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="en" lang="en" aria-label="English original" hidden>
<p><b>CHAPTER 24</b></p>
<p>Whoosh! Krrrunch! Kerpow! Here we are somewhere, but who knows where and when and which? Surely not Carmody, who found himself in a persuasive city much like New York. <i>Much</i> like; but was it?</p>
<p>‘<i>Is</i> this New York?’ Carmody asked himself.</p>
<p>‘How the hell should I know?’ a voice answered promptly.</p>
<p>‘It was a rhetorical question,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘I am quite aware of that; but, since I have my rhetorician’s papers, I answered it.’</p>
<p>Carmody looked around and saw that the voice had come from a large black umbrella in his left hand. He asked, ‘Are you my Prize?’</p>
<p>‘Well, of course I am,’ the Prize said. ‘I don’t suppose I look like a Shetland pony, do I?’</p>
<p>‘Where were you earlier, when I was in that model city?’</p>
<p>‘I was taking a short, well-earned vacation,’ the Prize said. ‘And there’s no use your complaining about it. Vacation time is stipulated in the contract between the Amalgamated Prizes of the Galaxy and the Recipient’s League.’</p>
<p>‘I wasn’t complaining,’ Carmody said. ‘I just … Forget it. This place certainly looks like my Earth. It looks like New York, in fact.’</p>
<p>He was in a city. There was heavy traffic, both human and vehicular. There were many theatres, many frankfurter stands, many people. There were many stores which proclaimed that they were going out of business and selling their entire stock regardless of cost. Neon signs flashed everywhere. There were many restaurants, most prominent of which were The Westerner, The Southerner, The Easterner, and The Northerner; all of these had specials on steak and shoe-string potatoes. But there was also The Nor’easterner, The Sou’wester, The East-by-Northeast, and the West-by-Northwest. A movie theatre across the street was featuring <i>The Apocrypha</i> (Bigger and Stranger than The Bible), with a cast of thousands. Near it was the Omphalos Discothèque wherein a folk-rock group calling itself. The Shits played raucous music, which was danced to by immature virgins in middleless dresses.</p>
<p>‘Lots of action,’ Carmody said, wetting his lips.</p>
<p>‘I hear only the jangle of cash registers,’ the Prize said, in a heavily moralistic voice.</p>
<p>‘Don’t be stuffy,’ Carmody said. ‘I think I’m home.’</p>
<p>‘I hope not,’ the Prize said. ‘This place gets on my nerves. Please look around you and make sure. Remember, similitude need not imply exactitude.’</p>
<p>There was an IRT subway entrance in front of him. He saw that he was at Fiftieth, Street and Broadway. Yes, he was home. He walked briskly to the subway and went down the stairs. It was familiar, exciting and saddening all at the same time. The marble walls were damp with ichor, and the gleaming monorail came out of one tunnel and disappeared into another …</p>
<p>‘Oh,’ Carmody muttered.</p>
<p>‘How’s that?’ the Prize asked.</p>
<p>‘Never mind,’ Carmody said. ‘On second thoughts, I think I’ll take a little stroll in the streets.’ He began to retrace his footsteps, moving quietly towards the rectangle of sky framed in the entrance. A crowd had formed, blocking his way. Carmody pushed through them, and the crowd pushed him back. The wet walls of the subway began to tremble, then to convulse rhythmically. The gleaming monorail pulled free of its stanchions, curled back on itself like a brazen tongue, and flicked out towards him. Carmody ran, bowling over the people who stood in his way. He was dimly aware that they rolled immediately to their feet, as though they had weighted bases. The marble pavement beneath him grew soft, syrupy. His feet were sticking, the figures were close around him, and the monorail was poised over his head.</p>
<p>Carmody shouted, ‘Seethwright! Get me out of this!’</p>
<p>‘Me, too!’ the Prize shouted.</p>
<p>‘Me, too!’ screamed the cunning predator; for it was he and none other, cleverly disguised as a subway, into whose mouth Carmody had strayed.</p>
<p>Nothing happened. Carmody had the terrible feeling that Seethwright was perhaps out to lunch, or on the toilet, or answering a telephone. The blue rectangle of sky became smaller as the exit sealed itself. The figures around him lost their resemblance to humans. The walls turned a purplish-red, began to heave and tremble, then to contract. The slender monorail curled hungrily around Carmody’s feet. Within the predator’s body, vast ululations were followed by protracted slaverings. (Carmody-eaters are notoriously piggish and lacking in any table manners.)</p>
<p>‘Help!’ Carmody screamed, as digestive juices ate away the soles of his shoes. ‘Seethwright, help me!’</p>
<p>‘Help him, help him!’ the Prize sobbed. ‘Or, if that seems too difficult, help me! Get me out of here and I will take advertisements in the leading newspapers, convene committees, form action groups, carry posters on the streets, all to the purpose of insuring that Carmody does not go unavenged. And I further pledge myself to –’</p>
<p>‘Stop babbling,’ a voice said, which Carmody recognized as belonging to Seethwright. ‘It’s disgraceful. As for you, Mr Carmody, you must, in future, make up your mind <i>before</i> stepping into the mouth of your predator. My office is not set up for hair’s-breadth rescues.’</p>
<p>‘But you will save me this time, won’t you?’ Carmody begged. ‘Won’t you? Won’t you?’</p>
<p>‘It is already done,’ Seethwright said. And when Carmody looked around, he saw that it was indeed already done.</p>
</section>
