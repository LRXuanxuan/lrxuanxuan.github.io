<h3>一、引言</h3>
<p>怀着几分踌躇，我站在这个高度专业化的学会面前讲话。我的感觉，大概就像一个人仅仅因为曾经写过一本关于魔毯的儿童故事，便答应到飞机设计师协会去谈航空事业的未来一样。这个真正具有划时代意义的学会，其《学报》作者们的科学知识以及他们那一大堆数学公式，实在令我望而生畏！我还是立刻把底牌摊开吧：我的科学训练，在我没能通过“伦敦大学入学考试”里当年所谓“有机化学”那一科时，就算到头了；而我的数学知识，比我的科学知识还要零碎得多。我在许多领域都浅尝辄止，却没有一个领域称得上专家。既然如此，我今晚唯一可能发挥的作用，便只能是从其他知识领域投来一点光，照一照行星际旅行这件事。</p>
<p>现代文明离不开形形色色的专家。在大多数领域，业余爱好者的时代已经过去。但正因为我们这个时代尤其是一个专家的时代，我们必须面对一种严重危险：人类也许会变成一个完全由专家构成的物种，每个专家都不明白自己的同行在做什么，也不明白他们为什么这样做，同时所有人又都不了解完整的人类生活究竟呈现怎样的图景。知识已经庞大到这样的程度：除了自己那个小小的专门角落，任何单个头脑都无权以权威口吻发言。然而，科学专家身份享有如此高的声望，以至于科学家很容易对超出自己专业能力范围的问题作出影响深远的断言，例如政治、伦理、宗教和哲学。我们时代最严重的问题之一，就是要设计出一种教育方法：既能在每个领域培养真正的专家，又能保证每一位专家同时成为一个具有相当知识、能够负责任的民主社会公民。即使做到了这一点，仍然需要某些人承担另一项工作：把思想各个正在生长的前沿彼此联系起来。这本来被认为是哲学家的任务，至少是某一类哲学家的任务。他们必须从各主要领域的专家那里学到一点东西，并把一个领域的发现同另一个领域的发现联系起来。这确实是一项可怕的工作；如果这些到处闯入别人领地的人不能同时具有智慧与谦逊，他们大概只会把事情弄得一团糟，并遭到各领域专家的一致痛骂。</p>
<p>因此，我今天敢于对诸位讲话，心情中既有谦卑，也有那种“愚人闯入天使都不敢涉足之地”的莽撞。我唯一可能做点有用事情的方式，就是试着说明：在我看来，诸位大胆而高度专门化的行星际旅行事业，怎样嵌入人类面对这个令人敬畏的宇宙时所进行的整体事业之中。</p>
<p>我要从一个假设开始，而贵会的工作已经充分证明这个假设并非无稽之谈。我假设：如果人类一切顺利，那么再过几十年，人类事实上便能够抵达其他行星，并在其表面着陆。当然，这个假设最后也可能证明并不成立。也许会出现意料之外的困难。或者另一方面，人类本身未必能“一切顺利”。人类的愚蠢完全可能导致文明毁灭、我们的物种灭绝，甚至地球上一切生命的灭亡。甚至猜想人类会把整个地球炸碎，使它化为一群新的小行星，也并非完全荒诞。掌握原子能之后，人类笨拙地抓住了一件威力无法估量的工具；它既可以造福，也可以作恶。J. B. S. 霍尔丹曾提出，这也许是一条自然法则：任何一个在统一全球社会之前便获得原子能的物种，都必然毁灭自己。就我而言，我还要补上一句：不仅是“在它统一全球社会之前”，而是“在它使自己服从真正的价值之前”。</p>
<p>在我们物种的全部历史中，大概从未出现过像今天这样重大的危机。据说，我们这个人类物种大约开始于二十五万至五十万年前。在这段时期的大部分时间里，人类生命这条河流都缓慢地蜿蜒穿过时间的平原。假定这段时期人类一代的平均长度约为二十五年，那么自智人出现以来，大概已经过去两万代。几乎在整个漫长时期内——它也许只相当于地球形成以来岁月的四千分之一——人类拥有的力量很小，人类事务的变化也极其轻微。部落当然会兴衰。技术改进很少被发现；而即使发现，也常常失传，经过漫长年代以后再度被发现。一个人童年时的生活条件，通常到他老年时仍大体相同。然而，这种缓慢而曲折的生产技术进步终于发展出了农业，后来又出现城市和文明。这似乎发生在大约六千年前，也就是二百四十多代以前。人类生命的河流于是稍稍加速，开始穿过急流。力量、闲暇和舒适增加了，但几乎总是只属于少数人。大众似乎反而越来越深地陷入奴役。直到这六千年快要结束的时候，也就是大约三百年前、十二代人以前，现代科学才开始真正作用于人类生活。因此，人类只在自己全部历史的最后千分之一阶段使用科学，而整个人类历史又只占地球形成以来时间的约四千分之一。到了我们这个时代，人类已经伸手抓向原子能。我们是原子时代的第一代人。变化已经比过去任何时候都迅速，很快还会以或善或恶的方式达到灾变般的速度。人类生命之河已经来到悬崖边缘。瀑布飞坠——将落向何方？完全有可能，我们就是全部人类世代中的最后一代。然而，如果没有意外，地球还应当能保持宜居，时间大概与它已经存在的时间一样长，也就是约二十亿年，相当于我们今天人类的八千万代。</p>
<p>人类未来似乎有三种可能：（1）迅速而彻底地灭绝；（2）建立一个覆盖全球的极权“蚁巢国家”，以原子能为基础，把所有人类个体都降格为机器人；（3）建立一种全新的人类世界，在那里，科学这盏阿拉丁神灯将被明智地使用，而不是继续交给那种目光短浅的愚蠢与赤裸裸权力欲的混合物——迄今为止，正是它在人类使用科学时扮演了如此悲剧性的角色。“人类获得了力量，却没有获得智慧”已经是一句陈词滥调。如果在最后一刻，或者说在零点到来前半分钟，人类仍不能稍微少愚蠢一点，那么它的命运就已经注定。反过来说，只要稍有一点智慧，我们便能够大幅改善一切人的生活条件，不论他们肤色如何，并给予每个人机会，使其能够发展和表达自己所具有的真正人类生活能力，以及在人类伟大共同事业中从事真正人类工作的能力。至于这种事业究竟是什么、应该是什么，我后面还会讨论。</p>
<p>给予所有人充分机会，如今已经不再只是乌托邦梦想。它至少在物质条件上已经可以逐步实现。阻挡道路的，只剩人类的无知、愚蠢与恶意。我还要立刻说明一点，后面我会再展开：这里的承诺绝不只是更多奢侈和猪一般的安逸。真正的承诺，是人类经验的深化与丰富。今天，人类持续遭受挫折、折磨和恐惧，所以大多数人只能用“奢侈”和“安全”来想象希望。然而，如果我们终于成功转过眼下这个危险的弯道，一个摆脱了致残性条件的新世代出现，那么人类的心灵终于可以真正开始欲求比奢侈和安全更多的东西。还要确信，那个有希望的世界并不会缺乏挑战、危险，甚至悲剧。它的新颖之处，只在于人类终于摆脱了婴儿期的疾病，第一次能够进入更危险、更烦恼，却也更广阔、更丰富、更清醒的青春期生活。</p>
</section>

<h3><span id="a1083"></span>I. Introduction</h3>
<p><span class="t28">I</span><span class="t31">T</span><span class="t30"> </span><span class="t31">IS</span><span class="t30"> </span><span class="t31">WITH</span><span class="t30"> </span>some hesitation that I address this very expert Society. I feel much as a man might feel who, merely because he once wrote a children’s story about a magic carpet, has undertaken to discourse to a society of aeroplane designers about the future of aviation. Contributors to the <span class="t24">Journal</span> of this truly epoch-making Society overawe me with their scientific knowledge and their wealth of mathematical formulae! Let me at once put my cards on the table by confessing that my training in science ended when I failed to pass the “London Matric” in what used to be called “organic chemistry,” and that my knowledge of mathematics is far more sketchy even than my knowledge of the sciences. I am a dabbler in many subjects, an expert in none. As such, my only function to-night must be to bring to bear on interplanetary travel light from other fields of knowledge.</p>
<p>Modern civilization cannot get along without experts of many kinds. In most fields the day of the amateur is past. But just because this is pre-eminently an age of experts, we have to face the serious danger that the human race may come to consist wholly of experts none of whom understands what his fellows are doing, or why they are doing it, and all of whom are ignorant of the pattern of human life as a whole. Knowledge has become so vast that no single mind can speak with authority save in relation to his own particular corner of it. But such is the prestige of scientific expertism that scientists are apt to make far-reaching pronouncements about matters lying beyond their special competence, for instance about politics, ethics, religion and philosophy. One of the most serious problems of our day is to work out an educational technique which, while producing real experts in each field, will ensure that each one of them will also be a reasonably well informed and responsible democratic citizen. Even when this end has been achieved, it will still be necessary for some people to tackle the work of correlating the various growing points of thought. This is supposed to be the task of philosophers, or of one particular sort of philosophers. They have to learn something from all the main kinds of experts, and to relate the findings in each field to the findings in others. This is indeed a formidable task; and unless these universal intruders combine intelligence with humility, they are likely to make a mess of it, and to be reviled by the experts in every field.</p>
<p>It is therefore with a combination of humility and the hardihood that leads fools to rush in where angels fear to tread that I venture to talk to you at all. The only useful thing I can do is to try to show you how, as it seems to me, your bold and highly specialized venture of interplanetary travel fits into the total venture of man in this formidable universe.</p>
<p>I start with an assumption amply justified by the Society’s work. I assume that, if all goes well with man, men will, in fact, be able to reach the other planets within a few decades, and able to effect landings on them. This assumption may, of course, turn out to be unjustified. Unexpected difficulties may arise. Or, on the other hand, all may <span class="t24">not</span> go well with man. His folly may quite well lead to the destruction of civilization, to the extermination of his species, and even to the extinction of all terrestrial life. It is not entirely fantastic to surmise that he may even blow up his planet, and reduce it to a new swarm of asteroids. In gaining control of atomic energy, man clumsily grasps an instrument of incalculable potency, both for good and for evil. J. B. S. Haldane has suggested that it may be a law of nature that any species that gains atomic power before unifying its world-society must destroy itself. I should myself say, not merely “before it unifies its world-society,” but “before it disciplines itself to the true values.”</p>
<p>Probably there has never before, in the whole career of our species, been so momentous a crisis, as that of our day. Our own human species is said to have begun, about a quarter to half a million years ago. For most of this period the river of human life has wandered sluggishly through the plains of time. If we consider the average length of a human generation over this period to be about twenty-five years, then some twenty thousand generations have passed since <span class="t24">Homo Sapiens</span> began. During almost the whole of that vast period (perhaps a four-thousandth part of the aeon that has passed since the earth’s formation) men had little power, and change in human affairs was very slight. Tribes, no doubt, rose and fell. Improvements in technique were rarely discovered; and discovered only to be lost again, and re-discovered ages afterwards. Conditions in a man’s childhood were generally much the same as conditions in his old age. But at last this slow and tortuous advance in the techniques of production achieved agriculture, and later the building of cities, the founding of civilization. This seems to have occurred some six thousand years ago, or a little over two hundred and forty generations. The river of human life accelerated somewhat, tumbling forward in rapids. Power and leisure and comfort increased; but almost always only for the few. The masses seem to have been more and more deeply enslaved. Not till almost the end of this six thousand years, say three hundred years ago or about twelve generations, did modern science begin to take effect on human life. Thus man has used science only during the last thousandth part of his whole career, and his career is the last four-thousandth of the period since the earth was formed. In our own day, man is snatching at atomic power. We are the first generation of the atomic age. Change is already far more rapid than ever before, and will soon become catastrophic, for good or ill. The river of human life has reached a precipice. The cataract plunges — whither? It is quite possible that we may be the last of all human generations. Yet, barring accidents, the earth should be habitable for a period as long as it has yet existed, say two thousand million years, or some eighty million of our present human generations.</p>
<p>There seem to be three possible futures for man: (1) actual and speedy annihilation; (2) the creation of a world-wide totalitarian ant-state, based on atomic power and the reduction of all human individuals to robots; (3) the founding of a new kind of human world, in which the Aladdin’s lamp of science will be used wisely, instead of being abandoned to that blend of short-sighted stupidity and downright power-lust that has played so tragic a part in the application of science thus far. It is a platitude that man has gained power without wisdom. If he does not at the eleventh hour, or half a minute before zero hour, become just a little less silly, his doom is sealed. On the other hand, given a modicum of wisdom, we shall be able greatly to raise the conditions of life for all human beings, no matter what their colour, and to afford to every one of them the chance to develop and express such capacity as he has for truly human living and truly human work in the great common enterprise of man. What that enterprise is or should be, I shall consider later.</p>
<p>This possibility of affording to all men full opportunity is now no merely Utopian dream. Its progressive attainment is at last physically possible. Nothing now stands in the way but the ignorance, the stupidity and the evil will of men. And let me say at once, though I shall later enlarge on this point, that the promise is not simply one of increased luxury and hoggish ease. Rather it is a promise of a deepening and enriching of human experience. Man’s present condition of constant frustration and torment and fear makes most men to-day think only in terms of the hope of luxury and security. But if ever we do successfully turn our present dangerous corner, and a generation appears that is freed from crippling conditions, then men’s minds will at last be able effectively to desire something more than mere luxury and security. Be sure, also, that in the promised world there will be no lack of challenge and danger and even tragedy. The novelty will be rather that man will have outgrown at last the diseases of mere infancy, and will be able to enter for the first time into the more dangerous, more troubled, but ampler and richer and more conscious life of adolescence.</p>
