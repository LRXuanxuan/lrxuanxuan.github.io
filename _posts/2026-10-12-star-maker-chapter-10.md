<p>在我们看来，这个银河系中无数世界的苦难似乎终于结束了。支持银河乌托邦的意志已经成为普遍共识，未来似乎必将一个荣耀接着一个荣耀。我们也确信，其他银河系中同样会出现这种进步。带着一种天真的乐观，我们期待奋斗着的精神能够迅速、彻底而最终地在整个宇宙中取得胜利。我们甚至设想，造星主也会因为自己的作品终于趋于完美而感到喜悦。为了用还能掌握的象征勉强表达不可言说之物，我们想象：在一切开始以前，造星主独自存在；为了爱，也为了共同体，他决定创造一个完美的生命，让她成为自己的伴侣。我们想象，他用自己对美的饥渴和爱之意志塑造她；但在塑造过程中，他也鞭笞她、折磨她，好让她最终能够战胜一切逆境，并正因为战胜这些逆境而获得一种连全能的造星主自身都永远无法达到的完善。我们把整个宇宙想象成这个生命。而在我们的天真想象中，我们似乎已经亲眼见证了宇宙成长的大半历程；剩下的只有这场成长的高潮：所有银河系通过心灵感应结合为一体，成为宇宙那唯一的、完全觉醒的精神，达到完善，永远值得造星主凝视与欣赏。</p>
<p>这一切在我们看来庄严而正确。然而，我们自己却并没有因此感到喜悦。银河系后期连续不断、节节胜利的进步景象已经使我们感到饱足，我们也不再对其他无数银河系抱有多少好奇。它们十有八九和我们自己的银河系大同小异。事实上，我们已经极度疲惫，也极度幻灭。在如此漫长的亿万年里，我们一直追随无数世界的命运。它们的激情对它们自己而言总是新鲜的，可对我们来说大多只是一再重复。我们分享了各种各样的痛苦，也分享了各种各样的荣耀和耻辱。而现在，当宇宙的理想——精神的完全觉醒——仿佛已经近在眼前时，我们却发现自己对此多少有些厌倦了。整个庞大而复杂的存在戏剧，究竟有没有被那个完善精神彻底认识、细细品味，又有什么关系？我们自己是否完成这场朝圣，又有什么关系？</p>
<p>在如此漫长的亿万年里，我们这支分散在整个银河系中的队伍，一直艰难维持着一个统一的共同心灵。无论何时，尽管“我们”作为个体彼此分离，实际上始终又是一个“我”——那个观察无数世界的单一观察者；但如今，就连维持这种身份本身也逐渐变成苦役。“我”被浓重睡意压倒；而分散存在的“我们”则渴望各自小小的故乡世界，渴望自己的家、自己的巢穴，也渴望重新拥有那种动物般迟钝的感觉，让它把我们隔绝在一切无限之外。尤其是我，这个英国人，渴望能够安全地睡在那间房里——她和我曾一起睡过的房间；白日的一切紧迫事务全都消失不见，剩下的只有睡眠，以及我们彼此对对方那种模糊、安宁的知觉。</p>
<p>然而，虽然我已经疲惫到无法忍受，睡眠却始终不来。我不得不继续和同伴们待在一起，也继续与那无数凯旋的世界同在。渐渐地，一个发现把我们从昏沉中唤醒。我们逐步看清，这些无数乌托邦世界系统真正占据内心的情绪，其实和“胜利”非常不同。在每一个世界中，我们都发现一种深刻信念：无论有限生命已经崇高到何种程度，它终究仍然渺小而无力。有一个世界里生活着某种诗人。我们向他讲述自己关于宇宙目标的设想，他回答说：“等宇宙苏醒时——如果她真能苏醒——她会发现自己并不是创造者唯一钟爱的伴侣，而只是一颗小小气泡，漂浮在没有边界、也没有底的存在之海上。”</p>
<p>起初，在我们眼中，那是一群神一般的世界精神势不可挡地前进：它们掌握宇宙的一切资源，前方又仿佛拥有整个永恒。然而现在，这一景象逐渐显露出完全不同的面貌。心智能力的巨大提升，以及遍及整个宇宙的共同心灵，使时间经验本身发生了改变。心灵所能把握的时间跨度大幅扩展。对觉醒世界而言，一个亿万年的时代只像挤满事件的一天。它们对时间流逝的感受，就像一个坐在独木舟里的人看着一条河：上游水流迟缓，随后进入急流，速度越来越快，直到不远的前方，它必然冲下一道最终瀑布，汇入大海——也就是生命永恒的终点，恒星全部熄灭。它们把所剩无几的喘息时间，与自己热切希望完成的伟大事业——让宇宙精神完全觉醒——相比较，于是发现，即使按最乐观估计也没有一刻可以浪费；而更可能的情况是，完成这项工作其实早已太迟。它们隐约有一种奇怪预感：某种无法预见的灾难正在等待自己。有时人们会说：“我们连恒星将为我们准备什么都不知道，更不用说造星主了。”有时又会说：“即使是我们掌握得最牢固的关于存在的知识，也绝不应该有一刻被当作真理。那不过是我们自己的视觉，把自己的颜色涂在存在之海某一道泡沫中某一颗气泡薄膜上，而我们只看见了那些颜色。”一切受造物以及它们的一切成就都注定不完整；正是这种感觉，反而赋予银河世界共同体一种短命而娇嫩花朵般的魅力与神圣。我们自己如今也越来越怀着一种美丽却岌岌可危的感觉来看待这个遍布银河的乌托邦。就在这种心境中，我们经历了一件非同寻常的事。</p>
<p>我们暂时停止探索，算是给自己放了一个假，希望在没有肉体束缚的太空飞行中恢复精神。我们把分散在各个世界中的全部成员重新聚拢，让自己集中成一个可以移动的单一视点；然后作为一个生命，我们在恒星与星云之间滑行、盘旋。不久，我们忽然起意，要一头冲进外部空间。我们不断加速，直到前方恒星变成紫色，后方恒星变成红色；又继续加速，直到前后星光都消失不见；最后，我们狂暴的速度把一切可见景象都抹去。在绝对黑暗中，我们沉思各个银河系的起源与命运，也沉思整个宇宙与那些微小家居生活之间令人震骇的反差——而我们如今又如此渴望回到那些生活中。</p>
<p>不久，我们停了下来。就在停止运动时，我们发现自己所处的情形并不像预料中那样。从中飞出的银河系确实远远落在身后，如今看起来不过像一团巨大的云；可它并没有呈现本应具有的清晰旋涡结构。经过一阵困惑，我们才意识到，自己正在看银河系存在的一个非常早期阶段——事实上，是它还不能真正称作“银河系”的年代。因为眼前这团云并不是恒星构成的云，而是一片连续的发光薄雾。它的核心只有朦胧的明亮，向外柔和地渐暗，最后在没有任何清晰边界的地方融入黑色天空。就连天空本身也完全陌生。那里虽然没有恒星，却密密麻麻分布着大量苍白云团。它们看起来都比我们刚离开的那一团更遥远，但其中好几团在天空中占据的范围仍像地球天空中的猎户座一样巨大。天空拥挤到这种程度，以至于许多巨大云团的薄纱状边缘彼此连成一片；另一些则只被狭窄的虚空水道分开，而透过这些空隙，还能看到更远处一层又一层星云，其中有些遥远到只剩一个光点。</p>
<p>很明显，我们已经沿时间向后旅行，来到了那些巨大星云彼此仍是近邻的时代；当时宇宙的爆炸性扩张还只刚刚把它们从原初那连续、拥挤的物质中分离出来。</p>
<p>我们观察时，很快便发现眼前事件正以不可思议的速度展开。每一团云都明显在收缩，同时向远方退去；它的形状也不断变化。每一个朦胧球体都稍稍变扁，并变得越来越清晰。由于退远而不断缩小，星云如今呈现为透镜形雾团，以各种不同角度倾斜在空间中。然而，就在我们注视之际，它们已经退到如此深远的宇宙背景中，以至于很难再观察其中变化。只有我们自己的故乡星云仍留在身边，像一个巨大椭圆横跨半边天空。于是，我们把注意力集中到它身上。</p>
<p>星云内部开始出现差异：有些区域的薄雾更明亮，有些更暗；还有浅淡条纹和旋涡，仿佛海浪上的泡沫。这些幽影般的结构缓慢移动，就像山坡上的一缕缕云。很快便可以看出，星云内部的物质流总体上服从某种共同模式。这个巨大的气体世界事实上正缓慢旋转，几乎像一场龙卷风；随着旋转，它还在继续变扁。如今它仿佛一块布满纹路而扁平的石片——正适合在水面打漂——却又离眼睛太近，近到无法聚焦，整个形象都显得模糊。不久，凭借我们这种新奇而近乎神迹般的视觉，我们注意到整片云团中这里那里开始出现显微尺度的、更强烈的光点，尤其集中在外层区域。我们继续观看，光点越来越多，而它们之间的空间越来越暗。恒星就这样诞生了。</p>
<p>巨大的云团仍在旋转，仍在变扁。很快，它已经成为一个由旋转恒星流和未凝聚气体细丝组成的圆盘；那些气体是原初星云最后正在瓦解的组织。这些气体仍依靠自身半独立的运动在整体中流动，不断改变形状，像活物一样爬行，伸出伪足，也像云朵一样清晰可见地逐渐消散；然而就在它们消散之处，一代又一代新恒星取而代之。星云核心此时正在凝缩成体积更小、边界也更清楚的一团，那是一个巨大而拥挤的光辉球体。圆盘中这里那里出现的光结与光团，则是胚胎中的星团。整个星云撒满这些蓟绒般的小球、羽毛般轻盈闪亮的仙境装饰；而事实上，每一个这样的微小光团内部都孕育着一个由恒星组成的小宇宙。</p>
<p>如今，它终于已经可以称作银河系。它仍以一种催眠般稳定不变的节奏清晰地旋转。纠结的恒星流像长发一样铺展在黑暗中。现在，它像一顶巨大、宽边的白色墨西哥草帽：帽冠是一团发光物质，帽檐则是一片薄纱般的恒星平面。它又像一顶正在旋转的枢机主教帽，帽沿上两条长长旋动的流苏，就是两道漫长的螺旋恒星流。它们磨损般散开的末端已经脱离主体，成为次银河系，围绕主要银河系统运行。整个结构像陀螺一样摇摆；当它在我们面前倾斜时，帽檐看起来变成越来越窄的椭圆，直到最终侧面朝向我们。此时，银河最外缘由不发光物质构成的一圈，在内部明亮的星云和恒星物质前方形成一条细薄、漆黑而布满结块的线。我们凝神窥视，竭力想看清这个闪烁、珍珠母般奇观的具体结构——它是整个宇宙中最大的一类物体。我们发现，这种新视觉虽然能够同时容纳整个银河系以及遥远的其他银河，却也能把每一颗恒星看成一个微小圆盘；而每颗恒星与最近邻之间的距离，就像北冰洋上的一只软木塞与南极洋上的另一只软木塞之间一样巨大。因此，尽管银河整体具有星云般、乳光般的美丽形态，它在我们眼中同时也是一片巨大的虚空，其中只有极其稀疏的微小闪光。</p>
<p>我们更仔细地观察恒星，发现它们虽然成群结队地流动，像一群群鱼，但不同恒星流有时会彼此穿透。此时，不同星流中的恒星仿佛会在轨迹交叉时彼此牵引，在从一个邻居的引力影响进入另一个邻居的影响范围过程中，划出巨大而舒展的曲线。</p>
<p>因此，尽管恒星彼此相隔极端遥远，它们看起来却常常奇异地像一些微小活物，从远处感知着彼此。有时两颗恒星会沿双曲线轨道相互绕过，然后各自远去；较少的时候，它们则会结合成双星。</p>
<p>时间在我们眼前流逝得如此迅速，亿万年的漫长时代被压缩进片刻之间。我们已经看见最早的恒星从星云组织中凝聚出来，形成红色巨星；虽然从遥远视角看，它们仍小得无法想象。令人惊讶的是，其中相当大一部分，也许因为自身旋转产生的离心作用，会裂成两个部分，形成双星，于是天空中这种彼此共舞的成对恒星越来越多。与此同时，巨星缓慢收缩，亮度不断升高。它们从红色变成黄色，再变成耀眼的白色和蓝色。新的年轻巨星在它们周围继续凝聚，而它们自己则进一步缩小，颜色再次转成黄色，最后变成阴燃般的暗红。不久，我们看见最年长的恒星一颗接一颗地熄灭，就像火堆里飞出的火星。这样的死亡出现得越来越频繁，增长缓慢，却始终稳定。有时，一颗“新星”突然闪耀又逐渐暗淡，在短短一刻里压过周围数不清的邻星；这里那里还有“变星”以我们无法想象的速度脉动。偶尔，我们会看见一对双星和第三颗恒星彼此靠得极近，其中一颗向伴星伸出一条由自身物质构成的细丝。我们竭尽这种超自然视觉，看见那些物质丝断裂，再凝聚成行星。于是，我们不禁敬畏：在没有生命的恒星大军之中，这些生命种子竟如此微小，也如此罕见。</p>
<p>然而，恒星本身却给人一种无法抗拒的生命感。真奇怪：这些纯粹的物理之物，这些不过是火球的东西，只是按照最微小粒子的几何规律旋转、旅行，竟会显得如此有生命，如此像是在探索和追寻。不过，整个银河系本身也同样充满生命感，同样像一个有机体：它那精细的恒星流纹路，仿佛活细胞内部的流动；向外舒展的旋臂几乎像触须；中央还有发光的核心。这样一个巨大而美丽的生物，难道不应该是活着的吗？难道它不应该能够以智慧体验自己，也体验自身之外的事物吗？</p>
<p>在这股狂想的潮流中，我们强迫自己停下来，提醒自己：生命只能在那些名为行星的罕见尘粒上站稳脚跟；眼前这一切躁动不息的珠宝般恒星，不过是白白燃烧的火。</p>
<p>带着越来越强烈的亲切与渴望，我们把注意力更加细致地投向最早的那些行星尘粒。它们从旋转火焰形成的细丝中凝聚出来，起初只是旋转、脉动的熔融液滴，随后表面结出岩壳，覆盖海洋薄膜，又披上大气。我们锐利的视线看见它们浅浅的水域中开始发酵出生命，生命很快扩散到海洋和大陆。我们看到少数早期世界苏醒到相当于人类的智慧层次；几乎紧接着，它们便陷入那场争取精神觉醒的巨大斗争，而最终能够胜出的世界更加稀少。</p>
<p>与此同时，新的行星仍不断诞生。虽然它们在恒星之间极为罕见，但全部加起来仍有成千上万颗；一个个新世界、一篇篇新的生命传记由此启程。我们看见另一个地球，看见它反复出现的荣耀与耻辱，直到最终窒息死亡。我们看见许多其他类人世界：棘皮世界、半人马世界等等。我们也看见人类在自己小小的地球上笨拙前进，在漫长历史中一次次从迟钝进入清醒，又再次跌入可怜的迟钝。一个时代接着一个时代，他的身体形态像云一样不断变化。我们观看他与火星入侵者进行绝望斗争；接着，一段在我们看来只是片刻、对他而言却包含许多个黑暗和光明时代的时间过去，我们又看见他因为恐惧月球坠毁，被迫离开地球，迁往不适宜居住的金星。再后来，一个在宇宙寿命中不过一声叹息的漫长时代过去，他为了躲避爆炸的太阳又逃往海王星，在那里重新退化为纯粹动物，继续度过漫长岁月。但此后他又一次向上攀升，终于达到最辉煌的智慧，却又在无法抗拒的大灾难中像飞蛾扑火一样被彻底烧毁。</p>
<p>这一整段漫长的人类历史，对亲历者而言充满最激烈的激情与悲剧；可在银河系的生命中，它却只是一次无足轻重、仿佛毫无结果、几乎可以忽略的努力，只持续了几个片刻。当它结束时，无数行星系统仍继续存在；这里只损失了一个，那里又损失一个；恒星之间这里又有一颗新行星诞生，那里又发生一场新的灾难。</p>
<p>在人类这段纷乱生命之前和之后，我们看见几十、几百种其他类人种族兴起；其中只有屈指可数的极少数注定能够苏醒到超过人类精神最高界限的层次，并在银河世界共同体中发挥作用。我们如今从远处看见它们生活在一颗颗微小的类地行星上，散落在巨大漂移的恒星流之间，努力应对那些社会与精神层面的世界性难题——而我们“现代”时代的人类才第一次真正开始面对这些问题。同样，我们再次看见各种其他种族：鹦鹉螺类、海底种族、鸟类、复合生命，以及罕见的共生种族和更加罕见的植物式生命。无论哪一种，能够成功走到乌托邦并参加世界共同伟业的都只有极少数——如果真有的话。其余全都倒在途中。</p>
<p>从遥远的观察点上，我们如今看见其中一个孤立次银河系里，共生文明取得了胜利。真正的世界共同体胚芽终于在那里出现。不久，这座岛宇宙中的恒星开始被一圈圈活着的珍珠环绕，直到整个次银河系都因无数世界而获得生命。与此同时，在银河主体中，那种嚣张而具有传染性的帝国疯狂兴起——此前我们已经近距离观察过它。可过去看起来像泰坦巨神之间战争的景象——伟大世界以无法想象的速度在太空中调动，以大屠杀摧毁彼此人口——如今从远处看来，却只是几个显微尺度火花的抽搐，是几只发光小动物在毫不关心它们的恒星大军包围下胡乱活动。</p>
<p>然而很快，我们看见一颗恒星突然炽烈爆发，把自己的行星全部摧毁。帝国杀死了比自己高贵得多的生命。第二次屠杀发生，第三次也随之而来。然后，在次银河系的影响下，帝国的疯狂逐渐消退，帝国本身也瓦解了。不久，我们疲惫的注意力再次被银河系中乌托邦势不可挡的到来吸引。对我们而言，这一变化最明显的标志就是人造行星稳定增加。一颗恒星接着一颗恒星开出花朵，一层又一层拥挤轨道上布满这些充满生命的珠宝、这些孕育精神的花朵。一个星座接着一个星座，整个银河系逐渐清楚地因无数世界而获得生命。每一个世界都由自身独特而数量庞大的敏感智慧个体居住，这些个体结合在真正的共同体中；而整个世界本身也成为一个活着的东西，拥有共同精神。每一套包含许多人口密集轨道的恒星系统，又形成一个共同体生命。整个银河系则被编织进统一的心灵感应网络，成为一个单一、智慧而热烈的生命——成为它那无数、多样而短暂个体共同拥有的精神与“我”。这个庞大共同体如今把目光投向自身之外的其他银河系。它决心在宇宙这一最广阔领域中继续生命与精神的冒险，于是不断通过心灵感应与其他银河系交流；与此同时，它形成各种奇异的实际抱负，开始以此前从未想象过的规模利用恒星能源。如今，每个恒星系统不仅都被一层捕获光能的薄纱包围，把原本逃逸的太阳能聚焦起来供智慧生命使用，以至于整个银河系都变得暗淡；许多不适合充当太阳的恒星甚至被直接分解，其庞大的亚原子能储藏被彻底开采。</p>
<p>突然，一个即使从远处看也明显与乌托邦格格不入的事件牢牢抓住我们的注意。一颗被许多行星环绕的恒星发生爆炸，摧毁了所有世界轨道环；爆炸之后，它自己也陷入苍白而疲惫的状态。随后又有第二颗、第三颗，还有银河系不同区域更多恒星发生同样的事。</p>
<p>为了查明这些惊人灾难的原因，我们再次通过一次意志行动把共同心灵分散开来，各自回到无数世界中的观察位置。</p>
</section>

<h3>X. A VISION OF THE GALAXY</h3>
<p>IT SEEMED TO us now that the troubles of the many worlds of this galaxy were at last over, that the will to support the galactic utopia was now universal, and that the future must bring glory after glory. We felt assured of the same progress in other galaxies. In our simplicity we looked forward to the speedy, the complete and final, triumph of the striving spirit throughout the cosmos. We even conceived that the Star Maker rejoiced in the perfection of his work. Using such symbols as we could to express the inexpressible, we imagined that, before the beginning, the Star Maker was alone, and that for love and for community he resolved to make a perfect creature, to be his mate. We imagined that he made her of his hunger for beauty and his will for love; but that he also scourged her in the making, and tormented her, so that she might at last triumph over all adversity, and thereby achieve such perfection as he in his almightiness could never attain. The cosmos we conceived to be that creature. And it seemed to us in our simplicity that we had already witnessed the greater part of cosmical growth, and that there remained only the climax of that growth, the telepathic union of all the galaxies to become the single, fully awakened spirit of the cosmos, perfect, fit to be eternally contemplated and enjoyed by the Star Maker.</p>
<p>All this seemed to us majestically right. Yet we ourselves had no joy in it. We had been sated with the spectacle of continuous and triumphant progress in the latter age of our galaxy, and we were no longer curious about the host of the other galaxies. Almost certainly they were much like our own. We were, in fact, overwhelmingly fatigued and disillusioned. During so many aeons we had followed the fortunes of the many worlds. So often we had lived out their passions, novel to them, but to us for the most part repetitive. We had shared all kinds of sufferings, all kinds of glories and shames. And now that the cosmical ideal, the full awakening of the spirit, seemed on the point of attainment, we found ourselves a little tired of it. What matter whether the whole huge drama of existence should be intricately known and relished by the perfected spirit or not? What matter whether we ourselves should complete our pilgrimage or not?</p>
<p>During so many aeons our company, distributed throughout the galaxy, had with difficulty maintained its single communal mentality. At all times ‘we’, in spite of our severalty, were in fact ‘I’, the single observer of the many worlds; but the maintaining of this identity was itself becoming a toil. ‘I’ was overpowered with sleepiness; ‘we’, severally, longed for our little native worlds, our homes, our lairs; and for the animal obtuseness that had walled us in from all the immensities. In particular, I, the Englishman, longed to be sleeping safely in that room where she and I had slept together, the day’s urgencies all blotted out, and nothing left but sleep and the shadowy, the peaceful awareness in each of the other.</p>
<p>But though I was fatigued beyond endurance, sleep would not come. I remained perforce with my colleagues, and with the many triumphant worlds.</p>
<p>Slowly we were roused from our drowsiness by a discovery. It gradually appeared to us that the prevailing mood of these countless utopian systems of worlds was at heart very different from that of triumph. In every world we found a deep conviction of the littleness and impotence of all finite beings, no matter how exalted. In a certain world there was a kind of poet. When we told him our conception of the cosmical goal, he said, ‘When the cosmos wakes, if ever she does, she will find herself not the single beloved of her maker, but merely a little bubble adrift on the boundless and bottomless ocean of being.’</p>
<p>What had seemed to us at first the irresistible march of god-like world-spirits, with all the resources of the universe in their hands and all eternity before them, was now gradually revealed in very different guise. The great advance in mental calibre, and the attainment of communal mentality throughout the cosmos, had brought a change in the experience of time. The temporal reach of the mind had been very greatly extended. The awakened worlds experienced an aeon as a mere crowded day. They were aware of time’s passage as a man in a canoe might have cognizance of a river which in its upper reaches is sluggish but subsequently breaks into rapids and becomes swifter and swifter, till, at no great distance ahead, it must plunge in a final cataract down to the sea, namely to the eternal end of life, the extinction of the stars. Comparing the little respite that remained with the great work which they passionately desired to accomplish, namely the full awakening of the cosmical spirit, they saw that at best there was no time to spare, and that, more probably, it was already too late to accomplish the task. They had a strange foreboding that unforeseen disaster lay in store for them. It was sometimes said, ‘We know not what the stars, even, have in store for us, still less what the Star Maker.’ And it was sometimes said, ‘We should not for a moment consider even our best-established knowledge of existence as true. It is awareness only of the colours that our own vision paints on the film of one bubble in one strand of foam on the ocean of being.’</p>
<p>The sense of the fated incompleteness of all creatures and of all their achievements gave to the Galactic Society of Worlds a charm, a sanctity, as of some short-lived and delicate flower. And it was with an increasing sense of precarious beauty that we ourselves were now learning to regard the far-flung utopia. In this mood we had a remarkable experience.</p>
<p>We had embarked upon a sort of holiday from exploration, seeking the refreshment of disembodied flight in space. Gathering our whole company together out of all the worlds, we centred ourselves into a single mobile view-point; and then, as one being, we glided and circled among the stars and nebulae. Presently the whim took us to plunge into outer space. We hastened till the forward stars turned violet, the hinder red; till both forward and hinder vanished; till all visible features were extinguished by the wild speed of our flight. In absolute darkness we brooded on the origin and the destiny of the galaxies, and on the appalling contrast between the cosmos and our minute home-lives to which we longed to return.</p>
<p>Presently we came to rest. In doing so we discovered that our situation was not such as we expected. The galaxy whence we had emerged did indeed lie far behind us, no bigger than a great cloud; but it was not the featured spiral that it should have been. After some confusion of mind we realized that we were looking at the galaxy in an early stage of its existence, in fact at a time before it was really a galaxy at all. For the cloud was no cloud of stars, but a continuous mist of light. At its heart was a vague brilliance, which faded softly into the dim outer regions and merged without perceptible boundary into the black sky. Even the sky itself was quite unfamiliar. Though empty of stars, it was densely peopled with a great number of pale clouds. All seemingly were farther from us than that from which we had come, but several bulked as largely as Orion in the Earth’s sky. So congested was the heaven that many of the great objects were continuous with one another in their filmy extremities, and many were separated only by mere channels of emptiness, through which loomed vistas of more remote nebulae, some of them so distant as to be mere spots of light.</p>
<p>It was clear that we had travelled back through time to a date when the great nebulae were still near neighbours to one another, before the explosive nature of the cosmos had done more than separate them out from the continuous and congested primal substance.</p>
<p>As we watched, it became obvious that events were unfolding before us with fantastic speed. Each cloud visibly shrank, withdrawing into the distance. It also changed its shape. Each vague orb flattened somewhat, and became more definite. Receding and therefore diminishing, the nebulae now appeared as lens-shaped mists, tilted at all angles. But, even as we watched, they withdrew themselves so far into the depth of space that it became difficult to observe their changes. Only our own native nebula remained beside us, a huge oval stretching across half the sky. On this we now concentrated our attention.</p>
<p>Differences began to appear within it, regions of brighter and of less bright mist, faint streaks and swirls, like the foam on the sea’s waves. These shadowy features slowly moved, as wisps of cloud move on the hills. Presently it was clear that the internal currents of the nebula were on the whole set in a common pattern. The great world of gas was in fact slowly rotating, almost as a tornado. As it rotated it continued to flatten. It was now like some blurred image of a streaked and flattish pebble, handy for ‘ducks and drakes’, held too near the eye to be focused.</p>
<p>Presently we noticed, with our novel and miraculous vision, that microscopic points of intenser light were appearing here and there throughout the cloud, but mainly in its outer regions. As we watched, their number grew, and the spaces between them grew dark. Thus were the stars born.</p>
<p>The great cloud still span and flattened. It was soon a disc of whirling star-streams and strands of uncondensed gas, the last disintegrating tissues of the primal nebula. These continued to move within the whole by their own semi-independent activity, changing their shapes, creeping like living things, extending pseudopodia, and visibly fading as clouds fade; but giving place to new generations of stars. The heart of the nebula was now condensing into a smaller bulk, more clearly defined. It was a huge, congested globe of brilliance. Here and there throughout the disc knots and lumps of light were the embryonic star-clusters. The whole nebula was strewn with these balls of thistle-down, these feathery, sparkling, fairy decorations, each one in fact pregnant with a small universe of stars.</p>
<p>The galaxy, for such it could now be named, continued visibly to whirl with hypnotic constancy. Its tangled tresses of star-streams were spread abroad on the darkness. Now it was like a huge broad-brimmed white sombrero, the crown a glowing mass, the brim a filmy expanse of stars. It was a cardinal’s hat, spinning. The two long whirling tassels on the brim were two long spiralling star-streams. Their frayed extremities had broken away and become sub-galaxies, revolving about the main galactic system. The whole, like a spinning top, swayed; and, as it tilted before us, the brim appeared as an ever narrower ellipse, till presently it was edge-on, and the outermost fringe of it, composed of non-luminous matter, formed a thin, dark, knotted line across the glowing inner substance of nebula and stars.</p>
<p>Peering, straining to see more precisely the texture of this shimmering and nacrous wonder, this largest of all the kinds of objects in the cosmos, we found that our new vision, even while embracing the whole galaxy and the distant galaxies, apprehended each single star as a tiny disc separated from its nearest neighbours much as a cork on the Arctic Ocean would be separated from another cork on the Antarctic. Thus, in spite of the nebulous and opalescent beauty of its general form, the galaxy also appeared to us as a void sprinkled with very sparse scintillations.</p>
<p>Observing the stars more closely, we saw that while they streamed along in companies like shoals of fishes, their currents sometimes interpenetrated. Then seemingly the stars of the different streams, crossing one another’s paths, pulled at one another, moving in great sweeping curves as they passed from one neighbour’s influence to another. Thus, in spite of their remoteness each from each, the stars often looked curiously like minute living creatures taking cognizance of one another from afar. Sometimes they swung hyperbolically round one another and away, or, more rarely, united to form binaries.</p>
<p>So rapidly did time pass before us that aeons were packed into moments. We had seen the first stars condense from the nebular tissue as ruddy giants, though in the remote view inconceivably minute. A surprising number of these, perhaps through the centrifugal force of their rotation, were burst asunder to form binaries, so that, increasingly, the heaven was peopled by these waltzing pairs. Meanwhile, the giant stars slowly shrank and gathered brightness. They passed from red to yellow, and on to dazzling white and blue. While other young giants condensed around them, they shrank still further, and their colour changed once more to yellow and to smouldering red. Presently we saw the eldest of the stars one by one extinguished like sparks from a fire. The incidence of this mortality increased, slowly but steadily. Sometimes a ‘nova’ flashed out and faded, outshining for a moment all its myriad neighbours. Here and there a ‘variable’ pulsated with inconceivable rapidity. Now and again we saw a binary and a third star approach one another so closely that one or other of the group reached out a filament of its substance toward its partner. Straining our supernatural vision, we saw these filaments break and condense into planets. And we were awed by the infinitesimal size and the rarity of these seeds of life among the lifeless host of the stars.</p>
<p>But the stars themselves gave an irresistible impression of vitality. Strange that the movements of these merely physical things, these mere fire-balls, whirling and travelling according to the geometrical laws of their minutest particles, should seem so vital, so questing. But then the whole galaxy was itself so vital, so like an organism, with its delicate tracery of star-streams, like the streams within a living cell; and its extended wreaths, almost like feelers; and its nucleus of light. Surely this great and lovely creature must be alive, must have intelligent experience of itself and of things other than it.</p>
<p>In the tide of these wild thoughts we checked our fancy, remembering that only on the rare grains called planets can life gain foothold, and that all this wealth of restless jewels was but a waste of fire.</p>
<p>With rising affection and longing we directed our attention more minutely toward the earliest planetary grains as they condensed out of the whirling filaments of flame, to become at first molten drops that span and pulsated, then grew rock-encrusted, ocean-filmed, and swathed in atmosphere. Our piercing sight observed their shallow waters ferment with life, which soon spread into their oceans and continents. A few of these early worlds we saw waken to intelligence of human rank; and very soon these were in the throes of the great struggle for the spirit, from which still fewer emerged victorious.</p>
<p>Meanwhile new planetary births, rare among the stars, yet, in all, thousands upon thousands, had launched new worlds and new biographies. We saw the Other Earth, with its recurrent glories and shames, and its final suffocation. We saw the many other humanesque worlds, Echinoderm, Centaurian, and so on. We saw Man on his little Earth blunder through many alternating phases of dullness and lucidity, and again abject dullness. From epoch to epoch his bodily shape changed as a cloud changes. We watched him in his desperate struggle with Martian invaders; and then, after a moment that included further ages of darkness and of light, we saw him driven, by dread of the moon’s downfall, away to inhospitable Venus. Later still, after an aeon that was a mere sigh in the lifetime of the cosmos, he fled before the exploding sun to Neptune, there to sink back into mere animality for further aeons again. But then he climbed once more and reached his finest intelligence, only to be burnt up like a moth in a flame by irresistible catastrophe.</p>
<p>All this long human story, most passionate and tragic in the living, was but an unimportant, a seemingly barren and negligible effort, lasting only for a few moments in the life of the galaxy. When it was over, the host of the planetary systems still lived on, with here and there a casualty, and here and there among the stars a new planetary birth, and here and there a fresh disaster.</p>
<p>Before and after man’s troubled life we saw other humanesque races rise in scores and hundreds, of which a mere handful was destined to waken beyond man’s highest spiritual range, to play a part in the galactic community of worlds. These we now saw from afar on their little Earth-like planets, scattered among the huge drift of the star-streams, struggling to master all those world-problems, social and spiritual, which man in our ‘modern’ era is for the first time confronting. Similarly, we saw again the many other kinds of races, nautiloid, submarine, avian, composite, and the rare symbiotics, and still rarer plant-like beings. And of every kind only a few, if any, won through to utopia, and took part in the great communal enterprise of worlds. The rest fell by the way.</p>
<p>From our remote look-out we now saw in one of the islanded sub-galaxies the triumph of the Symbiotics. Here at last was the germ of a true community of worlds. Presently the stars of this islet-universe began to be girdled with living pearls, till the whole sub-galaxy was alive with worlds. Meanwhile in the main system arose that flagrant and contagious insanity of empire, which we had already watched in detail. But what had before appeared as a war of titans, in which great worlds manœuvred in space with inconceivable speed, and destroyed one another’s populations in holocausts, was now seen as the jerky motion of a few microscopic sparks, a few luminous animalcules, surrounded by the indifferent stellar hosts.</p>
<p>Presently, however, we saw a star blaze up and destroy its planets. The Empires had murdered something nobler than themselves. There was a second murder, and a third. Then, under the influence of the sub-galaxy, the imperial madness faded, and empire crumbled. And soon our fatigued attention was held by the irresistible coming of utopia throughout the galaxy. This was visible to us chiefly as a steady increase of artificial planets. Star after star blossomed with orbit after crowded orbit of these vital jewels, these blooms pregnant with the spirit. Constellation after constellation, the whole galaxy became visibly alive with myriads of worlds. Each world, peopled with its unique, multitudinous race of sensitive individual intelligences united in true community, was itself a living thing, possessed of a common spirit. And each system of many populous orbits was itself a communal being. And the whole galaxy, knit in a single telepathic mesh, was a single intelligent and ardent being, the common spirit, the ‘I’, of all its countless, diverse, and ephemeral individuals.</p>
<p>This whole vast community looked now beyond itself toward its fellow galaxies. Resolved to pursue the adventure of life and of spirit in the cosmical, the widest of all spheres, it was in constant telepathic communication with its fellows; and at the same time, conceiving all kinds of strange practical ambitions, it began to avail itself of the energies of its stars upon a scale hitherto unimagined. Not only was every solar system now surrounded by a gauze of light traps, which focused the escaping solar energy for intelligent use, so that the whole galaxy was dimmed, but many stars that were not suited to be suns were disintegrated, and rifled of their prodigious stores of sub-atomic energy.</p>
<p>Suddenly our attention was held by an event which even at a distance was visibly incompatible with utopia. A star encircled by planets exploded, destroying all its rings of worlds, and sinking afterwards into wan exhaustion. Another and another, and yet others in different regions of the galaxy, did likewise.</p>
<p>To enquire into the cause of these startling disasters we once more, by an act of volition, dispersed ourselves to our stations among the many worlds.</p>
