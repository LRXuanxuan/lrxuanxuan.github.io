<p>最初发表于《奇幻与科幻杂志》（The Magazine of Fantasy and Science Fiction）</p>
<p>他们隔着小屋花园里的一张茶桌相对而坐。海伦向后靠着，冷冷地端详吉姆的脸。那是一张奇异地孩子气、几乎带着胎儿般稚嫩的脸：宽阔的前额、短而上翘的鼻子、微微噘起的嘴唇。是的，很孩子气；但在那双圆圆的黑眼睛里，却闪着一丝疯狂的光。她不得不承认，自己在某种程度上的确被这个古怪的年轻人吸引了——也许一部分是因为他那种孩子气，以及他笨拙而天真的求爱方式；但另一部分，却正是因为那道阴森的光。</p>
<p>吉姆探着身子，正说得起劲。他已经说了很久，但她早就没在听。她心里想的是：虽然自己受他吸引，可同时也很讨厌他。她为什么又跟他出来了？他瘦弱、以自我为中心。可她还是来了。</p>
<p>他的话里有一句忽然把她的注意力重新拉了回来。他似乎因为她没有听而恼火，整个人激动得厉害。她听见他说：“我知道你瞧不起我，可你大错特错了。我告诉你，我有力量。我本来还不想这么早把秘密告诉你，可见鬼，我现在就说。我正在发现很多关于心灵支配物质的东西。我能隔着距离控制物质，只要用意志去做。我会成为一种现代魔法师。我甚至已经能只靠意志杀死东西了。”</p>
<p>海伦是医学生，一向以自己机敏的唯物主义自豪。她轻蔑地笑了。</p>
<p>他的脸气得通红。“好吧！那我非给你看看不可。”</p>
<p>一只知更鸟正在灌木上歌唱。年轻人的视线离开姑娘的脸，牢牢钉在那只鸟身上。“看着那只鸟。”他说，声音几乎只是耳语。过了一会儿，鸟停止了歌唱。它看上去痛苦不堪，把头缩进身体里，随后连翅膀都没有张开，就从树上掉了下来。它仰躺在草地上，两脚朝天，死了。</p>
<p>吉姆盯着自己的牺牲品，发出一声被憋住似的胜利尖叫。然后他转眼看向海伦，一边用手帕擦着苍白黏腻的脸，一边说：“这次干得不错。我以前从没在鸟身上试过，只试过苍蝇、甲虫，还有一只青蛙。”</p>
<p>姑娘默默望着他，竭力不让自己显出吃惊。他开始向她解释自己的秘密。她再也不觉得无聊了。</p>
<p>他说，大约两年前，他开始对“这些超常现象之类的东西”产生兴趣。他参加过降神会，也读过心灵研究方面的书。如果不是怀疑自己本身就拥有某种奇异能力，他根本不会费这个心思。他其实从来不真正关心幽灵、思想传递之类的事。真正使他着迷的，是心灵直接影响物质的可能性。人们把这种能力叫作“心灵致动”（psychokinesis），对它知道得很少。但他才懒得管那些理论难题。他想要的只有力量。他给海伦讲起美国人曾用骰子做过的一些古怪实验：一次又一次掷骰子，同时强烈希望两个骰子都以六点朝上。多数时候当然不会如此；但实验次数足够多之后，把结果统计起来，就会发现六点出现的次数比纯粹偶然所应有的更多。看起来，心灵似乎确实能产生某种微弱影响。这便打开了惊人的可能性。</p>
<p>他开始自己做一些小实验，一方面依据那些研究者的发现，一方面也凭自己的想法。那种力量微弱得不可思议，所以必须把它用在那种哪怕最细小的作用也能产生可检测结果的情形里——只要稍稍把天平压向一边就行。</p>
<p>他用骰子没取得太大成功，因为——照他的解释——他始终弄不清自己究竟该做什么。骰子滚动得太快，他根本来不及把意志准确作用上去。因此，他只能得到美国人报告过的那种微小偏差。他不得不想出别的办法，给自己找到更好的着力点。他受过科学训练，于是决定尝试影响化学反应和简单的物理过程。他做了许多实验，也学到了许多东西。他能阻止一滴水使刀子生锈，能让一粒盐晶体在水里不溶解；他甚至能在一滴水里形成一枚极小的冰晶，最后只靠“把热量用意志赶走”而冻结整滴水——实际上，就是使分子的运动全部停止。</p>
<p>他给海伦讲起自己第一次成功杀生的经历——那是一次真正意义上的“显微镜级”成功。他先弄了一些极其腐败的死水，滴一滴在载玻片上。通过显微镜，他看见大群微生物四下游动。它们大多像短粗的小香肠，拖着波浪般摆动的尾巴，大小各不相同。他给它们分别起了绰号，什么“大象”“牛”“羊”“兔子”。他的想法是：也许自己能够让其中某一只体内的化学反应停止，从而杀死它。他查阅了很多关于这些微小生物内部机理的资料，知道最适合攻击哪个关键过程。可那些该死的东西不停乱窜，他根本无法长时间把注意力集中在某一只身上，总是在人群里跟丢目标。终于，有一只“兔子”游到了载玻片上较空旷的地方，他盯得够久，终于成功了。他强迫那个关键化学过程停止，而它果然停止了。那只生物不再动弹，此后一直静止不动。它几乎肯定已经死了。他说，那次成功让自己“感觉像上帝一样”。</p>
<p>后来，他学会通过冻结苍蝇和甲虫的脑部来杀死它们。接着他试过一只青蛙，却没有成功。他对生理学懂得还不够，找不到一个只要稍加干预便能致命的微小关键过程。于是他又查了大量资料，终于成功。他只需让脊髓中某些控制心跳的神经纤维停止传导即可。刚才用在知更鸟身上的，也正是这种方法。</p>
<p>“这还只是开始。”他说，“很快，整个世界都会匍匐在我脚下。你要是跟我站在一起，它也会匍匐在你脚下。”</p>
<p>在这一长篇独白里，姑娘一直聚精会神地听着，心里在厌恶和迷恋之间撕扯。整件事都仿佛散发着一种恶臭，但在这个时代，一个人也不能太娇气。再说了，也许所谓道德根本就没有什么真实基础。可即便如此，吉姆也是在玩火。奇怪的是，在他说话的过程中，他仿佛忽然长大了。不知为什么，他看起来不再那么笨拙、那么像个婴儿。他的激动，再加上她已经知道那力量是真实存在的，使他显出一种令人战栗的阴险魅力。但她决定保持谨慎和疏离。</p>
<p>吉姆终于停下来后，她暗暗装作打了个呵欠，说：“你可真聪明，不是吗？刚才那一手很厉害，虽然恶心得很。你要再走远一点，最后准会被绞死。”</p>
<p>他哼了一声。“你可不像是个胆小鬼。”</p>
<p>这句嘲讽刺痛了她。她愤怒地回答：“别胡说八道！难道就因为你能靠某种卑鄙的小把戏杀死一只鸟，我就该像你说的那样‘跟你站在一起’吗？”</p>
<p>吉姆的一生中还有一些事情，他并没有提起。对他来说，它们似乎和眼前的问题无关，但实际上关系极大。他从小就是个体弱者。他的父亲是职业足球运动员，瞧不起他，并把他的孱弱归咎于身体虚弱的母亲。夫妻二人几乎从蜜月之后就一直吵得鸡犬不宁。在学校里，吉姆受尽欺凌；因此，他一方面对强者生出深刻的仇恨，另一方面又近乎强迫症般渴望自己也成为强者。他脑子很聪明，靠奖学金进入一所地方大学。读本科时，他独来独往，刻苦攻读科学学位，并打算以原子物理研究为职业。那时，他最强烈的激情已经是对物理力量的崇拜，于是选择了这个最耀眼的领域。可不知为什么，他的计划出了岔子。尽管学业成绩相当不错，他最后却困在一家工业实验室里做低级工作。原本他只是把这个职位当成临时过渡，等着日后挤进某个研究原子物理的大机构。可在这潭死水里，他本就阴郁的性格越发苦毒。他觉得自己没有得到公平机会，能力不如他的家伙却一个个超过了他。命运在跟他作对。事实上，他逐渐发展出某种被迫害妄想。但真相是：他不擅长合作。他从未形成现代基础物理研究那种高度复杂的协作工作所必需的团队精神。此外，他对物理理论本身也没有真正兴趣，对必须进行高深理论学习这件事极不耐烦。他想要的是力量——属于他个人的力量。他明白，现代科研是一项合作事业；一个人在其中也许能得到耀眼的声望，却不可能作为个人获得真正可由自己支配的物理力量。心灵致动则不同，它也许能够给他梦寐以求的东西。于是，他的兴趣很快转向这个更有希望的领域。从此，实验室工作对他而言只剩下维持生计的意义。</p>
<p>在小屋花园那次谈话之后，他比以往任何时候都更狂热地投入自己的事业。他必须获得更惊人的能力，才能打动海伦。他认定，至少对自己而言，最有希望的路线就是进一步发展干预微小物理、化学过程的技巧，不论对象是无生命物还是生物。他学会让已经划着的火柴无法燃烧。他还试图绕过整个原子研究体系，直接用心灵致动去释放束缚在原子内部的能量。但这项激动人心的尝试完全失败了，也许是因为他虽然受过训练，却仍缺乏足够的理论物理知识，也接触不到适合搭建实验的设备。在生物方面，他则成功用对付知更鸟的同一种办法杀死了一条小狗。他确信，只要再多练习一些，自己很快就能杀人。</p>
<p>有一次，他遭遇了颇为惊险的事故。他决定试着让摩托车发动机的火花塞停止点火。他把摩托车架起来启动，然后开始“用意志”让火花失效。他把全部注意力集中在火花塞两极和其间跳动的电火花上，并强迫两极之间的空间变得不可穿透、成为绝缘体。当然，这个实验对物理过程的干预程度远远超过冻结一条神经纤维，甚至也超过阻止火柴燃烧。他竭力进行，汗水不断从身上淌下来。最后，发动机果然开始断断续续地失火。但他自己却发生了怪事：一阵可怕的眩晕和恶心袭来，随后他失去了知觉。等他醒来时，发动机又已经恢复正常运转。</p>
<p>这次意外成了一个挑战。他从来不真正关心实验的纯理论意义，可现在他被迫去问：当自己通过一次“意志行为”干涉物理过程时，究竟发生了什么？最显而易见的解释是，本该跨越火花塞间隙的物理能量不知怎么被导入了他自己的身体；也就是说，他实际上承受了如果直接摸上火花塞两极时会受到的那种电击。不过，真实原因恐怕没有这么简单，因为他的症状并不像遭受电击。也许更接近事实的说法是：抑制如此大量的物理能量，在他的精神中引发了某种深刻扰动；或者更粗略地说，那些物理能量以某种方式在他体内转化成了精神能量。支持这种理论的一点是：当他恢复意识之后，他处于一种异常兴奋、头脑异常活跃的状态，仿佛服下了某种强烈的兴奋剂。</p>
<p>无论真相如何，他采用了较简单的解释，并开始设法把闯入自己身体的能量引到别处，以保护自己。经过许多令人不安的实验之后，他发现，只要同时把注意力集中在火花塞和另一个活的生物身上，就能做到这一点；那个生物会“吸走电流”，并因此受害。一只麻雀就够用了。它被电死，而吉姆自己则能保持清醒，足以继续把发动机停下来。另一次，他拿邻居的狗当“避雷针”。那条狗倒了下去，但不久又恢复意识，随后在花园里疯跑，兴高采烈地狂吠。</p>
<p>他的下一个实验更刺激，也远远更加不可原谅。他来到乡下，占据一处小山丘，从那里可以看见一段相当长的公路。过了一会儿，一辆汽车驶入视野。他把注意力集中在汽车的火花塞上，并“命令”那里的电能逸入司机体内。汽车减速，在道路两侧之间摇摆不定，最后横着停在路中央。他能看见司机趴倒在方向盘上。车里没有别人。吉姆兴奋得不得了，等着看接下来会发生什么。不久，又有一辆车从相反方向开来，猛烈鸣笛，伴着刺耳的刹车声停下。司机跳下车，走到那辆停着的汽车旁，打开车门，看见了里面昏迷的人。这个惊恐的新来者正不知该怎么办时，那名司机恢复了意识。两人焦急地交谈了一阵，最后各自把车开走了。</p>
<p>吉姆觉得，现在自己已经足以打动女朋友了。自从杀死那只知更鸟以后，他们偶尔见过几面，他以自己那种笨拙、青春期般的方式向她求爱。她总是把他挡回去；但显然，自知更鸟事件之后，她对他更感兴趣了。她虽然有时故意装出鄙视他的样子，他却觉得她暗地里其实被自己吸引。</p>
<p>可有一天，他遭遇了一次很不愉快的意外。他下班后登上一辆公共汽车回家，爬上楼梯，在楼上找了个位子坐下。忽然，他发现海伦就坐在前面几排，身旁是一个卷发、穿运动外套的年轻男人。两人头靠得很近，正全神贯注地交谈。姑娘的头发拂过男人的脸颊。过了一会儿，她笑了起来，那笑声里有一种吉姆从未从她那里听到过的幸福。她转过脸去望向同伴，整张脸洋溢着活力和爱意——至少，在三排之后那个妒火中烧的情人眼里是如此。</p>
<p>一股毫无理性的狂怒席卷了他。他对姑娘的心思本就一无所知，又因为“自己的姑娘”——他就是这样看待海伦的——竟然去注意另一个男人而怒不可遏，于是嫉妒彻底占据了他，把一切其他考虑都挤了出去。他满脑子只剩下一个念头：毁掉情敌。他的目光死死锁住前方那段可恨的后颈。他疯狂地在脑中想象藏在皮肉之下的椎骨，以及椎管内那束神经纤维。神经电流必须停止；必须、必须停止。过了一会儿，那颗卷发的脑袋垂到海伦肩上，接着整个身体向前倒去。</p>
<p>凶手匆忙从座位上站起来，背对着刚刚开始的骚乱。他下了车，装作对这场灾难一无所知。</p>
<p>他步行继续回家时，仍然兴奋得厉害，脑中只有胜利的狂喜。但渐渐地，那股狂热退去，他不得不面对一个事实：自己成了杀人犯。他急切地提醒自己，既然道德只是一种迷信，那么感到内疚根本没有意义。可不幸的是，他确实感到内疚，而且内疚得可怕；尤其因为他根本不必害怕被抓住。</p>
<p>日子一天天过去，吉姆在他所谓“非理性的”罪恶感与令人陶醉的胜利感之间来回摆荡。世界确实已经在他脚下了。但他必须谨慎出牌。不幸的是，罪恶感一刻也不肯放过他。他无法正常入睡；一旦睡着，又会做可怕的噩梦。白天，他的实验也被一个幻想所妨碍：他觉得自己已经把灵魂卖给了魔鬼。这个念头因为本身荒唐至极，反而使他恼火，可他就是无法摆脱。他开始大量喝酒。但不久便发现，酒精会削弱他的心灵致动力，于是又以坚定的意志戒掉了酒。</p>
<p>从这种强迫性的罪恶感中寻求解脱的另一种可能，是性。但不知为什么，他始终无法迫使自己去面对海伦。他对她怀着一种毫无道理的恐惧。可她显然完全不知道，是他杀死了她的情人。</p>
<p>终于有一天，他们在街上偶然相遇，已经没有任何可能躲开。海伦看上去有些憔悴，但她朝他笑了笑，甚至主动提议一起喝杯咖啡、聊一聊。吉姆在恐惧与欲望之间撕扯，片刻后，他们已经坐在咖啡馆里。说了几句无关紧要的话之后，她开口道：</p>
<p>“请安慰安慰我吧！我最近受了可怕的打击。我和我哥哥坐在公共汽车上层。他在非洲待了三年，刚回来。我们正说着话，他忽然倒下，几乎当场就死了。他看上去明明非常健康。他们说，是某种侵袭脊髓的新病毒。”她注意到吉姆的脸已经变得死一般苍白。“你怎么了？”她惊叫道，“难道你也要死在我面前吗？”</p>
<p>他强迫自己镇定下来，告诉她，纯粹因为太同情她，自己才会一阵发晕。他那么爱她，怎么可能听到她的不幸而不受打击？令他松了口气的是，海伦完全相信了这个解释。她第一次向他露出那种明亮温暖的笑容——此前，他只见她对自己的哥哥这样笑过。</p>
<p>受到鼓舞，他趁势推进。他说，自己真心想安慰她，他们一定要尽快再见面。如果她对他的实验还有一点兴趣，哪天他可以给她看真正刺激的东西。他们约好下一个星期天去乡下。吉姆暗自决定，为了她再表演一次让汽车失控的把戏。</p>
<p>星期天是个明亮的夏日。他们坐在一节空荡荡的火车车厢里，一路谈了许多关于她哥哥的事。吉姆其实相当无聊，但还是表现出热烈的同情。海伦说，她从没想到他有这么温暖的一颗心。他握住她的手臂。两张脸越靠越近，彼此凝视。她忽然对这张古怪、甚至有点滑稽，却又少年气十足的脸产生压倒性的温柔。她告诉自己，这张脸上，童年的天真与成年人对力量的自觉融合在了一起。她感觉得到深藏其下的冷酷，而她竟欢迎这种冷酷。吉姆这边也意识到，她极其诱人。健康温暖的光泽已经回到她脸上。（还是说，那其实是爱的光泽？）饱满甜美的嘴唇、亲切而善于观察的灰眼睛，不仅激起了他的肉欲，也带给他一种从未体验过的、令人发软的温柔。他想起自己的罪行以及眼前的欺骗，痛苦不堪。脸上露出悲惨的神色。他松开她的手臂，俯身把头埋进双手。海伦困惑而怜惜地搂住他的肩，亲吻他的头发。忽然，他大哭起来，把头埋进她胸前。她抱紧他，像哄孩子一样轻声安慰，求他告诉自己究竟怎么了，可他只能呜咽着说：“哦，我太可怕了！我配不上你。”</p>
<p>不过到了当天晚些时候，他的情绪已经完全恢复。他们挽着手穿过树林。他告诉她自己最近的成功，最后讲到那次汽车实验。她既觉得吃惊又觉得有趣，却也因为他仅仅为了测试能力就冒险制造可能致命的事故，而在道德上感到震骇。可同时，驱使他走到这种地步的那种狂热又显然使她着迷。她的兴趣令他受宠若惊，她的温柔和身体的接近则使他陶醉。此时，他们正靠在那座小山丘上休息——吉姆原本打算在这里给她表演汽车把戏。他把头枕在她腿上，仰望她的脸，仿佛自己一生错失的全部爱都聚集在这张脸上。他知道自己扮演的更像是一个婴儿，而不是情人。但她似乎需要他这样，他也乐于接受这个角色。然而很快，性欲再次抬头，与之同时恢复的还有男性的自尊。他忽然产生一种无法遏制的渴望：必须以某种可怕的力量展示来证明自己神一般的本质。他变成了那种原始的野蛮人，非要在心爱的女人面前杀死一个敌人不可。</p>
<p>透过海伦被风吹动的头发，他看见高处有一个小东西在移动。起初，他以为那是一只小飞虫，随后才意识到，那是一架从远处飞来的飞机。</p>
<p>“看着那架飞机。”他说。声音来得如此突然，海伦吓了一跳。她抬头望去，又低头看他。他的脸因为用力而扭曲，双眼怒睁，鼻孔张大。他的样子残酷得令人作呕，海伦有一瞬间真想把他从自己腿上推开。但迷恋最终占了上风。“盯着飞机。”他命令道。她抬头，又低头，再抬头。她知道自己应该打破这个邪恶的魔咒。（世上似乎有一种叫“道德”的东西，不过那大概只是幻觉。）迷恋还是赢了。</p>
<p>过了一会儿，正在接近的飞机那四台发动机开始犹豫般断续起来，接着一台又一台停止工作。飞机滑翔了一阵，但很快便明显失去控制。它左右摇摆、踉跄，随后机头朝下，旋转着俯冲。海伦尖叫起来，却什么也没做。飞机消失在远处一片树林后。几秒钟后，一声沉闷的撞击传来，树林后方升起黑烟，形成一道倾斜的黑色烟柱。</p>
<p>吉姆从海伦腿上撑起身，转过来把她压倒在地。“这就是我爱你的方式。”他凶狠地低声说。接着，他疯狂亲吻她的嘴唇、她的脖颈。</p>
<p>她拼命使自己清醒，抗拒那种把自己完全交给这个疯子的冲动。她挣扎着摆脱他的控制。不久，两人站起来面对着彼此，气喘吁吁。“你疯了！”她喊道，“想想你都干了什么！你只是为了显摆自己有多聪明，就杀了人。然后你还来向我求爱。”她双手掩面，抽泣起来。</p>
<p>他仍沉浸在疯狂的亢奋中，大笑起来，随后开始嘲弄她。“你还自称现实主义者！你太娇气了。好吧，现在你知道我真正是什么样，也知道我能做什么了。看清楚！你是我的。无论你在哪里，我随时都能杀了你。我想对你做什么就做什么。你要敢阻止我，你就会跟那只知更鸟，还有——公共汽车上的那个男人一样。”她的双手从泪痕斑斑的脸上垂下，带着恐惧——以及温柔——混合的神色凝视着他。她平静地说：“你彻底疯了，可怜的孩子。你明明看上去那么温柔。哦，亲爱的，我到底该拿你怎么办？”</p>
<p>两人长久沉默。忽然，吉姆瘫倒在地，像孩子一样放声痛哭。她困惑地站在他身旁。</p>
<p>就在她想着该怎么办、并责怪自己为什么没能在一切太迟之前打破那场魔咒时，吉姆正陷在极端的自我厌恶中。随后，为了不再造成任何伤害，他开始把自己的那套技术用在自己身上。事情比他预料的更困难；因为他一旦开始失去意识，就也会失去对操作的控制。但他拼尽全部意志。海伦发现他忽然一动不动，跪到他身边时，他已经死了。</p>

<p>THEY CONFRONTED EACH OTHER ACROSS A TEA TABLE in a cottage garden. Helen was leaning back coldly studying Jim’s face. It was an oddly childish, almost foetal face, with its big brow, snub nose, and pouting lips. Childish, yes; but in the round dark eyes there was a gleam of madness. She had to admit that she was in a way drawn to this odd young man partly perhaps by his very childishness and his awkward innocent attempts at lovemaking; but partly by that sinister gleam.</p>
<p>Jim was leaning forward, talking hard. He had been talking for a long time, but she was no longer listening. She was deciding that though she was drawn to him she also disliked him. Why had she come out with him again? He was weedy and self-centered. Yet she had come.</p>
<p>Something he was saying recaptured her attention. He seemed to be annoyed that she had not been listening. He was all worked up about something. She heard him say, “I know you despise me, but you’re making a big mistake. I tell you I have powers. I didn’t intend to let you into my secret, yet; but, damn it, I will. I’m finding out a lot about the power of mind over matter. I can control matter at a distance, just by willing it. I’m going to be a sort of modern magician. I’ve even killed things by just willing it.”</p>
<p>Helen, who was a medical student, prided herself on her shrewd materialism. She laughed contemptuously.</p>
<p>His face flushed with anger, and he said, “Oh very well! I’ll have to show you.”</p>
<p>On a bush a robin was singing. The young man’s gaze left the girl’s face and settled intently on the robin. “Watch that bird,” he said. His voice was almost a whisper. Presently the bird stopped singing, and after looking miserable for a while, with its head hunched into its body, it dropped from the tree without opening its wings. It lay on the grass with its legs in the air, dead.</p>
<p>Jim let out a constricted squawk of triumph, staring at his victim. Then he turned his eyes on Helen. Mopping his pasty face with his handkerchief, he said, “That was a good turn. I’ve never tried it on a bird before, only on flies and beetles and a frog.”</p>
<p>The girl stared at him silently, anxious not to seem startled. He set about telling her his secret. She was not bored any more.</p>
<p>He told her that a couple of years earlier he had begun to be interested in “all this paranormal stuff.” He had been to séances and read about psychical research. He wouldn’t have bothered if he hadn’t suspected he had strange powers himself. He was never really interested in spooks and thought transference and so on. What fascinated him was the possibility that a mind might be able to affect matter directly. “Psychokinesis,” they called this power; and they knew very little about it. But he didn’t care a damn about the theoretical puzzles. All he wanted was power. He told Helen about the queer experiments that had been done in America with dice. You threw the dice time after time, and you willed them to settle with the two sixes uppermost. Generally they didn’t; but when you had done a great many experiments you totted up the results and found that there had been more sixes than should have turned up by sheer chance. It certainly looked as though the mind really had some slight influence. This opened up terrific possibilities.</p>
<p>He began to do little experiments on his own, guided by the findings of the researchers, but also by some of his own ideas. The power was fantastically slight, so you had to test it out in situations where the tiniest influence would have detectable results, just tipping the scales.</p>
<p>He didn’t have much success with the dice, because (as he explained) he never knew precisely what he had to do. The dice tumbled out too quickly for him. And so he only had the slight effect that the Americans had reported. So he had to think up new tricks that would give him a better opening. He had had a scientific training, so he decided to try to influence chemical reactions and simple physical processes. He did many experiments and learned a lot. He prevented a spot of water from rusting a knife. He stopped a crystal of salt from dissolving in water. He formed a minute crystal of ice in a drop of water and finally froze the whole drop by simply “willing away” all the heat, in fact by stopping all the molecular movement.</p>
<p>He told Helen of his first success at killing, a literally microscopic success. He brewed some very stagnant water and put a drop on a slide. Then through the microscope he watched the swarm of microorganisms milling about. Mostly they were like stumpy sausages, swimming with wavy tails. They were of many sizes. He thought of them as elephants, cows, sheep, rabbits. His idea was that he might be able to stop the chemical action in one of these little creatures and so kill it. He had read up a lot about their inner workings, and he knew what key process he could best tackle. Well, the damned things kept shifting about so fast he couldn’t concentrate on anyone of them for long enough. He kept losing his victim in the crowd. However, at last one of the “rabbits” swam into a less populous part of the slide, and he fixed his attention on it long enough to do the trick. He willed the crucial chemical process to stop, and it did stop. The creature stopped moving and stayed still indefinitely. It was almost certainly dead. His success, he said, made him “feel like God.”</p>
<p>Later he learned to kill flies and beetles by freezing their brains. Then he tried a frog, but had no success. He didn’t know enough physiology to find a minute key process to check. However, he read up a lot of stuff, and at last he succeeded. He simply stopped the nerve current in certain fibres in the spinal cord that controlled the heartbeat. It was this method also that he had used on the robin.</p>
<p>“That’s just the beginning,” he said. “Soon I shall have the world at my feet. And if you join up with me, it will be at your feet too.”</p>
<p>Throughout this monologue the girl had listened intently, torn between revulsion and fascination. There was a kind of bad smell about it all, but one couldn’t afford to be too squeamish in these days. Besides, there was probably nothing in morality, anyhow. All the same, Jim was playing with fire. Strange, though, how he seemed to have grown up while he was talking. Somehow he didn’t look gawky and babyish anymore. His excitement, and her knowledge that his power was real, had made him look thrillingly sinister. But she decided to be cautious and aloof.</p>
<p>When at last Jim was silent, she staged a concealed yawn and said, “You’re clever, aren’t you! That was a good trick you did, though a horrid one. If you go much further, you’ll end on the gallows.”</p>
<p>He snorted and said, “It’s not like you to be a coward.”</p>
<p>The taunt stung her. Indignantly she answered, “Don’t be ridiculous! Why should I join with you, as you call it, merely because you can kill a bird by some low trick or other?”</p>
<p>In Jim’s life there had been certain events which he had not mentioned. They seemed to him irrelevant to the matter in hand, but they were not really so at all. He had always been a weakling. His father, a professional footballer, despised him and blamed the frail mother. The couple had lived a cat-and-dog life almost since their honeymoon. At school Jim had been thoroughly bullied; and in consequence he had conceived a deep hatred of the strong and at the same time an obsessive yearning to be strong himself. He was a bright lad and had secured a scholarship at a provincial university. As an undergraduate, he kept to himself, worked hard for a scientific degree, and aimed at a career of research in atomic physics. Already his dominant passion was physical power, so he chose its most spectacular field. But somehow his plans went awry. In spite of his reasonably good academic qualifications, he found himself stuck in a low-grade job in an industrial lab, a job which he had taken on as a stopgap till he could capture a post in one of the great institutions devoted to atomic physics. In this backwater, his naturally sour disposition became embittered. He felt he was not getting a fair chance. Inferior men were outstripping him. Fate was against him. In fact he developed something like a persecution mania. But the truth was that he was a bad cooperator. He never developed the team spirit which is so necessary in the immensely complex work of fundamental physical research. Also, he had no genuine interest in physical theory and was impatient of the necessity of advanced theoretical study. What he wanted was power, power for himself as an individual. He recognized that modern research was a cooperative affair and that in it, though one might gain dazzling prestige, one would not gain any physical power as an individual. Psychokinesis, on the other hand, might perhaps give him his heart’s desire. His interest rapidly shifted to the more promising field. Henceforth his work in the lab was a mere means of earning a livelihood.</p>
<p>After the conversation in the cottage garden he concentrated more eagerly than ever on his venture. He must gain even more spectacular powers to impress Helen. He had decided that for him, at any rate, the promising line was to develop his skill at interfering with small physical and chemical processes, in lifeless and in living things. He learned how to prevent a struck match from lighting. He tried to bypass the whole of atomic research by applying his power of psychokinesis to the release of energy pent up in the atom. But in this exciting venture he had no success at all, perhaps because in spite of his training, he had not sufficient theoretical knowledge of physics, nor access to the right kind of apparatus for setting up the experiment. On the biological side he succeeded in killing a small dog by the same process as he had applied to the robin. He was confident that with practice he would soon be able to kill a man.</p>
<p>He had one alarming experience. He decided to try to stop the sparking of his motorcycle engine. He started up the bike on its stand and set about “willing” the spark to fail. He concentrated his attention on the points of the sparking plug and the leaping spark and “willed” the space between the points to become impenetrable, an insulator. This experiment, of course, involved a far greater interference with physical processes than freezing a nerve fibre or even preventing a match from lighting. Sweat poured from him as he struggled with his task. At last the engine began to misfire. But something queer happened to himself. He had a moment of horrible vertigo and nausea and then he lost consciousness. When he recovered, the engine was once more running normally.</p>
<p>This mishap was a challenge. He had never been seriously interested in the mere theoretical side of his experiments for its own sake, but now he had perforce; to ask himself what exactly was happening when by an “act of will” he interfered with a physical process. The obvious explanation was that in some way the physical energy that should have crossed the gap between the points had been directed into his own body; in fact that he had suffered the electric shock that he would have had if he had touched the points. It may be doubted whether the true explanation was as simple as this, for his symptoms were not those of electric shock. It might be nearer the truth to say that the inhibition of so much physical energy caused some sort of profound psychical disturbance in him; or else, to put the matter very crudely, that the physical energy was in some way converted into psychical energy in him. This theory is borne out by the fact that, when he recovered consciousness, he was in a state of great excitement and mental vigour, as though he had taken some stimulating drug.</p>
<p>Whatever the truth of the matter, he adopted the simpler theory and set about sidetracking the intruding energy so as to protect himself. After much anxious experimentation, he found that he could do so by concentrating his attention both on the sparking plug and on some other living organism, which then “drew off the electricity” and suffered accordingly. A sparrow sufficed. It died of the shock, while he himself remained conscious long enough to stop the engine. On another occasion he used his neighbour’s dog as a “lightning conductor.” The animal collapsed, but soon recovered consciousness and careened about the garden barking hilariously.</p>
<p>His next experiment was more exciting, and much, much more reprehensible. He went into the country and took up a position on a knoll, whence he could see a fairly long stretch of road. Presently a car came into sight. He concentrated his attention on the sparking plugs and “willed” the electrical energy to escape into the driver. The car slowed down, vacillated between the two sides of the road, and came to a standstill across the fairway. He could see the driver slumped over the steering wheel. There was no one else in the car. Greatly excited, Jim waited to see what would happen. Presently another car came in the opposite direction, hooted violently, and drew up with screeching brakes. The driver emerged, went to the derelict car, opened a door, and was confronted by the unconscious occupant. While the horrified newcomer was wondering what to do, the other recovered consciousness. There was an anxious conversation, and finally both cars went their separate ways.</p>
<p>Jim now felt ready to impress his girlfriend. Since the killing of the robin, they had occasionally met, and in his awkward and adolescent way he had tried to make love to her. She had always discouraged him; but she was obviously more interested in him since the robin incident. Though she sometimes affected to despise him, he felt that she was secretly drawn to him.</p>
<p>But one day he had an unpleasant surprise. He had boarded a bus to take him home from his work. He climbed the stairs and settled into a seat. Suddenly he noticed Helen sitting a few seats ahead with a curly-headed young man in a sportscoat. The couple were deep in conversation with their heads bent together. The girl’s hair brushed his cheek. Presently she laughed, with a ring of happiness such as he had never before heard from her. She turned her face toward her companion. It was aglow with vitality and love. Or so it seemed to the jealous lover three seats behind.</p>
<p>Irrational fury swept over him. He was so ignorant of the ways of girls, and so indignant that “his girl” (for so he regarded her) should take notice of another man, that jealousy wholly possessed him, to the exclusion of all other considerations. He could think of nothing but destroying his rival. His gaze seized upon the nape of the hated neck before him. He passionately conjured up images of the hidden vertebrae and the enclosed bundle of nerve fibres. The nerve current must cease; must, must cease. Presently the curly head sank on Helen’s shoulder, and then the whole body fell forward.</p>
<p>The murderer hurriedly rose from his seat and turned his back on the incipient commotion. He left the bus, as though ignorant of the disaster.</p>
<p>Continuing his journey on foot, he was still so excited that he had no thought but exultation over his triumph. But gradually his frenzy subsided, and he faced the fact that he was a murderer. Urgently he reminded himself that after all there was no point in feeling guilty, since morality was a mere superstition. But alas, he did feel guilty, horribly guilty; the more so since he had no fear of being caught.</p>
<p>As the days passed, Jim alternated between what he regarded as “irrational” guilt and intoxicating triumph. The world was indeed at his feet. But he must play his cards carefully. Unfortunately his guilt gave him no peace. He could not sleep properly; and when he did sleep, he had terrifying dreams. By day his experiments were hampered by the fantasy that he had sold his soul to the devil. This notion infuriated him with its very silliness. Yet he could not rid himself of it. He began drinking rather heavily. But he soon found that alcohol reduced his psychokinetic power, so he firmly broke himself of the habit.</p>
<p>Another possible form of relief from his obsessive guilt was sex. But somehow he could not bring himself to face Helen. He was irrationally afraid of her. Yet she must be quite ignorant that he had killed her lover.</p>
<p>At last he met her accidentally in the street. There was no possibility of avoiding her. She was rather wan, he thought, but she smiled at him and actually suggested a talk over a cup of coffee. He was torn between fear and desire, but presently they were seated in a cafe. After some trivial remarks, she said.</p>
<p>“Please comfort me! I have had a terrible shock quite recently. I was on the top of a bus with my brother who has been in Africa for three years. While we were talking, he collapsed and died almost instantly. He seemed perfectly fit. They say it was some new virus in the spinal cord.” She noticed that Jim’s face had turned deadly pale. “What’s the matter?” she cried. “Are you going to die on me too?”</p>
<p>He pulled himself together and assured her that sheer sympathy for her had made him feel faint. He loved her so much. How could he help being upset by her misfortune? To his relief Helen was completely taken in by this explanation. She gave him, for the first time, the glowing smile he had formerly seen her turn upon her brother.</p>
<p>Encouraged, he pressed home his advantage. He said, he did so want to comfort her. They must meet again soon. And if she was at all interested in his experiments, he would show her something really exciting some time. They arranged a trip in the country the following Sunday. He privately decided to repeat for her benefit his trick with a passing car.</p>
<p>Sunday was a bright summer day. Sitting together in an empty railway carriage, they talked a good deal about her brother. He was rather bored, but he expressed ardent sympathy. She said she never imagined he had such a warm heart. He took her arm. Their faces drew close together, and they looked into each other’s eyes. She felt an overwhelming tenderness for this strange, rather grotesque though boyish face, wherein, she told herself, the innocence of childhood was blended with an adult consciousness of power. She felt the underlying grimness, and she welcomed it. Jim, for his part, was realizing that she was very desirable. The warm glow of health had returned to her face. (Or was it a glow of love?) The full, sweet lips, the kindly, observant grey eyes, filled him not only with physical desire but a swooning gentleness that was new to him. The recollection of his guilt and present deception tormented him. An expression of misery came over his face. He let go her arm and bowed forward with his head in his hands. Perplexed and compassionate, she put an arm round his shoulders, and kissed his hair. Suddenly he burst into tears and buried his head on her breast. She hugged him and crooned over him as though he were her child. She begged him to tell her what was the matter, but he could only blubber, “Oh, I’m horrible! I’m not good enough for you.”</p>
<p>Later in the day, however, he had quite recovered his spirits, and they walked arm in arm through the woods. He told her of his recent successes, culminating with the car incident. She was impressed and amused, but also morally shocked by the irresponsibility of risking a fatal accident merely to test his powers. At the same time she was obviously fascinated by the fanaticism that drove him to such lengths. He was flattered by her interest, and intoxicated by her tenderness and her physical proximity. For they were now resting on the little knoll where he intended to do his trick with the car, and he was lying with his head in her lap, gazing up at her face, where all the love that his life had missed seemed to be gathered. He realized that he was playing the part of an infant rather than a lover. But she seemed to need him to do so, and he was happy in his role. But soon sexual desire began to reassert itself and with it masculine self-respect. He conceived an uncontrollable lust to demonstrate his godlike nature by some formidable display of his powers. He became the primitive savage who must kill an enemy in the presence of the beloved.</p>
<p>Looking up through Helen’s fluttering hair, he saw a small object moving. For a moment he took it for a gnat, then realized that it was a distant airplane approaching.</p>
<p>“Watch that plane,” he said; and she was startled by the abruptness of his voice. She looked up, and down again at him. His face was contorted with effort. His eyes glared, his nostrils dilated. She had an impulse to fling him from her, so brutal he looked. But fascination triumphed. “Keep your eyes on the plane,” he commanded. She looked up, then down, then up again. She knew she ought to break the devilish spell. (There was something called morality, but a delusion, probably.) Fascination had triumphed.</p>
<p>Presently the advancing plane’s four engines hesitated, and ceased one by one to fire. The plane glided for a while, but soon gave evidence of being out of control. It vacillated, staggered, and then was in a nose dive, spiralling. Helen screamed, but did nothing. The plane disappeared behind a distant wood. After a few seconds there was a muffled crash, and smoke began to rise from behind the wood, a leaning black plume.</p>
<p>Jim raised himself from Helen’s lap, and turning, pressed her backward to the ground. “That’s how I love you,” he whispered fiercely. Then he furiously kissed her lips, her neck.</p>
<p>She made a violent effort to pull herself together and resist the impulse of self-abandonment to this lunatic. She struggled to free herself from his grip; and presently the two stood facing each other, panting. “You’re mad,” she cried. “Think what you have done! You have killed people just to show how clever you are. And then you make love to me.” She covered her face with her hands and sobbed.</p>
<p>He was still in a state of crazy exaltation, and he laughed. Then he taunted her. “Call yourself a realist! You’re squeamish. Well, now you know what I am really like; and what I can do. And see! You’re mine. I can kill you at any moment, wherever you are. I shall do whatever I like with you. And if you try to stop me, you’ll go the way of the robin and — the man on the bus.” Her hands dropped from her tear-stained face. She stared at him in mingled horror — and tenderness. She said quietly, “You’re quite mad, you poor boy. And you seemed so gentle. Oh, my dear, what can I do about you?”</p>
<p>There was a long silence. Then suddenly Jim collapsed on the ground, blubbering like a child. She stood over him in perplexity.</p>
<p>While she was wondering what to do, and blaming herself for not breaking the spell before it was too late, he was in an agony of self-loathing. Then he started to use his technique upon himself, so that no more harm should be done. It was more difficult than he expected; for as soon as he began to lose consciousness he also lost his grip on the operation. But he made a desperate effort of will. When Helen, noticing his stillness, knelt down by him, he was dead.</p>
