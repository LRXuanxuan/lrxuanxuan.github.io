<h2>ii. 虔诚的保罗</h2>
<p>从最初那次不幸的冲击中恢复以后，保罗发现自己痛苦地在两种心境之间来回摆动。在一种心境里，他努力用一副崭新的犬儒主义铠甲保护受伤的灵魂：她不过是一只动物，她的行为不过是某些晦涩生理事件的表现；而他自己说到底也一样。自己曾如此珍视的“爱”，根本不存在任何内在的崇高价值。</p>
<p>可在另一种心境里，他却又紧紧抓住一种信念：即使自己的爱情已经触礁，爱本身——一个人对另一个人的相互洞察与崇敬——仍然是善，而且是生命中最高的善。其他一切善，要么微不足道，要么只是通往这种最高善的工具。此前许多星期里，他曾生活在一个过去根本够不到的精神高度；如今陷入孤独，他开始一遍遍翻看自己已经获得的宝藏：对一个名叫凯瑟琳的具体人类，形成了一种前所未有地鲜明、压倒性而又令人喜悦的认识。</p>
<p>孤独当然痛苦，但这份财富已经永远无法从他手里夺走。越是反复审视，他越忍不住追问：凯瑟琳这种存在究竟是一个例外，还是存在本身的一份正常样品？此前，他越来越习惯把宇宙理解成机械运动的复杂网络，以及散布群星的巨大黑暗；最多也只会把它理解成一股有生命却不具人格的趋势，推动万物走向某个人类根本无法设想的目标。</p>
<p>然而现在，他开始觉得，这一切都只是干旱荒地，因为那里没有给那种最卓越的东西——被爱点燃的人格——留下任何永久居所。终于，他以新的意义感到“爱是神圣的”。那句勇敢的断言“上帝就是爱”，在他眼里突然获得了一种清楚的新解释。</p>
<p>它一定意味着：人类所知道的最好事物，在超越人类范围的存在层次中也同样存在；意味着人的人格与人的爱情，既不是人格和爱的唯一形式，也不是最高形式；意味着那个大写的“爱”，不是简单存在于两个人之间的一种关系，而在某种意义上本身就是一个“人格”，一个无处不在、具有神性的“人格”，并且它才是宇宙真正的统治力量。</p>
<p>如果这确实是真的——而那么多人都声称自己相信——那么所有人类的爱，即便暂时受挫，最终也必定得到永恒圆满。甚至他对凯瑟琳的爱，以及凯瑟琳同样神圣、只不过如今转向别处的爱，也一定会在某种“永恒”之中获得成果。</p>
<p>我不准备在这里详细描述保罗心中犬儒冲动和虔敬冲动之间的斗争。那是一场不断反复的战斗。他把双手插在灰色法兰绒裤口袋里，胳膊夹着六七本书，沿高尔街漫步时，会随着伦敦稀薄的春日空气一起把快活吸进肺里；等绕过贝德福德广场，上帝似乎又重新安坐天堂。</p>
<p>到了查令十字路，他可能钻进那家被称作“炸弹店”的书店，又立刻撞上一批新的怀疑。后来卡维尔护士纪念碑将矗立在附近，以同时纪念她的勇气和她同胞的粗俗；保罗走过那片地点时，有时会突然受到一种短暂而无法理解的恐怖感染，而那恐怖其实来自我预见未来的心灵。不久，巨大火车站那种平静的忙碌又会让他稍稍安心；可在乘车返回南郊住处的路上，他却又会因为周围乘客脸上仿佛罩着绵羊、牛、猪和猴子的面具，再次跌入绝望。最后，当他赤着头走上俯瞰自家的郊区高地时，也许又能够——尽管已经有些疲惫——重新相信上帝。</p>
<p>在保罗看来，每当自己陷入犬儒状态，就会明确地变得更渺小、更卑贱、更低劣；而当他再次在自己心灵城垛上展开那面信仰“爱之上帝”的旗帜时，则觉得自己恢复了尊严。他许多同时代人也经历着类似的情绪摆动，而且他们和保罗一样，都误以为问题只存在两个选择：一边是经过现代化改造的旧信仰，另一边则是彻底放弃人的尊严。</p>
<p>然而，保罗和他的同时代人都错了。假如人的精神真有可能取得胜利，那么它必须战胜的地方，不是在信仰之中，而是在彻底的幻灭和厌恶之中。</p>
<p>正当保罗沉浸在宗教困惑里时，一群同学引起了他的兴趣。这些人认为，解决现代世界问题的方法，是把现代男女重新变成真诚的基督徒。他们把保罗介绍给一个年轻牧师，整个团体都把此人视为自己的精神领袖。</p>
<p>最初，这个年轻人用力过猛的握手和认真逼视，只让保罗产生另一种形式的反感与怀疑；很久以前，他面对家庭医生那种令人毛骨悚然的过分热情时，也有过类似感觉。他还很讨厌一件事：圈内这些“选民”私下里竟把自己的导师称为“大天使”。</p>
<p>不过，随着了解加深，保罗也像其他人一样逐渐落入了牧师的魅力。在这个开始崇敬人格、同时又开始厌恶“唯物主义”的新阶段里，这个人在保罗眼中仿佛一个精神贵族：他能够走进世界，却不被世界吞没，甚至连脚都不会弄脏。他是“属于另一个世界的人”，但不是因为想逃离这个世界，而是因为无论走到哪里，都仿佛随身带着一种不属于此世的空气。</p>
<p>就像某些水生昆虫潜进深水时，会随身带一个气泡供自己呼吸，这个牧师仿佛也把一团天上的以太带入尘世，用来维持自己的精神生命。</p>
<p>保罗是这样看的。但在他体内的海王星客人看来，事情并不完全一样。保罗那场恋爱期间，我当然也不得不忍受大量乏味和平庸的内容；但那对男女深处始终保持的单纯和真诚，不断给我带来清新感。到了这件新事件中，我却必须看着保罗沉溺于一种非常令人厌烦的自我欺骗。他让自己对年轻牧师这个具体人格的赞美，遮蔽了对宇宙本身的观察。</p>
<p>当然，这种偏离其实是成长中的必要阶段，也是为我后续操作准备实验培养基所不可缺少的步骤；但对观察者而言，仍然十分乏味。并不是说保罗新的热情完全误入歧途。远非如此。即使从海王星角度看，这个所谓“大天使”在有限意义上确实可以算精神贵族，因为他毫无疑问拥有大多数同类无法达到的洞察力与道德英雄主义。</p>
<p>然而，这是一个被环境败坏了的贵族。他没有能力抵抗一个精神上极其平庸的环境。生活中，他始终忠实表达自己所谓“上帝的超人性人道”；可是，对自己视野中另一种更严峻的东西，他却几乎完全没有给予公正评价。事实上，他甚至从来不敢向自己彻底承认这一面，因为若按照自身宗教教义来看，那会赤裸裸变成一种“超人性的非人性”景象。</p>
<p>保罗在许多场合频繁见到“大天使”：公共集会中，在追随者家里，也在那个牧师替码头区教区那些“未经雕琢的钻石”般男孩组织的少年俱乐部里。他也去参加牧师主持的礼拜。那些礼拜以一种非常矛盾的方式打动了保罗。</p>
<p>最初，他多少带着疑虑观察整个环境：人数稀少、略带异味的会众，他们几乎什么也不真正理解，却显然极度依恋牧师本人；退化而俗气的哥特式建筑；颜色刺眼的彩窗；那些有悠久历史、如今已经陈腐不堪，却仍能让保罗感动的音乐；穿白袍的小唱诗班孩子偷偷含着糖；祭坛上黄铜与天鹅绒构成的整套器具；外面大街交通传来的闷响；以及偶尔某艘轮船在泰晤士雾中朝中国或阿根廷摸索前进时响起的汽笛。</p>
<p>而这一切中央，站着身材高挑、白袍、金发的“大天使”，用那种压抑克制、却又能点燃听者的声音吟诵礼文。保罗处在虔诚状态时，那些早已被说烂的句子会因为他的声音突然变得新鲜、急迫，带着一种刺痛而近乎耀眼的清晰意义；可在较少出现的犬儒状态里，同样一套仪式听上去又和鹦鹉背熟的礼节短语没有更多意义。</p>
<p>“大天使”对保罗的影响，有一部分纯属身体层面。这个年轻人被牧师仍显健壮的体形、纤细而坚定的嘴唇、轮廓漂亮的鹰钩鼻吸引；在他眼里，这些外貌仿佛凝结了几个时代的正直。牧师的举止也极具吸引力。有时像一只凉爽的手放在额头上，令他安静；有时又猛地抓住他，把他摇醒，重新投入生命。</p>
<p>但真正最打动保罗的，是这个人对自己宗教信仰和实践那种崇高、近乎傲慢的自信，以及他面对迷路的“羊群”时，活泼、带玩笑意味的亲切。保罗本人的信仰很弱，对同类的爱也更多存在于理论而非实践。可“大天使”看起来是一个真正的基督徒。他确实实践自己宣讲的一切。</p>
<p>他真的爱别人，而且不是把他们只当作等待拯救的灵魂，而是把每个人视为独特个体。他确实能在每个人身上看见某种特殊而美丽的东西。他接受别人本来的样子，也像一个人自发照顾自身需要一样自发地为别人服务。通过他，保罗终于开始真正感受到对其他“人类动物”的温暖；而获得这种感受本身，又让他兴奋地相信自己确实正在升到一种更宽广、更慷慨的生活。</p>
<p>正因如此，他也格外愿意接受“大天使”宗教背后的形而上结论。这宗教本来也就是他童年时代的宗教，只不过从前几乎从未认真思考，却始终心不在焉地默认相信。</p>
<p>保罗开始觉得，自己现在终于以一种几乎像初等数学直觉般确定的方式看清：不仅宇宙的统治原则就是爱，而且这种爱的实际化身就是耶稣。更进一步，在“大天使”影响下——而牧师又因为保罗不断追问，被迫说出一些是否正统很可疑的话——保罗甚至断言，宇宙从头到尾都是天国性的，其中一切事物，包括撒旦，都共同服务于爱的完美表达，也就是耶稣本性的完美表达。</p>
<p>至于耶稣究竟具有怎样的本性，保罗并不是从《圣经》中学到，而是通过观察“大天使”本人学到的。这个牧师从早到晚显然一直被一种神圣主人的临在感支撑；凡靠近他的人，多少都会受到这种感觉感染。他散发出一种确信：从最终意义看，一切事物都恰如其分；可与此同时，他又能激起别人强烈热情，去纠正眼前明显错误的事情。</p>
<p>当然，他也绝不是彩窗里那种毫无血肉的圣徒。保罗骄傲又亲昵地意识到“大天使”非常鲜活的人性，甚至意识到他那些小小弱点；而这些弱点恰恰使崇拜者更加喜欢他。比如，他对葡萄干有一种古怪的热爱。经历一天艰难工作后，他会一把一把吃葡萄干，而且吃得近乎挑衅。它们对他仿佛同时具有葡萄在宗教上“神圣”和世俗上“可口”的双重吸引力。</p>
<p>一点一点地，保罗变成一个认真的基督徒。他并不是在头脑里，而更像是在胸腔深处进行这样的推理：“像‘大天使’这样完美的人，关于上帝怎么可能弄错？他太聪明了。难道他会骗我？他又太真诚、太有爱。如果这个人说上帝就是爱，那就一定是真的。”</p>
<p>每当怀疑重新出现，他都会跑去找“大天使”，从那里获得力量，再把怀疑消灭。有一次正是如此。两个人坐在牧师的起居室里谈话，壁炉中的火仿佛散发着乐观主义，女房东的猫睡在炉边地毯上。就在这一次，保罗经历了自己认为真正属于宗教的体验。</p>
<p>“大天使”一直耐心扼住他的怀疑。“保罗，牢牢抓住这一点，”他说，“只有爱真正重要。凡是世界中为了让爱能够存在而必须存在的东西，都因此得到了辩护。我们周围真正是什么样的世界？我说的不是你最爱的星星和星云，也不是你那些斤斤计较的自然定律。那些当然很奇妙，也是上帝房子的一部分。但它们只不过是地板。我说的是人的世界。你在那里看见什么？你会看见撒旦仍然逍遥在外。他一直如此，而且在时间终结以前永远如此。为什么？因为一种不必永远战斗的爱，根本算不上爱，就像还没出生的胎儿根本不能算成年人。因此，保罗，我们应该感谢上帝：正因为他如此爱我们，他才创造撒旦来折磨我们。”</p>
<p>牧师停了一下，又说：“这可是异端！你瞧，人要是把这些老问题想得太多，就会说成什么样。别想那些。只想基督。去感受他的神性。你现在难道感觉不到他就在这个房间里吗？”</p>
<p>“大天使”抬起手，望着保罗，仿佛自己正在聆听天使合唱。有一会儿，两人都沉默不语。渐渐地，保罗开始觉得整个房间都活了起来，四处发光，也仿佛被某种临在低声充满。</p>
<p>这是眼前这个人的临在吗？是的，可一定也同时是耶稣的临在。只是这个房间吗？不，是整个宇宙。仿佛整个宇宙都在某种方式中被收拢进这个小房间，而且宇宙整体明显被神圣的爱彻底浸透。</p>
<p>一切都温暖、明亮、温柔而真实；或者，一切都在英勇地战胜“恶”，而那个恶不仅被击败，甚至以某种方式显明为从来就不真正是恶。对于正在崇拜中的保罗来说，连群星以及宇宙外部那些宇宙都像羊羔一样蜂拥进这个小房间，来接受这位牧羊人、这位至高“大天使”耶稣的安慰。</p>
<p>它们带着各自小小的烦恼，脚痛、胃痛；耶稣则用爱的魔法替它们治好这些小麻烦，奇迹般治到仿佛那些痛苦从来没有真正存在，只不过是给他的爱提供一次表现机会。保罗和诸宇宙像一群小白羊，一起依偎在这个“爱”的怀抱里。</p>
<p>他再一次觉得自己接触到了现实；而这一次，自己一定已经真正穿透到宇宙最深的核心。经验异常奇妙，也异常陌生，却同时拥有一种神秘的熟悉感，像来自某段早已遗忘的存在；那段存在遥远到仿佛根本不处在时间之内。</p>
<p>保罗觉得一股巨大能量涌进身体。他感受到一种古老而渴望已久的信仰力量。经历那么多年贫瘠以后，他终于重新充满一种新的生命力，像电池被完全充足，已经准备好把自己投入一场全新的、发光的生命。</p>
<p>微笑着、仿佛变了形的“大天使”把一只手放在保罗膝盖上。保罗说：“我终于感觉到耶稣了。”</p>
<p>然而，当保罗和“大天使”把自己交给这场迷人的体验时，房间里确实有一个“临在”同他们在一起，只不过性质与这两只地球动物自己在想象中召唤出来的东西完全不同。</p>
<p>那是一个人的临在：他曾看见一个又一个人类物种从混沌中艰难抢出一点舒适和一点不可靠的信念，随后又一次次崩塌进苦难、绝望，甚至痛苦至极的境地。他本人还预见着“人”即将彻底灭绝；而在这一事件、乃至一切存在中，他确实看见压倒一切的美，却并不认为这能给一个因爱而痛苦的人类个体带来任何安慰。</p>
<p>很奇怪，反倒正是我在保罗心中的存在——我这个冷静审视他的存在——给了他“自己正在与爱之上帝结合”的感觉一种实际真实性。牧师的劝告使他注意到自己内部一种模糊感觉：此前有些时刻，它已经短暂扰乱过他，使他觉得自己仿佛以某种方式被“附体”。</p>
<p>现在，我发现他好像在窥视自身深处，寻找这感觉的来源，并且相信自己在那里将会看见耶稣充满爱意的目光。假如他真的发现占据自己身体的究竟是什么，假如他可以说和自己这个海王星寄生者面对面相见，那整场异象必定立即粉碎，而他毫无疑问会把我当成魔鬼。</p>
<p>因此，我极力躲避他的察觉。可以说，我在精神上屏住呼吸，唯恐自己心灵任何一点轻微活动，都会让他看见比那团模糊“临在”更多的东西；而他刚刚把这团临在以如此奇异的方式误解了。</p>
<p>对我来说，维持完全静止并不容易，因为保罗的狂热景象深深触动了我，使我同时想要怜悯，又想发笑。究竟发生了什么？先撇开我这个“临在”所带来的复杂因素——它给他的幻象提供了一层虚假真实性——保罗的这次经验本身，就是第一人类整个宗教史的缩影：一堆彼此原本并无关系的因素，被可怜地混在一起。</p>
<p>首先，部分由于我早已向他灌输极其丰富的宇宙意象，保罗确实真正面对过宇宙的庄严。其次，他同凯瑟琳相处时，也确实非常鲜明地体验过一种被称为“爱”的伟大卓越。</p>
<p>但基督教传统通过他敬爱的牧师作用在他身上，又同他自己的愿望相结合，结果说服他把“爱”本身归给那个无情的宇宙。这种混淆，还有一部分是他自身极遥远过去对他开的玩笑。因为此刻宗教体验中那种陌生熟悉感、甜美安慰与和平，说到底不过是他早年模糊渴望乳房温柔与子宫乐园的一道回声。</p>
<p>从此以后，保罗把自己看成基督的士兵。他发动了一场禁欲主义与全面纪律化的运动。例如，他给自己制定规则，限制进食。每当真能遵守规则，唯一结果就是每顿饭结束时都像狼一样饥饿，而且全天都被食物念头纠缠。</p>
<p>他又尝试睡在卧室地板上，可每次还没到半夜，就带着罪恶感偷偷爬回床里。整个自律运动始终半心半意，也几乎毫无效果，因为他根本不真正相信这些行为具有精神功效。他做这些事，不是因为一个饥渴寻找上帝的灵魂真心需要它们，而只是因为认真信教的人“应该”这样做，而他想把自己变成其中一员。</p>
<p>保罗很喜欢替“大天使”做各种零碎工作，也开始去少年俱乐部帮忙。不幸的是，他很快发现自己根本不擅长和男孩们打交道，因为他几乎没有他们欣赏的任何特质。他拳击不行，台球不行，嘴上斗狠也不行；更缺少威信，因为心底其实害怕这些男孩。</p>
<p>但为了“大天使”，也为了自我纪律，他仍坚持留在俱乐部。最后，他负责小卖部。男孩们会用半便士之类的小账占他便宜；每晚结账时，他又卑怯地从自己口袋里补上损失。卖咖啡和面包卷的空档，他会躲在柜台后读书。但这种偷偷阅读又让他良心不安；只要“大天使”在附近，他便会把书收起来，努力和男孩们表现得亲切随和。</p>
<p>保罗成功说服自己：几乎所有行为，都是那个最高动机的表达——要在现代世界做一个基督士兵。但事实上，他有许多完全独立的兴趣。他同“大天使”那一小群学生崇拜者一起，过着丰富、经常非常欢闹的生活；也经历过几段试探性的恋爱。他学习勤奋，也取得一些成绩；大量阅读当代文学和通俗科学。只是凡涉及科学思考，他都为了宗教而进行极其严格的自我审查。</p>
<p>保罗的宗教热情最主要的表达，并不是行动，而是写一种近似《圣经》语气的自由诗。他劝自己相信，自己有一种必须把精神经验化成语言的“冲动”，自己正受到“启示”。</p>
<p>但在寄生者看来很清楚，这个阶段的作品并不是被强烈经验从他体内硬生生拧出来的。这些诗不过是文学练习，技巧上模仿别人，也极少被真正原创的想象赋予生命。“大天使”会赞赏这些文字，因为思想感情都很“正确”；但他只把它们看成游戏，而不是一个男人替上帝完成的工作。他看不出来，尽管这些诗本身失败了，对保罗而言却非常重要。下面值得举一首，因为它很好地说明保罗此时的心态。</p>
<p>人</p>
<p>看哪，人之子民，</p>
<p>他们犯罪，</p>
<p>可他们的心却属于神！</p>
<p>他们因自私而把苦难堆给彼此；</p>
<p>却也为了爱而死。</p>
<p>他们像枯叶一样被风吹来吹去；</p>
<p>却也为了爱而站稳不动。</p>
<p>他们为了面包践踏彼此；</p>
<p>却也为了一个梦而死。</p>
<p>把黄金撒进他们中间，他们便成为野兽；</p>
<p>把上帝指给他们看，他们便成为上帝之子。</p>
</section>

<h2>ii. PAUL DEVOUT</h2>
<p>WHEN P AUL HAD recovered from the first shock of his misfortune, he emerged to find himself painfully oscillating between two moods. In the one he strove to protect his wounded soul under a bright new armour of cynicism. She was just an animal, and her behaviour was the expression of obscure physiological events. And so, after all, was his own. The love that he so prized had no intrinsic virtue whatever. In the other mood, however, he clung to the faith that though this love of his had foundered, nevertheless love, the mutual insight and worship of one human person for another, was good in itself, and indeed the supreme good of life. All other goods, it seemed, ‘were either negligible, or instrumental to this greatest good. He had recently lived for many weeks on a plane that was formerly beyond him. Now in his loneliness he began to go over again and again the treasure that he had acquired, namely his new and overwhelmingly vivid and delightful apprehension of a particular human being, called Katherine. Bitter as was his loneliness, this treasure could never be taken from him. And the more he pored over it, the more he asked himself whether it was exceptional, or whether it was a fair sample of existence. Hitherto he had increasingly thought of the universe in terms of mechanical intricacies and the huge star-sprinkled darkness, or at best in terms of a vital but impersonal trend of all things toward some goal inconceivable to man. But now he began to regard all this as mere aridity in which there was no abiding place for the one superlative excellence, human personality, love-inspired. It was borne in upon him at last with new significance that love was’ divine ‘. He began to find a new and lucid meaning for the brave statement, ‘God is Love ‘. It meant, surely, that this best of all things known to man was also present in spheres beyond man’s sphere; that human personality and love were not the only nor the highest forms of personality and love; that Love, with a capital L, Love which was not merely a relation between persons but somehow itself a Person, an all-pervading and divine Person, was after all the governing power of the universe. If this were indeed so, as many professed to believe, then all human loves must, in spite of temporary frustration, be secure of eternal fulfilment. Even his love for Katherine and her no less divine, though now distracted, love for him must somehow, ‘in eternity’, have its fruition.</p>
<p>I must not here describe the struggle that took place in Paul’s mind between his cynical and his devotional impulses. It was a fluctuating battle. Wandering along Gower Street, with his hands in the pockets of his grey flannel trousers, and half a dozen books under his arm, he would breathe in cheerfulness with the dilute spring air of London, till, as he pursued his course round Bedford Square, God was once more in his heaven. In Charing Cross Road he would stray into the ‘Bomb Shop’, and be confronted with new doubts. Walking over the spot where subsequently Nurse Cavell’s monument was to proclaim her courage and her countrymen’s vulgarity, the poor boy would sometimes be infected by a momentary and unintelligible horror, derived from my own foreseeing mind. Soon the placid bustle of the great railway station would bring comfort once more; but on the journey to his southern suburb he would be flung again into despair by the faces of sheep, cattle, pigs and monkeys that masked the spirits of his fellow-travellers. Then at last, walking bare-headed on the suburban down that overhung his home, Paul might once more, though rather wearily, believe in God.</p>
<p>It seemed to Paul that in his cynical mood he was definitely smaller, meaner and more abject than when he was once more unfurling on the battlements of his own heart the banner of his faith in the God of Love. In many of his contemporaries also much the same fluctuation of mood was occurring, and to them as to Paul it seemed that the issue lay between the old faith, however modernized, and the complete abnegation of human dignity. Yet Paul and his contemporaries were mistaken. It was not in faith but in utter disillusionment and disgust that the human spirit had to triumph, if ever it was to triumph at all.</p>
<p>While he was absorbed in his religious perplexity, Paul was intrigued by a group of fellow-students whose aim it was to solve the troubles of the modern world by making modern men and women into sincere Christians. They introduced him to a young priest, whom the whole group regarded as their spiritual leader. At first this young man’s emphatic hand-grip and earnest gaze roused in Paul nothing but a new variant of the disgust and suspicion which, long ago, he had felt toward the ghastly heartiness of the family doctor. He was also repelled by the fact that the elect secretly referred to their master as the Archangel. But as he became better acquainted with the priest, he, like the others, began to fall under his spell. To Paul in his new phase of reverence for human personality, his new revulsion from ‘materialism’, this man appeared as a spiritual aristocrat, as one who could move about the world without being swallowed up by the world, without so much as dirtying his feet. He was ‘other-worldly’, not in the sense that he sought to escape from this world, but that he carried round with him an atmosphere which was not this world’s atmosphere. Like those water-insects which take down with them into the deep places a bubble of air for breathing, he took down with him into this world a celestial ether to maintain his spiritual life.</p>
<p>So it seemed to Paul. But to his Neptunian guest the matter did not appear in the same light. During Paul’s love affair I had of course to put up with much that was tedious and banal, but I had been constantly refreshed by the underlying simplicity and sincerity of the amorous couple. In the new incident, however, I had to watch Paul indulging in a very tiresome self-deception. He allowed his admiration for the person of this young priest to obscure his view of the universe. This aberration was indeed a necessary phase in his growth, a necessary process in the preparation of the experimental culture upon which I was to operate. But it was none the less a tiresome phase for the observer. Not that Paul’s new enthusiasm was wholly misguided. Far from it. Even from the Neptunian point of view, this ‘Archangel’ was indeed in a limited sense a spiritual aristocrat, for undoubtedly he was gifted with a vision and a moral heroism impossible to most of his fellows. But he was an aristocrat debased by circumstance. He had not been able to resist an environment which was spiritually plebeian. Though in his life he faithfully expressed what he called the superhuman humanity of his God, he almost wholly failed to do justice to another and more austere feature of his own vision, a feature which indeed he never dared fully to acknowledge, even to himself, since in terms of his own religious dogma it appeared starkly as a vision of superhuman inhumanity.</p>
<p>Paul saw the Archangel often and in many circumstances, in public meetings, at the homes of his followers, and at the boys’ club which the priest had organized for the young ‘rough diamonds’ of his dockland parish. He saw him also at his church services. These impressed Paul in a perplexing manner. He noted, at first with some misgiving, the setting in which they took place, the scant and rather smelly congregation, so uncomprehending, but so obviously devoted to the person of the priest; the debased Gothic architecture and strident coloured windows; the music, so historic, so trite, yet to Paul so moving, the surpliced urchins of the choir, furtively sucking sweets, the paraphernalia of the altar, brass and velvet; the muffled noise of traffic in the great thoroughfare outside; and the occasional interruptions by some ship’s steam-whistle as she nosed her way through the Thames fog toward China or the Argentine. In the midst of all this stood the tall, white-robed, fair-haired Archangel, intoning with that restrained yet kindling voice of his, which to Paul in his more devout mood made the hackneyed words of the service novel, urgent, significant with a piercing, blinding lucidity. In his rarer cynical mood, however, the same performance seemed no more meaningful than the ritual phrases of a parrot.</p>
<p>The Archangel’s influence on Paul was partly physical. The younger man was attracted by the still athletic figure, the delicate firm lips, the finely cut aquiline nose. They seemed to him to embody ages of righteousness. In the priest’s manner, too, he found a strong attraction. It soothed him, like a cool hand on the brow. Yet also it gripped him and shook him into life. But chiefly Paul was impressed by the man’s sublime confidence, almost arrogance, in his own religious faith and practice, and by his lively bantering affection for the straying sheep of his flock. Paul’s faith was weak. His love of his fellow-men was more theoretical than practical. But the Archangel, seemingly, was a real Christian. He practised what he preached. He really did love his fellows, not merely as savable souls but as unique individuals. He really did see something peculiar and beautiful in each person. He accepted others as he found them, and served them with the same spontaneity as a man serves his own needs. Through him Paul began at last to feel a real warmth toward his fellow human animals, and in doing so he felt exultantly that he was definitely rising to an ampler and more generous life. Because of this he became extremely ready to receive the metaphysical implications of the Archangel’s religion, which also had been the religion of his own childhood, seldom seriously contemplated but always absent-mindedly believed.</p>
<p>Paul fancied that he now saw at last, with almost the intuitive certainty of elementary mathematics, not only that the governing principle of the universe was love, but that the actual embodiment of that love was Jesus. Moreover, under the influence of the Arch-angel, who had been stimulated by Paul’s searching questions to make statements of doubtful orthodoxy, Paul now affirmed that the universe was celestial through and through, that all things in it, including Satan, worked together for the perfect expression of love, the perfect expression of the nature of Jesus. And the nature of Jesus, Paul learned not from the Bible but from contemplation of the Archangel. For the Archangel was very obviously sustained from morning till evening by a sense of the presence of his divine master. All who came near him were infected with something of this sense. He radiated a conviction of the ultimate rightness of all things, and at the same time he fired men with a zeal for putting the apparently wrong things right. Not that he was a mere stained-glass saint. Paul was proudly, lovingly conscious of the Archangel’s vivid humanity, even of his little weaknesses, which served greatly to endear him to his admirers. He had, for instance, a quaint passion for raisins. After a trying day he would eat them by the handful, almost defiantly. They seemed to have for him the double attraction of the grape’s sacred and profane significance.</p>
<p>Little by little Paul became an earnest Christian. Deep down in his breast, rather than in his mind, he argued thus, ‘Can such a perfect being as the Archangel be mistaken about God? He is too wise. Can he be deceiving me? He is too sincere, too loving. If this man says that God is Love, it is so.’ If ever his doubts returned, he would run to the Archangel to gain strength to abolish them. Once when this had happened, and the two were talking in the priest’s sitting-room, before a fire that radiated optimism, while the landlady’s cat lay asleep on the hearth-rug, Paul had what he considered a real religious experience. The Archangel had been patiently throttling Paul’s doubts. ‘Hang on to this, Paul,’ he said, ‘Love alone matters. And whatever is needed in the world for the existence of love is justified. What kind of a world is it that we actually find around us? I don’t mean your beloved stars and nebulae and your pettifogging laws of nature. No doubt they are marvellous, and all part of God’s house. But they are only the floor-boards. I mean the human world. What do you find there? You find Satan still at large. He always was, and always will be, till time is finished. And why? Because love that has not got to be for ever fighting is no more love than the unborn babe is a man. And so, Paul, we must thank God that, of his great love for us, he made Satan to torment us.’ He paused, then continued: ‘That’s heresy! You see what happens if one thinks too much about these old problems. Think of Christ only. Feel his god-head. Can’t you feel his presence in this room now?’</p>
<p>The Archangel raised his hand and looked at Paul as though listening to angel choirs. For some moments both remained silent. Gradually it came to seem to Paul that the room was all alive, all aglow, all a-murmur with a presence. Was it the presence of this man? Yes, but surely also it was the presence of Jesus. The room? Nay, the universe. The whole universe, it seemed, was somehow gathered into that room, and the whole of it was manifestly infused with the divine love. Everything was warm and bright, tender and true, or else heroically triumphant over an Evil that was not merely defeated but somehow shown never to have been really evil at all. To the adoring Paul it seemed that the very stars and outer universes came flocking into that little room, like lambs, to be comforted by this Shepherd, this supreme Archangel Jesus. They brought with them their little troubles, their sore feet and their stomachaches, and he, with the magic of his love, cured these little troubles so miraculously that they never had existed at all, save as occasions for his love. Paul and the universes nestled together like little white lambs in the bosom of this Love. Once more it seemed to him that he had made contact with reality, that he had penetrated this time surely to the very heart of the universe. The experience was very wonderful, very strange, yet also mysteriously familiar, with the familiarity of some forgotten existence, an existence so remote that it seemed entirely outside time. It seemed to Paul that an immense energy flooded in upon him. He felt in himself the ancient, the longed-for strength of faith. At last, after all his barren years, he was tense with a new vitality, fully charged, ready to spend himself in a new effulgent life.</p>
<p>The smiling, transfigured Archangel laid a hand on Paul’s knee. Paul said, ‘At last I feel Jesus.’</p>
<p>Now while Paul and the Archangel were yielding themselves to this glamorous experience, there was indeed a presence’ with them in the room, though one of a very different nature from that which these two Terrestrial animals themselves had conjured in their imaginations. It was the presence of a man who had seen human species after human species wrest for itself out of chaos some slight amenity and some precarious faith, only to collapse into misery, into despair, often into agony. It was the presence of one who himself looked forward to the impending extinction of Man; who found in this event, and in all existence, overmastering beauty indeed, but for the love-sick human individual no consolation. Strange that it was my presence in Paul’s mind, my coldly scrutinizing presence, that had lent actuality to his sense of union with the God of Love! The exhortation of the priest had made him notice in himself an obscure feeling which had already on earlier occasions fleetingly disturbed him with a sense that in Some way he was ‘possessed’. I now found him peering, as it were, into the recesses of his being in search of the source of this feeling, believing that what he would see would be the love-gaze of Jesus. Had he discovered what it was that actually’ possessed’ him, had he come, so to speak, face to face with his Neptunian parasite, his vision would have been shattered, and no doubt he would have taken me for the Devil. But I was at pains to elude detection. Mentally I held my breath, as it were, lest any slight movement of my mind should reveal more of me than that vague’ presence’ which he had so fantastically misinterpreted.</p>
<p>It was not easy for me to maintain my immobility, for the spectacle of Paul’s fervour stirred me deeply, both toward pity and toward laughter. For what was it that was happening to him? Apart from the complication of my presence’, which lent a spurious actuality to his vision, his experience was an epitome of the religious history of the First Human Species, a pitiable confusion of factors irrelevant to one another. In the first place Paul, partly through the wealth of cosmic imagery with which I had already drenched him, had indeed come face to face with the majesty of the universe. In the second place he had with Katherine experienced very vividly the great excellence which is called love. But the Christian tradition, working on him through his revered priest, had combined with his own desire so as to persuade him to attribute love to the pitiless universe itself. This confusion was caused partly by a trick played upon him by his own remote past. For the strange familiarity and the delicious consolation and peace which Paul had savoured in this religious moment were after all but an echo of his own obscure yearning for the tenderness of the breast and the elysium of the womb.</p>
<p>Henceforth Paul thought of himself as a soldier of Christ. He undertook a campaign of asceticism and general discipline. For instance, he made rules to limit his eating. When he succeeded in keeping them, the only result was that he rose from every meal with a wolfish craving, and thoughts of food haunted him throughout the day. He tried sleeping on his bedroom floor, but always before the night was over he crept guiltily into bed. His self-discipline was always half-hearted and ineffective, for he had no real faith in its spiritual efficacy. He did it, not with the earnestness of the God-hungry soul, but because earnestly religious persons were supposed to do so, and he wanted to be one of them.</p>
<p>Paul found great satisfaction in doing odd jobs for the Archangel. He took up work at the boys’ club. Unfortunately he soon found that he was not much good with boys, having few of the attributes which they admired. He was useless at boxing and billiards, useless at back-chat. And he had no authority, for in his heart he was frightened of the boys. But for the Archangel’s sake, and also for self-discipline, he stuck to the club. Finally he took charge of the canteen. He was cheated over halfpence, and at the end of the evening he basely made up the losses out of his own pocket. In intervals of selling coffee and buns be sat behind the counter reading. But this furtive practice caused him much heart-searching and when the Archangel was about, he put his book away and tried to be genial with the boys.</p>
<p>Paul succeeded in persuading himself that nearly all his actions were expressions of his master-motive, to be a soldier of Christ in the modern world. But as a matter of fact he had many quite independent interests. He lived a varied and often hilarious life with the little band of the Archangel’s student admirers. He had also several tentative amorous passages. He worked industriously and with some success. He read much contemporary literature and some popular science. But all his scientific thought he censored rigorously for religion’s sake.</p>
<p>Paul’s religious fervour expressed itself chiefly not in action but in writing free verse of a quasi-biblical character. He persuaded himself that he had an ‘urge’ to produce verbal formulations of his spiritual experience, that he was’ inspired’. But to his parasite it was clear that what he produced in this phase was not wrung from him by the intensity of experience. These poems were but literary exercises, imitative in technique, and seldom vitalized by any original imagination. The Archangel applauded these effusions because they were Correct in sentiment, but he regarded them as merely play, not as a man’s work for God. He could not perceive that though they failed they meant much to Paul. It is worth while to give one example for the light which it throws on Paul’s mind at this time.</p>
<p>MEN</p>
<p>Behold the sons of men.</p>
<p>who sin.</p>
<p>whose hearts are divine!</p>
<p>In selfishness they heap misery on one another;</p>
<p>yet for love they die.</p>
<p>They are blown about like dead leaves;</p>
<p>yet for love they stand firm.</p>
<p>For bread they trample one another.</p>
<p>yet for a dream they die.</p>
<p>Scatter gold among them, and they are beasts;</p>
<p>show them God, and they are sons of God.</p>
