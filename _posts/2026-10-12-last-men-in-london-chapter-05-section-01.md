<h2>i. 海王星人对战争的态度</h2>
<p>在你们荷马式的史诗里，人类的冲突总有一些看不见却强大的存在在旁观望，并暗中左右。每个英雄的成就，不仅取决于他自己的德性，也取决于某位偏爱他的神祇给了什么灵感、又施了什么手段。诸神各自站队：有的帮助希腊人，有的帮助特洛伊人。</p>
<p>而在我将要描述的那场战争里——欧洲战争，那场号称“终结一切战争的战争”，也是把你们这个物种推上衰亡道路的重要事件——每一次局势转折，同样都被某些无形存在注意着；不过这些看不见的观察者既不是神，也不偏袒任何一方。特洛伊平原上曾有诸神注目；同样，在佛兰德斯平原，在皮卡第、香槟和阿尔贡的丘陵，在蒂罗尔荒凉险峻的高地，在俄罗斯的沼泽和草原；又同样在阿拉伯、两河流域和萨洛尼卡，在海上和高空——事实上，凡是战争直接存在，或者其影响间接抵达的地方，都有某些无人察觉的存在在观看。</p>
<p>他们不是那种心智比创造自己的凡人还低劣的男神女神，不会只为了自己那点虽属天界却同样狭隘的争斗，就拿人类当棋子。他们是真正的人类心灵。他们并不试图让这边或那边获胜；他们只想记录并体味事态那不可避免的进程，同时也想在你们当中那些能够接受其影响的人身上，点燃一点他们自己那种高昂兴致的微光。</p>
<p>我们第一次研究你们这场战争时，经常会在那个时代发现一些当时无法解释的现象；它们仿佛暗示着，我们自己某种尚未来临的影响已经作用其中——也就是说，从海王星人的时间位置来看，那时我们其实还没有来得及施加这种影响。举一个最明显的例子：最初研究时，我们始终不明白，为什么萨洛尼卡那段战争没有按原本可能出现的规模继续发展。后来，随着海王星时间向前推进，我们才发现责任竟在我们自己。因为到了那个时刻，我们发现若干身居陆海军高位的人恰好能够接受我们的影响。于是我们把他们推向迟钝和无所作为，故意阻止了一连串后来本可能发生、而且会比真实历史更加令我们厌恶的事件。</p>
<p>不过，这种大规模影响极少可能实现。而且，我们后来对于使用它也已经谨慎到了近乎畏缩的程度；因为不止一次，我们事后都为自己的干预感到后悔。</p>
<p>我在别处已经说过，在我们的世界里，一个成熟个体已经完全摆脱私人利己主义的陷阱。他的意志指向整个物种的福祉。然而，在忠于“人的精神”与虔敬地接受命运实际结果之间发生冲突时，他的心仍有可能被撕裂。即使作为个体，我们也能超越那种构成一切行动根源的人类忠诚；而作为种族心灵时，这一点更为清楚。我们能够在宇宙自身的形式中看见一种美，其高度凌驾于任何人类胜利之上。</p>
<p>所以，尽管我们面对你们时代的事件时，已经完全超脱于你们那许多古老目标之外，我们仍然会同你们一起感受你们作为一个物种、试图变得更加真正“人类”的挣扎。你们失败时，或者我们未能帮助你们时，我们会悲伤。每当我们在你们世界里采取行动，也经常被怀疑撕扯：我们究竟会改善这个物种的命运，还是反而将它毁坏？</p>
<p>不过，在我们这里，怜悯和疑虑始终被另一种直觉所调和：从最高的视野看来，整个存在虽然严酷，却是卓越的。即使作为个体，我们也确信宇宙具有无上的美；但在那种独一无二的种族经验里，这一洞见会发展得远为充分，并且会对作为个体的我们产生一种非常奇特、近乎至福的影响。</p>
<p>如果我们的记忆可信，那么，当种族心灵回望已经完成的全部事件纹理时，它会发现每一件事情都是宇宙美之中不可缺少的一个特征。在那种最高体验里，人仿佛以一种全新的辨别力观看宇宙；对于过去已经发生的一切，他都不愿它与事实有任何不同。</p>
<p>因此看起来，凡是我们自己曾经影响过历史进程的地方，那种影响确实对宇宙之美作出了贡献；而在我们克制自己没有出手，或者出手却没能实现计划的地方，那种不作为与失败也同样是最好的，也同样参与形成了那种只有种族心灵才能清楚看见的超越性完善。</p>
<p>但请不要以为，既然看来凡已经发生之事在宇宙尺度上都是正确的，那么人的努力就没有必要。恰恰只有通过努力，宇宙这一宏大主题才能继续展开。同时，似乎又有某种没有向我们显现的影响，在控制所有人类努力的最终结果，使它们无论以失败还是胜利告终，都实际参与塑造整体那卓越而完满的形式。</p>
<p>我说的是“似乎”。因为即使对我们而言，也并不清楚事情是否必然如此。只不过到目前为止，我们精神上的高度越是增长，就越频繁、越清楚地体验到：事实确实如此。</p>
<p>现在，让我们回到你们的战争。也许根本无需说明：在那些极少数我们确实干预过战争进程的场合，我们从来没有仅仅为了让一边战胜另一边而行动。唯一的例外，是偶尔会有某个不幸的观察者受地球心态感染太深，以至于完全失去了海王星人的完整性与理解力。这样的灾难很少发生。通常，我们的观察者都能完好保持自己的理智与超然。</p>
<p>你们也许会以为：既然在你们第一次世界大战前后，我们的观察者如此大量地集中出现，那么我们对这场战争的兴趣，大概和你们通常对它的兴趣一样。若如此想，你们就错了。</p>
<p>我们确实进入过你们面对战争时的感受。你们怎样感受痛苦和绝望、荣耀与羞耻，我们也同样完整地感受到，因为我们直接参与了这些经验。不过在整个过程中，我们通常都保留着自己的超然。那些通过你们的心灵被我们尝到、仿佛足以毁灭整个世界、甚至让上帝本身都受到谴责的事件，我们同时也看见了它们更广阔的关系，并知道它们不过是那伟大整体中显微镜下才看得见的细节。</p>
<p>因此，我们能够带着一种同情与反讽混合的态度来看待你们的痛苦和激情。这种态度在较低层次上，就像成年人面对儿童游戏时的狂热，或者他们因为育儿室里某点小烦恼而遭受的悲伤。</p>
<p>例如，你们将军的战略，在我们看来只具有这样一种兴趣：就像你们自己的心理学家观察类人猿被最简单的智力问题难住。黑猩猩面对放在够不着地方的水果，也许会看出，也许看不出木棍、木箱或绳子的用途；同样，你们的统帅们也痛苦地偶尔看见、更多时候却完全错过那些军事可能性，而它们对于任何一个海王星智能来说从一开始就显而易见。</p>
<p>一次又一次，我们看见那些肩章闪闪发亮的统帅，被连你们自己较聪明的人都未必不能解决的问题堵住去路。一次又一次，因为某个战略家或战术家愚蠢，甚至仅仅因为他个人的虚荣，成千上万条生命被毁掉，代价惨重才获得的军事成果又被放弃。</p>
<p>虽说这种背叛本来就是我们前来研究的材料，而且通常我们也能够平静面对，但我们所有人都遇到过这样的时刻：这类失误会激起一种带着娱乐意味的恼怒；或者，如果一个观察者已经在地球心态中浸泡太久，甚至会突然产生真正的暴怒。</p>
<p>你们战时的政治人物，比将军们更清楚地向我们展示了你们这个物种根本性的软弱。我们对于这场战争的政治层面本身并没有多大兴趣；只有当政治现象表现出你们天性中远比政治更深的因素时，我们才会注意。</p>
<p>在你们政治领袖的种种举止里，甚至比在普通人的生活中更清楚地，我们看见私人利益、爱国主义，以及另一种更新、更艰难的忠诚之间那种隐蔽而毁灭性的斗争。后一种忠诚首先服务于整个物种，或者说，服务于作为“人”的那种本质精神。</p>
<p>在普通人身上，公共利益和私人利益之间的冲突没有那些制定国家与军事政策的人身上那么戏剧化，后果也没有那么重大。按理说，行为会决定巨大问题的人，应当更有能力严格控制自利倾向；他们行动所具有的巨大公共意义，也应当迫使他们不断以毫不留情的洞察审查自己的动机。</p>
<p>然而并非如此。许多表面上完全为了国家利益作出的决策，实际却由嫉妒、妒恨或自尊心决定。而我们发现，在绝大多数情况下，作决定的人本人却从未怀疑过自己的诚实。</p>
<p>同样，正如你们后来自己也逐渐认识到的，那些用世界主义理想或宗教语言表达出来的政策，实际上往往只受民族侵略性、民族恐惧，或者某种甚至更加见不得人的私人动机驱使。</p>
<p>既然如此，你们战争时代的外交史对我们而言就根本不是“高级政治”的记录，而只是纠缠成团的一堆心理学资料。</p>
<p>你们自己对战争的兴趣，经常不过是一种对于恐怖的贪欲，是对流血和毁灭的迷恋。可在我们看来，你们那些恐怖并不算什么。因为我们沿着人类历史的漫长年代上下观察，已经见过许多更加彻底、更加惨烈的毁灭。</p>
<p>我们看见过文明程度远超你们梦想的物种，在迅速的灾难里被彻底消灭，也看见过他们在漫长灾难中一点点灭绝。我们看见过整个人口在肉体痛苦中翻滚、尖叫。与几十年之后等待着你们的那些事情相比，你们这种规模有限、还相当“无菌”的相互屠杀甚至显得近乎家常、近乎温和，所以它并不会因为恐怖本身而吸引我们。</p>
<p>当然，它具有一切人类悲剧都会具有的感染力；但无论从规模还是从痛苦强度看，都并不特别。通过你们的心灵和你们的肉体，我们确实也会把它体验成独一无二、足以打碎世界并判世界有罪的事件。战争那些年压在许多人身上的绝望痛苦，我们也非常充分地品尝过。</p>
<p>我们会在想象中收缩自己的视野，把它缩进你们被战争封锁的眼界里，于是也完整进入由此产生的厌世。甚至，在真正进入这种眼界以后，有时反倒是我们震惊于你们感受得还不够深。因为即使你们之中最敏感的人，也受到自己粗糙神经组织的保护；正因为这种组织粗糙，他的心理也随之迟钝而麻木，所以他实际上并没有承受与自己短视的世界厌恶相称的全部痛苦。</p>
<p>那么，如果既不是恐怖，也不是政治或军事兴趣，究竟是什么让我们成千上万地涌到你们的战争里观察？也许是你们个人的英雄行为，是为了某种事业而超越自我的奉献？又或者，是你们偶尔对敌人表现出的慷慨，是你们摸索着摆脱民族主义迷狂、尝试感受人类统一性的那些努力？</p>
<p>你们战争里凡真正英勇、真正忠诚的东西，我们当然都带着敬意观看过。即使我们不得不对这些英雄主义被浪费在什么目标上微笑，我们仍然承认：就其程度而言，它确实是英雄主义；而对于你们这种精神高度的存在，这是一种珍贵而艰难的德性。</p>
<p>我们也忠实记录了你们之中每一次超越民族主义的时刻。不过，我们不能同意你们把自己的英雄主义视为人类成就真正的花冠，也不能把你们偶尔的慷慨和胆怯的世界主义看成精神上的崇高凯旋。</p>
<p>在我们的世界里，凡形势要求自我牺牲的地方，我们早已习惯一种毫不妥协、毫无保留的自我克制。谁若做不到，我们会认为他精神失常。至于世界主义，对我们海王星上一千个民族中的任何成员来说，把某一民族的利益置于另一民族之上，荒谬程度就像一个人偏袒自己的左眼、反对自己的右眼。</p>
<p>如果连你们的英雄主义对我们来说都如此平常，不过像育儿室里孩子鼓起的一点勇气，那么战争中究竟有什么值得我们注意？答案说起来很简单，但要理解它的全部意义，还需要进一步说明。</p>
<p>我们前来，是为了观看你们这个物种面对一种此前从未被迫面对的局势；也是为了观看你们没能以任何足以拯救自己的方式处理这个局势，结果让一种致命的精神毒素——一种自体中毒——进入了自身。</p>
<p>我们知道这场失败不可避免。因为我们已经观察过你们的天性怎样演化，也观察过你们的世界环境怎样发展。我们看着这两个因素彼此合作，在某个时刻造成世界变化的猛烈加速，随后又造成一团你们无力解决的问题。</p>
<p>我们看见你们不断发展的天性，与不断发展的世界之间进行一场势均力敌的赛跑；偏偏又正是你们自己的天性不断刺激世界，让它持续加速。最后，世界赢了。</p>
<p>早在你们历史非常久远的阶段，我们就已经看见你们体内某种新能力最初的萌芽；只要再给它足够时间，它完全有可能成熟到足以驾驭你们如今身处的这种世界。可是，你们的“现代”世界来得太早。战争前那个世纪里，它以越来越快的速度发展。你们既没有足够的智力，也没有足够的道德完整性去应付这个勇敢的新世界。</p>
<p>终于，到1914年，偶然事件把一个关键选择摆在你们面前，而你们选错了。你们必然会这样选择。以你们当时的样子，几乎肯定只能如此。但你们也只能责怪自己，因为这个选择是经过考虑的，是你们本质天性的真实表达。</p>
<p>真正适合新世界局势的那种更新行为方式，确实已经在这里那里试探性地出现过；而且毫无疑问，在非常多人心中，至少也有一点倾向于这种行为。但是，到处都有双重压制：一方面是政府权力的严格压制，一方面是每个人心中原始倾向的压制。于是，更勇敢、也是唯一理智的行为方式无法真正出现，只能在零星地点间歇而无力地闪现。</p>
<p>所以，你们不可避免地迈出了通向灾难的第一步。</p>
<p>这才是我们前来观看的戏剧，而不是你们的恐怖、英雄壮举、战略才能或者政策。演员是数以百万计的欧洲人与美国人。最初，真正的戏剧冲突发生在每一个人的心里。随后，它变成两类人之间的斗争：一边是人数众多、旧时代倾向最终获胜的人；另一边是极少数能够战胜这种倾向的人。</p>
<p>你们那次选择的直接结果是战争；但在作出选择的同时，你们也启动了一长串因果链，而它注定会在你们全部未来中继续展开。</p>
<p>战争进行期间，我们看见你们之中洞察力较强的心灵不仅被肉体痛苦和恐惧折磨，也被一种越来越强、却始终没有公开承认的确信折磨：他们已经默认参与了一场巨大而不可挽回的背叛，背叛的是仍在沉睡中的“人的精神”；他们因为盲目、怯懦，或者两者兼有，出卖了未来唯一的希望。</p>
<p>当然，这种背叛往往同时又是一种英雄行为：一个人为了自己以为的民族利益，超越了纯粹私人利益。但这丝毫不能改变另一件事：他仍然背叛了自己体内那个尚未完全形成、却更加高贵的天性；而只有那种天性，才可能保证人类未来的福祉。</p>
<p>战争本身还在进行时，这种毒素——这种深刻却几乎不被承认的羞耻——由于军事形势的紧迫性而暂时被掩盖。然而战争结束以后，它开始产生越来越强的作用。我们已经详细观察过这种作用，而我也会把它描述出来。</p>
<p>等我先告诉你们，从你们自己的眼睛内部看去、同时又经过我们观察之后，这场战争究竟是什么样子，我还会继续说明：战争以后，一种微妙的麻痹与绝望怎样攫住你们最优秀的心灵，并逐渐渗透整个社会机体；我还会告诉你们，你们怎样无论在理论还是实际事务中，都生产出大量症状——用你们心理学家的术语说，就是“防御机制”——其无意识的目的，正是帮助你们自己遮蔽对那场背叛的完整认识，也遮蔽对于其后果的清楚知觉。</p>
<p>为了让你们能够充分体会这场戏剧的全部尖锐意味，我首先必须简略报告一下我们对于你们这个物种的天性、过去经历，以及那种最终必然导致背叛的微妙力量平衡，究竟得出了什么结论。</p>
</section>

<h2>i. THE NEPTUNIAN ATTITUDE TO THE WAR</h2>
<p>IN YOUR H OMERIC saga the human conflict is observed and swayed by invisible but mighty presences. The achievement of each hero is the issue not simply of his own virtue but of the inspiration and machination of some favouring divinity. The gods and goddesses take sides, some with the Greeks, others with the Trojans.</p>
<p>In the war which I am to describe, the European War, the War to End War, which played so great a part in setting your species toward decline, every turn of events was noted by invisible presences, but these unseen observers were neither divine nor partisan. As on the Trojan plains, so on the plains of Flanders, the hills of Picardy and Champagne and Argonne, Tyrol’s wild heights, the bogs and steppes of Russia, so equally in Arabia, Mesopotamia and Salonika, on the sea and in the upper air, in fact, wherever war was present or indirectly felt, there watched invisible and unsuspected presences. These were not gods and goddesses mentally inferior to the humanity that had created them, interested in men only to use them for their own petty though celestial strife; they were actual human minds. They strove not to give victory to one side or the other; they were concerned only to record and relish the ineluctable course of events, and to kindle in such of you as were open to their influence some glimpse of their own high zest.</p>
<p>When first we examined your war, it was not uncommon for us to observe in that epoch features which, inexplicable at the time, suggested that some still future influence of our own must be at work upon them, influences which we had not yet, from the Neptunian point of view, been able to exert. Thus to take the most striking example, in our earliest study we had been perplexed by the fact that the Salonika episode did not develop on the scale that might have been expected. Later in Neptunian time, we discovered that we ourselves were responsible. For it now turned out that certain persons in high military and naval posts were open to our influence. We accordingly swayed them toward inertia, and thus deliberately prevented a sequence of events which would have been far more repugnant to us than those which actually occurred. But such large-scale influence is very seldom possible. Moreover we have become extremely chary of indulging in it; for not infrequently we have come later to regret our meddling.</p>
<p>In our world, as I have said elsewhere, the mature individual has wholly escaped the snares of private egoism. His will is for the racial good. But his heart may still be tom asunder in respect of conflicts between loyalty to the spirit of man and piety toward the actual issue of fate. Even as individuals, but far more clearly as the race mind, we pass beyond the human loyalty which is the spring of all our action, and see in the form of the cosmos a beauty superior to any human triumph.</p>
<p>Now although we regard events in your epoch with complete detachment from your many archaic aims, we do feel with you in your racial struggle to become more fully human. When you fail, or when we fail to help you, we grieve. When we take action in your world, we are often tom by doubt as to whether we shall in fact improve or mar the fortune of the race. But in us pity and doubt are tempered by our intuition that in the highest view the whole, although severe, is excellent. Even as individuals, we have this conviction of the superlative beauty of the cosmos. But in the unique racial experience this insight is far more developed, and has a very curious and beatific effect upon us as individuals. For, if our memory is to be trusted, the race mind, when it looks back on the texture of events accomplished in the past, finds each one of them to be a needed feature in the cosmical beauty. In that supreme experience, seemingly, man looks upon the cosmos with a new discerning vision, and would not have any past event to be other than in fact it is. Thus it seems that, in so far as we ourselves have influenced the course of events, our influence has indeed contributed to the cosmical beauty; yet in so far as we have refrained or failed to achieve what we planned, that inaction and that failure were also for the best, were contributory to that supernal excellence which is clearly seen only by the racial mind.</p>
<p>But do not suppose that, since apparently whatever has occurred is cosmically right, therefore human endeavour is unnecessary. Only by means of endeavour can the great theme of the cosmos proceed. But also, by some influence which is not revealed to us, the issue of all human endeavour is seemingly controlled so that in its failures no less than in its triumphs it does in fact contribute to the excellent and perfect form of the Whole. I say ‘seemingly’; for even to us it is not clear that it must be so; although hitherto, the more we have increased in spiritual stature, the more often and the more clearly have we experienced instances of its being in fact so.</p>
<p>Let us now revert to your war. It is perhaps needless to say that, on those rare occasions when we have influenced the course of events in your war, we have never been concerned merely to give victory to one side, or to the other; save when, as has sometimes befallen, an unhappy observer has become so infected by the terrestrial mentality that he has lost completely his Neptunian integrity and understanding. Such disasters are rare. For the most part our observers maintain their sanity and their detachment perfectly. .</p>
<p>You may be tempted to suppose that the great concentration of our observers in and around the period of your first world war implies that we are interested in your war for the same reasons as you yourselves are normally interested in it. In this you would be mistaken. We do indeed enter into your feelings in regard to the war. No less fully than you yourselves have felt them, we feel your agony and despair, your glory and shame; for we have participated in them directly. But throughout, we have normally preserved our own detachment. Events which through your minds we have savoured as world-shattering and God-condemning we have seen also in their wider relations, and recognized as but microscopic features in the great whole. And so we have been able to regard your agony and passion with that blend of sympathy and irony which, upon a lowlier plane, the adult feels towards the ardours of children in their games, or the grief of children in their nursery troubles.</p>
<p>The strategy of your generals, for instance, has for us only the kind of interest which your own psychologists have found in watching apes baflled by the simplest problems of intelligence. As chimpanzees, tantalized by fruit which lies beyond their reach, may achieve or miss insight into the potentialities of sticks, packing-cases or ropes, so your commanders painfully achieved, or more often missed, insight into military potentialities which lay all the while patent to every Neptunian intelligence. Time after time we saw these gilt-edged commanders frustrated by problems which even your own brighter minds might well have surmounted. Again and again we saw thousands of lives destroyed, and hard-won military gains abandoned, through the stupidity, or even the mere personal conceit, of some single strategist or tactician. And though this kind of betrayal was the very stuff that we had come to study, and normally we could regard it with equanimity, there have been, for all of us, occasions when such lapses have kindled a certain amused exasperation, or, if we have been too long subjected to the terrestrial mentality, even a momentary rage.</p>
<p>Your war-time politicians, perhaps more clearly than your generals, displayed for us the essential weakness of your kind. We are not greatly interested in the political aspects of your war, save as expressions of factors in your nature much deeper than politics. In the antics of your political leaders, even more clearly than in humbler lives, we saw the insidious, devastating struggle between private interest, patriotism, and that newer, more difficult allegiance which serves before all else the race, or the essential spirit that is man. In the commonalty, public and private interest stood in less dramatic and less momentous conflict than in those who framed national and military policy. It might have been expected that those upon whose acts great issues hung would hold their self-regarding propensity the more firmly under control, that the vast public import of their conduct would induce them constantly to scrutinize their motives with relentless penetration. But no. Decisions which were ostensibly concerned only with the national good were determined in fact by envy, jealousy, or pride. Yet in most cases the agent himself, we discovered, never doubted his own honesty. Similarly, as you yourselves later came to recognize, policies which were expressed in the language of cosmopolitan idealism or of religion, were in fact inspired by nothing but national aggressiveness or fear, or by some even more disreputable private motive. This being so, the diplomatic history of your war period interests us not at all as a record of high policy, but solely as a tangle of psychological data.</p>
<p>With yourselves, interest in your war is often the mere lust of horror, the fascination of bloodshed and destruction. To us, your horrors are unimpressive; for, ranging up and down the aeons of human history, we have observed many more complete and harrowing devastations. We have seen races, civilized beyond your dreams, completely annihilated in swift, or again in long-drawn-out, disaster. We have seen whole populations writhing and shrieking in physical agony. Your restricted and aseptic mutual slaughter, almost homely and kindly in comparison with that which lies a few decades ahead of you, has therefore no horrific interest for us. It has of course the appeal of every human tragedy; but neither in magnitude nor in intensity of suffering is it at all remarkable. Through your minds and your flesh we do, indeed, experience it as unique, world-shattering and world-condemning. We savour very thoroughly the agony of despair which oppressed so many of you in the years of war. Imaginatively we contract our vision within the limits of your war-bound vision, and enter fully into your consequent world-disgust. Indeed, having entered there, we are sometimes shocked that you do not feel more deeply. For even the most sensitive of you is protected from suffering the full agony appropriate to your own short-sighted world-disgust by the mere crudity of his nervous organization, and the consequent obtuseness and callousness of his mentality.</p>
<p>If it was not horror nor yet political or military interest that caused us to flock in thousands to observe your war, what was it? Was it perhaps your acts of individual heroism, your self-transcendence in devotion to a cause? Or was it your rare impulses of generosity toward the enemy, your groping efforts to escape the obsession of nationalism, and to feel the unity of man? We have indeed watched respectfully whatever in your war was truly heroic, devoted. Even when we have been forced to smile at the ends upon which that heroism was squandered, we have recognized that in its degree it was indeed heroism, a virtue which to beings of your stature is precious and difficult. We have also recorded faithfully every occasion on which any of you has transcended nationalism. But we cannot agree with you in regarding your heroism as the very flower of human achievement, or t your generosity and your timid cosmopolitanism as sublime triumphs of the spirit. In our world we are accustomed to a completely relentless self-abnegation in all situations which demand it. Any failure in this respect we attribute to insanity. And as for cosmopolitanism, to put the interest of one nation before that of another seems to any member of any of our thousand Neptunian nations no less preposterous than for a man to favour his left eye against his right.</p>
<p>If even your heroism is commonplace to us, seeming no more remarkable than nursery pluck, what is it that we find in your war to attract our interest? The answer can be simply stated, but its full significance needs some further development. We came to see your species face a situation such as it had never hitherto needed to face. We came to see it fail to grapple with that situation in any manner which could have saved it from a fatal spiritual poison, or auto-intoxication. We knew that this failure was inevitable. We had watched your nature evolve and your world situation develop. We had watched these two factors cooperate to produce at a certain date a violent acceleration of world -change, and subsequently a tangle of problems with which you could not cope. We had observed a neck-and-neck race between your developing nature and the developing world, which your nature itself kept stimulating into constant advance. The world won. We had seen far back in your history the first stirrings of a new capacity in you, which, given time, might well have so matured as to master even the kind of world in which you now find yourselves. But your ‘modern’ world came too soon. In the century before the war it developed with increasing acceleration. You had neither the intelligence nor the moral integrity to cope with your brave new world. When at last in 1914 accident posed you with a crucial choice, you chose wrongly. It was inevitable that you should do so. Being such as you were, you would most certainly choose as you did. But you have only yourselves to blame, for your choice was a considered expression of your own essential nature. The newer kind of behaviour, which alone was really appropriate to the new world-situation, did indeed make here and there a tentative appearance, and no doubt in very many persons there was at least some leaning toward that behaviour; but everywhere a rigorous suppression, both by governmental authority and by the primitive disposition within each individual mind, prevented the more courageous and the only sane behaviour from occurring; save here and there, spasmodically and ineffectively. And so inevitably you took the first step toward disaster.</p>
<p>This was the drama which we came to watch, and not your horrors or your heroics or your strategic prowess or your policies. The actors were the millions of Europeans and Americans. The dramatic conflict took place at first within each mind. It then became a struggle between those many in whom the archaic disposition was victorious, and those few in whom it was defeated. The immediate upshot of your choice was war; but in that choice you set in motion a sequence of causes and effects destined to develop throughout your future. During the war itself we saw your more percipient minds tortured and warped, not only by physical pain and fear, but by the growing though unacknowledged conviction that they had acquiesced in a great and irrevocable treason against the still-slumbering spirit of man; that through blindness or cowardice or both, they had betrayed that which was the only hope of the future. Very often, of course, this betrayal was at the same time itself an heroic transcendence of mere self-regard for the supposed good of a nation; but it was none the less betrayal of that half-formed and nobler nature upon which alone depended man’s future well-being. During the war itself the working of this poison this profound and almost unrecognized shame, was obscured by the urgency of the military situation. But after the war it acted with ever-increasing effect. This effect we have observed in detail, and I shall describe it. When I have told you of the war itself as it appears to us through your eyes, I shall tell how, after the war, a subtle paralysis and despair fastened upon your best minds, and percolated throughout your social organism. I shall tell how both in theory and in affairs you produced a spate of symptoms which your psychologists would call ‘defence-mechanisms’, designed unwittingly to conceal from yourselves the full realization of your treason and the clear perception of its effects.</p>
<p>That you may appreciate this drama in all its poignancy, I must first report briefly our findings in respect of the nature and the past career of your species, and of the delicate balance of forces which issued inevitably in this betrayal.</p>
