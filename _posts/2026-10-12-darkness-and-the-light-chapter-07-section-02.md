<h2>二、反抗帝国的战争</h2>
<p>战争终于爆发了。我已经讲过，在那条“黑暗”的历史线上，这场战争如何导致人类最有希望的社会被摧毁；而在“光明”的历史线上，结局完全不同。两个帝国不仅已经被传教士从内部严重侵蚀，各地叛乱频繁发生；更重要的是，所有国家的光明仆从，尤其是西藏人，都带着一种内在的、不可动摇的胜利确信。</p>
<p>和黑暗历史线中一样，西藏边境仍由那种能使入侵者退化为幼儿心智的微生物保护。但在黑暗历史线里，这段由微生物争取来的喘息时间只是被用于加强防御；在光明取得胜利的历史线上，它却被转化成一次主动进攻的机会。</p>
<p>出乎所有人的预料，那支规模虽小、训练却极其精良，而且机械化程度很高的西藏军队，在一支同样规模有限却装备完善的空军支持下，向俄国帝国控制的克什米尔推进。在俄罗斯人还来不及从微生物攻击的影响中恢复以前，西藏军便发动攻击，并取得惊人的胜利。俄国帝国主义者匆忙调集庞大的新军队和航空力量对付入侵者；然而，由于低效率、腐败，尤其由于士气低落以及公开的不忠，帝国军几乎没有进行像样抵抗，很快便陷入混乱撤退。西藏军紧追不舍，而当地居民自己也不断攻击俄军。</p>
<p>克什米尔当然早已爆发有组织的起义，而帝国军的失败保证了起义成功。整个山地很快获得解放。当地的光明仆从组成临时政府，新国家则立即同西藏结成紧密联盟。</p>
<p>这场意外胜利造成的精神震动极其巨大。俄国本土，尤其莫斯科，很快发生严重骚乱。一支奉命前往收复失地的军队因兵变而完全失去行动能力。与此同时，从克什米尔经阿富汗、波斯、土耳其一直延伸到爱琴海的整片山地，都开始反抗压迫者。希腊、英国与斯堪的纳维亚也出现零星起义。在西藏以北，新疆以及外蒙古的山地部分击败了当地帝国军。</p>
<p>与此同时，西藏陆空军主力并没有因为西线胜利停下来休整，而是从国土最西端急速转移到最东端。因为中国这个远比俄国强大的敌人，此时正对拉萨以及西藏整个相对富裕的东部地区实施猛烈轰炸。</p>
<p>对西藏而言，此时最迫切的事情，是立刻对中华帝国取得一次明确而震撼人心的胜利，从而也在中国内部启动那种已经开始侵蚀俄国帝国的加速崩坏过程。东部西藏居民能够撤入战争前多年就修建好的深层掩体，因此避开了此时落在城市、牧群和珍贵灌溉系统上的毁灭性攻击。人们这时才发现，政府多年前便认定战争不可避免，早已建造大量地下军工厂。</p>
<p>然而，中国的攻击实在太猛烈；如果持续下去，西藏抵抗早晚会被打瘫。在克什米尔极其成功的突袭战术，对中国帝国主义者却无法复制，因为中国已经在川边地区集结了一支规模庞大的军队。这支军队的作战效率无可怀疑，只是它对帝国主人的忠诚从未真正受过考验。</p>
<p>经过长时间讨论，西藏领导者最终决定，他们除了主动走向灾难、并期待奇迹之外，别无选择。或者更准确地说，他们怀着一种近乎神授的胜利确信，因此判断：唯一通向胜利的道路，就是受到灵感驱动的鲁莽冒险。</p>
<p>西藏空军虽然在数量上远远少于中国空军，却表现得更加灵活、更有技术，也勇猛得近乎狂热。他们竭尽全力摧毁敌军机场，把炸弹和使人幼儿化的微生物投向川边地区正在推进的军队，同时向中国大型工业中心投撒传单。与此同时，西藏陆军在边境进行绝望防御。</p>
<p>这里没有必要细述战斗过程。有一段时间，看起来西藏的抵抗已经彻底崩溃，可领导者和士兵仍维持着那种看似非理性的信心。人们不断说：“撑住，撑住，潮水会转向的。”而事情果然如此。敌军攻势无论在空中还是地面，都开始减弱。</p>
<p>大量投奔西藏一方的逃兵带来消息：川边居民已经成千上万地牺牲自己，在帝国军后方制造混乱。帝国军内部的精神状态，也从对这场讨厌边境战争的无聊服从，逐渐变成低声抱怨和怀疑。空军则因连续受挫，职业自尊遭到严重打击。</p>
<p>西藏领导人判断，大胆下注的时候已经到了。他们没有利用短暂喘息恢复力量、准备承受敌人下一轮打击，而是把全部国力投入一次公然违反一切传统战争原则的进攻。明明自己才是弱势一方，他们却把几乎所有兵力都变成伞兵，像洪水一样撒向整个川边，几乎把西藏本土完全暴露出来。</p>
<p>这一行动产生的效果，就像往一整片森林里到处投放燃烧弹一样惊人。中国军队面对数量庞大、四面八方落下来的伞兵完全陷入困惑；他们从未想到，这竟是一个已经濒临失败的敌人最后孤注一掷的行动。部分部队当然服从军官命令，围捕从天而降的入侵者；但另有许多部队反而转而支持这些伞兵。整个川边迅速陷入混乱。</p>
<p>西藏陆军仅剩的一点地面兵力便趁势进入川边，几乎没有遇到严重抵抗。他们站在省份东缘的高地上，向下望着四川丘陵低地，对自己的成功也感到难以置信。与此同时，长江流域各处爆发骚乱，并迅速传播到中国多数大城市。</p>
<p>不过，中华帝国比俄罗斯帝国更加坚韧。帝国空军通过轰炸，迫使许多起义城市重新屈服。长江流域原已溃败的帝国军也重新集结，并得到新部队增援。四川东部的叛军被击溃并遭到屠杀。西藏那场近乎不可思议的推进，最终在宜昌之前被挡住。</p>
<p>西藏领导人十分清楚：除非中国各地受统治人口自己起来攻击帝国主义者，否则中国人民不可能真正获得解放。最初看来，这种全国性起义似乎很可能发生；但中国民族主义仍是一种极其强烈的感情，而统治者非常善于利用它。</p>
<p>西藏领导者在内在“灵性”驱使他们前进时固然大胆，甚至常常鲁莽，可他们同时也是现实主义者。他们没有继续试图深入中国腹地，而是巩固已经获得的阵地，然后等待。他们还向中国人民广播，大意是：“我们不是征服者，也不想建立帝国。如果你们希望获得自由，就起来反抗；那时我们会继续前进帮助你们。如果你们不愿如此，我们会让你们自行生活。我们只会保护那些已经由我们解放的民族，而且只有他们愿意接受帮助时，我们才会留下。”</p>
<p>西藏人在说这些话时很清楚，中国统治者已经严重高估西藏力量，害怕自己的整个帝国被彻底毁灭，而且此时正有意谋求和平。</p>
<p>帝国主义者认为，只要能够暂时避免帝国立即解体，将来便可以重新调动两个帝国的全部资源，把西藏永远消灭。因此，他们提出召开和平会议。最终达成的协议，使中国本土几乎保持完整。</p>
<p>西藏人在自己占领的地区举行公民投票，并尊重四川多数居民继续留在帝国体系内的意愿。但川边地区，以及中南亚高原其余大片区域——包括阿富汗——则选择摆脱帝国主义者统治。伊朗、伊拉克和土耳其的起义已经被俄国军队镇压。如今，中亚这些获得自由的民族组成了一个“山地联邦”；由于文明水平和军事声望，西藏在其中居于主导地位。</p>
<p>从纯政治角度看，这场战争的结果其实并不十分壮观。甚至可以把它描述成帝国的某种胜利，因为两个帝国重新夺回了最初被叛军控制的大量领土。而且西藏本身在空袭中遭受了极其严重的破坏：拉萨被摧毁，大多数地面工厂失去功能，大量成年男性死于战争。相比之下，帝国一方的损失同其总人口和资源相比非常微小。</p>
<p>但从心理层面看，战争影响极其深远。俄中两个帝国虽然长期彼此敌对、仇恨根深蒂固，却曾认为有必要联合起来摧毁一个弱小而“野蛮”的国家——而在战前看来，两个帝国无论哪一个单独行动，都应该能够轻易消灭它。然而山地民族不仅成功自卫，甚至发动反击，最终反倒是两个帝国主动请求和平。</p>
<p>在每个国家，帝国主义者虽然公开高声庆祝自己的“胜利”，私下却深感惊恐；而他们的敌人则一点一点意识到，这场战争已经在人类历史上打开一个崭新、充满希望的篇章。</p>
<p>在和平会议上，西藏坚决拒绝承诺停止在帝国领土内进行宣传。恰恰相反，他们公开声明，将尽一切力量支持每个国家争取自由的斗争；只要出现机会，并且某场叛乱的目标在他们看来确实出自“光明意志”，他们就会援助叛乱者。两个帝国甚至无力迫使西藏修改这些条款；仅仅这一事实，就足以说明帝国权威已经遭到多么严重的打击。</p>
</section>

<h2>ii. WAR AGAINST THE EMPIRES</h2>
<p>AT LAST WAR came. I have told how, in the theme of darkness it resulted in the destruction of man’s most promising society. In the theme of light the issue was far otherwise. Not only had the empires been effectively undermined by the missionaries, so that rebellions were frequent; more important was the fact that the servants of the light in all countries, and specially in Tibet, were armed with an inner certainty of victory. As in the darker theme, the Tibetan frontier was defended by microbes which reduced the invaders to infantilism. But whereas in the dark theme the respite thus secured was used merely for strengthening the defence, in the theme of the triumphing light it was turned into an opportunity for attack. Against all probability, the small but highly trained and highly mechanized Tibetan army, supported by its small but well-appointed air force, pushed forward into the imperial territory of Kashmir. There it attacked before the Russians had had time to recover from the effects of the microbe, and it gained a surprising victory. The Russian imperialists hastily concentrated vast new armies and air forces upon the invaders; but owing to a combination of inefficiency, corruption, and above all half-heartedness and positive disloyalty the imperial armies put up a feeble resistance, and were presently retreating in disorder, closely pursued by the Tibetans, and constantly attacked by the natives themselves. Organized revolt had of course broken out in Kashmir, and the imperialists’ defeat ensured its success. The whole of this mountainous land was soon freed. A temporary government was set up by the Kashmiri servants of the light, and the new state formed a close alliance with Tibet.</p>
<p>The moral effect of this surprising victory was immense. In Russia itself, particularly in Moscow, there was serious disorder. An army which was ordered to proceed to the recovery of the lost territory, was incapacitated by mutiny. Meanwhile the whole mountainous tract stretching from Kashmir through Afghanistan, Persia, and Turkey to the Aegean Sea rose against the oppressors. In Greece, in Britain, and in Scandinavia isolated rebellions were started. To the north of Tibet, Sinkiang and the more mountainous part of Outer Mongolia overcame the local imperial forces. Meanwhile the main Tibetan land and air armament, far from resting on their success, were hurried from the western to the eastern end of the country where the Chinese, a much more formidable enemy, were heavily bombing Lhasa and the whole comparatively rich eastern part of Tibet.</p>
<p>It was desperately important for the Tibetans to secure at once some positive and spectacular success against the Chinese Empire, so as to start in China also that process of galloping decay which was already at work in the rival empire. The people of eastern Tibet were able to retire to their deep shelters, prepared long before the war, and to escape the destruction which now fell upon their cities, their herds, their precious irrigation system. It now appeared that the government, convinced many years ago of the inevitability of war, had established a great number of underground munition factories. But the attack was too heavy to be endured for long without the crippling of the Tibetan resistance. The method of surprise, which had succeeded so well in Kashmir, was impossible against the Chinese imperialists, for they had concentrated an immense force in Chwanben. The efficiency of this army was beyond question. Its loyalty to its imperial master had never been tested. After much discussion the Tibetan leaders decided that there was nothing for it but to court disaster and hope for a miracle. Or rather, divinely confident of victory, they saw that the only way to it was the way of inspired foolhardiness. The Tibetan air force, though heavily outnumbered, proved far more resourceful and skilful than the Chinese, and their courage was fanatical. They did their utmost to destroy the enemy aerodromes. They dropped bombs and the microbes of infantilism on the advancing army in Chwanben. They scattered leaflets on the great industrial centres. At the same time the Tibetan land forces put up a desperate defence upon the frontier.</p>
<p>There is no need to give details of the fighting. At one time it seemed that resistance had broken, yet the Tibetan leaders and fighters maintained their irrational confidence. ‘Hang on, hang on,’ it was said. ‘The tide will turn.’ And sure enough it did. The enemy’s attack began to weaken, both in the air and on land. Deserters, who came oyer in large numbers to the Tibetan side, told that the population of Chwanben had sacrificed itself in thousands so as to create confusion behind the lines. The spirit of the imperial army was changing from bored acceptance of this tiresome frontier war to whispering complaint and doubt. The air force was suffering from badly damaged professional pride. The Tibetan leaders judged that the moment had come for the great gamble. Instead of using the lull to recuperate and prepare to withstand the next blow, they threw the whole Tibetan strength into an attack which violated all the accepted principles of warfare. Though they were the weaker side, they flooded the whole of Chwanben with parachute troops, leaving Tibet almost undefended. The effect was as spectacular as the result of peppering a forest with incendiary bombs. Bewildered by the multitude of the parachutists, and never imagining that this move was the last effort of a beaten enemy, the Chinese troops fell into disorder. Some, of course, obeyed their officers and rounded up the aerial invaders, but many others rallied to the parachutists themselves. The whole of Chwanben fell into chaos. The minute remnant of the Tibetan land army advanced into Chwanben without meeting serious opposition. From the eastern heights of the province they looked down upon the hilly lowlands of Szechwan, amazed at their own success. Disorder now broke out all along the Yangtze Valley and spread to most of the great cities of China.</p>
<p>But the Chinese Empire was tougher than the Russian. The imperial air force bombed many of the revolting cities into submission. The routed imperial armies in the Yangtze Valley were rallied and stiffened with fresh troops. The rebels in the eastern part of Szechwan were overcome and massacred. The fantastic Tibetan advance was checked before Ichang.</p>
<p>The leaders of Tibet knew well that the peoples of China could not be freed unless the imperialists were everywhere attacked by their own subject population. This seemed at first likely to happen; but Chinese nationalism was a strong sentiment, and the rulers were able to make good use of it. The Tibetan leaders, though daring and even foolhardly when their daemon urged them forward, were also realists. Instead of trying to press on into the heart of China, they consolidated the positions they had gained, and waited. They also broadcast to the people of China, saying in effect, ‘We are not conquerors. We desire no empire. If you want freedom, rebel, and we will press on to help you. Otherwise we shall leave you alone. We shall merely defend those peoples whom we have already freed, and only if they wish us to help them.’ All this they said knowing that the Chinese rulers had an exaggerated idea of the Tibetan power, that they feared the complete destruction of their empire, and that they were in the mood to arrange a peace.</p>
<p>The imperialists believed that if they could stave off the immediate disintegration ‘of their empire they could later gather all the resources of both empires to crush Tibet for ever. They therefore proposed a peace conference. The final settlement was one which left China itself almost intact. The Tibetans held plebiscites in their conquered territories, and respected the wish of the majority in Szechwan to remain within the imperial system. Chwanben, however, along with the rest of the great plateau of southern central Asia, including Afghanistan, chose to be free from the rule of the imperialists. The rebellions in Iran, Iraq, and Turkey had been crushed by the Russian forces. The freed peoples of Central Asia now formed a Mountain Federation, which was dominated by the Tibetans in virtue of their civilization and military prestige.</p>
<p>The effect of the war was from the political point of view by no means spectacular. It might even be represented as a kind of victory for the empires, since they recovered much territory that had at first been lost to the rebels. Moreover Tibet had been very seriously crippled from the air. Lhasa was destroyed. Most of the surface factories had been put out of action. A large proportion of Tibetan adult males had been killed in the fighting. On the imperial side the damage was very small in proportion to total population and resources. But psychologically the effect of the war was far-reaching. The empires, in spite of their traditional and inveterate hostility, had thought it worth while to combine to crush weak and ‘barbarian’ state which, it had seemed, could easily have been destroyed by either of them alone. Yet the mountain people had not only successfully defended themselves but had counter-attacked, and in the end it was the empires that sued for peace. In every country the imperialists, in spite of their loud rejoicing over their ‘victory’, were secretly dismayed; while their enemies gradually came to realize that the war had opened a new and hopeful chapter in the history of man. At the peace conference the Tibetans had firmly refused to agree to refrain from propaganda in imperial territories. Indeed they declared that they would do all in their power to support the struggle for freedom in every country, and that whenever opportunity offered they would assist rbellion so long as its aims seemed to them to spring from the will for the light. The mere fact that the empires were unable to alter these provisions showed how far their authority had been damaged.</p>
