<h2>第十六章</h2>
<p>仿佛隔着极其遥远的距离，卡莫迪听见一个声音说道：“你怎么看，医生？还能为他做点什么吗？”</p>
<p>他认出了那个声音。是奖品。</p>
<p>“钱我来付。”另一个声音说。卡莫迪认出那是莫兹利。“你觉得还能救他吗？”</p>
<p>“可以救。”第三个声音说道，想必就是医生。“医学科学承认的从来不是‘可行性’的界限，而只是‘可忍受性’的界限；后者属于病人的限制，不属于我们的限制。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪努力想睁眼或张嘴，却发现自己完全无法动弹。</p>
<p>“所以情况很严重，是吧？”奖品问。</p>
<p>“这个问题很难精确回答。”医生说。“首先，我们必须先划分类别。比如说，医学科学就比医学伦理简单。我们银河医学协会的成员理应保存生命；与此同时，我们又应该以接受治疗的特定生命形式的最大利益为行动准则。可当这两条命令彼此矛盾时，我们应该怎么办？比如德文五号星的乌伊奇人，他们会请求医生治愈他们的‘生命’，帮助他们达到渴望的目标——死亡。我得说，这是一项该死地困难的工作，而且只有等一个乌伊奇人已经年迈体衰以后才有可能成功。那么，对于这种正常欲望的奇异逆转，伦理学究竟该怎么说？我们应该服从乌伊奇人的愿望，去实施银河几乎每个角落都认为令人发指的行为？还是应该按照我们自己的标准行动，从而把乌伊奇人判处一种字面意义上真正‘比死亡更糟’的命运？”</p>
<p>“这跟卡莫迪有什么关系？”莫兹利问。</p>
<p>“没太大关系。”医生承认。“不过我想你们也许会觉得有趣，而且还能帮助你们理解，我们为什么不得不收这么高的诊费。”</p>
<p>“他情况到底严不严重？”奖品不耐烦地追问。</p>
<p>“只有死人才能被说成真正处于严重状态。”医生郑重宣布。“而且即便死亡，也有例外。比如五日可逆死亡——学名Pentathanaluna——虽然外行人流传着各种庸俗谣言，但其实并不比普通感冒严重多少。”</p>
<p>“可卡莫迪到底怎么样？”莫兹利问。</p>
<p>“他明确没有死。”医生安慰似的说。“他只是处于一种——或者说近似于——深度休克的状态。说得简单一点，他可以算是晕过去了。”</p>
<p>“你能把他弄醒吗？”奖品问。</p>
<p>“你的用词不够明确。”医生说。“我的工作本来就已经够困难了，没必要再——”</p>
<p>“我是说，你能不能把他恢复到原来的功能状态？”奖品问。</p>
<p>“哎呀！这要求可相当高。如果你认真想上片刻，我相信你也会承认。什么叫他‘原来的功能状态’？你们两个谁知道吗？即使奇迹般地允许病人本人参与自己的治疗，他自己又会知道吗？人格每时每刻都在发生上百万种微妙变化，其中一些只需一次心跳就足以触发；我们凭什么知道，哪一种状态才最具他的典型特征？失去的人格难道不像失去的一秒钟吗——我们能够逼近它，却永远无法真正复制？先生们，这些可都是分量相当沉重的问题。”</p>
<p>“重得该死。”莫兹利说。“你就尽量把他恢复得接近原样吧。很难吗？”</p>
<p>“对我来说不难。”医生说。“我在这个行业干了很长时间。我已经习惯最骇人的景象，也习惯最恐怖的操作。当然，这不是说我已经变得麻木；只是出于悲哀的职业需要，我学会了把注意力从那些足以灼伤灵魂、却又是本行业必须实施的程序上移开。”</p>
<p>“我的老天，医生！”奖品叫道。“你到底要对我哥们干什么？”</p>
<p>“我必须手术。”医生说。“这是唯一可靠的方法。我会先解剖卡莫迪——这是用外行能听懂的说法——把他的四肢和器官放进保存液里。然后用稀释的K-5溶液把他软化。接着，我会从不同孔口把他的脑和神经系统抽出来。下一步是把神经系统和大脑接到生命模拟器上，按照严格控制的时间序列逐个激发突触。这样，我们就能检查有没有断路、坏阀门、堵塞之类的问题。假定不存在这些故障，我们再把大脑拆开，最终抵达心灵与身体之间的交互点。我们会极其小心地把这个部件取下，检查所有内外连接。如果到这里一切正常，我们就打开交互点储液槽——当然要先检查有没有泄漏——然后测量其中的意识液位。如果意识偏低或已经耗尽（这类案例几乎总是如此），我们就分析残留物，再配制一批新的。新配制的意识要经过全面测试，然后注射进交互点储液槽。最后重新组装整个躯体，再用生命模拟器重新启动病人。整个过程大致就是这样。”</p>
<p>“嚯——！”奖品说道。“我连条狗都不会这么治！”</p>
<p>“我也不会。”医生说。“至少要等犬类再进化一些。你们希望我实施这项手术吗？”</p>
<p>“这个嘛……”奖品沉吟。“我想我们总不能就让他一直昏迷着躺在这里，对吧？”</p>
<p>“当然不能。”莫兹利说。“可怜的家伙一直指望我们，我们不能辜负他。医生，履行你的职责！”</p>
<p>整个谈话期间，卡莫迪一直在和自己失灵的身体机能搏斗。他越听越恐惧，也越来越确信：自己的朋友能够造成的伤害，恐怕连敌人都想象不出来。现在，他使出一股泰坦般的力气，猛地睁开双眼，又硬生生把舌头从上颚扯开。</p>
<p>“不准手术！”他声音嘶哑地吼道。“你们谁敢给我做他妈的手术，我就把谁那颗该死的心挖出来！”</p>
<p>“他的各项功能已经恢复了。”医生听起来颇为高兴。“你们知道，很多时候，当着病人的面口头描述我们的手术流程，比手术本身更能起到镇痛作用。当然，这属于安慰剂效应，不过绝不应该小看。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪挣扎着想站起来，莫兹利扶他起身。他第一次看清医生：那是个又高又瘦、神情忧郁、穿着黑衣服的男人，长得和亚伯拉罕·林肯一模一样。奖品则已经不再是铜坩埚；显然在刚才的压力下，它变成了一个侏儒。</p>
<p>“需要我就叫我。”医生说完便离开了。</p>
<p>“发生什么了？”卡莫迪问。“那艘飞船，那些人——”</p>
<p>“我们刚好及时把你拉出来。”奖品说。“不过那根本不是什么飞船，小子。”</p>
<p>“我知道。那到底是什么？”</p>
<p>“那，”莫兹利说，“就是你的捕食者。你自己一路走进了它嘴里。”</p>
<p>“看来确实如此。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“而且就因为这一点，你可能已经失去了回地球的唯一机会。”莫兹利说。“我觉得你最好坐下，卡莫迪。你现在只剩下几个选择，而没有一个特别诱人。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪坐了下来。</p>
</section>
<section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="en" lang="en" aria-label="English original" hidden>
<p><b>CHAPTER 16</b></p>
<p>As from a vast distance, Carmody heard a voice say: ‘What do you think, Doctor? Can you do anything for him?’</p>
<p>He recognized that voice; it was the Prize.</p>
<p>‘I’ll pay for it,’ another voice said. He recognized this as Maudsley. ‘Do you think you can do anything for him?’</p>
<p>‘He can be saved,’ said a third voice, presumably that of the doctor. ‘Medical science admits no limits to the feasible, only to the tolerable, which is the patient’s limitation, not ours.’</p>
<p>Carmody struggled to open his eyes or his mouth, but found that he was completely immobilized.</p>
<p>‘So it’s serious, huh?’ asked the Prize.</p>
<p>‘That is a difficult question to answer with precision,’ the doctor said. ‘To begin with, we must assign categories. Medical science is easier than medical ethics, for example. We of the Galactic Medical Association are supposed to preserve life; we are also supposed to act in the best interests of the particular form we treat. But what should we do when these two imperatives are in contradiction? The Uiichi of Devin V, for example, seek a physician’s aid to cure them of life and help them achieve their desired goal of death. It is a damnably difficult task, let me say, and only possible when an Uiichi has grown old and enfeebled, But what does ethics have to say about this strange reversal of normal desire? Are we to do as the Uiichi desire, and perform acts which are reprehensible in nearly every corner of the galaxy? Or are we to act upon the basis of our own standards, and thus doom the Uiichi to a fate quite literally worse than death?’</p>
<p>‘What has this got to do with Carmody?’ Maudsley asked.</p>
<p>‘Not very much,’ the doctor admitted. ‘But I thought you might find it interesting, and it will help you see why we must charge the high fees we do.’</p>
<p>‘Is he in a serious condition?’ the Prize demanded.</p>
<p>‘Only the dead can be said to be in a really serious condition,’ the doctor stated. ‘And even then, there are exceptions. Pentathanaluna, for example, which laymen refer to as Five-Day Reversible Death, is really no worse than a common cold, despite vulgar rumours to the contrary.’</p>
<p>‘But what about Carmody?’ Maudsley asked.</p>
<p>‘He is definitely not dead,’ the doctor said soothingly. ‘He is merely in a state of – or tantamount to – deep shock. To put it more simply, he has, in a manner of speaking, fainted.’</p>
<p>‘Can you pull him out of it?’ the Prize asked.</p>
<p>‘Your terms are unclear,’ the Doctor said. ‘My work is difficult enough without –’</p>
<p>‘I mean, can you restore him to his original state of function?’ the Prize asked.</p>
<p>‘Well! That is rather a large order, as I think you will admit if you give it a moment’s thought. What <i>was</i> his original state of function? Does either of you know? Would he know himself if, miraculously, he could be consulted in his own cure? Of the million subtle alterations of personality, some of which take place at the mere instigation of a heartbeat, how can we know which was most characteristically <i>his</i>? Is not a lost personality like a lost second – something we can approximate but never truly reproduce? These, gentlemen, are questions of some weight.’</p>
<p>‘Damnably heavy,’ Maudsley said. ‘Suppose you just get him as near to what he was as you can. Will that be very tough?’</p>
<p>‘Not on me,’ the doctor said. ‘I have worked for a considerable time in my profession. I have become inured to the most ghastly sights, accustomed to the most hideous procedures. That is not to say that I have grown <i>callous,</i> of course; I have merely learned through sad necessity to direct my attention away from the soul-searing procedures which my profession demands of me.’</p>
<p>‘Cripes, Doc!’ the Prize said. ‘What do you gotta do to my buddy?’</p>
<p>‘I must operate,’ the doctor said. ‘It is the only reliable way. I shall dissect Carmody (speaking in layman’s terms) and put his limbs and organs into a preserving solution. Then, I shall soften him in a dilute solution of K-5. I will draw his brain and nervous system out through various orifices. The procedure then is to hook up the nervous system and brain to a Life-Simulator, and fire the synapses in carefully timed series. Thus we see if there are any breaks, bad valves, stoppages, and the like. Assuming the absence of these, we disassemble the brain, coming at last to the interaction point between mind and body. Removing this very carefully, we check all internal and external connections. If everything is all right up to this point, we open the interaction-point reservoir, looking for leaks, of course, and then checking the level of consciousness within. If it is low or depleted (and in cases like these, it almost always is) we analyse the residue and create a new batch. This new batch of consciousness is tested exhaustively, then injected into the interaction- point reservoir. All parts of the corpus are then reassembled, and the patient can be reanimated with the Life-Simulator. That’s pretty much the whole process.’</p>
<p>‘Hooee,’ said the Prize. ‘I wouldn’t treat a dog that way!’</p>
<p>‘Nor would I,’ the doctor said. ‘Not until the canine race has evolved further. Do you wish me to perform this operation?’</p>
<p>‘Well …’ The Prize mused. ‘I guess we can’t just leave him lying around unconscious, can we?’</p>
<p>‘Of course we can’t,’ Maudsley said. ‘The poor fellow has been counting on us and we must not fail him. Doctor, do your duty!’</p>
<p>Carmody had been struggling with his malfunctioning functions through this entire conversation. He had listened with steadily mounting terror and with the growing conviction that his friends could do him more harm than his enemies could even imagine. Now, with a titanic effort, he burst open his eyes and wrenched his tongue away from the roof of his mouth.</p>
<p>‘No operation!’ he croaked. ‘I’ll cut your goddamned heart out if you try any goddamned operation!’</p>
<p>‘He has recovered his faculties,’ the doctor said, sounding quite pleased. ‘Often, you know, a verbalization of our operating procedure in the patient’s presence serves as a better anodyne than the operation itself. It is a placebo effect, of course, but certainly not to be sneered at.’</p>
<p>Carmody struggled to stand up, and Maudsley helped him to his feet. He looked at the doctor for the first time, and saw a tall, thin, mournful man in black clothes, who looked exactly like Abraham Lincoln. The Prize was no longer a cauldron. Evidently under the stress, he had, changed into a dwarf.</p>
<p>‘Send for me if you need me,’ the doctor said, and departed.</p>
<p>‘What happened?’ Carmody asked. ‘That spaceship, those people –’</p>
<p>‘We pulled you out just in time,’ the Prize said. ‘But that was no spaceship, keed.’</p>
<p>‘I know. What was it?’</p>
<p>‘That,’ Maudsley said, ‘was your predator. You walked right into his mouth.’</p>
<p>‘I guess I did at that,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘And by doing that, you may have lost your only chance of getting back to Earth,’ Maudsley said. ‘I think you’d better sit down, Carmody. You have only a few choices now, and none of them is particularly enticing.’</p>
<p>Carmody sat down.</p>
