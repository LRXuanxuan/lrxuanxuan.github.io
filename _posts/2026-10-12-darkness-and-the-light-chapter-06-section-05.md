<h2>五、人类的终结</h2>
<p>这种漫长的平衡状态其实并不牢固。早晚会有某场比往常更广泛的灾祸把整个物种消灭。只是，人类最终的灭亡方式却出乎我的意料。</p>
<p>老鼠陪伴人类经历了整个漫长历史。事实上，早在人类出现在地球之前，鼠类就已经牢固地生活在这里；而命运也注定，它们最终还会比人类活得更久。人类这个物种一直把相当一部分力量耗费在防御老鼠上。即使在物质文明的鼎盛时期，这种无处不在的啮齿动物也会吃掉大量粮食储备，并传播瘟疫。随着人类智力衰退，老鼠变成了严重得多的威胁。它们从人类粮仓中夺走的份额越来越大，数量则以惊人的速度繁殖。在人类漫长退化的最后阶段，“捕鼠人”成了最受尊敬的职业。只有人类中最聪明的人，才能应付这种低等物种虽有限却完全够用的天生狡猾。一世纪又一世纪，人类勉强抵挡住这个可怕敌人，但优势始终极其微弱，而且代价巨大。</p>
<p>终于，危机到来了。某种波及全球的气候变化，似乎突然使人类生存变得更加困难；而作为人类寄生者的老鼠也同样陷入困境。饥饿驱使鼠群改变习性。它们不再满足于洗劫人的食物储备，而开始直接袭击人类。</p>
<p>最初，它们会趁婴儿短时间无人看守时把孩子吃掉，也会攻击熟睡的成年人。有时，一大群饥饿的鼠会伏击孤身猎人，先咬住他的腿，再沿身体爬上去，用门齿牢牢挂在肉上，扑咬喉咙，把人拖倒在地，然后活活吃掉。看起来，鼠类可能发生了某种突变，使其作为食肉动物的能力得到增强，因为袭击大型哺乳动物、尤其袭击人类的事件越来越普遍。人类此时的体型已经明显缩小，而老鼠却变得更加肥大。</p>
<p>后来，鼠群甚至不再局限于偷偷攻击儿童、熟睡者和落单的人。它们集结成庞大“军队”，直接侵入零散的人类聚居地，把居民全部消灭。一世纪又一世纪，人类打一场注定失败的战争。一个部落接一个部落灭绝，一个国家接一个国家变得无人居住，直到最后，只在条件最优越的一片地区，还剩下几个被逼到绝境的家庭潜伏在森林中，以树根和虫子为食。</p>
<p>每逢满月，他们仍会庄严集会，齐声吟唱抵御鼠类敌人的咒语，并带着愚蠢的骄傲宣称自己高于一切野兽。从这些仰头嚎叫的嘴里发出的、几乎已经毫无意义的行话，是他们最后仅存的一点“人类资格”。其中仍潜藏着文明语言荒诞扭曲后的残片——一些词语的祖先，曾经在莎士比亚、柏拉图和孔夫子的时代活生生地存在过。</p>
<p>这批人类最后的残余，也许又坚持了几十年，也许坚持了几个世纪。他们不仅要面对鼠群，还要面对无数其他害兽、疾病以及恶劣天气。在这种持续不断的战争中，脆弱的人类身体与亚人类心智结合在一起，使灭绝成为不可避免的结果。终于在某个时刻，没有任何人为之哀悼，也没有任何存在注意到，最后一个人类被消灭了。</p>
<p>这个行星曾经不止一次踉踉跄跄地达到清醒心智，如今却永远沉入昏睡。地球上已经没有任何物种有能力重新进化到人类的层次。那支从人类手中掉落的火炬，再也不会由新的奔跑者拾起，继续向前传递。</p>
<p>在不可计量的漫长年代里——那时间远远超过整个人类历史的长度——地球继续自转，也继续绕太阳运行，表面只属于无数低等生物。</p>
<p>与此同时，太阳像所有同年龄、同规模的恒星一样，因为内部能量释放越来越迅速而逐渐变得更热。地球上那些高度特化的生物类型首先陆续毁灭；比较低等的种类则适应着越来越炎热的气候。越来越多的海水蒸发进入大气，终年不散的云层遮蔽天空。慢慢地，地球环境终于越过任何陆生物种能够适应的极限。海洋开始沸腾，沙土开始熔化，大气则逐渐逸散到外层空间。</p>
<p>然而太阳日益增强的热量，却曾一度有利于天王星上生命的演化。那里也像地球一样，缓慢出现了繁多物种；也像地球一样，这些物种一个接一个达到高度特化的顶点，从此无法再向更高层次演化。终于，和地球曾经发生过的一样，有一种只“专门化于适应性本身”的生物类型站到了清醒心智的门槛上。</p>
<p>但就在这时，太阳也像此前无数恒星一样爆发为新星，把自己的全部行星熔为一体。</p>
<p>这些极其遥远的事件并不是我亲眼见到的。它们似乎只是通过我与那些超越人类的共同探索者的心灵接触，以一种模糊方式传入我的意识。</p>
<p>我个人的直接经验始终局限于地球上的事件。而当地球那短暂闪烁的清醒之光终于熄灭，我的注意力也立刻从这整条悲哀的时间支流上撤走——在这条未来中，黑暗意志取得了胜利。</p>
<p>因为与此同时，在另一条时间之流中，还有一些几乎同样痛苦、却又光辉得多的事件，正在我面前展开。</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" class="stapledon-body" lang="en" hidden>
<h2>v. THE END OF MAN</h2>
<p>THIS PROLONGED EQUILIBRIUM was insecure. Sooner or later some more than usually widespread scourge would extinguish the species. The end came in a manner that I had not expected. The rat had accompanied man through all his adventures. Indeed, long before man appeared on the earth, the rat was well-established. And it was destined to survive him. A considerable part of the energy of the human race had always been devoted to defence against the rat. Even at the height of material civilization this ubiquitous rodent devoured much of man’s food stores and infected him with plagues. With the decline of human intelligence the rat became a much more serious menace. It exacted a far heavier toll on his food stores. It multiplied extravagantly. In the last long phase of human degeneracy the rat-catcher was the most honoured profession. Only the most intelligent of men could cope with the limited but adequate native cunning of the inferior species. Century by century man held his own against this formidable enemy, but only by a narrow margin and at great cost.</p>
<p>At last there came a crisis. Some climatic change covering the whole planet seems to have made life rather suddenly more difficult for man, and therefore for his parasite. Driven by starvation, the rats began to change their habits. Not content with ravaging man’s food stores, they attacked men themselves. They began by devouring the babies whenever they were left for a while unguarded. Sleeping adults were also attacked. Sometimes a host of hungry rodents would waylay a lonely hunter, seize his legs, clamber up his body, hang on to his flesh with their incisors, bite at his throat, drag him to the ground and devour him alive. It seems probable that some mutation in the rat had increased its efficiency as a carnivorous beast, for attack on large mammals and particularly on men became increasingly common. Men were by now much reduced in stature, rats increased in weight. There came a time when the rats no longer confined their attention to stealthy attacks on children and sleeping adults or to persons isolated from their fellows. They gathered in great armies and invaded the scattered settlements, exterminating their inhabitants. Century by century men fought a losing battle. Tribe after tribe was exterminated, country after country depopulated, until only in the most favoured region a few hard-pressed families lurked in the woods, feeding on roots and worms, meeting at the full moon in solemn conclave to chant their spells against the rodent enemy, and assert with stupid pride their superiority over all beasts. The almost meaningless jargon which issued from these baying mouths was their one remaining title to humanity. In it there still lurked fantastic corruptions of civilized speech, relics which had lived in the times of Shakespeare, Plato, Con-fu-tsze. For a few decades, perhaps centuries, these ultimate remnants of mankind hung on to life, attacked not only by the rats but many other pests and plagues, and by the weather. In this constant warfare their frail human physique combined with their sub-human mentality to make extinction inevitable. At some time or other, unmourned and unnoticed, the last human being was destroyed.</p>
<p>The planet which had once and again haltingly attained the lucid mentality sank now for ever into torpor. For no species remained on earth capable of evolving to the human level. The torch which had fallen from the hand of man could never be picked up and carried forward by a fresh runner. For incalculable aeons, for a period immeasurably longer than the whole career of mankind, the terrestrial globe spun and circled, its surface possessed by a host of lowly creatures.</p>
<p>Meanwhile the sun, like all stars of his age and size, was growing hotter, through the increasingly rapid release of energy in his interior. The more highly specialized biological types on the Earth were gradually destroyed. The lowlier kinds became adapted to an ever more torrid climate. More and still more of the ocean vaporized into the atmosphere, shutting out the heavens with perennial cloud. Little by little conditions on the earth passed beyond the limit of adaptability of any terrestrial species. The ocean began to boil, the sands to melt, the atmosphere to vanish into outer space. The increasing heat of the sun, however, had favoured the evolution of life on Uranus. Slowly, as on Earth, there appeared a multitude of species. And as on Earth these one by one reached a climax of specialization beyond which no further evolution was possible to them. At last, as on Earth, one single type, specialized only for versatility, stood at the threshold of lucidity. But then the sun, as so many stars before him, exploded into the nova state, fusing all his planets.</p>
<p>These remote events I did not witness. They seem to have been obscurely borne in upon my mind through contact with the minds of my superhuman fellow explorers.</p>
<p>My personal experience was confined to terrestrial events. And as soon as earth’s brief flicker of lucidity had ended, my attention was withdrawn from this whole sad stream of time, in which the will for darkness had prevailed. For other scarcely less agonizing but glorious events were all the while unfolding before me.</p>
</section>
