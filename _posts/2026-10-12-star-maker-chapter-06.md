<section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="zh" lang="zh-CN" aria-label="中文译文">
<p>绝不能以为，银河系中智慧种族的正常命运就是取得胜利。到目前为止，我主要讲的是那些比较幸运的“棘皮人”和“鹦鹉螺人”世界；它们最终确实成功进入了更加觉醒的状态，而对于另外成百上千个遭遇灾难的世界，我几乎连提都没有提。这种选择不可避免：一来篇幅有限，二来这两个世界连同下一章将要介绍的那些更加奇异的星球，后来都对整个银河系的命运产生了重大影响。然而，还有许多达到“人类”等级的世界，其历史同样丰富。那里每一个个体的生命并不比别处单调，也同样充满痛苦与欢乐。有些世界成功了；有些则在最后阶段迅速或缓慢地崩溃，反而因此获得一种悲剧的壮丽。只不过，它们没有在银河系的主线故事中扮演特殊角色，所以我只能略过；至于更多甚至从未达到“人类”等级的世界，就更只能沉默。如果我把篇幅都耗在这些世界各自的命运上，就会像一个历史学家试图描述共同体中每个人的一生，却完全忽略整体历史的形态。</p>
<p>我已经说过，随着我们目睹越来越多世界毁灭，宇宙的巨大浪费和表面上的无目的性越来越令我们沮丧。如此多的世界经历如此漫长的痛苦，好不容易几乎就要达到社会和平与幸福，杯子却在嘴边被永远夺走。灾难往往只源于气质或生物本性中的一点微小缺陷。有些种族智慧不够，有些则缺乏足够的社会意志，无法应对一个统一世界共同体必然出现的问题。有些在医学尚未成熟之前，就被一种突然崛起的细菌消灭；另一些败给气候变化，更多则因为大气流失而灭亡。有时，终结来自行星系统撞入稠密的尘埃云、气体云，或者巨型流星群。还有不少世界毁于卫星坠落。较小的卫星一代又一代穿行于星际空间中极其稀薄、却无处不在的自由原子云，会不断损失动量；它的轨道于是逐渐收缩，起初很慢，后来越来越快。它会在较大行星的海洋中掀起惊人巨潮，淹没大部分文明。再后来，随着行星引力施加的潮汐应力越来越强，这颗巨大的月亮开始解体。首先砸到人类头顶的是它的海洋，形成席卷全球的洪水；接着是山脉；最后则是核心那炽热而规模惊人的碎块。倘若一个世界没有以这些方式结束，那么即使要等到银河系晚年，它最终也必然以另一种方式结束：行星自身的轨道也会不可逆地收缩，迟早把每一个世界带得离太阳太近，超过生命能够适应的界限；所有生命便会一代又一代地干枯、灼烤，直至死亡。</p>
<p>目睹这些规模巨大的灾难时，我们一次又一次被沮丧、恐惧和惊骇攫住。对那些世界最后幸存者所产生的剧烈怜悯，也成为我们这场教育的一部分。</p>
<p>那些已经发展到最高程度、却遭到毁灭的世界其实不需要我们的怜悯，因为它们的居民似乎能够平静地迎接自己珍爱的一切走向终结，甚至怀有一种奇怪、不可动摇的欢乐——在冒险这个早期阶段，我们完全无法理解这种欢乐。可是，能够达到这种状态的世界少之又少；而在浩大的世界总数中，真正能够走到所有生命都在摸索追求的社会和平与充实，也只是极少数。至于那些发展程度较低的世界，即使在各自不完善本性所允许的狭窄范围内，也只有很少的个体真正从生命中得到满足。毫无疑问，几乎每一个世界总有零星一两个人，不仅获得幸福，而且获得一种超越一切理解的喜悦。但此时，我们已经被上千个种族的苦难和徒劳压得喘不过气，于是竟觉得这种喜悦本身、这种狂喜——无论只存在于零散个体，还是遍及整个世界——终究也应该被判为虚假；那些找到它的人，大概只是被自己私人而异常良好的精神状态麻醉了。否则，他们怎么可能对周围的恐怖如此不敏感？</p>
<p>支撑我们继续朝圣的动力，正是过去曾驱使地球人寻找上帝的那种饥渴。是的，我们每一个人离开自己的故乡行星，都是为了弄清一个问题：从整个宇宙来看，我们在心中隐约认识、又笨拙珍视的那种精神——地球上有时称之为“人道”的精神——究竟是宇宙之主，还是一个被放逐者；究竟全能，还是被钉在十字架上。而现在，一个答案似乎越来越清楚：如果宇宙确有某位主宰，那就不是这种精神，而是另一种东西。它创造无穷世界喷泉的目的，对自己创造出来的生命并不具有父亲般的关怀，而是陌生的、非人的、幽暗的。</p>
<p>然而，和沮丧同时增强的，还有另一种饥渴：无论这个宇宙真正的精神究竟是什么，我们都越来越想看见它，并且无畏地面对它。随着朝圣继续，我们一次又一次从悲剧走向闹剧，从闹剧走向荣耀，再从荣耀常常走向最终悲剧；与此同时，一种感觉越来越强烈——某个可怕而神圣、同时又荒谬到无法想象、致命到无法想象的秘密，仿佛就在我们伸手够不到的地方。我们反复被撕扯在恐惧与迷恋之间，也被撕扯在对宇宙——或者造星主——的道德愤怒，与一种没有理性根据的崇拜之间。</p>
<p>同样的冲突，也存在于所有与我们具有相近心智高度的世界中。观察这些世界，观察它们过去成长的各个阶段，又尽我们所能摸索下一个精神发展层次之后，我们终于清楚看见任何一个世界朝圣旅程的最初几步。每个正常智慧世界哪怕处在最原始时代，也总有一些心灵产生冲动，想要寻找并赞颂某种普遍存在之物。起初，这种冲动总与寻求强大力量保护自己的欲望混杂在一起。智慧生命不可避免地推论：既然值得赞颂，那一定就是“力量”；所谓崇拜，也不过是安抚强者。于是，它们构想出宇宙中那位全能暴君，并把自己想象成他的宠儿。可是随着时间过去，先知们逐渐看清：赞颂之心真正崇敬的并不只是力量。理论于是又把“智慧”、或者“法则”、或者“正义”扶上王座。等一个时代都服从某个虚构的立法者，或者服从所谓神圣法则本身以后，这些生命又发现：这些概念仍不足以描述那种不可描述的荣耀——心灵在万物中都会遇见它，又在无言中珍视它。</p>
<p>然而这时，在我们访问过的每一个世界里，崇拜者面前都会分出不同道路。有些人希望只靠向内探寻的冥想，最终与被帷幕遮蔽的神面对面。他们试图清除一切较低级、较琐碎的欲望，努力以无私的眼光和普遍的同情看待万物，希望这样就能使自己与宇宙精神同一。许多人确实沿着自我完善和觉醒的道路走得很远。但也正因为这种向内吸收，大多数人逐渐对那些尚未觉醒同伴的苦难变得迟钝，对整个种族的共同事业也不再关心。在不少世界里，恰恰是种族中最富生命力、最优秀的心灵，蜂拥走上了这条精神道路。</p>
<p>由于种族最优秀的注意力全部投入内在生活，物质与社会发展便停滞下来。关于物质自然和生命的科学始终没有发展；机械动力不为人知，医学和生物学技术也同样如此。于是，这些世界陷入停滞，迟早会败给某种原本完全可能避免的事故。</p>
<p>对于更具实践气质的生命来说，还有第二条虔敬道路可走。在所有世界中，这类人都愉快地把注意力投向周围宇宙；而他们最常发现自己所崇拜之物的地方，是同类人格本身，以及人格之间由相互理解与爱构成的共同体纽带。在自己和彼此身上，他们把“爱”看得高于一切。</p>
<p>于是，他们的先知告诉他们：你们一直崇拜的那个存在——普遍精神、创造者、全能者、全知者——同时也是全爱者。因此，应该通过彼此之间实际的爱，以及服务于“爱之神”来进行崇拜。于是，在或长或短的一个时代中，他们软弱地努力去爱，努力使彼此成为共同体的成员。他们编织出一套套理论，为“爱之神”的理论辩护；建立祭司阶层和神殿，为爱服务。又因为他们渴望永生，便有人告诉他们，爱就是获得永恒生命的道路。于是，本来不求回报的爱反而被误解了。</p>
<p>在大多数世界里，这些务实心灵最终压过冥想者。实践好奇心与经济需求迟早会催生物质科学。生命用科学探查所有领域，却无论在原子中、银河系中，甚至在“人”自己的心中，都找不到“爱之神”的任何签名。与此同时，机械化的狂热、主人对奴隶的剥削、部族战争的激情，以及对精神上一切较为觉醒活动越来越严重的忽视与粗化，使它们心中那点微弱的赞颂之火，比任何更早时代都更加黯淡，低到连它们自己都认不出来。爱之火也一样：它长期被教义强迫吹出的气流煽旺，如今却因为众生命彼此之间普遍迟钝而窒息，只剩偶尔一点闷燃的温热，而且最常被误认为不过是肉欲。饱受折磨的生命终于带着苦笑与愤怒，把“爱之神”的形象从心中推下王座。</p>
<p>于是，这些不幸生命既没有爱，也没有崇拜，只能赤手面对一个被机械化与仇恨撕裂、问题越来越可怕的世界。</p>
<p>这正是我们在自己的世界里再熟悉不过的危机。银河系各处有许多世界永远无法越过它。但少数世界中，某种我们当时还无法清楚想象的奇迹，却把这些世界的平均心智提升到更高层次。关于这件事，后面还会再讲。此刻只需指出：凡是发生这种变化的少数世界，我们都会无一例外地在其心灵超出我们接触范围之前，察觉它们对宇宙产生一种全新的感受；而这种感受对我们来说极难分享。直到我们学会在自己内部也唤起一点类似感受，才有能力继续追踪这些世界的后来命运。</p>
<p>与此同时，随着朝圣继续，我们自己的欲望也开始改变。我们渐渐怀疑：我们把自己以及所有世界同类身上最珍贵的、具有神圣意味的人道精神，要求为宇宙之主，这种要求本身会不会反而是一种不敬。我们越来越不再坚持“爱”必须高踞群星之后的王座；相反，我们越来越只想继续向前，打开自己的心，无所畏惧地接受任何进入理解范围的真理。</p>
<p>在这段朝圣早期接近末尾时，曾有一个时刻，我们思想和感情完全一致，彼此说道：“如果造星主就是爱，那么我们知道，这必定正确。但如果他不是爱，如果他是另一种非人的精神，这也必定正确。假如他什么都不是，假如群星以及一切事物并非他的创造，而是自存；假如我们崇敬的精神只是自身心灵创造出的一个精妙造物，那么这同样必定正确，而且只有这一种可能能够正确。因为我们不知道，爱最高的位置究竟是在王座上，还是在十字架上。我们不知道究竟何种精神统治一切，因为王座上坐着的是黑暗。我们知道，而且亲眼见过：在群星的荒野中，爱确实被钉上十字架；而这也是应当的——为了证明爱自身，也为了王座的荣耀。爱，以及一切人道之物，我们都珍藏在心里。但我们也向王座致敬，也向王座上的黑暗致敬。无论那里坐着的是爱还是非爱，我们的心都超越理性而赞颂它。”</p>
<p>然而，要让我们的心真正与这种全新而陌生的感受协调一致，还必须走很远的路；我们对于那些虽然多样、却仍属于人类心智等级的世界，也还有许多需要理解。现在，我必须尝试勾勒几个与我们自己的世界极不相同、但在根本精神成熟度上并未更高的世界类型。</p>
</section>
<section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="en" lang="en" aria-label="English original" hidden>
<h3>VI. INTIMATIONS OF THE STAR MAKER</h3>
<p>IT MUST NOT be supposed that the normal fate of intelligent races in the galaxy is to triumph. So far I have spoken mainly of those fortunate Echinoderm and Nautiloid worlds which did at last pass triumphantly into the more awakened state, and I have scarcely even mentioned the hundreds, the thousands, of worlds which met disaster. This selection was inevitable because my space is limited, and because these two worlds, together with the even stranger spheres that I shall describe in the next chapter, were to have great influence on the fortunes of the whole galaxy. But many other worlds of ‘human’ rank were quite as rich in history as those which I have noticed. Individual lives in them were no less varied than lives elsewhere, and no less crowded with distress and joy. Some triumphed; some in their last phase suffered a downfall, swift or slow, which lent them the splendour of tragedy. But since these worlds play no special part in the main story of the galaxy, they must be passed over in silence, along with the still greater host of worlds which never attained even to ‘human’ rank. If I were to dwell upon their fortunes I should commit the same error as a historian who should try to describe every private life and neglect the pattern of the whole community.</p>
<p>I have already said that, as our experience of the destruction of worlds increased, we were increasingly dismayed by the wastefulness and seeming aimlessness of the universe. So many worlds, after so much distress, attained so nearly to social peace and joy, only to have the cup snatched from them for ever. Often disaster was brought by some trivial flaw of temperament or biological nature. Some races had not the intelligence, some lacked the social will, to cope with the problems of a unified world-community. Some were destroyed by an upstart bacterium before their medical science was mature. Others succumbed to climatic change, many to loss of atmosphere. Sometimes the end came through collision with dense clouds of dust or gas, or with swarms of giant meteors. Not a few worlds were destroyed by the downfall of a satellite. The lesser body, ploughing its way, age after age, through the extremely rarefied but omnipresent cloud of free atoms in interstellar space, would lose momentum. Its orbit would contract, at first slowly, then rapidly. It would set up prodigious tides in the oceans of the larger body, and drown much of its civilization. Later, through the increasing stress of the planet’s attraction, the great moon would begin to disintegrate. First it would cast its ocean in a deluge on men’s heads, then its mountains, and then the titanic and fiery fragments of its core. If in none of these manners came the end of the world, then inevitably, though perhaps not till the latter days of the galaxy, it must come in another way. The planet’s own orbit, fatally contracting, must bring every world at last so close to its sun that conditions must pass beyond the limit of life’s adaptability, and age by age all living things must be parched to death and roasted.</p>
<p>Dismay, terror, horror many a time seized us as we witnessed these huge disasters. An agony of pity for the last survivors of these worlds was part of our schooling.</p>
<p>The most developed of the slaughtered worlds did not need our pity, since their inhabitants seemed capable of meeting the end of all that they cherished with peace, even a strange unshakable joy which we in this early stage of our adventure could by no means comprehend. But only a few, very few, could reach this state. And only a few out of the great host of worlds could win through even to the social peace and fulness toward which all were groping. In the more lowly worlds, moreover, few were the individuals who won any satisfaction of life even within the narrow bounds of their own imperfect nature. No doubt one or two, here and there, in almost every world, found not merely happiness but the joy that passes all understanding. But to us, crushed now by the suffering and futility of a thousand races, it seemed that this joy itself, this ecstasy, whether it was supported by scattered individuals or by whole worlds, must after all be condemned as false, and that those who had found it must after all have been drugged by their own private and untypical well-being of spirit. For surely it had made them insensitive to the horror around them.</p>
<p>The sustaining motive of our pilgrimage had been the hunger which formerly drove men on Earth in search of God. Yes, we had one and all left our native planets in order to discover whether, regarding the cosmos as a whole, the spirit which we all in our hearts obscurely knew and haltingly prized, the spirit which on Earth we sometimes call humane, was Lord of the Universe, or outlaw; almighty, or crucified. And now it was becoming clear to us that if the cosmos had any lord at all, he was not that spirit but some other, whose purpose in creating the endless fountain of worlds was not fatherly toward the beings that he had made, but alien, inhuman, dark.</p>
<p>Yet while we felt dismay, we felt also increasingly the hunger to see and to face fearlessly whatever spirit was indeed the spirit of this cosmos. For as we pursued our pilgrimage, passing again and again from tragedy to farce, from farce to glory, from glory often to a final tragedy, we felt increasingly the sense that some terrible, some holy, yet at the same time unimaginably outrageous and lethal, secret lay just beyond our reach. Again and again we were torn between horror and fascination, between moral rage against the universe (or the Star Maker) and unreasonable worship.</p>
<p>This same conflict was to be observed in all those worlds that were of our own mental stature. Observing these worlds and the phases of their past growth, and groping as best we might toward the next plane of spiritual development, we came at last to see plainly the first stages of any world’s pilgrimage. Even in the most primitive ages of every normal intelligent world there existed in some minds the impulse to seek and to praise some universal thing. At first this impulse was confused with the craving for protection by some mighty power. Inevitably the beings theorized that the admired thing must be Power, and that worship was mere propitiation. Thus they came to conceive the almighty tyrant of the universe, with themselves as his favoured children. But in time it became clear to their prophets that mere Power was not what the praiseful heart adored. Then theory enthroned Wisdom, or Law, or Righteousness. And after an age of obedience to some phantom lawgiver, or to divine legality itself, the beings found that these concepts too were inadequate to describe the indescribable glory that the heart confronted in all things, and mutely prized in all things.</p>
<p>But now, in every world that we visited, alternative ways opened out before the worshippers. Some hoped to come face to face with their shrouded god solely by inward-searching meditation. By purging themselves of all lesser, all trivial desires, by striving to see everything dispassionately and with universal sympathy, they hoped to identify themselves with the spirit of the cosmos. Often they travelled far along the way of self-perfecting and awakening. But because of this inward absorption most of them became insensitive to the suffering of their less-awakened fellows and careless of the communal enterprise of their kind. In not a few worlds this way of the spirit was thronged by all the most vital minds. And because the best attention of the race was given wholly to the inner life, material and social advancement was checked. The sciences of physical nature and of life never developed. Mechanical power remained unknown, and medical and biological power also. Consequently these worlds stagnated, and sooner or later succumbed to accidents which might well have been prevented.</p>
<p>There was a second way of devotion, open to creatures of a more practical temperament. These, in all the worlds, gave delighted attention to the universe around them, and chiefly they found the worshipped thing in the persons of their fellow-beings, and in the communal bond of mutual insight and love between persons. In themselves and in each other they prized above all things love.</p>
<p>And their prophets told them that the thing which they had always adored, the universal spirit, the Creator, the Almighty, the All-wise, was also the All-loving. Let them therefore worship in practical love of one another, and in service of the Love-God. And so for an age, short or long, they strove feebly to love and to become members one of another. They spun theories in defence of the theory of the Love-God. They set up priesthoods and temples in service of Love. And because they hungered for immortality they were told that to love was the way to attain eternal life. And so love, which seeks no reward, was misconceived.</p>
<p>In most worlds these practical minds dominated over the meditators. Sooner or later practical curiosity and economic need produced the material sciences. Probing every region with these sciences, the beings found nowhere, neither in the atom nor in the galaxy, nor for that matter in the heart of ‘man’ either, any signature of the Love-God. And what with the fever of mechanization, and the exploitation of slaves by masters, and the passions of inter-tribal warfare, and the increasing neglect or coarsening of all the more awakened activities of the spirit, the little flame of praise in their hearts sank lower than it had ever been in any earlier age, so low that they could no longer recognize it. And the flame of love, long fanned by the forced draught of doctrine, but now suffocated by the general obtuseness of the beings to one another, was reduced to an occasional smouldering warmth, which was most often mistaken for mere lust. With bitter laughter and rage the tortured beings now dethroned the image of the Love-God in their hearts.</p>
<p>And so without love and without worship the unhappy beings faced the increasingly formidable problems of their mechanized and hate-racked world.</p>
<p>This was the crisis which we in our own worlds knew so well. Many a world up and down the galaxy never surmounted it. But in a few, some miracle, which we could not yet clearly envisage, raised the average minds of these worlds to a higher plane of mentality. Of this I shall speak later. Meanwhile I will say only that in the few worlds where this happened, we noticed invariably, before the minds of that world passed beyond our reach, a new feeling about the universe, a feeling which it was very difficult for us to share. Not till we had learned to conjure in ourselves something of this feeling could we follow the fortunes of these worlds.</p>
<p>But, as we advanced on our pilgrimage, our own desires began to change. We came to wonder whether, in demanding lordship of the universe for the divinely humane spirit that we prized most in ourselves and in our fellow-mortals in all the worlds, we were perhaps impious. We came less and less to require that Love should be enthroned behind the stars; more and more we desired merely to pass on, opening our hearts to accept fearlessly whatever of the truth might fall within our comprehension.</p>
<p>There was a moment, late in this early phase of our pilgrimage, when, thinking and feeling in unison, we said to one another, ‘If the Star Maker is Love, we know that this must be right. But if he is not, if he is some other, some inhuman spirit, this must be right. And if he is nothing, if the stars and all else are not his creatures but self-subsistent, and if the adored spirit is but an exquisite creature of our minds, then this must be right, this and no other possibility. For we cannot know whether the highest place for love is on the throne or on the cross. We cannot know what spirit rules, for on the throne sits darkness. We know, we have seen, that in the waste of stars love is indeed crucified; and rightly, for its own proving, and for the throne’s glory. Love and all that is humane we cherish in our hearts. Yet also we salute the throne and the darkness upon the throne. Whether it be Love or not Love, our hearts praise it, out-soaring reason.’</p>
<p>But before our hearts could be properly attuned to this new, strange feeling, we had still far to go in the understanding of worlds of human rank, though diverse. I must now try to give some idea of several kinds of worlds very different from our own, but not in essentials more mature.</p>
