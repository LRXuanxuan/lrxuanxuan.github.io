<h2>四、为伟大任务作准备</h2>
<p>从这以后，我与遥远未来人类之间的联系变得越来越不确定。其实，我能够一直保持联系到这个阶段，本身就已经近乎奇迹。若没有那些与我共同观看未来的超人存在不断影响我，这件事根本不可能发生。然而如今，即使他们在场，也已经无法给我足够帮助。我想，原因在于人类动物的心智开始以一种新的方式超出我的精神范围。过去，我一直受到一个严重限制：我没有这些未来人类的文化背景；但直到此前，他们头脑本身的实际能力并没有比我这一代人高出太多。如今，人类事务开始包含对我而言完全没有意义的主题。事件变得越难理解，我就越难维持联系。</p>
<p>尽管如此，我还是得到了一些关于历史总体进程、以及少数重大事件的大致印象。那场大争论解决以后，人类重新恢复了根本上的目标统一。各个村庄继续过着繁忙而多样的生活，并保持遍及世界的往来。科学家继续耐心探索、发明。古典派让人类文化朝无穷无尽的方向继续展开。前行者则坚持自己的精神探索。随着思想与情感的一般水平提高，新的经验领域不断被打开。一代接一代，人类的能力和机会都越来越大。但与此同时，每一代也越来越清楚地知道：所有这种持续的乌托邦生活，其实都只是在为一场伟大的考验作准备；而在人类还没有准备好面对它以前，存在本身的基础随时可能坍塌。群星可能突然像尘土一样被扫去。人类亲爱的、美丽的家园可能粉碎，人类自身也可能彻底灭绝。</p>
<p>这种知识似乎并没有沉重压在人们身上。每一代都正视它，再使自己适应它。然而，由于这种可能性始终潜藏在每个人意识背景里，整个人类的精神气候已经与前行者作出那项奇异发现以前大不相同。当时，对无限人类进步的展望产生了某种自满；如今，无止境前进的期待被突然毁灭的可能性取代，同时又伴随一种脆弱的希望，指向完全陌生的新地平线。于是，这个物种一方面更强烈地珍惜普通世俗生活——由个人的欢乐与悲伤构成的生活——另一方面又更持续、更坚定地忠于精神。毫无疑问，一个忙于私人事务的普通人，很少会经常有意识地思考物种前途；反正他会觉得，人类大概至少能活过自己这一生。但每当进入静观状态，那种万物流逝的感受便会深深进入心灵，于是行星上的物质景象——森林、山岭、大海——总带上一层额外的痛楚与珍贵。日常生活里的习惯动作，例如穿衣、进食；工作中的技术；平凡细小的友善行为；熟悉声音的语调——这一切都会因为更加不稳定而更加可爱，因为它们仿佛每天都悬在未知深渊的边缘。同时，个人行为标准似乎也因此提高，因为整个物种已经接受挑战，决心活得超出自己通常的天性。</p>
<p>我能够意识到，文化的重心正在逐渐移动。形而上学吸引了越来越多人类注意力，自然科学则趋向退居第二位。每一个人都会接受某种精神纪律。前行者数量大幅增加，影响也越来越深入社会。</p>
<p>这些事情我虽然只能从外部、而且十分模糊地理解，至少还勉强能够意识到。真正超出我理解能力的，是社会与文化生活具体内容如何变化。在这种环境里，生活越来越简朴是很自然的。奢侈品的需求持续下降。艺术也剥去了繁复的装饰细节。另一方面，一种简练、目的明确的艺术却以越来越重要、同时也有所改变的方式介入生活。人们用文字、音乐、色彩和造型，源源不断创造帮助精神生活的象征物；其中绝大多数风格，我完全无法欣赏。还有一个令人意外的现象：虽然所有人都生活在随时可能毁灭的威胁下，人类却热衷于修建巨大而耐久的神殿，并把那些逐渐不再用于日常生活的全部技巧与精力都倾注其上。亚原子技术提供了极其丰富的新材料，使一种比我们所知任何建筑都更加大胆、更加高耸而且色彩斑斓的建筑成为可能。新材料也带来一套令我陌生的新建筑法则。世俗生活的建筑都很简单，也不追求长久；只有神殿被建成永久之物——可即便是神殿，也经常被拆毁，为更精美的建筑让路。</p>
<p>文化中一个特别醒目的方面，是人际交往技术出现巨大进步。语言长成一片茂密森林，为情绪与直觉中新出现的一切细微差别，也为人格的一切类型与色调创造词汇。新世界的公民只需借助这套丰富语言象征，与陌生人相处一小时，就能对其人格获得极为深入的认识。此外，人类还发展出一套精细的表意系统，用来表现心理和精神现象。某种介乎小说家和抽象画家之间的艺术家，只需要仔细画出若干看起来有些像汉字的符号，就能够呈现几个人从出生到死亡全部交往关系的基本形态。与这些表意图形相比，即使已经极大改进的口头语言也显得笨重。仅仅一页精心书写的符号，就能传达一整部传记。由此产生了一种新的视觉艺术：它使用高度抽象、却负载着人类全部情感与思想经验的符号，获得了伟大诗歌那种影响深远的力量。</p>
<p>这种表意艺术我至少还能理解到足以把握其大致性质；但它显然也象征了许多我完全无法进入的经验范围。神殿装饰中，它占据极重要位置；还有一些对我始终毫无意义的表意符号，却似乎能对任何认真凝视它们的人产生某种神秘力量。</p>
<p>我与未来人类的联系变得越来越模糊、越来越断续，最后只能偶然捕捉到少数重大而且往往非常奇怪的事件。有时，例如我仿佛看到大批男女主动选择死亡，因为他们觉得自己已经无法再发挥有益作用。有时，人类内部的一致会因为某个完全超出我理解的问题而被打破，争论非常尖锐，却从来不带报复性。随后，整个世界会认为有必要通过一次全球性的忏悔来恢复和谐。</p>
<p>终于——我不知道这已经又过去几百年还是几千年——出现了这样一代人：他们认为自己已经为那项伟大任务做好充分准备。人们规定二十五年以后举行“圣年”，在那一年发动最高努力。从此直到圣年到来，必须停止生育，也停止一切面向未来的社会与经济活动。人类仍必须生产足够食物，让这个苦修中的人口继续活着，也必须维护神殿；除此之外，整个物种的一切能量都应完全投入那项伟大任务。</p>
<p>这一时期的世界异常奇特而严峻。最初到处都不再有婴儿，随后没有儿童，再后来连青少年也消失了；世界上只剩青年男女以及更年长者。人口当然迅速下降。整个生活完全受那项精神事业支配，而它不可避免地超出我的理解范围。人们常常专注精神活动到完全忘记肉体世界，甚至忘记吃饭；如果邻人不提醒，有些人真的会饿死。不过大多数人仍然维持某种正常生活，只是始终处在一种遥远的超脱状态。</p>
<p>人们把完全禁食的日期定在第二十五年接近末尾的时候。在此以前，全世界都将逐步减少食物摄入，好让精神不再受到肉体活力牵制。等到彻底停止进食的日子到来，一切私人住宅都将被遗弃。全体人口要聚集到普布和神殿中，禁食、静观，在自身内部创造那种极端的精神清明；如今人们满怀信心地相信，这种清明能够摧毁“泰坦”，同时使意识抵达一种关于全部存在更加清晰、更加明亮、更加真实的视野。整个人类种族将在这场冒险中耗尽生命而死。地球会重新交还给低于人类的自然。也许有一天，另一个世界的访客会发现那些巨大神殿的废墟，却完全不知道建造它们、又死在其中的人已经战胜“泰坦”，从而拯救了所有雪花宇宙中的全部存在。当然，人们设想的这种“拯救”，并不是让个体生命在外部意义上永生，而是使各个智慧种族免于在自身潜能尚未充分实现、尚未达到精神成熟与最高至福直观以前就过早灭绝。</p>
</section>

<h2>iv. PREPARATION FOR A GREAT TASK</h2>
<p>FROM THIS TIME forward my contact with the human race in the far future became more and more uncertain. It was of course something of a miracle that I had been able to keep in touch even thus far. Without the constant influence of the superhuman beings who were my fellow spectators even this would have been utterly impossible. But now even their presence could not sufficiently aid me. This was due, I think, to the fact that the mentality of human animals was beginning to outreach my mental range in a new manner. I had always been grievously hampered by the fact that I had not the cultural background of these future men, but the actual calibre of their minds had not hitherto been greatly superior to that of my own generation. Now, however, human affairs began to include themes which were wholly meaningless to me. And as events became less intelligible I was less able to maintain contact.</p>
<p>I did, nevertheless, receive certain general impressions of the course of history and of a few outstanding events. After the settlement of the great dispute mankind recovered its fundamental unity of purpose. The villages carried on their busy and varied lives and their worldwide intercourse. The scientists continued their patient explorations and inventions. The classicists pursued the development of human culture into endless exfoliation. The forwards persisted in their spiritual exploration. As the general level of thought and feeling was raised, new spheres of experience were constantly explored. Generation succeeded generation with ever increasing capacity and opportunity. But also each generation came more surely into the knowledge that all this continuous Utopianism was in fact but a preparation for a great ordeal, and that before the race was ready to face that ordeal the very foundations of existence might crumble. The stars might suddenly be swept away like dust. Man’s dear and beautiful home might be shattered, and man himself annihilated.</p>
<p>This knowledge did not seem to weigh heavily on men. Each generation faced it and accommodated themselves to it. But its presence in the background of every mind changed the temper of the race into something very different from that of the age before the forwards had made their strange discovery. Then, the prospect of limitless human advancement had bred a certain complacency; now, the expectation of endless progress was succeeded by the possibility of sudden destruction, and by the frail hope of utterly new horizons. The mental climate of the race therefore changed to an intenser appreciation of its ordinary mundane life, compact of personal joys and sorrows, and at the same time a more constant loyalty to the spirit. No doubt the ordinary man, intent on his private affairs, gave little conscious thought to the prospect of the race, which, he felt, would probably last out his time anyhow. But in his phases of contemplation the sense of fleetingness would enter deeply into his mind, so that at all times the physical features of the planet, the woods, the hills, the sea, affected him with an added poignancy. The customs of daily life, such as dressing and eating, the technique of his work, the little common acts of friendliness, the intonations of familiar voices, all these became more dear because more precarious, because balanced from day to day on the brink of the unknown. At the same time the standard of personal conduct was seemingly raised by the sense that the species as a whole had accepted the challenge to live beyond its normal nature.</p>
<p>I was able to realize that there was a gradual shift, so to speak, of the centre of gravity of culture. Metaphysics was absorbing more and more of human attention. The natural sciences tended to fall into a second place. Spiritual discipline was undertaken by every member of the race. The numbers of the forwards greatly increased, and their influence became more far-reaching.</p>
<p>All this I could realize, though vaguely and externally. What passed my comprehension was the changing detail of social and cultural life. It was natural in the circumstances that living should be greatly simplified. Luxuries were less and less in demand. The arts were shorn of their luxurious detail. On the other hand art of a stripped and purposeful kind played an increasing though an altered part in life. In words, in music, in colour and plastic form, men created a ceaseless flood of symbolic aids to the spirit, mostly in styles which I could not at all appreciate. Surprisingly, also, though living under the threat of annihilation, men were addicted to erecting great and durable temples, upon which they lavished all the skill and care which was ceasing to find an outlet in ordinary life. Sub-atomic technique, by its wealth of new materials, had made possible a far more daring, soaring, and colourful architecture than is known to us. Along with the new materials came new architectural canons, strange to me. The architecture of mundane life was simple and impermanent. The temples alone were built to last; yet they were often demolished to make room for finer structures.</p>
<p>One striking aspect of culture was a vast development of the technique of personal intercourse. Language blossomed into a great forest of terms for all the new subtleties of emotion and intuition, and all the types and shades of personality. The citizen of the new world could by the Use of this rich linguistic symbolism become intimately aware of a stranger’s personality in an hour. There was also a subtle ideography of psychological and spiritual phenomena. By the careful drawing of a number of Chinese-looking symbols an artist who was something between a novelist and an abstract painter could present the essential form of the intercourse of several human beings from birth to death. In comparison with these ideograms verbal language, though so greatly improved, was a cumbersome medium. A single meticulously inscribed page could convey a whole biography. Thus arose a new visual art, which, by means of highly abstract signs charged with the emotional and intellectual experience of the race, obtained the far-reaching effect of great poetry.</p>
<p>This ideographic art I could at least comprehend sufficiently to grasp its general nature, but it must also have symbolized ranges of experience beyond my reach. It played a great part in the decoration of the temples; and certain ideograms, which remained meaningless to me, seemed to have a mystical power over anyone that earnestly contemplated them.</p>
<p>My contact with future mankind became more and more vague and intermittent, until I received but random intimations of a few outstanding and often very strange events. Sometimes, for instance, I seemed to see that great companies of men and women had chosen to destroy themselves because they felt that they could no longer play a useful part. Sometimes the concord of the race was broken by a keen but never a vindictive dispute about some matter which lay beyond my understanding. It would then be found necessary to restore harmony by a world-wide penance.</p>
<p>At last, after how many centuries or millennia I know not, there arose a generation which felt itself fully equipped for the great task. A Sacred Year was appointed for the supreme effort, a quarter of a century ahead. Meanwhile procreation was to cease, and all forward- looking social and economic activity. Enough food must still be produced to keep the ascetic population alive, and the temples must be kept in good order. Apart from this necessary work, the energy of the race must be concentrated wholly on the great task.</p>
<p>It was a strange and austere world in this period. No t babies were anywhere, then no children, then no adolescents; only young men and women and their elders. Population, of course, rapidly declined. Life was wholly dominated by the spiritual enterprise, which inevitably lay beyond my comprehension. It was not uncommon for people to be so abstracted from the physical world that they forgot to feed, and so would have starved to death, had not some neighbour recalled them. Most individuals, however, still carried on a normal life, though in a state of remote detachment.</p>
<p>A date was appointed, towards the end of the twenty-fifth year, after which no more food was to be eaten. Meanwhile feeding was to be progressively reduced throughout the world so as to leave the spirit unhampered by bodily vigour. When the time came for the complete cessation of feeding, all private houses were to be deserted. The population was to gather into the poobs and temples, to fast and contemplate, and create in themselves that extreme spiritual lucidity which, it was now confidently believed, would destroy the ‘titans’ and attain a clearer, brighter, truer view of all existence. Under the stress of this adventure the exhausted race would die. The earth would be given over once more to sub-human nature. Visitors from some other world might some day discover the ruins of the great temples, not suspecting, perhaps, that those who had built them and died in them had conquered the ‘titans’, and had thereby secured the salvation of all beings in all the snowflake universes; the salvation, it was surmised, not of external life for individuals, but of escape from premature racial extinction before the potentiality of the race was fulfilled by the attainment of spiritual maturity and the supreme beatific vision.</p>
