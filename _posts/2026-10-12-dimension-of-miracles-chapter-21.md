<h2>第二十一章</h2>
<p>“好吧，进来，进来。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪眨了眨眼。他已经不再和白垩纪的一只恐龙交换意见了。现在他到了另一个地方。那是一间狭小、昏暗而肮脏的房间。石头地板冰凉，冷意直透脚底。窗户上蒙着煤烟。高高的蜡烛在穿堂风里不安地颤抖。</p>
<p>一名男子坐在一张高大的卷盖书桌后面。他有一张瘦削修长的脸，一只长鼻子从脸上突出来。眼窝深陷。左脸颊正中有一颗褐色的痣。嘴唇又薄又没有血色。</p>
<p>那人说：“鄙人是尊敬的克莱德·比德尔·西斯赖特。您当然就是卡莫迪先生——莫兹利先生好心把您的情况转介给了我们。请坐，先生。希望您从莫兹利先生的星球一路过来还算愉快？”</p>
<p>“挺好。”卡莫迪说着坐了下来。他知道自己的态度不太客气，可这些毫无预警的场景转换已经开始让他受不了了。</p>
<p>“莫兹利先生身体可好？”西斯赖特满面笑容地问。</p>
<p>“他很好。”卡莫迪说。“我这是在哪儿？”</p>
<p>“您进来的时候，办事员没有告诉您吗？”</p>
<p>“我没见到什么办事员。我甚至都没看见自己是怎么进来的。”</p>
<p>“哎呀，哎呀。”西斯赖特轻轻咂舌。“接待室一定又跑出相位了。我已经让人修过十几次，可它总会重新失去同步。这对客户很恼人，对我那可怜的办事员就更糟——他会跟着接待室一起脱离相位，有时候一两个星期都回不到家人身边。”</p>
<p>“那可真够倒霉的。”卡莫迪说，发觉自己已经接近歇斯底里。“如果您不介意，”他死死控制住自己的声音，“就告诉我这是什么地方，以及我到底该怎么从这里回家。”</p>
<p>“镇定些。”西斯赖特先生说。“来杯茶怎么样？不要？好吧。您所谓的这个‘地方’，是<strong>银河安置局</strong>。我们的公司章程就挂在墙上，如果您有兴趣，可以读一读。”</p>
<p>“我是怎么到这里来的？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>西斯赖特先生微微一笑，把十根指尖抵在一起。“很简单，先生。我收到莫兹利先生的信后，就安排了一次检索。办事员在地球 B3444123C22 上找到了您。显然，那不是您该待的地方。我的意思是，莫兹利先生已经尽了最大努力，可他毕竟不是干安置业务的。因此，我冒昧把您转送到了这里。不过，如果您希望回到刚才提到的那个地球——”</p>
<p>“不，不。”卡莫迪说。“我只是想知道怎么回事……我是说，您刚才说这里是一个银河安置服务机构，对吧？”</p>
<p>“是<strong>银河安置服务机构</strong>。”西斯赖特温和地纠正道。</p>
<p>“好吧。所以我现在不在地球上。”</p>
<p>“当然不在。或者说得更严谨一些，您并不位于地球构型中任何一个可能的、或然的、潜在的或时间性的世界上。”</p>
<p>“好，很好。”卡莫迪说。他呼吸粗重。“那么，西斯赖特先生，您去过那些地球中的任何一个吗？”</p>
<p>“恐怕一直无缘领略。您知道，我的工作把我牢牢拴在办公室里，假期则都在我们家的乡间别墅——”</p>
<p>“对！”卡莫迪突然咆哮起来。“你从来没去过地球——至少你是这么说的！既然如此，你他妈为什么坐在一间活像狄更斯小说里搬出来的鬼房间里，点着蜡烛，脑袋上还戴着高礼帽？啊？来，回答我！因为我早就知道那个该死的答案了：肯定是哪个狗娘养的给我下了药，然后这一整套混账透顶的疯梦都是我做出来的——包括你，你这个咧嘴傻笑、长着张斧头脸的王八蛋！”</p>
<p>卡莫迪瘫回椅子里，喘得像台蒸汽机，得意洋洋地瞪着西斯赖特。他等着眼前的一切融化，等着奇形怪状的东西出现又消失，等着自己在自己的公寓、自己的床上醒来；或者朋友家的床上也行，哪怕医院病床都行。</p>
<p>什么也没发生。卡莫迪的胜利感一点一点漏光了。他困惑到了极点，却又突然累得根本懒得在乎。</p>
<p>“您的这阵发作结束了吗？”西斯赖特先生冷冷地问。</p>
<p>“结束了。”卡莫迪说。“抱歉。”</p>
<p>“别放在心上。”西斯赖特轻声说。“您承受了很大的压力，这一点谁都能理解。不过，除非您能控制自己，否则我什么也帮不了您。理智也许能把您带回家；狂暴的情绪宣泄只会让您寸步难行。”</p>
<p>“我真的很抱歉。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“至于这间似乎把您吓得不轻的房间，”西斯赖特继续道，“是我特意为您布置的。时代风格只能做到大致相近——时间仓促，我也只能做到这个程度。目的只是让您感觉像在家里。”</p>
<p>“您想得很周到。”卡莫迪说。“这么说，您的外貌也是——”</p>
<p>“对，正是如此。”西斯赖特先生微笑着说。“除了房间，我也把自己装饰了一番。其实没什么麻烦的。很多客户都很欣赏这种小小的贴心之处。”</p>
<p>“事实上，我确实挺欣赏。”卡莫迪说。“现在习惯一点以后，还真有种让人放松的感觉。”</p>
<p>“我正希望它能起到安抚作用。”西斯赖特说。“至于您提出的‘这一切都发生在梦里’的命题——嗯，这个命题有一定可取之处。”</p>
<p>“有吗？”</p>
<p>西斯赖特点头点得很用力。“作为一个命题，它确实颇有价值；但作为对您当前处境的陈述，它并不成立。”</p>
<p>“哦。”卡莫迪说着，靠回椅背。</p>
<p>“严格说来，”西斯赖特继续道，“想象中的事件和真实发生的事件之间，并没有什么重要区别。您在二者之间建立的对立，完全是语言上的。卡莫迪先生，您并没有梦见这一切；不过我提到这一点，也只是附带提供一条信息。即使这一切真是梦，您仍然必须采取完全相同的行动。”</p>
<p>“这些我都听不太懂。”卡莫迪说。“不过我姑且相信这是真的。”他犹豫了一下，又说，“可我真正不明白的是——为什么这一切偏偏是这个样子？我是说，银河中心看起来有点像无线电城；恐龙博格说话的方式，也不像任何恐龙——哪怕是会说话的恐龙——理应有的样子；还有——”</p>
<p>“请别激动。”西斯赖特先生说。</p>
<p>“抱歉。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“您是在问我，现实为什么偏偏是它现在的样子。”西斯赖特说。“可这个问题没有解释。您只能学着调整自己的先入之见，使之适应您实际遇到的东西。您不能指望现实来迁就您——至少绝大多数时候不能。事情很古怪，这没办法；事情很熟悉，同样也没办法。我说明白了吗？”</p>
<p>“我想明白了。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“好极了！您确定不喝茶？”</p>
<p>“不，谢谢。”</p>
<p>“那么，我们该想办法送您回家了。”西斯赖特说。“再没有什么比回到亲爱的老地方更能振奋精神了，对吧？”</p>
<p>“一点没错！”卡莫迪说。“会很困难吗，西斯赖特先生？”</p>
<p>“不，我想我不会把它形容成‘困难’。”西斯赖特说。“当然，它会很复杂，很严苛，甚至还有一定危险。不过我不认为这些东西算得上困难。”</p>
<p>“那什么才叫真正困难？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“解二次方程。”西斯赖特立刻回答。“我就是不会，尽管已经试过上百万次。先生，<strong>那</strong>才叫困难！好了，现在来处理您的情况。”</p>
<p>“您知道地球在哪里吗？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“‘何处’不是问题。”西斯赖特说。“您已经去过正确的‘何处’了，只不过没什么用，因为‘何时’错得太离谱。不过现在，我想我们可以不费太大周折就锁定属于您的那个‘何时’。真正棘手的是‘哪一个’。”</p>
<p>“它会把我们难住吗？”</p>
<p>“完全不会。”西斯赖特说。“我们只需要逐个筛选，找出您究竟属于哪一个‘哪一个’。程序完全直截了当；用你们地球人的说法，就像在桶里打鱼一样容易。”</p>
<p>“我从来没试过。”卡莫迪说。“真的很容易吗？”</p>
<p>“那得看鱼有多大，桶又有多大。”西斯赖特告诉他。“比如说，在浴缸里打死一条鲨鱼几乎不费吹灰之力；可要在一只大酒桶里击中一条小鲦鱼，就会是一项相当艰巨的事业。尺度决定一切。不过无论摆在您面前的是哪一种任务，我想您都能理解它在本质上的直截了当与简单。”</p>
<p>“我想是吧。”卡莫迪说。“不过我忽然想到，我寻找‘哪一个地球’的过程也许确实直截了当又简单，却可能因为选择序列无穷无尽而根本无法完成。”</p>
<p>“这话并不完全正确，不过说得非常漂亮。”西斯赖特眉开眼笑。“复杂性常常很有用，您知道。它有助于把问题界定并辨认清楚。”</p>
<p>“那……现在怎么办？”</p>
<p>“现在开始工作。”西斯赖特说着，利落地搓了搓手。“我和我的工作人员已经整理出一批‘哪一个世界’。我们很有把握，您的世界就在其中。不过当然，只有您自己能判断究竟哪一个才对。”</p>
<p>“所以我挨个看一遍，然后作决定？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“大致如此。”西斯赖特说。“准确地说，您必须挨个<strong>活一遍</strong>。然后，只要您确定了，就向我们示意：我们是否击中了属于您的概率世界，还是只到了一个变体。如果是您的世界，事情就结束了。如果只是变体，我们就把您送到下一个‘哪一个世界’。”</p>
<p>“听起来还算合理。”卡莫迪说。“这种概率世界很多吗？”</p>
<p>“正如您刚才怀疑的那样，数量无穷无尽——至少从实际意义上说如此。不过我们完全有理由期待很快成功。除非——”</p>
<p>“除非什么？”</p>
<p>“除非您的捕食者先找到您。”</p>
<p>“我的捕食者！”</p>
<p>“它仍然紧跟着您的踪迹。”西斯赖特先生说。“而且，您现在已经知道了，它很会设陷阱。这些陷阱会采用从您自己的记忆中摘取的场景。我想可以把它们叫作‘地球化场景’——专门用来麻痹和欺骗您，让您相信眼前的一切，从而毫无戒心地走进它嘴里。”</p>
<p>“它会干扰您送我去的那些世界吗？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“当然会。”西斯赖特说。“搜索过程中不存在任何避难所。恰恰相反——搜索越精确、掌握的信息越充分，危险就越多。您刚才问过我梦与现实的问题。好，那么答案就在这里：凡是帮助您的东西，都会公开地帮助您；凡是想伤害您的东西，则会隐蔽行动，借助幻觉、伪装和梦境。”</p>
<p>“难道您对捕食者一点办法也没有？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“没有。即使有，我也不会采取。捕食是必要的宇宙条件。就连诸神最终也要被命运吞食。您不会成为普遍法则的例外。”</p>
<p>“我就知道您会说类似的话。”卡莫迪说。“可您能不能至少给我一点帮助？比如提示一下：您送我去的世界，和捕食者制造的世界会有什么不同？”</p>
<p>“在我看来，区别显而易见。”西斯赖特说。“可您和我并不共享同一种感知方式。我的洞见对您没用，卡莫迪；您的洞见对我也没用。不过话说回来，直到现在您都成功甩开了捕食者。”</p>
<p>“只是运气好。”</p>
<p>“这不就对了吗！我本领很多，可半点运气也没有。谁知道接下来的考验最需要哪一种品质？我不知道，先生，您当然也不知道！所以打起精神来，卡莫迪先生。懦夫从来赢不到漂亮星球，对不对？仔细检查我送您去的世界；对捕食者制造的幻象场景保持极端警惕；一旦有机会脱身，就赶快离开——可也别因为害怕而丧失勇气，错过真正属于您的那个世界。”</p>
<p>“如果我一不小心错过了呢？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“那您的搜索就永远不会结束。”西斯赖特告诉他。“只有您能告诉我们自己属于哪里。如果由于这样或那样的原因，您没能在最可能的一批世界中认出自己的世界，我们就必须继续搜查那些只是‘比较可能’的，再搜查‘不太可能’的，最后搜查‘最不可能’的。地球的概率世界数量当然并非真正无限；可从您的角度看，跟无限也没什么区别。您自身所拥有的存续时间根本不够把它们全查一遍，然后再从头开始。”</p>
<p>“好吧。”卡莫迪将信将疑地说。“我想也没有别的办法。”</p>
<p>“至少在我能为您提供的帮助范围内，没有别的办法。”西斯赖特说。“而且我怀疑，任何办法都不可能完全不需要您亲自参与。不过，如果您愿意，我可以替您打听一下其他银河定位技术。那会花上一段时间——”</p>
<p>“我恐怕没有那段时间。”卡莫迪说。“我觉得捕食者离我已经不远了。西斯赖特先生，请把我送进那些概率地球吧。另外，也请接受我的感谢，谢谢您的耐心和关照。”</p>
<p>“多谢。”西斯赖特显然很高兴。“但愿第一个世界就是您要找的那个。”</p>
<p>西斯赖特按下书桌上的一个按钮。什么也没发生——直到卡莫迪眨了一下眼。随后事情发生得快得惊人：卡莫迪重新睁开眼睛，发现自己正正好好站在地球上。</p>
<p>或者说，站在一个相当像地球的仿制品上。</p>
</section>

<p><b>CHAPTER 21</b></p>
<p>‘Well, come in, come in.’</p>
<p>Carmody blinked. He was no longer exchanging views with a dinosaur in the Cretaceous age. Now he was somewhere else. He was in a small, dingy room. The floor was of stone, chilly to his feet. The windows were covered with soot. Tall candles trembled uneasily in the draught.</p>
<p>A man was seated behind a high rolltop desk. The man had a long nose jutting out of a long, bony face. His eyes were cavernous. There was a brown mole in the middle of his left cheek. His lips were thin and bloodless.</p>
<p>The man said, ‘I am the Honourable Clyde Beedle Seethwright. And you are Mr Carmody, of course, whom Mr Maudsley so kindly referred to us. Do take that chair, sir. I trust that your trip from Mr Maudsley’s planet was a pleasant one?’</p>
<p>‘It was fine,’ Carmody said, sitting down. He knew he was being ungracious, but the abrupt transitions were beginning to get him down.</p>
<p>‘And Mr Maudsley is well?’ Seethwright said, beaming.</p>
<p>‘He’s fine,’ Carmody said. ‘Where am I?’</p>
<p>‘Didn’t the clerk tell you on your way in?’</p>
<p>‘I didn’t see any clerk. I didn’t even see myself come in.’</p>
<p>‘My, my,’ Seethwright said, and clucked mildly. ‘The reception room must have gone out of phase again. I’ve had it fixed a dozen times, but it keeps on desynchronizing. It is vexing for my clients, and even worse for my poor clerk, who goes out of phase with it and sometimes can’t get back to his family for a week or more.’</p>
<p>‘That is a real tough break,’ Carmody said, and found himself near hysteria. ‘If you don’t mind,’ he said, keeping a tight control on his voice, ‘just tell me what this place is and how I’m supposed to get home from here.’</p>
<p>‘Calm yourself,’ Mr Seethwright said. ‘Perhaps a cup of tea? No? This “place,” as you refer to it, is The Galactic Placement Bureau. Our articles of incorporation are on the wall, if you would care to read them.’</p>
<p>‘How did I get here?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>Mr Seethwright smiled and pressed his fingertips together. ‘Very simply, sir. When I received Mr Maudsley’s letter, I had a search made. The clerk found you on Earth B3444123C22. This was obviously the wrong place for you. I mean to say, Mr Maudsley had done his best, but he is <i>not</i> in the placement service. Therefore, I took the liberty of transporting you here. But if you wish to return to that aforementioned Earth –’</p>
<p>‘No, no,’ Carmody said. ‘I was just wondering how … I mean, you said that this is a Galactic Placement Service, right?’</p>
<p>‘<i>The</i> Galactic Placement Service,’ Seethwright corrected gently.</p>
<p>‘OK. So I’m not on Earth.’</p>
<p>‘No indeed. Or, to put it more rigorously, you are not on any of the possible, probable, potential or temporal worlds of the Earth configuration.’</p>
<p>‘OK, fine,’ Carmody said. He was breathing heavily. ‘Now, Mr Seethwright, have <i>you</i> ever been to any of those Earths?’</p>
<p>‘I’m afraid I have never had the pleasure. My work keeps me pretty well tied to the office, you see, and I spend holidays at my family’s cottage at –’</p>
<p>‘Right!’ Carmody thundered abruptly. ‘You’ve never been to Earth, or so you claim! In that case, why in God’s name are you sitting in a goddamned room like out of <i>Dickens</i> with <i>candles</i> yet and wearing a stove-pipe hat? Hey? Just let’s hear you answer that one, because I already <i>know</i> the damned answer, which is that some son of a bitch must have <i>drugged</i> me and I dreamed this whole damned cockomamie thing including <i>you,</i> you grinning hatchet-faced bastard!’</p>
<p>Carmody collapsed in the chair, breathing like a steam engine and glaring triumphantly at Seethwright. He waited for everything to dissolve, for funny shapes to come and go, and for himself to wake up in his own bed in his own apartment, or maybe in a friend’s bed or even in a hospital bed.</p>
<p>Nothing happened. Carmody’s sense of triumph trickled away. He felt utterly confused, but he was suddenly too tired to care.</p>
<p>‘Are you <i>quite</i> over your outburst?’ Mr Seethwright asked frigidly.</p>
<p>‘Yes, I’m over it,’ Carmody said. ‘I’m sorry.’</p>
<p>‘Don’t fret,’ Seethwright said quietly. ‘You have been under a strain; one appreciates that. But I can do nothing for you unless you keep control of yourself. Intelligence may lead you home; wild emotional outbursts will get you nowhere.’</p>
<p>‘I really am sorry,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘As for this room, which seems to have startled you so, I had it decorated especially for you. The period is only approximate – the best I could do on short notice. It was done to make you feel at home.’</p>
<p>‘That was thoughtful of you,’ Carmody said. ‘I suppose that your appearance –’</p>
<p>‘Yes, precisely,’ Mr Seethwright said, smiling. ‘I had myself decorated as well as the room. It was no trouble, really. It is the sort of little touch which so many of our clients appreciate.’</p>
<p>‘I do appreciate it, as a matter of fact,’ Carmody said. ‘Now that I’m getting used to it, it’s sort of restful.’</p>
<p>‘I hoped you would find it soothing,’ Seethwright said. ‘As for your proposition that all of this is happening to you in a dream – well, it has some merit.’</p>
<p>‘It has?’</p>
<p>Mr Seethwright nodded vigorously. ‘It has definite merit as a proposition, but it has no validity as a statement of your circumstances.’</p>
<p>‘Oh,’ Carmody said, and sat back in the chair.</p>
<p>‘Strictly speaking,’ Seethwright went on, ‘there is no important difference between imaginary and real events. The opposition you create between them is entirely verbal. You are not dreaming any of this, Mr Carmody; but I mention that only as a point of incidental information. Even if you were dreaming it all, you would have to pursue the same course of action.’</p>
<p>‘I don’t understand all that,’ Carmody said. ‘But I’ll take your word that this is real.’ He hesitated, then said, ‘But the thing I <i>really</i> don’t understand – why is all of this <i>like</i> this? I mean, the Galactic Centre looked a little like Radio City, and Borg the dinosaur didn’t talk like any dinosaur, even a <i>talking</i> dinosaur, ought to talk, and –’</p>
<p>‘Please, don’t excite yourself,’ Mr Seethwright said.</p>
<p>‘Sorry,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘You want me to tell you why reality is the way it is,’ Seethwright said. ‘But there is no explanation for that. You must simply learn to fit your preconceptions to what you find. You must not expect reality to adapt itself to you, except very infrequently. It can’t be helped if things are strange; and it also can’t be helped if things are familiar. Am I getting through?’</p>
<p>‘I think so,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Splendid! You’re sure you won’t have some tea?’</p>
<p>‘No, thank you.’</p>
<p>‘Then we must see about getting you home,’ Seethwright said. ‘Nothing like the dear old place to pick up one’s spirits, eh?’</p>
<p>‘Nothing at all!’ Carmody said. ‘Will it be very difficult, Mr Seethwright?’</p>
<p>‘No, I don’t think I would characterize it as <i>difficult</i>,’ Seethwright said. ‘It will be complicated, of course, and rigorous, and even somewhat risky. But I do not consider any of those things to be <i>difficult.</i>’</p>
<p>‘What <i>do</i> you consider truly difficult?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Solving quadratic equations,’ Seethwright answered at once. ‘I simply cannot do them, even though I’ve tried a million or more times. That, sir, is a difficulty! Now let us proceed to your case.’</p>
<p>‘Do you know where Earth is?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘“Where” poses no problem,’ Seethwright said. ‘You have already been to Where, though it didn’t do you much good, since When was so far off the mark. But now I think we can pin down your particular When without undue travail. It’s the Which which gets tricky.’</p>
<p>‘Is that likely to stop us?’</p>
<p>‘Not at all,’ Seethwright said. ‘We must simply sort through and find which Which you belong in. The process is perfectly straightforward; like shooting fish in a barrel, as your people would say.’</p>
<p>‘I’ve never tried that,’ Carmody said. ‘Is it really easy?’</p>
<p>‘That depends upon the size of the fish and the size of the barrel,’ Seethwright told him. ‘It is, for example, nearly effortless to pot a shark in a bathtub; whereas it is a considerable, undertaking to bring down a minnow in a hogshead. Scale is everything. But whichever project is before you, I think you can appreciate its essential straightforwardness and simplicity.’</p>
<p>‘I suppose so,’ Carmody said. ‘But it occurs to me that my search for <i>Which</i> Earth may be straightforward and simple, but may also be impossible to complete due to the interminability of the series of selections.’</p>
<p>‘That’s not quite true but it’s very nicely said,’ Seethwright said, beaming. ‘Complication is often very useful, you know. It helps to specify and identify the problem.’</p>
<p>‘Well … What happens now?’</p>
<p>‘Now we go to work,’ Seethwright said, rubbing his hands together briskly. ‘My staff and I have put together a selection of Which-worlds. We confidently expect that your world will be one of them. But of course, only you can determine the right one.’</p>
<p>‘So I look them over and decide?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Something like that,’ Seethwright said. ‘Actually, you must <i>live</i> them over. Then, as soon as you are sure, signify to us whether we’ve hit your probability-world or a variant. If it’s your world, that’s the end of it. If it’s a variant, then we move you on to the next Which-world.’</p>
<p>‘That sounds reasonable enough,’ Carmody said. ‘Are there a lot of these probability-worlds?’</p>
<p>‘An interminable number, as you suspected earlier. But we have every hope of early success. Unless –’</p>
<p>‘Unless what?’</p>
<p>‘Unless your predator gets to you first.’</p>
<p>‘My predator!’</p>
<p>‘He is still on your trail,’ Mr Seethwright said. ‘And as you know by now, he is reasonably adept at setting snares. These snares take the form of scenes culled from your own memories. “Terraform scenes,” I suppose we could call them, designed to lull and deceive you, to convince you to walk unsuspecting into his mouth.’</p>
<p>‘Will he interfere with your worlds?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Of course he will,’ Seethwright said. ‘There’s no sanctuary in the searching process. On the contrary – the better and more informed the search, the more fraught it is with dangers. You had asked me earlier about dreams and reality. Well, here is your answer. Everything that helps you does so openly. Everything that seeks to harm you does so covertly, by the use of delusions, disguises, and dreams.’</p>
<p>‘Isn’t there anything you can do about the predator?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Nothing. Nor would I if I could. Predation is a necessary circumstance. Even the Gods are eventually eaten by Fate. You will not be an exception to the universal rule.’</p>
<p>‘I thought you’d say something like that,’ Carmody said. ‘But can you give me any help at all? Any hints on how the worlds you send me to will differ from the worlds of the predator?’</p>
<p>‘To me the differences are obvious,’ Seethwright said. ‘But you and I do not share the same perceptions. You could not make use of my insights, Carmody; nor I of yours. Still, you have managed to elude the predator so far.’</p>
<p>‘I’ve been lucky.’</p>
<p>‘There you are! I have a great deal of skill, but no luck whatsoever. Who can tell which quality will be most needed in the trials ahead? Not I, sir, and certainly not you! Therefore be of stout heart, Mr Carmody. Faint heart ne’er won fair planet, eh? Look over the worlds I send you to, be extremely cautious of the predator’s scenes of delusion, get out while the getting’s good; but do not be unmanned by fear into passing up your true and rightful world.’</p>
<p>‘What happens if I do pass it by inadvertently?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Then your search can never end,’ Seethwright told him. ‘Only you can tell us where you belong. If, for one reason or another, you do not locate your world among the most likely, then we must continue our search among the merely likely, and then the less likely, and finally the least likely. The number of probability-worlds of Earth is not infinite, of course; but from your viewpoint, it might as well be; you simply do not have enough inherent duration to search through them all and then begin again.’</p>
<p>‘All right,’ Carmody said doubtfully. ‘I don’t suppose there’s any other way.’</p>
<p>‘There’s no other way I can help you,’ Seethwright said. ‘And I doubt if there is any way at all that would not involve your active participation. But if you wish, I can make inquiries into alternative galactic location techniques. It would take a while –’</p>
<p>‘I don’t think I have a while,’ Carmody said. ‘I think my predator is not far behind me. Mr Seethwright, please send me to the probability-earths, and also accept my gratitude for your patience and interest.’</p>
<p>‘Thank you,’ Seethwright said, obviously pleased. ‘Let us hope that the very first world will be the one you are seeking.’</p>
<p>Seethwright pressed a button on his desk. Nothing happened until Carmody blinked. Then things happened very quickly indeed, for Carmody unblinked and saw that he was smack dab on Earth. Or on a reasonable facsimile thereof.</p>
