<h2>i. 埋没的超人</h2>
<p>我一直在描述这样一种局面：倘若普通男女的智力与操守再坚强一点点，你们的世界本来几乎可以一跃而从混乱走向组织，从初现的瓦解走向一种新的、充满活力的秩序。许多人显然已经意识到，自己不过是某出宏大而悲惨的闹剧中的木偶，却没有一个人能够终止这出戏。现在我要讲述的是另一种不那么壮观的事件模式。它就在你们中间上演，却没有向任何人显露自身的意义。在这个故事里，灾难并非由于天赋的智力或操守不足，而是因为一个野蛮的环境压在了若干对你们的世界而言过于优秀的心灵之上。</p>
<p>当我们的观察者注视着你们这个物种徒劳地应付那些超出自身能力的问题时，他们也在你们星球的许多地区发现了一个新的、也更为真正“人类”的物种的萌芽。这些更为卓越的生灵所遭遇的悲剧命运，是整个人类历史中最非凡的事件之一。此处我只能勾勒这个惊人故事的大概轮廓。</p>
<p>在你们物种的整个历程中，生物力量偶尔会产生一些个体，身上隐约显现出更高类型的可能性。几乎每一次，这种希望都仅仅因为新突变在生理上的不稳定而破灭。新的脑结构要么伴随着真正的身体畸形，要么至多依托于正常的身体构造，而这些构造不足以承受新器官带来的负荷。欧洲与美洲工业革命的大动荡期间，这类生物突变大为增多。于是便第一次出现了一个真正值得重视的可能：也许在某个地方会出现这样一种类型，它既具有更高等的心智，又拥有足以支撑新型脑器官的体格。</p>
<p>工业主义最初阶段过去、生活条件略有改善之后，这些异常个体要维持生存便不再那么困难。与此同时，人道主义的发展也使人们开始照料这些残缺的超常者——尽管人们总是把他们误认为智力低下者。到十九世纪末、二十世纪初，我们在西方世界的城市和工业地区，后来又在工业化的亚洲，零零星星发现了数百个这样的人。他们在生物学构造上各不相同，但都具备某些相似因素；若运气够好，这些因素本应产生超常的智力与超常的操守。然而，他们中许多人遭受严重的身体畸形折磨；另一些人的大体解剖结构虽与同类无异，却同样受到生物化学失调的妨碍；还有一些人在其他方面完全正常，却被过重的大脑拖垮。不过，也有少数人出身于体质格外强健、身材又足够魁梧的血统，因而至少在有利条件下，能够在不过度吃力的情况下支撑并滋养自己那并不特别巨大的头颅。</p>
<p>人们本来很可以预期，这些个体既然潜在地远比正常人中最杰出者聪明，便会迅速上升到权力位置，同时通过繁殖成功确立自己的类型。照理说，不过几代之后，他们就该完全支配那个更早、更低等的物种。然而事实并非如此。即便在那极少数既有心智优势、又有健全身体的人当中，环境也以一种致命而讽刺的方式与他们为敌。先说繁殖这个较简单的问题：这些高级个体中的大多数，即使身体健康、性机能正常，也会强烈地引起正常异性的反感。他们不符合任何公认的性魅力范型。他们既不是“古典型”，也不是“黑人型”，更不是“蒙古型”——而当时的“电影”似乎只承认这几种美。实际上，他们的确显得怪诞。多数人的脑袋看上去相对于身体过大，而且常有粗壮的牛颈。更糟的是，许多人的面部表情带着一种令人不安的笨拙，在正常人眼里时而像疯子，时而像婴儿，时而又像恶魔。因此，他们几乎不可能找到配偶。</p>
<p>我们的观察者更感兴趣的是：这些潜在的高级生物为什么没能在一个由低等者组成的世界中确立自己。尽管他们在生物学上彼此差异很大，事实上分别源自完全独立的突变，他们却有一个共同点，那就是童年和青春期异常漫长。新的、更复杂的脑组织若不与异常缓慢的生长速度相伴，就无法正常发育。因此，那些能够存活下来的个体不仅外表总显得“发育迟缓”，在某些心智方面看起来也同样如此。一个十岁的男孩可能只有六岁儿童的模样；而在精神上，尽管他有些方面已经比父母发展得更远，在他们看来却仍是无可救药地无能。若以同样的尺度衡量，正常的人类儿童和幼年猿类相比，也会显得绝望地落后，尽管实际上他的智力和前途都远胜后者。同样，这些高级儿童几乎无一例外地被父母和监护人视作令人忧心的低能儿。</p>
<p>这种新型个体的“迟缓”并不只源于生理成长的缓慢，也因为他们具有更细腻的感知能力，能从童年经验中发现远多于正常儿童的乐趣和谜题。他们在感觉与运动实验阶段停留如此之久，后来又在游戏阶段停留如此之久，部分原因就在于这些领域在他们看来充满了无穷兴趣。同样，等到他们终于进入青春期，自我意识和对他人的意识刚刚破晓时，那种经验对他们而言又如此辉煌、如此压倒一切，以至于他们必然需要许多年才能适应。</p>
<p>这种表面上的迟缓如此严重，以至于他们常被长辈，乃至同龄人，不只是看作较差，而是看作有严重缺陷；他们甚至常常真的被当成疯子。于是，尽管实际上更为优越，他们成长过程中却怀着一种毁灭性的自卑感，而且随着自己越来越清楚地意识到与他人的差异，这种感觉反而日益加深。缓慢的性发育又加重了这种劣等感；他们到了二十岁，性发育程度大约只相当于正常的十三岁儿童。也许有人会问：如果这些人真的如此聪明，他们怎么会在自我判断上受骗得这么厉害？答案一部分在于他们严重的生理迟熟，另一部分则在于他们出生其中的低等文化拥有压倒性的威望。到了三十岁，他们仍只有正常十八岁青年的成熟程度；而当他们发现自己的思维方式与整个世界的思想存在根本分歧时，便只能断定是世界正确。还必须记住，他们都是孤立的个体，任何一个人都几乎不可能遇到另一个同类。除此之外，由于他们的生命机能维持在一种极其脆弱的平衡上，他们全都寿命短促。几乎没有人活过四十五岁——而这个年龄，本来才刚刚是他们接近心智成熟的门槛。</p>
<p>如我所说，他们童年时期的一个特点，是兴趣看起来落后于同龄人，而这被当成了心智有限的症状。随着年岁增长，这种“局限”似乎反而更加明显。正常青年到了该一心想着如何压倒同辈、在世界上闯出名堂的年龄，这些怪人却似乎完全不会“把自己当回事”。在学校和大学里，凡是只关乎个人晋升的事情，无论如何都无法使他们下定决心全力投入。他们承认，为了不成为共同体的负担，个人有必要“照顾好自己”；但他们无论如何也看不出，在人生这场伟大的游戏、这场个人才干的角斗表演中战胜他人，到底有什么重要意义——而这恰恰一直是第一代人类最主要的关切。因此，他们始终无法召唤出一个人想要飞黄腾达所必需的那种强势、冷酷和一根筋的进取心。看起来，他们的心灵竟有限到了无法形成真正有效的自尊与自我中心情结。</p>
<p>这些人的兴趣在另外一些方面也显得可怜地“狭窄”。即便是少数身体健康、热爱运动的人，也绝不肯把注意力严肃地集中在竞技体育上，更不用说牺牲全部闲暇时间去追逐一个球了。在那些踢足球和看足球的人眼中，这些孤独者简直懒散到了近乎犯罪的程度。他们偶尔也会欣赏赛马的场面，却无论如何提不起下注的兴趣。血腥运动令他们深恶痛绝，但他们并不因此憎恨参与其中的人，因为他们自己内心没有被压抑的嗜血冲动。他们似乎也完全不会欣赏军事荣耀和国家威望。尽管在某些方面极具社会性，他们却无法把忠诚固定在某一个特定群体而排斥其他群体。就连道德敏感性，在正常人看来也仿佛缺失了，或扭曲到了无法辨认的程度。他们不仅会毫无羞愧地违反安息日戒律，即便身处人人都视守安息日为天经地义的地区；在性的问题上，他们更是严重冒犯公众，因为他们似乎完全感觉不到性有什么“不洁”。无论言语还是行为，他们都恬不知耻。若不是缺乏性吸引力，他们的放荡倾向几乎没有什么能加以限制。劝说对他们完全无效，只会引出一大串论证；这些论证当然荒谬，却又出奇地难以驳倒。只有强制才能约束他们；然而不幸的是，尽管这些人如此缺乏自尊心与个人竞争欲，任何阻止他们按照自己认为正确的方式行事的企图，却都可能激起一种狂热抵抗。为此，他们甘愿承受最极端的痛苦而不退缩。这种近乎恶魔般的英雄气概，几乎是他们唯一能从同类那里赢得一点尊重的品质；但由于这种勇气通常服务于邻人无法理解的目的，尊重往往又被嘲笑或愤怒压倒。只有当这种英雄主义用来援救受苦的同类时，正常人才能充分理解。有时，他们会以惊人的勇敢把人从火灾、溺水或虐待中救出。但另一次，同一个曾经冒着几乎必死的危险救下市民或儿童的人，却可能在某位受欢迎或身居要职的人遇险时拒绝承担哪怕一点风险。人们向昔日的英雄百般呼吁，他却只会嗤之以鼻，说自己比对方更有价值，不能为了一个低等者拿自己的性命冒险。</p>
<p>这些被上帝遗弃般的生物尽管能够真诚献身，却被认为缺陷严重到了无法理解宗教的程度。他们坐在教堂里时，表现出的领悟力仿佛与猫狗无异。父母和监护人试图让他们体会那些“根本的宗教真理”，结果只会使他们困惑或厌烦。然而，驻留在这些极度困惑却异常敏感的年轻心灵中的海王星观察者发现，他们往往会被两种最根本的宗教经验猛烈地震动并提升：一种是对一切生命的战斗性的爱，另一种是平静而炽烈的顺从。尽管自己的肉体备受折磨，他们仍比正常人种更能品味人类存在这一景观。</p>
<p>他们中的大多数，无论男女，都表现出极强的好奇心；其中一两个人甚至成功地把这种好奇心导入科学训练。不过总的说来，他们在科学上毫无进展，因为一开始学习时，他们就无法看出科学那些基本假设和方法为何有效。确有一两人有毅力把训练坚持到底，并以惊人的轻松掌握了海量事实、论证和理论；但他们却不会使用这些知识。他们似乎把全部科学工作看成一种与现实关系不大的游戏，一种颇为幼稚的技巧游戏，而其中那些任意制定的规则又没能严格一致地贯彻游戏真正的精神。并且，他们越深入任何一门科学的知识体系，就越以轻浮的眼光看待它，仿佛完全不会欣赏专业技术和理论中的精微之处。事实上，不只在科学里，而是在一切智力领域，他们都不断受到一种过分清醒的洞察力的妨碍——而在正常心灵看来，这种清醒反倒像愚钝。比如哲学常常吸引他们天然的好奇心，可各学派共同认可、或共同不自觉预设的原则，他们连最基本的头绪也理不出来。老师们无一例外地只能断定他们完全没有形而上学感觉；只有一点例外：人们经常注意到，他们对于那些老师自己也反对的理论，倒有惊人的识错能力。真相是，在每一座思想大厦的地基上，他们都会发现一些无法接受、或根本找不到清楚含义的假设；而在大厦内部，他们又到处遇见自己看不出逻辑联系的论证。</p>
<p>有一个领域，确实有好几个这样的人认真尝试过发挥影响，那就是政治和社会改良。但他们在这里比在其他地方还要无能。他们永远看不出公认政治目标的重要性，也无法理解那些众所承认的党派格言有什么效力。当政治家论证某项政策必然会带来某种结果时，这些不幸的异类只能懊恼自己太愚笨，竟跟不上论证。当他们自己坦白会怎样处理某个问题时，又要么因“缺乏真正的感情”而遭到咒骂，要么因幼稚的理想主义而被嘲笑。不论哪种情况，人们都会指责他们不了解人性；而这种指责在某种意义上确实公允，因为他们自己往往根本意识不到，自己与旧物种成员之间究竟隔着多么巨大的鸿沟。</p>
<p>我刚才说的是少数受过足够教育、因而还能思考理论问题或公共事务的人。但绝大多数甚至到不了这一步。出生在优裕环境中的那些人，通常很快就会同自己社会的禁忌发生冲突，继而被排斥，于是沉入社会底层。出生在贫困家庭的人也经常被同伴放逐；而他们又完全不适合在工业主义这场巨大的群犬争斗中为自己杀出一条路，因此几乎总会滑落到最卑微的职业。他们成了码头工、女杂工、农业工人、最低级的小职员、极小的店主，尤其常见的是流浪汉。值得注意的是，总的说来，他们在社会下层反而比在上层得到更多同情和理解。原因在于，生活越简单、越少受精致文化装饰，他们天生的智力与操守就越不容易同那些更“有教养”的人所引以为傲、却仍很粗陋的文化发生明显冲突。事实上，那些落入所谓“下层阶级”的人，往往从同伴那里得到一种奇怪的、轻蔑与敬意混合的态度。人们一方面把他们视作没用的怪人、畸人，像某种发育突然停住的儿童，叫他们“傻子”“怪胎”“神经病”；另一方面又相信他们拥有一种不合实际、却好得不适合这个世界的古怪智慧。不仅如此，就连实际事务，人们也常来向他们讨主意；因为他们似乎把一种近乎神圣的天真和忘我，同一种精明乃至狡黠结合在一起。这种精明从不用来谋求个人上进，却在正常人看来近乎恶魔。</p>
<p>这些未能完成自身发展的超人，最常滑向的一种生活，是流浪与沉思。他们常常成为游民，从一座大城漂到另一座大城，徒步穿过农业地区，补锅修器、磨剪刀、修瓷器、偷猎、偷窃、砸石头、收庄稼。少数人成了邮差，另一些人成为货车或公共汽车司机；但不论做什么工作，他们往往效率惊人，却很少能长久待在必须服从正常人种成员的职位上。女性比男性更不幸，因为女人要过流浪生活困难得多。有些人成了女裁缝。少数由于缺乏性吸引力而免受侵扰，能够直面道路上的艰辛和危险。另一些外貌多少正常的女性，则把卖淫看成最不令人厌恶的谋生办法。</p>
<p>每当我们的观察者研究这些未能实现的新物种近似形态时，他们无一例外地发现：其心灵模式明确高于正常人，却又远没有达到第二代人类的水平——后者要到你们时代约一千万年之后才会开始自己的历史。以头颅并不比你们大多少的生物而言，你们同时代的这些高级者确实拥有惊人的智力。他们的心灵在组织性和统一性方面也同样非凡。正常人格常被欲望与忠诚的冲突撕裂，分化成两个或更多彼此联系不完善的不完整系统；而这些人却能保持整体性，并选择在当时条件下事实上能够实现最大善的行动。他们几乎完全没有自私，因为他们自我洞察的能力明确比正常人种深入。和第二代人类一样，他们也天生对高级心智活动怀有兴趣，对智力运用和审美喜悦的渴望，并不比对食物和饮水的基本需要稍弱。可惜的是，由于他们都是孤立个体，又在童年时期被一个水准低下的世界压倒，这些渴望始终得不到健康的满足。他们仿佛天生可能成为运动员，却从出生起就被训练着以怪诞而拘束的方式只使用某几组肌肉，其余身体则完全荒废。因此，他们从未真正发展自己的能力，整个心智生活始终被一种虚假和徒劳感纠缠。并且，我们还发现，在他们身上存在一种强烈而清醒的天生忠诚：忠诚于人之精神、乃至宇宙之精神逐渐觉醒这一最高冒险。他们很容易把自己和他人看成某种伟大共同事业的容器与工具。然而不幸的是，由于他们同邻人之间存在可怕的精神孤绝，他们永远无法全心全意地献身共同生活。尽管遭受轻蔑，尽管自己也心绪混乱，那些活过二十五岁或三十岁的人终究不得不观察到：同胞的心灵与自己采用的是另一种织法——更粗陋、更混乱，结构更简单，却内部更加矛盾。不久他们还会有理由怀疑，真正把精神事业放在心上的似乎只有自己，而整个世界都在轻浮度日。他们每个人都像一个被猿群养在丛林中的孤独人类儿童。等他终于开始认识到自己与他人的鸿沟时，也同时认识到，猿类的抚养已经给自己造成了无法修复的损害。在这种处境中，他通常只好满足于过一种对个人友善而幽默的生活；而这种生活始终扎根于他自身那不可动摇的观照狂喜之中。</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" class="stapledon-body" lang="en" hidden>
<h2>i. SUBMERGED SUPERMEN</h2>
<p>IHAVE BEEN describing a situation in which, had the intelligence and the integrity of average men and women been slightly more robust, your world might have passed almost at a leap from chaos to organization, from incipient dissolution to a new order of vitality. Many minds were clearly aware that they were puppets in a huge and tragic farce, though none were able to put an end to it. I have now to tell of a different and much less imposing pattern of events, which was enacted in your midst without revealing its significance to any man. In this case disaster was brought about, not by any lack of native intelligence or of native integrity, but by the action of a savage environment on minds too gifted for your world.</p>
<p>While our observers were watching the forlorn efforts of your species to cope with problems beyond its powers, they detected also in many regions of your planet the promise of a new and more human species. The tragic fate of these more brilliant beings is amongst the most remarkable incidents in the whole history of Man. Here it is impossible to do more than outline this amazing story.</p>
<p>Throughout the career of your species, biological forces have now and then thrown up individuals in whom there was some promise of a higher type. Nearly always this promise was frustrated simply by the physiological instability of the new mutation. The new brain forms were associated either with actual distortions of body, or at best with normal structures which were inadequate to bear the novel strain. During the great ferment of industrial revolution in Europe and America, these biological mutations became much more numerous. Thus was afforded a serious possibility of the emergence somewhere or other of a type which should combine superior mentality with a physique capable of supporting the new cerebral organs.</p>
<p>When the earliest phase of industrialism was passed and conditions had improved somewhat, it became less difficult for abnormal beings to preserve themselves. The growth of humanitarianism, moreover, tended to foster these crippled supernormals along with the subnormals for whom they were invariably mistaken. Toward the end of the Nineteenth and the beginning of the Twentieth Centuries, we found here and there in the cities and industrial areas of the Western World, and later in industrial Asia, many hundreds of these individuals. They were of diverse biological constitution, but similar in respect of the factors which, with good fortune, should produce supernormal intelligence and supernormal integrity. Many of them, however, were tormented by gross bodily malformation; some, though more or less like their fellows in general anatomy, were no less hampered by biochemical maladjustments; some, though otherwise normal, were crippled by excessive weight of brain. But a few, sprung from stock of exceptionally tough fibre and ample proportions, were able to support and nourish their not excessively large heads without undue strain, at least in favourable conditions.</p>
<p>Now it might have been expected that these individuals, potentially far more brilliant than the most gifted of the normal kind, would have risen very rapidly to positions of power; and that, at the same time, they would have successfully established their type by propagation. Within a few generations, surely, they would have completely dominated the earlier and inferior species. But this was not to be. Even among the very small minority who combined mental superiority with bodily health, circumstance was fatally and ironically hostile. With regard to propagation, to take the simpler matter first, most of these superior beings, even those who were healthy and sexually potent, were strongly repellent to normal members of the opposite sex. They did not conform to any recognized pattern of sexual beauty. They were neither of the classical nor of the negroid nor of the mongolian styles, which alone were favoured by the ‘cinema’. Indeed they were definitely grotesque. Their heads, in most cases, looked too big for their bodies, and they were apt to have bull necks. In many of them, moreover, there was a disturbing uncouthness of facial expression, which to the normal eye seemed sometimes insane, sometimes infantile, sometimes diabolic. Consequently it was almost impossible for them to find mates.</p>
<p>More interesting to our observers was the failure of these potentially superior beings to assert themselves in a world of inferiors. Though they were biologically very diverse, springing in fact from entirely independent mutations, these beings were alike in one respect, namely their very prolonged childhood and adolescence. The new and more complex brain organization could not develop successfully unless it was associated with an abnormally slow rate of growth. Therefore such of these beings as were able to survive were always ‘backward’ not only in appearance, but in some respects, mentally also. Thus a boy of ten years would look like a child of six; and mentally, though in some respects already more developed than his parents, he would seem to them to be hopelessly incompetent. Judged by similar standards, the normal human child would appear hopelessly backward in comparison with the baby ape, although in reality he would be far more intelligent and promising. Similarly, these superior children were invariably considered by their parents and guardians as distressingly inferior.</p>
<p>This backwardness of the new individual was due, not solely to the slowness of physiological growth, but also to the fact that with more delicate percipience he found in the experiences of childhood far more to delight and intrigue him than the normal child could gather. These beings remained so long in the phase of perceptual and motor experimentation, and later so long in the phase of play, partly because for them these fields were so crowded with interest. Similarly, when at length they attained adolescence, the dawn of self-consciousness and other-consciousness was for them so brilliant and overwhelming that they had inevitably to take many years to adjust themselves to it.</p>
<p>So great was this seeming backwardness that they were often regarded by their elders, and by their own generation also, not merely as inferior, but as seriously deficient. Often they were actually treated as insane. Thus in spite of their superiority they grew up with a devastating sense of their own incapacity, which increased as they became increasingly aware of the difference between themselves and others. This illusion of inferiority was aggravated by their slow sexual development; for at twenty they were sexually equivalent to a normal child of thirteen. But, it may be asked, if these beings were really so brilliant, how could they be so deceived about themselves? The answer is given partly by their serious physiological backwardness, but partly also by the overwhelming prestige of the inferior culture into which they were born. At thirty they had still the immaturity of the normal youth of eighteen; and finding a fundamental discrepancy between their own way of thinking and the thought of the whole world, they were forced to conclude that the world was right. It must be remembered, moreover, that they were isolated individuals, that no one of them would be likely ever to come across another of his kind. Further, owing to the precarious balance of their vital economy, they were all short-lived. Hardly any survived beyond forty-five, the age at which they should have been upon the brink of mental maturity.</p>
<p>One characteristic of their childhood was, as I said, a seeming backwardness of interest, which was taken to be a symptom of mental limitation. Now with the passage of years this limitation seemed to become more pronounced. At an age when the normal young man would be dominated by the will to make his mark in the world by eclipsing his fellows, these strange beings seemed quite incapable of ‘taking themselves seriously’. At school and college they could never be induced to apply themselves resolutely to matters which concerned only private advancement. While admitting that for the community’s sake it was necessary that individuals should’ look after themselves’, so as not to be a burden, they could never be made to see the importance of personal triumph over others in the great game of life, the great gladiatorial display of personal prowess, which was ever the main preoccupation of the First Men. Consequently they could never conjure up the necessary forcefulness, the relentlessness, the singleminded pushfulness which alone can advance a man. Their minds, it seemed, were so limited that they could not form a really effective sentiment of self-regard.</p>
<p>There were other respects in which the interest of these beings seemed to be pitiably limited. Even those few who enjoyed physical health and delighted in bodily activity, could never be persuaded to concentrate their attention earnestly upon athletics, still less to give up all their leisure to the pursuit of the ball. To the football-playing and football-watching population these lonely beings seemed almost criminally lax. Further, while enjoying occasionally the spectacle of a horse race, they could raise no interest whatever in betting. Blood sports they heartily loathed, yet without hate of those who practised them; for they had no blood lust secretly at work within themselves. They seemed also to be incapable of appreciating military glory and national prestige. Though in some ways extremely social, they could not pin their loyalty to one particular group rather than another. Moral sensitivity, too, seemed either absent or so distorted as to be unrecognizable. Not only did they break the sabbath without shame, even in districts where sabbatarianism was unquestioned, but also in respect of sex they were a source of grave offence; for they seemed wholly incapable of feeling that sex was unclean. Both in word and act they were shameless. Nothing but their sexual unattractiveness restricted their licentious tendencies. Persuasion was entirely ineffective, calling from them only a spate of arguments which, though of course fantastic, were very difficult to refute. Compulsion alone could restrain them; but unfortunately, though these beings were so lacking in self-pride and personal rivalry, any attempt to prevent them from behaving as seemed good to them was apt to call forth a fanatical resistance, in pursuance of which they would readily suffer even the extremes of agony without flinching. This capacity for diabolic heroism was almost the only character that earned them any respect from their fellows; but since it was generally used in service of ends which their neighbours could not appreciate, respect was outweighed by ridicule or indignation. Only when this heroism was exercised to succour some suffering fellow-mortal could the normal mind fully appreciate it. Sometimes they would perform acts of superb gallantry to rescue people from fire, from drowning, from maltreatment. But on another occasion the very individual who had formerly gone to almost certain death to rescue a fellow-citizen or a child, might refuse even a slight risk when some popular or important personage was in danger. The former hero would scoff at all appeals, merely remarking that he was worth more than the other, and must not risk his life for an inferior being.</p>
<p>Though capable of earnest devotion, these god-forsaken creatures were thought to be too deficient to appreciate religion. In church they showed no more percipience than a cat or dog. Attempts by parents and guardians to make them feel the fundamental religious truths merely puzzled or bored them. Yet Neptunian observers, stationed in these sorely perplexed but sensitive young minds, found them often violently disturbed and exalted by the two fundamental religious experiences, namely by militant love of all living things and by the calm fervour of resignation. Far more than the normal species, and in spite of their own racked bodies, they were able to relish the spectacle of human existence.</p>
<p>Most of them, both male and female, showed a very lively curiosity, which in one or two cases was successfully directed towards a scientific training. Generally, however, they made no headway at all in science, because at the outset of their studies they could not see the validity of the assumptions and method of the sciences. One or two of them did, indeed, have the enterprise to go through with their training, acquiring with remarkable ease an immense mass of facts, arguments and theories. But they proved incapable of making use of their knowledge. They seemed to regard all their scientific work as a game that had little to do with reality, a rather childish game of skill in which the arbitrary rules failed to develop the true spirit of the game with rigorous consistency. Moreover, the further they explored the corpus of any science, the more frivolously they regarded it, being apparently unable to appreciate the subtleties of specialized technique and theory. Not only in science, but in all intellectual spheres, they were, in fact, constantly hampered by a lucidity of insight which seemed to the normal mind merely obtuseness. In philosophy, for instance, whither their natural curiosity often led them, they could not make head or tail even of principles agreed upon or unconsciously assumed by all schools alike. Their teachers were invariably forced to condemn them as entirely without metaphysical sense; save in one respect, for it was often noticed that they were capable of remarkable insight into the errors of theories with which their teachers themselves disagreed. The truth was that at the base of every intellectual edifice these beings found assumptions which they could not accept, or in which they could find no clear meaning; while throughout the structure they encountered arguments in which they could not see a logical connexion.</p>
<p>There was one sphere in which several of these beings did earnestly try to make themselves felt, namely in politics and social improvement. But here they were even more ineffective than elsewhere. They could never see the importance of the accepted political aims or the validity of the recognized party maxims. When politicians demonstrated the inevitable results of this policy or that, these unhappy misfits could only deplore their own stupidity in not being able to follow the argument. When they themselves confessed how they would deal with some problem or other, they were either cursed for their lack of true feeling or derided for their childish idealism. In either case they were charged with being ignorant of human nature; and this charge was, in a manner, just, for they had seldom any conception of the gulf between themselves and the members of the older species.</p>
<p>I have been speaking of those few who were educated so far as to be able to take thought for matters of theoretical interest or public concern. But the great majority never attained this level. Those who were born into comfortable circumstances generally came into conflict with the taboos of their society, and were ostracized; so that they sank into the underworld. Those also who were born into poor families were frequently outlawed by their fellows; and being entirely unfit to make their way in the huge dog-fight of industrialism, they nearly always subsided into the humblest occupations. They became dock-labourers, charwomen, agricultural labourers, clerks of the lowest order, very small shop-keepers, and especially vagrants. It was remarkable that they received on the whole more sympathy and understanding in the lower than in the higher reaches of society. This was because in the simpler, less sophisticated, manner of life their native intelligence and integrity did not come into obvious conflict with the proud but crude culture of more ‘educated’ folk. Indeed, those whose lot fell among the ‘lower’ classes of society were generally treated by their peers with a strange blend of contempt and respect. Though they were regarded as ineffective cranks or freaks, as children whose development had somehow been arrested, as ‘daft’, ‘fey’, ‘mental’, they were also credited with an odd kind of impracticable wisdom that was too good for this world. Not only so, but even in practical matters their advice was often sought; for they seemed to combine a divine innocence and self-disregard with a shrewdness, a cunning, which, though it was never used for self-advancement, struck the normal mind as diabolic.</p>
<p>The kind of life into which these abortive supermen gravitated most frequently was a life of wandering and contemplation. Often they became tramps, drifting from one big town to another, trekking through agricultural districts, tinkering, sharpening scissors, mending crockery, poaching, stealing, breaking stones, harvesting. A few became postmen, others drivers of motor vans or buses; but though, whatever their occupations, they worked with incredible efficiency, seldom could they remain for long in posts which entailed subordination to members of the normal species. The women were more unfortunate than the men, for vagrancy was less easily practised by women. Some became seamstresses. A few, protected by sexual unattractiveness, faced the difficulties and dangers of the road. Others, blessed with more or less normal looks, chose prostitution as the least repugnant way of earning a livelihood.</p>
<p>Our observers, whenever they studied these unfulfilled approximations to a new species, found invariably a mental pattern definitely far superior to the normal, but also one which was much less developed than that of the Second Men, whose career was destined to begin some ten million years after your day. These superior contemporaries of yours had indeed, for beings whose heads were not much larger than your own, remarkable intelligence. They were remarkable also in respect of the organization and unity of their minds. Where the normal personality, torn by conflicts of desire and loyalty, would split into two or more incomplete systems imperfectly related to one another, these preserved their integrity, and chose that action which did in fact offer the greatest good possible in the circumstances. They were almost entirely without selfishness, for they had a capacity for self-insight definitely more penetrating than that of the normal species. Like the Second Men, they had also an innate interest in the higher mental activities, a craving for intellectual exercise and aesthetic delight no less imperious than the simple needs for food and drink. But unfortunately, since they were isolated individuals overwhelmed in childhood by a world of inferior calibre, they were unable to satisfy these cravings wholesomely. They were like potential athletes trained from birth to use certain muscles in grotesque and cramping actions, and the rest of their bodies not at all. Thus they never developed their powers, and were haunted by a sense of falsity and futility in all their mental life. They were beings, moreover, in whom we found a vigorous and lucid innate loyalty toward the supreme adventure of the awakening spirit in man, and in the cosmos. They were very ready to regard themselves and others as vessels, instruments of a great corporate endeavour. But, unfortunately, in their terrible spiritual isolation from their neighbours, they could never whole-heartedly give themselves to the communal life. In spite of the contempt with which they were treated, in spite of their own confusion of mind, those of them who survived beyond the age of twenty-five or thirty could not but observe that the minds of their fellow-men were woven upon a different pattern from their own, a pattern at once cruder and more confused, less intricate yet more discrepant. Moreover, they soon found reason to suspect that they themselves alone had the cause of the spirit at heart, and that the rest of the world was frivolous. Each of them was like an isolated human child brought up by apes in the jungle. When at last he had begun to realize the gulf between himself and others, he realized also that he had been damaged past repair by his simian upbringing. In these circumstances he would, as a rule, content himself with a life of personal kindliness and humour, which was ever rooted in the strength of his own unshakable ecstasy of contemplation.</p>
