<h3>第十五章</h3>
<h3>奇异的三角关系</h3>
<p>我已经讲过，我是怎样在坦-伊-沃尔找到普拉克茜和天狼星的；也已经讲过，当时我十分清楚，若企图劝她离开天狼星，只会把她从我身边推得更远。直到第一次见面几天之后，在同普拉克茜谈过许多次以后，我才真正意识到，她与这条狗之间的关系已经亲密到了何种程度。这个发现对我是一次冲击，但我竭力不让自己的反感流露出来；因为普拉克茜发现我愿意理解她，很快便像决堤一样倾诉起来，把自己与天狼星在感情上的整个故事都坦白给我听。她反复谈过这个主题许多次以后，我发现自己渐渐把那种“受辱恋人”惯常会有的情绪搁到了一边。我不能不承认，把这两个如此不同的生命结合在一起的激情，既深刻又慷慨。但也正因为如此，我反而更加害怕，自己也许再也无法把这个奇异的爱人赢回来。而我又深信，为了她自己，同样也为了我，她最终必须重新回到人类的生活方式之中。</p>
<p>假期剩下的几天里，我有许多时间待在坦-伊-沃尔，有时只同普拉克茜在一起，有时则和他们两个一起。天狼星白天一直很忙，普拉克茜却允许自己放下不少农活来陪我。我们常一起在菜园里干活。在屋里，我也帮她打扫、做饭之类。我还替她做了好几样省力的小装置。我一向手还算巧，很享受给她装架子、装窗帘杆，以及改进洗碗处的布置。天狼星的睡篮也需要修理，不过我觉得最好暂时别碰它，等以后我同他真正建立友好关系再说。做这些零碎手工活时，我们会一直聊天，有时很认真，有时又恢复到从前那种熟悉的互相打趣。我甚至偶尔会拿她的“犬类丈夫”开玩笑；但有一次——当时她正在洗碗，我在擦干——她忽然哭了起来。此后我就谨慎多了。</p>
<p>我当然早已打定主意，要把普拉克茜一点点从目前这种生活中引出来，但并不是要硬把她从天狼星身边撕走。我从未建议她跟我离开。事实上，我计划的一部分，恰恰是要说服她和天狼星相信：我完全接受他们继续保持亲密关系，也接受他们目前这种生活方式。我给小屋做的各种改进，正是为了加强这种印象。它们还无意间起到了另一个作用：让我能够在天狼星面前占一种相当卑劣的优势，因为这种事情他自己无法帮忙。我看得出，我的手巧使他心里难受；意识到这一点后，我也为自己伤害他而羞愧。可只要有机会，我还是忍不住想胜过他，想让我的爱人开心。毕竟，我对自己说，爱情和战争里一切手段都算公平。但我确实感到羞耻；尤其因为天狼星反倒以一种近乎非人的宽宏，鼓励我只要能帮普拉克茜就尽管去帮。也许从长远看，我这样放纵自己反而是好事，因为他的宽宏大量迫使我很快认识到，他究竟有多么高贵的精神，也迫使我真正怀着温暖的敬意对待他——不只是因为普拉克茜爱他，而是因为他本人值得。</p>
<p>起初，我同天狼星之间极其尴尬。有一阵我甚至担心，我们两人同时待在一间屋子里，最终会让双方都无法忍受。他并没有试图赶我走，而是以友好而有礼的态度待我；但我看得出，他非常不愿意把普拉克茜单独留给我。显然，他害怕她随时可能从自己的生活里消失。我们之间还有一个造成紧张的原因：刚开始时，我极难听懂他说话。虽然后来我学会了比较轻松地理解他那粗怪的英语，可第一次去威尔士时，即使他一个词一个词慢慢说，还反复说许多遍，我也常常完全摸不着头脑。在这种情况下，我们几乎不可能真正把彼此的立场说清。不过，在分别之前，我至少成功消除了最初那层寒意；我让他看出，我无意扮演一个嫉妒的情敌，也不会因为普拉克茜同他的关系而谴责她。我甚至向他保证，我不想插进他们之间。对此，他说了一小段话，我费了很大力气才听懂，大意是：“可你确实想插进我们之间。我不怪你。你想让她永远和你一起生活。而且显然，她迟早必须同你，或者同某个男人一起生活。我给不了她所需要的一切。她现在这种生活，对她来说只能是暂时的。只要她想走，她就必须走。”这番话里有一种尊严，也有一种清醒，使我因为自己没有同样坦率而感到受了责备。</p>
<p>我费了些心思周旋，终于把假期延长，于是得以同普拉克茜和天狼星相处大约十天；不过我每天晚上都很自觉地回自己的旅馆睡。天狼星曾建议我干脆住在小屋里，我却指出，那样只会平添一层丑闻。对我来说，这种情形有一种奇怪而折磨人的意味：我曾经是、某种意义上也仍是普拉克茜公认的恋人，却只能在花园门口吻她道晚安，而天狼星则很体贴地留在屋里。有时，一想到自己正把她留给那个她以奇异方式爱着的非人生命，我便会突然产生一种几乎近于恐怖的反感；我总是极力把它掩饰起来。有一次，我大概还是不知怎么把自己的痛苦传给了她，她忽然热烈地抱住我。一阵喜悦与饥渴席卷过来，我失去分寸，脱口说道：“亲爱的，跟我走吧。这种生活对你完全不对。”她却从我怀里挣开：“不，亲爱的罗伯特，你不明白。从人的意义上说，我确实非常爱你；可是——我该怎么说呢？——从超越人的意义上，在精神上，因此也在肉身上，我爱的是另一个亲爱的，那个奇异的爱人。而对他来说，永远不可能有别人，只有我。”我抗议道：“可他给不了你真正需要的东西。他自己也这么说。”她回答：“当然，他给不了我作为一个姑娘最需要的东西。可我不只是一个姑娘。我和别的姑娘都不一样。我是普拉克茜。而普拉克茜只是‘天狼星—普拉克茜’的一半，需要另一半；另一半也需要我。”她停了一下。我还没想好怎么回答，她便说：“我得回去了。他也许会以为我再也不回去。”她匆匆吻了我一下，赶紧朝小屋门口走去。</p>
<p>第二天是星期日。威尔士人守星期日守得可怕地严格。除了给牲畜喂食，农场里什么活都不能干，于是天狼星有一天空闲。我吃过早饭后去了坦-伊-沃尔，发现普拉克茜独自在菜园里干活，看上去多少有些不自在。她说天狼星今天出门去了，要到日落以后才回来。我很惊讶，追问之下她解释道：“他说他的野性情绪又上来了。那东西会突然抓住他，过后又自己离开。他翻过里诺格山，走罗马台阶，去迪弗林附近一个农场，找那条会让他发疯的格温——一条很漂亮的超级牧羊母犬。照时间算，她现在正好该适合他。”我露出了厌恶和同情的神色；她立刻说：“我不介意。以前，在我还不明白的时候，我确实介意过。可现在我觉得这很自然，也很正常。再说——”我催她把话说完，她却只是沉默着继续挖土。我强行把她的活停下来。她看着我的眼睛，笑着，什么也没说。我吻了吻她被阳光晒暖的脸颊。</p>
<p>那一天，小屋里发生了属于人类恋人的亲昵，也有许许多多交谈。可是，尽管我的爱人热烈回应我的爱抚，我仍知道，她始终把自己最深处的那部分保留着。有时，我会恐惧地想象，一头野兽曾怎样笨拙地碰触、蹂躏眼前这具此刻正被我如此自然拥抱的甜美人体。可另一些时候，我又觉得，怀里这个轻捷的生命虽然有着人类、甚至近乎神圣的人形，内里却根本不是人，而是一头精致的小鹿般的兽，或者狐狸，或者一只纤巧的猫，只是暂时变成了女人的模样。甚至连她的人形本身都不完全像典型的人类：那么瘦削、柔韧，肌肉细致而有弹性，她确实更像一只小鹿，而不是姑娘。有一次她说：“啊，哪怕只是一小会儿，重新做人也真美、真美！亲爱的，我们的身体多么合适。”可当我说道：“普拉克茜，亲爱的，这才是你本来应该拥有的生活。”她却回答：“这是我的身体本来应该拥有的，可在精神上，我永远不可能完全属于你。”那一刻，我真恨那头畜生天狼星！她感觉到了我的恨，忽然哭起来，像一只被捕获的动物那样在我怀里挣扎，直到挣脱出去。不过争吵很快便和解了。剩下那一天，我们像恋人一样度过：在山坡上闲逛，在花园里坐着，一起准备、吃掉一顿又一顿饭。</p>
<p>太阳已经低低挂在西边时，我准备离开她，可她说：“等等天狼星吧。我真的很希望你们能成为朋友。”直到很晚，我们坐在小厨房里说话时，才听见花园门响。过了一会儿，天狼星推开门，站在灯光里眨着眼睛，鼻孔翕动，闻着我们留下的气味。她向他张开双臂；等他走到身边，她把那颗巨大的头拉到自己脸颊旁。“你们两个做朋友吧。”她说着，握住了我的手。天狼星定定看了我一会儿。我笑了。他缓缓摆了摆尾巴。</p>
<p>假期最后几天，我见天狼星的时间比之前多得多。我们不再彼此回避，而这时我也已经比较能听懂他说话。一天早晨，普拉克茜在乳品间帮普格太太做事，我便同天狼星和他的学生们一起去了高处牧场。看着他用一连串吠声和带旋律的呼喊控制这些聪明、却仍低于人类心智的动物，实在奇妙；这些声音对我来说完全无法理解。更奇妙的是，看着那些狗按他的命令扑住某一只羊，把它按倒在地，供主人检查蹄子或嘴巴；有时他还会从鞍囊里取药给羊处理——顺便说，那些鞍囊是由其中一条学生犬背着的。工作间隙，我们谈普拉克茜和她的将来，也谈战争，谈人类这个物种的前景。谈话仍很困难，因为他常常得重复自己的话；但我们之间确实渐渐建立起真正的友谊。回家路上，他说：“趁普拉克茜还住在这里，你要常来看我们。这对普拉克茜有好处。对我来说，得到你的友谊也很好。也许有一天，会轮到我去看望你们两个——如果你们愿意接待我。”我忽然对他感到一阵暖意，于是说：“如果将来我和她真的有一个家，那当然也必须是你的家。”</p>

<h3><a id="a582"></a>CHAPTER XV</h3>
<p><span class="t28">I</span><span class="t30"> HAVE </span><span class="t31">ALREADY</span><span class="t30"> </span>told how I found Plaxy with Sirius at Tan-y-Voel, and how clear it was to me that any attempt to persuade her to leave him would have alienated her from me. It was not till some days after our first meeting, and after many talks with Plaxy, that I realized how intimate her relations with the dog had become. The discovery was a shock to me, but I took pains not to betray my revulsion; for Plaxy, finding me sympathetic, soon poured out in a flood of confession the whole story of her emotional relations with Sirius. When she had many times dwelt upon this theme I found myself putting aside the conventional feelings of the outraged lover. I could not but realize that the passion, which united these two dissimilar creatures was deep and generous. But this made me all the more fearful lest I should never win my strange darling back again. And I was deeply convinced that for her own sake, no less than for mine, she must in the end be won back to human ways.</p>
<p>During the few remaining days of my leave I spent much time at Tan-y-Voel, sometimes with Plaxy alone, sometimes with the two of them. Sirius was busy all day, but Plaxy allowed herself a good deal of time off duty to be with me. We used to work together in the garden. Indoors I helped her to clean and cook, and so on. I also constructed a number of labour-saving gadgets for her. I have always been fairly apt with my hands, and I thoroughly enjoyed putting up shelves and curtain rods, and improving the arrangements for washing up. Sirius’s sleeping-basket needed repairing, but it seemed better not to put this right until much later, when I had established friendly relations with him. While I was occupied with all these little manual tasks we would talk, sometimes seriously, sometimes with the old familiar banter. Sometimes I even ventured to tease her about her “canine husband”; but on one of these occasions (she was washing up and I was drying) she broke into tears. After that I was more tactful.</p>
<p>It was my fixed intention, of course, to wean Plaxy from her present life, though not to tear her from Sirius. I made no suggestion that she should come away with me. Indeed it was part of my plan to persuade her and Sirius that I fully accepted the continuance of their intimacy and of their present regime. My improvements in the house were calculated to strengthen this impression. They also accidentally served another purpose. They allowed me to take a rather mean advantage over Sirius, who could not himself help in this way. I could see that my handiness galled him; and, seeing it, I was ashamed of hurting him. Yet whenever an opportunity offered I failed to resist the temptation of triumphing over him and delighting my darling. After all, I told myself, all is fair in love and war. But I was ashamed; the more so because Sirius with inhuman generosity encouraged me to help Plaxy whenever possible. Perhaps it was in the long run a good thing that I indulged myself in this manner, for Sirius’s magnanimity forced me to realize quickly how fine a spirit he was, and compelled me to treat him with warm respect, not merely for being loved by Plaxy, but for his own sake.</p>
<p>Relations with Sirius were at first very awkward, and I feared at one time that our presence in the same house would prove intolerable to both of us. He made no attempt to get rid of me. He treated me with friendly politeness. But I could see that he hated to leave Plaxy with me. Obviously he feared that she might at any moment vanish out of his life. One source of strain between us was that I had at first very great difficulty in understanding his speech. Though in the end I learned to follow his uncouth English fairly easily, during that first visit to Wales I was often entirely at a loss, even when he spoke word by word and with many repetitions. In these circumstances it was almost impossible for us to come to terms at all. However, before we parted I succeeded at least in dispelling the initial chill by showing him that I had no intention of acting as the jealous rival, and that I did not condemn Plaxy for her relations with him. I went so far as to assure him that I did not want to come between them. To this he replied with a little speech which I laboriously made out to be, “But you do want to come between us. I don’t blame you. You want her to live with you, always. And obviously she must live with you, or some man. I cannot give her all that she needs. This life is only temporary for her. As soon as she wants to, she must go.” There was dignity and sanity in this statement, and I felt rebuked for my lack of candour.</p>
<p>By judicious wangling I was able to secure an extension of leave, so that I could spend some ten days with Plaxy and Sirius, conscientiously returning to my hotel every night. Sirius suggested that I should sleep at the cottage, but I pointed out that if I did there would be an added scandal. It seemed strangely tantalizing to me, who had been, and in a manner still was, Plaxy’s acknowledged lover, to kiss her good night at the garden gate while Sirius tactfully remained indoors. A revulsion little short of horror (which I was at pains to dissemble) would sometimes seize me at the thought of leaving her with the non-human being whom she strangely loved. On one of these occasions I must have somehow infected her with my own distress, for she suddenly clung to me with passion. A surge of joy and hunger swept over me, and I lost my presence of mind so far as to say, “Darling, come away with me. This life is all wrong for you.” But she disengaged herself, “No, dear Robert, you don’t understand. Humanly I do love you very much, but — how can I say it? — super-humanly, in the spirit, but therefore in the flesh also, I love my other dear, my strange darling. And for him there can never possibly be anyone but me.” I protested, “But he can’t give you what you really need. He said so, himself.” “Of course not,” she answered, “he can’t give me what as a girl I need most. But I am not just a girl. I am different from all other girls. I am Plaxy. And Plaxy is half of Sirius-Plaxy, needing the other half. And the other half needs me.” She paused, but before I had thought of an answer she said, “I must go. He may be thinking I shall never come back.” She kissed me hastily and hurried to the cottage door.</p>
<p>The next day was Sunday, which the Welsh keep with dreadful strictness. No work could be done on the farm, beyond feeding the beasts, so Sirius was free. I went round to Tan-y-Voel after breakfast and found Plaxy working in the garden, alone and rather self-conscious. Sirius, she said, had gone off for the day, and would not be back till after sunset. I was surprised, and in answer to my questions she explained, “The wild mood is on him, he says. It takes him and then leaves him. He has gone over the Rhinogs by the Roman Steps to a farm near Dyffryn to his crazy-making Gwen, a beautiful super-sheep-dog bitch. She should be ripe for him just now.” I showed signs of disgust and sympathy; but she promptly said, “I don’t mind. I did mind once, before I understood. But now it seems quite natural and right. Besides — —” I pressed her to continue, but she went on digging in silence. I forcibly stopped the digging. She looked me in the eyes, laughing, and said nothing. I kissed her warm sunned cheek.</p>
<p>There was human love-making in the cottage that day, and a great deal of talk. But though my darling responded to my caresses with ardour, I knew that she was all the while withholding her inmost self. Sometimes I found myself imagining with horror how a beast had awkwardly mauled the sweet human form that I now so fittingly embraced. Sometimes, on the other hand, it seemed to me that, after all, the lithe creature in my arms, though humanly, divinely shaped, was inwardly not human at all but some exquisite fawn-like beast, or perhaps a fox or dainty cat transformed temporarily into the likeness of a woman. Even the human form was not quite human; so spare and supple and delicately muscular was it that she did indeed seem more fawn than girl. Once she said, “Oh lovely, lovely to be human again, even for a little while! How we fit, my dear!” But when I urged, “This, Plaxy darling, is what you are meant for,” she answered: “This is what my body was meant for, but in spirit I cannot ever be wholly yours.” How I hated the brute Sirius in that moment! And she, sensing my hate, burst into tears, and struggled in my arms like a captive animal till she had freed herself. But the quarrel was soon made up. We spent the rest of that day as lovers do, wandering on the hill, sitting about in the garden, preparing and eating meals.</p>
<p>When the sun was low in the west I prepared to leave her, but she said, “Wait for Sirius. I do so want you to be friends.” Not till late in the evening, when we were sitting talking in the little kitchen, did we hear the garden gate. Presently Sirius opened the door and stood blinking in the lamp-light, his nostrils taking the scent of us. She held out both arms towards him, and when he reached her she drew his great head to her cheek. “Be friends, you two,” she said, taking my hand. Sirius looked at me steadily for a moment, and I smiled. He slowly waved his tail.</p>
<p>During my last few days I saw more of the dog than I had done before. We no longer avoided one another; and by now I could understand his speech rather better. One morning, while Plaxy was helping Mrs. Pugh in the dairy, I went out with Sirius and his pupils to the high pastures. It was wonderful to watch him controlling these bright but sub-human creatures with barks and singing cries that were to me quite unintelligible. It was wonderful, too, to see them at his bidding capture a particular sheep and hold it down on the ground while their master examined its feet or mouth, sometimes treating it from the panniers, which, by the way, were carried by one of the pupils. Between whiles we talked of Plaxy and of her future, and of the war, and of the prospects of the human species. Conversation was difficult, because he had so often to repeat himself, but gradually we established a genuine friendship. On the way home he said, “Come often to see us while Plaxy is still here. It is good for Plaxy. And it is good for me too to have your friendship. Some day, perhaps, it will be my turn to visit you two, if you will have me.” I felt a sudden warmth towards him, and I said, “If she and I have ever a home, it must certainly be your home too.”</p>
