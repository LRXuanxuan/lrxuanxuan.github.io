<p>上帝播下一粒种子，于是开出一朵花。上帝是神圣的，世界便是祂的花。</p>
<p>从前有一个穷人，他有一块田，终日在田里劳作。他有一个女儿，是他唯一的孩子，他爱她。日落时分，劳作结束，他望着自己的田地；暮色就在他的凝望中降临，群星也一颗颗升起。上帝的花在他头顶盛放，他却不知。但他把女儿从屋里叫出来，把手放在她头上，说：“今年收成不错，我给你买鞋和长袜。”于是女孩在黑暗里欢喜起来；而他在她身上看见了上帝。</p>
<p>黎明时，一支军队从东方而来，把田地蹂躏殆尽。他们放火烧了房屋和家什，又凌辱了女儿。愤怒给了那人力量，他杀死了三个敌人；其余的人却打破了他的头，把他扔在一旁。等他们做完一切，便离开了；女孩死了。</p>
<p>那人一整天躺在那里，什么也不知道。到了傍晚，他睁眼看见天空。一颗明亮的星把宁静带给他，使他一时忘了自己的疼痛，只想着上帝。可当他稍稍转过身，看见女孩，一切便又想了起来。他爬到她身边，吻她的头发。随后，他立下了一个誓愿。</p>
<p>所以伤好之后，他立刻去当了兵。他同伙伴们一道投入大战，心里始终记着女儿。他日复一日以杀敌为乐，直到被敌人的鲜血灌醉。</p>
<p>有一次，他遇到一个垂死的敌兵。那敌兵说：“求你留下来，陪我到死。”他皱着眉，慢慢走过去，在那人身边停下。敌兵又说：“求你跪下来，握住我的手。”他便跪下，握住敌人的手，等着他死。敌兵说：“我有两个儿子，我的妻子爱我。”两个人沉默了。随后，那敌兵死了。</p>
<p>他把尸体留给乌鸦和蚂蚁，自己却满怀悲伤地走开。他的精神垮了，便躺倒在地。他看见地面上一大群蚂蚁彼此厮杀；而在他身旁，却有一棵巨大而古老的树，树叶多得数不清。风吹动整棵树的叶子，汇成一个宏大的声音。那声音给了他安宁，于是他睡着了。</p>
<p>夜里，他醒来，看见无数星辰。树叶的低语仿佛成了群星的歌声。大地也在歌唱，处处的生命都在歌唱；军队在唱，死者也在唱。他还听见自己的女儿，她领着所有声音一同歌唱。于是那人一直听到黎明，听到太阳升起。他面对太阳站起来，又立下一个誓愿。</p>
<p>他去找战友，对他们说：“弟兄们，杀人是可耻的；与其杀人，不如死去。让我们到我们的弟兄那边去，和他们讲和吧。”众人却说：“你能说服一百万人吗？不，我们必须保卫土地。”后来命令下达，要他们进攻，那人却拒绝前进。一名军官看见了，劝他服从。他只说：“弟兄，杀人是可耻的；与其杀人，不如死去。”那军官十分悲伤，然后杀死了他。</p>
<p>上帝播下一粒种子：种子朝着美冒险生长。所有灵魂的终点，便是那朵花的美。</p>
<p>从前有一个出身高贵的年轻人，他不肯杀人。有敌人起来攻打他的人民，他所有的朋友都成了士兵；只有他悲伤地留在家中，独自在田野里行走。可敌人夺走牲畜和庄稼，屠杀百姓；年轻人心中再没有安宁，因为他开始怀疑自己。于是他登上一座山，去向上帝发问。他看见玉米田、村舍，还有远处的城市。他说：“哪怕失去我的灵魂，我们也必须救下人民。”</p>
<p>于是他怀着沉重的心下山，做了士兵。他领着人们上战场，有人因此死去。每逢战斗结束，他就独自走到一旁，扑倒在地，为死者和伤者哭泣。他喊道：“上帝啊，求你把我从杀戮中解救出来，我的灵魂已经厌恶它。”</p>
<p>然而他又一次走上战场，杀戮极其惨烈。结束以后，他站在死者中间沉思。他说：“死亡是什么？死亡有什么恶？死亡不过是深沉的睡眠，痛苦不过是一场梦。哪里有生命，哪里就有斗争；灵魂也是从斗争中生长出来的。一切悲苦的终点都是上帝。”</p>
<p>后来许多次，他都强迫自己领兵作战。他只想着人民和事业，不肯再看那些尸体。他立下英勇而仁慈的功绩，人人都爱戴他。</p>
<p>一天，他率部进攻时，有一名士兵拒绝前进。他催那人进攻，那人却说：“弟兄，杀人是可耻的；与其杀人，不如死去。”军官害怕其他人也被影响，害怕事业因此失败，于是杀死了那个人；但他十分悲伤。</p>
<p>军官继续投入战斗，他们因他而获得胜利。成千上万的敌人遭到屠杀，被一路击溃；年轻人也成了伟大的统帅，为所有士兵所敬重。但他只为事业而活；而他依旧悲伤。</p>
<p>有一个漆黑的夜晚，人们把他朋友的尸体抬了回来。于是他走进风雨中，独自想着朋友。雨打在他身上，天空一片晦暗；可他记起群星，并渴望看见它们。狂风压弯树木，叶片和细枝被扯落，抽打着他的脸。他向上帝高声呼喊：“你到底要我怎样？与其杀人，死去好得多。”</p>
<p>第二天清晨，人们押来一个敌方青年，说他是间谍。军官望进那年轻人的眼睛，却看不出半点欺诈。年轻人说：“杀人者！我的工作，是让各族人民彼此讲和。人民诅咒战争，他们也诅咒你。”军官却命人放了他，并对他说：“弟兄，既然杀人是可耻的，那么死去远胜过杀人。”随后军官走到外面，结束了自己的生命。</p>
<p>上帝播下一粒种子，祂确信花朵终会开放；但在人看见花蕾破开以前，人总免不了怀疑。</p>
<p>从前有一个聪明的年轻人，是个技艺精湛的铁匠。他同雇主发生争执，雇主便威胁他。年轻人劝同伴们站在自己这边，因为他说：“正义在我这一边。”于是大家停工，支持他。一个月后，他们来对他说：“我们已经疲惫了。”他回答：“事业是正义的。”于是他们离开。又过了一个月，他们再次来，说：“我们的妻子和孩子都在挨饿。”他仍说：“事业是正义的。”他们又离开。再过一个月，他们来说：“我们输了。”他说：“哪怕你们死去，事业也是正义的。”可众人还是回去工作，抛下了他。</p>
<p>年轻人从一个城市流浪到另一个城市，寻找工作，也寻找真理。一天夜里，黎明将至，他专心读一本书；可他渐渐厌倦了书中的智慧，便打开窗，越过屋顶与烟囱望去，看见一颗星。他想：“群星毫无目的地散在天上；人也毫无目的地散在世间，并没有上帝。”又想：“群星彼此不相撞，人却彼此冲突；我要让大地像天空一样有秩序。”</p>
<p>但两支大军分别从东方和西方而来，年轻人被抓去当兵。他一边被带走，一边思索，说：“所有民族原本是一体；发动战争是愚蠢的；我不干。”他们对他大为恼火，却始终无法说服他，于是把他送去矿井做工，在那里可以强迫他劳动。他终日在地下干活，黑暗也一点点进入他的灵魂。他说：“富人策划战争，是怕人民起来反抗。让那些富人——强盗和杀人犯——统统毁灭吧。”</p>
<p>年轻人逃出矿井，奔走于各族人民之间，劝他们和平。可敌军抓住他，把他当作间谍，押到一名军官面前。年轻人以人民的名义诅咒那个军官；军官却放了他，说：“弟兄，既然杀人是可耻的，那么死去远胜过杀人。”说完军官便结束了自己的生命，年轻人却感到高兴。可士兵们围着军官的尸体，像孩子一样哭泣，因为他们爱他。年轻人因此感到羞愧。</p>
<p>他穿过双方军队，逃到自己国家的边境。他因为那名军官，也因为上帝，而陷入困惑。此刻他坐在路旁思索，仰望蓝天，想在那里寻找上帝。一队大车驶来，车上装着人和他们的家当；最后一辆车远远落在后面，因为拉车的马已经老了。车里坐着一个老人和一个女孩，由女孩赶车。年轻人与这辆车并行，问道：“你们都是些什么人？”女孩看了他一眼，他觉得她身上有一种神圣的东西。可她移开目光，只说：“敌人打过来了。”年轻人因为他们的遭遇心中一痛，便喊道：“愿敌人受诅咒！”女孩却问：“你是谁，竟这样诅咒人？”他回答：“我是一个和平的人。”女孩望进他的眼睛，说：“你真的是吗？”</p>
<p>年轻人困惑地走开了，为老人和女孩感到悲伤。整整一天，他都想着她，夜里也一样。他梦见自己站在群星之间，安排星辰运行；那个军官来到他面前，因为让一颗星偏离了轨道而忏悔。于是他诅咒军官，把他打入地狱。可那个女孩忽然在他面前升起，责备他说：“他的血在你手上，我父亲的血也在你手上。是你的自以为义杀了他们。你这渺小的灵魂，竟把自己当成上帝来玩。”</p>
<p>清晨，年轻人去当了兵，为人民作战。他的骄傲被剥去了，变得比最卑微的人还要谦卑。冬天来临时，他上了战场，而且心甘情愿地战斗。</p>
<p>上帝播下一粒种子：花蕾缓慢生长；祂愿意何时采摘，便何时采摘。</p>
<p>从前有一个已经干不动活的老人，由女儿照料。傍晚时，两人坐在自家门口，老人说着自己壮年时的往事，女孩则低头缝衣。忽然有人从屋前跑过，大喊：“敌人！敌人！”老人愤怒地站起来，说：“愿上帝击打他们！”女孩却把他扶进屋，为出逃做准备。她从箱子里拿出积蓄——九枚金币——包在手帕里打了结，藏在身上。随后几个敌军士兵闹哄哄地闯进来，想找些乐子，一看见女孩便兴奋起来。女孩站到他们面前，说：“朋友们，我们所有的东西都可以给你们；但我和我的父亲不属于你们，我们属于上帝。”士兵们看出她身上有一种神圣，便感到羞愧；不过他们告诉她，马上这里会有战斗，要她赶紧带着父亲和家当离开。于是女孩把老马套上车，把父亲扶进车里，又把家里的东西收拾好装上。最后她爬到父亲身旁，赶车离去。</p>
<p>第二天在路上，他们遇见一个没有穿军装的年轻人。女孩知道他并不是胆小鬼，而是真正的和平主义者；她在心中尊敬他，也记住了他。</p>
<p>他们继续赶了五天路，终于来到分配给他们安置的地方。那里给了他们一块地和一间木屋，他们便住了下来。女孩把屋子收拾得让父亲住得舒服，又开地种菜。因为年轻男人都上了战场，她还受雇到田里做工。可一同劳动的人常常侧目看她，因为她总说：“但愿所有人都成为和平的人。”</p>
<p>冬天到来，严寒和大雪也来了，老人病得快要死去。他说：“愿上帝惩罚敌人，是他们把我们害成这样。”女孩却回答：“唉，他们也是上帝的孩子，祂同样爱他们。”她又说：“你还记得那个和平的年轻人吗？”老人说：“哪怕河流把鲜血倾进大海，各族人民像秋叶一样凋零；哪怕所有土地都化为废墟；终有一天，大地仍会充满和平的人。”</p>
<p>他把手放在女儿身上，为她祝福，说：“上帝需要像你这样的人，我的女儿，我的宝贝。”随后他死了，只留下女孩独自哭泣。她用干净的亚麻布把老人收拾得整整齐齐，守着遗体坐到黎明，一直想着死亡。</p>
<p>一个春天的傍晚，她穿过田野回家时，那个曾说“我是一个和平的人”的年轻人站在她面前。他说：“因为你，我去当了兵，因为我的心被你刺痛了。因为你，我脱下自以为义的外衣，心甘情愿地战斗。可有一次，我持着武器追赶一个人，他忽然绊倒了。因为他倒在地上，我的手怎么也刺不下去。对杀戮的巨大恐惧一下攫住了我，我像疯子一样逃走。从此我再也不当兵了，哪怕因此而死。我只是想把这些告诉你，所以找了你好多好多天。”女孩听了他的话，十分惊异；她开始爱上他。两个人便在田野间徘徊，舍不得分开；最后他们走进她的菜园，站在一片绿色植物之间。她说：“看那些星星，它们也是上帝的孩子。它们一定彼此相爱，并不互相杀害。”年轻人却告诉她，星星其实是巨大的太阳和世界。她便问：“那么住在那些世界里的人呢？”她举起双手，向天上的那些族类问候，说：“我素不相识的兄弟姐妹们，你们也劳动、哭泣和相爱吗？若是如此，我爱你们。你们也憎恨、杀戮和战争吗？即便如此，我仍爱你们。”于是两人在群星的威严和彼此的神秘面前沉默良久。年轻人说：“在你让我看见之前，我不知道上帝会是什么。祂是所有群星的威严，也是一个女孩的灵魂。”</p>
<p>有人来搜捕那个年轻人，以逃兵罪将他逮捕。他们说：“勇敢的人正在死去，你却躲在这里和一个娼妇厮混。”年轻人暴怒之下挣脱他们，还打伤了人；可他们最终制服了他，并杀死了他，把尸体带走。</p>
<p>女孩一动不动地站在菜园小径上，四周都是绿色植物。她再次向天空、向那些天上的族类举起双手，呼喊道：“同我一起哭吧，同我一起哭吧，各位族人。他们把我的朋友带走了。”可她自己却没有流泪。她从一颗星望向另一颗星，为死亡而震惊。因为朋友仿佛就在近旁，一种巨大的恐惧与喜悦同时攫住了她。</p>
<p>上帝播下一粒种子。它不会失败，哪怕秋日里叶子尽数枯萎。</p>
<p>大地成了战场，城市成了瓦砾堆。所有男人都在战斗，再没有人劳动。军队饥饿而疲惫，却仍在厮杀。妇女和孩子的尸体无人掩埋，横陈露天，可新的婴儿仍不断出生。瘟疫吞噬各族人民；大地污秽不堪。</p>
<p>战争越来越凶猛，双方都无法取胜。人民开始反抗，混乱蔓延。然而每个国家都有和平的人在反对战争，也有和平的女人拒绝再生下儿子供人屠杀。士兵们开始在战斗间隙同敌人往来，甚至成为朋友；可命令一下，他们又回去彼此杀戮。每个人都对自己说：“战争就是地狱，其中没有任何好处。”可对邻人，他却说：“我们正在为伟大的事业受苦。”于是战争吞噬万物，一切恶都不断增长。</p>
<p>战场上有一个女人，正在照料伤员。烈日灼烧着他们，四周没有水。伤员们开始说胡话，可谁也无能为力。女人忙着照料他们，心里却一直深深思索：人竟能为了战争忍受这么多，可若为了和平，却什么也不敢做。</p>
<p>这时，一大群人向这里奔逃，敌人紧追在后，一边追一边屠杀。女人怀着怜悯站起来，试图阻止，可根本拦不住。人群怒吼着从她身边席卷而过，只留下新倒下的伤员。过了一阵，敌军押着俘虏回来。他们本想把她也抓走；可一股神圣的愤怒突然降临在她身上，使那些士兵竟不敢动手。她喊道：“朋友们，你们都憎恨战争，为什么还要战斗？你们难道疯了吗，明明会爱，却又彼此杀害？还是你们只是懦夫，竟不敢把武器扔下？整个世界都渴望和平，可整个世界又都在害怕。看看这片战场，这就是你们的杰作！你们为它高兴吗？你们恨它，你们当然恨它，因为你们是人，不是狼。你们有妻子、有母亲、有爱人，还有信赖你们的孩子。六月的蓝天下，你们怎么下得了手杀人？唉，我们所有人都把上帝弄丢了，所以我们再也没有喜悦。可上帝就在每一个会爱的人身上，就在每个人的心里。把武器扔下，扔掉它们。与其杀人，不如死去。宁可作为和平的人死去，也不要活着制造战争。只要你们敢这样做，别人也会敢，更多的人也会敢；这样一来，战争就非结束不可。”</p>
<p>人群围拢过来听她说话，每个人都知道她说的是真的；因为每个人心里都有一个声音在回应她的声音，那是每个人心里的上帝在说话。人群中响起低低的应和，于是所有人都知道，所有人其实都赞同。他们开始把武器扔在地上，忽然间，所有人都欢呼起来。女人便催促他们分散到乡间各处，去宣讲和平。她说：“大多数人都会被杀，但这是为了和平。”</p>
<p>忽然，他们的敌人发动了攻击，而这些人任由自己被制服。大多数人很快便被杀死，可他们死时仍赞美和平。敌人震惊了，杀戮的手开始迟疑；不久，他们也扔掉武器，成为和平的人。</p>
<p>这支敌我混杂的队伍散向四方，劝人们停止战争。许多人因此殉难，但他们在喜悦中死去。各族人民也已经准备好倾听，于是这个消息不断传播。终于，大家约定在某一天停止一切战争，把所有武器集中起来销毁。到了那一天，约定果然实行了。每个人都握住一个昔日敌人的手，立下誓言。所有军队都列队回家，家家户户充满喜悦。</p>
<p>随后，人们开始重建被毁掉的一切，开始推进和平时代的伟大事业。到处仍有悲伤，仍有战争留下的苦难；但已经有了希望。人依然会争吵，也依然会抓取眼前能抓到的东西；可一种新的精神同样开始破晓。人类的灵魂已经因苦难而受到磨炼，足以迎接一个新时代。那将是认识上帝的时代，也是喜悦的时代。</p>
<p>女人回到自己的村庄，为自己安了一个家。她种菜拿去集市卖，还养了些家禽。每周她都提着满满一篮东西去赶集。邻居家的孩子们都喜欢她，还给她取了一个亲昵的小名。夜里，她常常走进花园仰望群星，向它们询问那个已经死去的朋友。她随自己的心意给星星取名字，因为她对它们已经非常熟悉。渐渐地，她开始听见那音乐——所有群星共同唱出的歌。她也终于认识了自己的朋友，而他就是上帝。后来，等到喜悦重新回到这个世界，她死去了。</p>
<p>上帝播下一粒种子，于是开出一朵花。上帝是神圣的，世界便是祂的花。</p>

  <section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="en" lang="en" aria-label="English original" hidden>
<p>God sowed a seed, and there came a flower.<br>Holy is God, and the world His flower.</p>
<p>THERE was a poor man who had a field, wherein he laboured all day. He had a daughter, an only child, and he loved her. At sunset, after his work, he looked at the field; and twilight fell upon him looking, and the stars came out. God’s flower hung over him open, and he knew it not. But he called his daughter from the house, and laid his hand on her head. And he said, “The field bears well: I will buy thee shoes and stockings.” So, she made merry in the darkness; and he saw God in her.</p>
<p>At dawn there came an army out of the East, and laid waste the field. They set fire to the house and the goods, and used the daughter foully. Anger strengthened the man against his enemies, and he killed three of them. But the rest struck open his head, and threw him away. When they had done, they went; and the girl died.</p>
<p>The man lay all day, knowing nothing. But in the evening he looked up, and saw the sky. And a bright star comforted him with peace; so that he cared not for his pain, thinking of God only. But when he turned a little he saw the girl, and remembered. He crept to her and kissed her hair. And he made a vow.</p>
<p>Therefore when his wound was healed up, he made haste to be a soldier. He went with his companions to the great war, mindful of his daughter. He rejoiced in killing the enemy every day, till he was drunken with the blood of them.</p>
<p>It happened that he came on one dying, that was an enemy. The enemy said, “Stay with me, I pray thee, while I die.” He went up to hint slowly to stay with him, frowning upon him. But the enemy said, “Kneel, I pray thee; hold my hand.” He kneeled and took the hand of the enemy, awaiting death. The enemy said, “I have two boys, and my wife loves me.” They were silent. And the enemy died.</p>
<p>The man left him for the crows and the ants, but he went away grieving. And his spirit flagged, and he lay down. He saw a host of ants on the ground killing one another; but beside him was a great and old tree, whose leaves were innumerable. The wind stirred all the leaves of the tree, making one great sound. The sound gave peace to the man, and he slept.</p>
<p>He woke in the night, and the stars were innumerable. The murmur of the leaves seemed the song of all the stars. And the earth sang also, and life everywhere; and the armies sang, and the dead sang. And he heard his daughter, leading all. Therefore the man listened until the dawn, and until the sun rose. And he stood up before the sun, and made a vow.</p>
<p>He went to his comrades and said, “Brothers, it is a shame to kill; it were better to die. Let us go over to our brothers, and make peace.” But they said, “Wilt thou persuade a million? Nay, we must guard the land.” But when they were told to attack, the man would not. An officer saw him, and urged him. But the man said, “Brother, it is a shame to kill; it were better to die.” The officer was grieved, and killed him.</p>
<p>God sowed a seed: it adventured after beauty.<br>The Goal of all Souls is the beauty of that flower.</p>
<p>There was a young man of noble blood, who would not kill. An enemy rose up against his people, and all his friends became soldiers; but the young man stayed at home grieving, and walked alone in the fields. But the enemy devoured the cattle and the harvest, and slaughtered the people; and the young man had no peace with himself, for he doubted. So he went on to a mountain to question with God. He saw the cornfields and the cottages, and the city far away. And he said, “Though I lose my soul, we must save the people.”</p>
<p>So he went down with a heavy heart, and became a soldier. He took men into battle, and men were killed. But after the battle he went aside and threw himself on the ground, and wept for the killed, and for the wounded. He cried, “Oh, God deliver me from killing, for my soul sickens.”</p>
<p>But again he went into battle, and the slaughter was great. And when it was done, he stood among the dead thinking. He said, “What is death? What evil is in it? Death is deep sleep, and pain is a dream. Where is life, there is strife; and thence grew the soul. And the goal of all grief is God.”</p>
<p>Many time afterwards he took men into battle, forcing himself. He thought of the people and the cause only, and would not see the dead. He did deeds of valour and kindness, and was beloved.</p>
<p>One day when he led his men to attack, a man would not. And he urged the man to attack; but the man said, “Brother, it is a shame to kill; it were better to die.” The officer feared lest others should be corrupted, and the cause lost. So he killed the man: but he grieved.</p>
<p>The officer went into battle, and they gained the victory through him. The enemy were slaughtered in thousands, and driven headlong; and the young man became a great commander, honoured of all soldiers. But he lived for the cause; and he grieved.</p>
<p>It happened on a dark night that they brought in his friend dead. So he went out into the wind and the rain, to think about his friend. The rain beat upon him, and there was no clear sky; yet he remembered the stars, desiring them. There was a great wind that bent down the trees; and the leaves and little branches were torn off, scourging his face. He cried aloud to God, saying, “What wilt thou of me? It is far better to die than to kill.”</p>
<p>In the morning they brought him a young man of the enemy, saying that he was a spy. But he looked into the eyes of the young man and found no guile in them. The young man said, “Slayer! My work is to make peace between the peoples. The peoples curse war: they curse thee.” But the officer told them to release him, and said to him, “Brother, since it is a shame to kill, it is far better to die.” And the officer went outside, and killed himself.</p>
<p>God sowed a seed, certain of the flower;<br>But man must doubt till the bud burst.</p>
<p>There was a young man of intelligence, a skilled iron worker. He quarrelled with his masters, so that they threatened him. But he urged his companions to stand by him, for he said, “the right is with me;” and they stopped work and stood by him. After a month they came to him and said, “We are weary;” but he answered, “The cause is just;” and they departed. After another month they came again and said, “Our wives and our children starve;” but he said, “The cause is just;” and they departed. But after another month they came and said, “We are beaten.” He said, “Though ye die, the cause is just.” But they went back to their work, deserting him.</p>
<p>The young man wandered from city to city, seeking work and the truth. And it happened one night before dawn that he read deeply in a book; but he grew weary of its wisdom, and opened the window; and he looked up among the roofs and the chimneys, and saw a star. He thought, “The stars are thrown hither and thither for no purpose; men are thrown hither and thither, and there is no God.” And he thought, “The stars clash not, but men clash; I will make order in earth as in heaven.”</p>
<p>But two great armies came out of the East and the West, and the young man was taken away to be a soldier. But he considered while they took him and said, “All peoples are one: it is foolish to make war: I will not.” They were angry with him, but he would not be persuaded. So they took him to work in the mines where they could compel him. He laboured under ground all day, and the darkness entered into his soul. He said, “The rich contrive war, lest the people should rebel. Perish the rich, robbers and murderers.”</p>
<p>The young man escaped from the mines, and went between the peoples making peace. But the enemy seized him as a spy and took him before an officer; and the young man cursed the officer, in the name of the peoples. But the officer set him free, and said, “Brother, since it is a shame to kill, it is far better to die.” Then the officer killed himself, and the young man was glad. But the soldiers wept over their officer like children, because they loved him. The young man was ashamed.</p>
<p>He escaped through both armies into the borders of his own country. And he was perplexed because of the officer, and because of God. Now he sat by the roadside thinking, and looking into the blue sky for God. There came a number of carts, wherein were folk and their goods; and the last cart lagged sorely, for the horse was old. An old man and a girl were in the cart, and the girl drove. The young man went along with the last cart and asked, “Who are all ye?” The girl looked at him, and he saw that she was holy. But she turned her eyes from him and said, “The enemy came upon us.” His heart smote him because of them, so that he cried, “Cursed be the enemy.” But she said, “Who art thou that cursest?” He answered, “I am a man of peace.” She looked into his eyes, saying, “Art thou so?”</p>
<p>The young man went away perplexed, grieving for the old man and the girl. All day long he thought about her, and at night. And he dreamed that he stood among the stars, ordering their courses, and the officer came to him, penitent because he had led one star astray. Therefore he cursed the officer, and sent him to hell. But the girl rose before him, reproaching him; and she said, “His blood is upon thee, and the blood of my father is upon thee. Thou hast killed them in thy self-righteousness. Thou little soul, who playest at God.”</p>
<p>In the morning the young man became a soldier to fight for the people. He was stripped of his pride and became humbler than the humblest. And when winter began he went into battle, and fought gladly.</p>
<p>God sowed a seed: slowly buds the flower:<br>God will pluck when he wills.</p>
<p>There was an old man past work, whose daughter tended him. They sat in the doorway of their house in the evening; and the old man talked about his prime, and the girl sewed. But one came running by the house who cried, “The enemy, the enemy!” The old man rose up in anger and said, “God strike them!” But she led him into the house and made him ready for a journey; and she took the savings from the chest, nine gold pieces, and knotted them in a handkerchief, and hid them on her. Then soldiers of the enemy came in riotously, seeking entertainment, and when they saw the girl, rejoiced over her. But she stood before them and said, “Friends, all that we have is yours, but my father and I are not yours, but God’s.” They saw that she was holy, and they were ashamed; but they told her to go away thence with her father and their household goods, for there was to be a battle. So she harnessed the old horse to the cart, and set her father in the cart, and gathered the household goods together, and packed them in the cart. Then she climbed up beside her father and drove away.</p>
<p>Upon the road next day they met a young man who was not a soldier. She knew that he was indeed no coward, but a man of peace, and in her heart she honoured him for it, and remembered him.</p>
<p>They continued on the way five days till they came to the place allotted to them. They were given ground and a wooden hut, and there they dwelt. The girl made the house pleasant for her father, and tilled the ground for vegetables. She hired herself out to labour in the fields, for all the young men were at the war. But they that worked with her looked askance at her; for she said, “Would that all were men of peace.”</p>
<p>With winter came great cold and the snow; and the old man sickened toward death. He said, “God punish the enemy, who brought us to this.” But she answered, “Alas, they are God’s children and He loves them.” And she said, “Rememberest thou the young man of peace?” He said, “Though the rivers pour blood into the sea, and the peoples die off like autumn leaves; though all lands be wrecked; yet shall the earth be filled full with men of peace.”</p>
<p>He laid his hands on his daughter, blessing her; and he said, “God has need of such as thee, my daughter, my darling.” Then he died, and she was alone weeping. She laid him out fairly with clean linen, and sat with the dead till dawn, thinking about death.</p>
<p>On a spring evening as she came home through the fields, the young man stood before her who had said, “I am a man of peace.” He said, “Because of thee I became a soldier, for my heart smote me. Because of thee I put off my self-righteousness, and fought gladly. But it happened that I chased a man with steel and he tripped. And my hand would not strike him, because he had tripped. A great horror of killing came over me, so that I fled like one mad. I have done with soldiering for ever, though I die for it. That I might tell thee, I have sought thee very many days.” Now the girl wondered at his words; and she began to love him. And they two wandered about among the fields, loath to part; but at last they came into her garden, and stood still among the green things. She said, “See the stars, God’s children also. Surely they love, and kill not.” But he told her about the stars, that they are great suns and worlds; and she said, “They that dwell in those worlds, what of them?” She lifted up her hands to heaven, greeting those peoples; and she said to them, “Brothers and sisters whom I know not; do ye work and weep and love? Then I love you. Do ye hate and kill and make war? Still I love you.” Then were they two silent a while before the majesty of the stars, and the mystery of one another. He said, “I knew not what God might be, till thou didst show me. He is the majesty of all the stars, and he is the soul of one girl.”</p>
<p>Men came seeking him who arrested him as a deserter. They said, “Brave men are dying, and thou lurkest with a harlot.” The young man broke loose raging, and hurt them. But they overpowered him and killed him, and took away the body.</p>
<p>The girl stood still in the pathway of the garden, among the green things. She lifted up her hands again to the heavens, and to the peoples therein; and she cried to them, “Weep with me, weep with me, ye peoples. They have taken my friend.” But she wept not. She stood looking from star to star, amazed at death. A great terror and joy seized her because of the near prescence of her friend.</p>
<p>God sowed a seed. It shall not fail.<br>Though in autumn the leaves wither.</p>
<p>The earth was a battlefield, and the cities heaps of ruins. All men were fighting: there were none to work. The armies were hungry and very tired, and still they fought. Women and children lay dead in the open unburied, yet more babies were born. Pestilences ate the peoples; the earth was foul.</p>
<p>Fiercer and fiercer grew the war, and neither side could conquer. The peoples began to rebel, and confusion grew. Yet in all lands were men of peace, working against war; and women of peace, who would not bear sons for the slaughter. Soldiers began to mingle with the enemy and be friends with them between the battles; yet at a word of command they would go back to kill. Everyone said to himself, “War is Hell; there is no good in it.” But to his neighbour he said, “We suffer in a great cause.” And so war devoured all things and all evil grew.</p>
<p>There was a woman on a battlefield, tending the wounded. The sun burned them and there was no water. They began to rave, but nothing could be done. The woman was busy over them, but all the while she thought deeply, wondering that men endured so much for war, but for peace they dared nothing.</p>
<p>Now a great mass of men ran thither, chased by the enemy; who slaughtered them as they ran. The woman stood up against them compassionate, but she could not restrain them. They all swept past her raging, and she was left with the newly fallen. But after a while the enemy returned bringing prisoners. They would have seized her; but a holy anger came over her, so that they dared not. She cried, “Friends, ye all hate war; why must ye fight? Are ye mad, that ye can love and yet kill? Or are ye cowards, that ye dare not throw down your weapons? The whole world wants peace, and the whole world is afraid. See the battlefield, your work! Are ye glad of it? Ye hate it, ye hate it, for ye are men, not wolves. Ye have wives and mothers and sweethearts, and children trust you. How can ye kill under the blue sky in June? Oh, we have all lost sight of God, and so we have no joy. Yet God is in everyone that loves; he is in everyone’s heart. Throw down your weapons, throw them down. Better die than kill. Better die men of peace, than live making war. If ye dare, others will dare, and others and others; and so war must end.”</p>
<p>A crowd gathered round her to listen, and each man knew that she spoke the truth; for in everyone’s heart a voice answered her voice, the God in each speaking. A murmur rose from the crowd, so that all knew that all approved. And they began throwing down their weapons; and suddenly all shouted for joy. Then the women urged them to scatter over the country-side to speak for peace. And she said, “Most will be killed, but it is for peace.”</p>
<p>Suddenly their enemy attacked them, and they let themselves be overpowered. Most were quickly destroyed, but they died praising peace. The enemy were amazed, and faltered in the killing; and soon they also threw away their weapons, and became men of peace.</p>
<p>All that mixed host spread abroad to persuade men to stop war. Many were martyred, but they died in joy. And the peoples were ready to hear; so the word spread. At last it was agreed that on a certain day all war should cease, and all weapons be gathered together and destroyed. And on that day it was done. Each man took a vow, holding the hand of one that had been an enemy. All the armies marched home, and in their homes was joy.</p>
<p>Then men began to build again what had been destroyed, and to set on foot the great works of peace. Everywhere there was sorrow still, and the misery that war had made; but there was hope. Men began to quarrel and to grasp what was within reach; but a new spirit also dawned. The souls of men had been chastened for the beginning of a new age. It shall be an age of knowing God, and an age of joy.</p>
<p>The woman went back to her village and made a home for herself. She grew green stuff for market, and kept fowls. She went to market every week, carrying a full basket. Her neighbours’ children loved her and gave her a pet name. And often at night she went into the garden to look at the stars, and to ask them about her friend who was dead. She named the stars according to her fancy, knowing them so well. She grew to hear the music that is the song of all the stars. And she knew her friend, and he was God. Then in the time when joy had come back into the world, she died.</p>
<p>God sowed a seed, and there came a flower.<br>Holy is God, and the world His flower.</p>
