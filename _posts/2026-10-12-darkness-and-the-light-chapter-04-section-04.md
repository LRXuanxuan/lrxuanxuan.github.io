<h2>四、西藏的毁灭</h2>
<p>很长一段时间里，西藏人仍保持着良好精神，不断通过无线电鼓励全世界那些正在受折磨的光明仆人。然而轰炸越来越猛烈。两个帝国几乎把全部力量都集中起来，要消灭这些英勇的游牧者。当时流传着一个笑话：西藏落下来的不是雨点，而是炸弹。敌方空军终于成功用病原体污染了蓄水设施。疾病像火一样在人群中蔓延。由于过去长期免于传染病侵袭，西藏人已经失去常见的自然抵抗力。与此同时，彻底和平主义者，以及那些暗中信奉帝国传播的“合成信仰”的人，都在劝政府投降。从这些“第五纵队”成员的角度看，和平的确极其值得渴望；因为随着全国土地逐渐被防御病毒浸染，他们自己也正在一步步变成白痴。许多原先坚定信仰光明的人，如今也因长期战争造成的身体衰弱，再也无力抵抗病毒。很快，人们便看出，假以时日，绝大多数人口终究都会屈服。</p>
<p>拉萨被彻底抹去，也等于摧毁了这个国家教育与精神生活的中枢。起初，各地大型宗教机构还能成功接替其工作，继续维持人民的精神训练；但这些机构也一个接一个遭到摧毁。老一代仍有过去教育留下的精神基础，可年轻人的教育——过去一直是国家最迫切的任务——如今却不得不让位于防御的紧急要求。因此，青少年越来越难以抵抗病毒。即使在西藏最繁荣的时候，人口本来就不多；战争如今又大幅削减了人口。进步政权时期，西藏人曾经是全世界最健康的民族。本地人的强健体质，与卓越的公共卫生体系彼此配合。如今那些日子已经过去：战争不仅带来了病原体，也摧毁了卫生服务。牦牛群同样遭受重大损失。饥荒进一步削弱人民的体力。最糟糕的是，原本就十分稀缺的水源，因为水坝不断遭到轰炸而大幅减少。</p>
<p>体质与意志较弱的人已经公开要求投降，甚至开始策划背叛。然而背叛后来被证明实际上不可能，因为背叛本身意味着精神解体，而精神一旦解体，人也就会立刻向无处不在的病毒屈服。</p>
<p>边境之外，由光明仆人组织的起义早已全部被镇压。如今，西藏独自面对整个世界。唯一的希望是：既然帝国强权的胜利看起来已经确定无疑，它们或许会开始彼此争吵，并把武器用来互相毁灭。然而俄罗斯与中国的统治阶级此时已经以一种非理性、近乎强迫症式的恐惧和仇恨看待西藏。他们在意识层面真诚相信自己是正义的，也相信自己的社会作用不可或缺；可与此同时，在无意识深处，他们又被一种自己不敢向自己承认的罪疚折磨着——这种罪疚既是社会性的，也是精神性的。面对一个已经把这种罪疚从自身清除、并要求全世界也接受同样净化的共同体，他们感到强烈怨恨与厌恶。更何况，西藏社会展现出一些奇异的力量；帝国主义者内心深处知道，那其实正是光明的力量，可在清醒意识中，他们却把它们谴责为恶魔力量。因此，他们对西藏的行动表现出一种可怕的执拗，就像某个人在身上发现一种恐怖疾病最早出现的微小脓疱，认定这是自己罪恶结出的果实，于是决心把感染部位整个剜掉。</p>
<p>真正的危机发生在西藏政府内部。一个派别宣布，继续抵抗不仅已经毫无意义，而且本身就是错误，因为这只会白白牺牲生命。主张投降的人显然并没有背叛精神；他们没有表现出任何感染病毒、心智解体的迹象。因此，这场争论发生在同样正直、同样完整的人之间。最终，和平派取得胜利。那些仍决心不惜一切代价维护自由的人，则撤入喜马拉雅北坡的荒野。</p>
<p>西藏投降了。而在承认失败所带来的巨大精神冲击之下，几乎全体人口都屈服于病毒。仍保持清醒的人徒劳地试图保护那些变得像幼儿一样的同胞，免得他们伤害自己；但后者已经连普通日常情境都无法应付，成千上万地死去。腐烂的尸体散布平原，又进一步加剧瘟疫。清醒者无能为力，而且人数不断减少。与此同时，投降并没有带来和平。胜利者不敢进入被征服的国家，因为害怕自己也感染病毒；因此，他们继续从空中执行彻底灭绝西藏人的政策。最终，这项政策取得了成功。喜马拉雅山区残存的人又悲惨地生存了几年，但最后，这些最后的精神仆人还是被俄罗斯飞行员发现。从那以后，他们藏身的高山谷地与峡谷遭到系统轰炸，直到一切有人居住过的痕迹完全消失。</p>
<p>即便如此，帝国主义者仍不敢进入西藏，因为害怕病毒。他们首先展开了一次恐怕是有史以来规模最大的“去污染”行动。飞机系统性地向整个辽阔地区喷洒强力消毒剂，不仅把病毒彻底杀死，也把一切动植物生命的痕迹一并消灭。世界上曾经最发达的共同体之家，就这样被变成了一片荒漠。</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" class="stapledon-body" lang="en" hidden>
<h2>iv. THE DESTRUCTION OF TIBET</h2>
<p>FOR LONG THE Tibetans remained in good heart, sending constant radio encouragement to the tormented servants of the light throughout the world. But the bombing increased. The whole strength of the two empires was concentrated on the destruction of the heroic nomads. According to a current jest Tibet had bombs instead of raindrops. The enemy air forces succeeded in infecting the reservoirs with disease- germs. Disease spread like fire through the population. Prolonged freedom from infection had deprived it of the normal powers of resistance. Meanwhile the pure pacifists, and also the secret believers in the synthetic faith which was propagated from the empires, were urging the government to surrender. From the point of view of the ‘fifth-columnists’ peace was indeed earnestly to be desired; for the gradual impregnation of the whole land with the virus of defence was already reducing them to imbeciles. Many whose faith in the light had been strong were now so physically enfeebled by the strains of war that even they could no longer resist the virus. It soon became evident that in time the great mass of the population would succumb.</p>
<p>The obliteration of Lhasa had destroyed the educational and spiritual nerve-centre of the state. For a while the great provincial religious institutions successfully carried on the task of maintaining the spiritual discipline of the population. But one by one these were destroyed. The older generation were still fortified by their past schooling, but the education of the young, formerly the state’s most urgent task, had now perforce to be neglected in favour of the insistent demands of defence. Consequently it became increasingly difficult for adolescents to resist the virus. Even at the height of Tibet’s prosperity the population had been small. Warfare had now greatly reduced it. Under the progressive regime the Tibetans had been the world’s healthiest people. Native toughness had co-operated with a magnificent health service. Those days were gone, for war had not only introduced disease germs but destroyed the health service. Moreover there had been heavy casualties among the herds of yak. Famine was still further weakening the stamina of the people. Worst of all, the water supply, always meagre, had been greatly reduced by the constant bombing of the dams.</p>
<p>Already the weaker brethren were openly demanding surrender and even plotting betrayal. But betrayal turned out to be impossible because it involved spiritual disintegration, and therefore surrender to the all- pervading virus.</p>
<p>Beyond the frontiers the rebellions organized by the servants of the light had long since been crushed. Tibet now faced the world alone. The only hope was that, since the victory of the imperial powers seemed now certain, they would begin to quarrel with one another and use their armaments for mutual destruction. But the Russian and Chinese ruling classes now regarded Tibet with unreasoning, obsessive terror and hate. Consciously believing in their own righteousness and their social usefulness, they were at the same time unconsciously tormented by a guilt which they dared not confess to themselves, a guilt which was both social and spiritual. Against a community which had purged itself of that guilt, and demanded a world-wide purge, they felt bitter resentment and loathing. Moreover the Tibetan community had manifested strange powers which the imperialists in their own hearts knew to be the powers of light, but which consciously they condemned as diabolical. Thus their action against Tibet showed all the persistence of one who, discovering on his body the first minute pustule of some frightful disease, believes it to be the fruit of his own sin, and resolves to cut out the infected part.</p>
<p>For the Tibetans the crisis came when a party within the government itself declared that further resistance was not only futile but wrong, since it involved the useless sacrifice of lives. The advocates of surrender were clearly not guilty of treason against the spirit, for they showed no signs of succumbing to the virus. The disagreement was between persons of equal integrity. In the end the peace party triumphed. Those who were still determined to maintain their freedom at all costs withdrew into the wild country on the northern slopes of the Himalayas.</p>
<p>Tibet surrendered; and, under the shock of this recognition of defeat, practically the whole population succumbed to the virus.. Those who retained their sanity strove in vain to protect the hosts of their childish compatriots from coming to hurt; but these, unable to cope with ordinary situations, were killed off in thousands. Their decaying bodies littered the plains and added to the pestilence. The sane were helpless, and their numbers constantly decreased. Meanwhile surrender had not brought peace. The victors dared not enter the conquered country, lest they should succumb to the virus. They therefore continued their efforts to exterminate the Tibetan people from the air. In this policy in due season they succeeded. For a few years the Himalayan remnant miserably survived, but in the end these last servants of the spirit were discovered by the Russian airmen. Henceforth their high valleys and gorges were systematically bombed until all trace of habitation had vanished.</p>
<p>The imperialists still dared not enter the country, for fear of the virus. They first undertook what must have been the greatest of all decontamination operations. Aeroplanes systematically sprayed the whole vast area with a strong disinfectant which destroyed not only the virus but every trace of animal and vegetable life. The home of the world’s most developed community was thus turned into a desert.</p>
</section>
