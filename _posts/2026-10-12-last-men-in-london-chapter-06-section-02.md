<h2>ii. 欧洲选择战争</h2>
<p>1914年8月，你们中较有思考能力的人已经说过：欧洲若爆发一场大战，很可能造成文明的“崩溃”。他们预计，战争会立刻带来彻底的经济和社会混乱。</p>
<p>这项预言错了。他们低估了自己物质文明的恢复能力。</p>
<p>但战争确实造成了一场灾难，只不过比1914年任何人预见的都更加微妙，也更加深刻。它动摇了人对于自身天性的信心。从那以后，你们整个物种都将患上一种“种族神经症”，由罪感、恐惧、自卑和仇恨混合而成。</p>
<p>受到侵蚀的不只是文明，而是一个物种自身的完整性。</p>
<p>在那些决定命运的日子里，最后通牒和宣战书从一座首都飞向另一座首都，欧洲人口却完全没有准备好采取唯一可能拯救自己的路线。</p>
<p>他们既没有勇气，也没有想象力进行一场普遍拒战。</p>
<p>另一方面，他们也已经无法像更早世代那样，天真无罪地接受战争。</p>
<p>过去的人即使个人厌恶战争必然带来的苦难，也可以带着清白良心去打仗。但自上一场欧洲大战以来，人心已经开始发生变化。</p>
<p>群众虽然还不可能真正拒绝战争，却也不再可能毫无罪感地接受它。</p>
<p>即使那些表面上完全没有经历自我拷问的人，也很少能彻底免疫于一种自己并不自觉的羞耻与怨恨。正是这种心境，构成你们被战争折磨的各国人口最典型的精神底色；而此前任何战争中，它都从未如此广泛出现。</p>
<p>观察你们这个物种在不同类型心灵中怎样面对“战争”这个新事实，是一件极有意思的事情。</p>
<p>绝大多数人都被完全打了个措手不及。他们按照自己物种一贯方式生活，此前从未认真思考过公共事务。</p>
<p>民族国家之间的竞争也许能激起一点感情色彩浓厚的兴趣，但“整个种族如何生活”几乎完全超出他们理解范围。</p>
<p>他们全部精力都花在经济个人主义的漩涡里，努力让自己和家庭不要沉下去。因此不可避免地，他们最关心的是私人实现，以及获得私人实现最主要的手段——钱。</p>
<p>国家事务、种族事务、宇宙事件，只有在影响自己经济利益时才真正值得注意；最多也不过作为满足好奇、惊叹或者嘲笑的材料。</p>
<p>他们生产和消费，买进和卖出；按照仪式化规则玩各种球类游戏；又乘坐机械交通工具从这里移动到那里，不断寻找一个永远逃开他们的目标。</p>
<p>他们进行不被社会允许的性关系；或者在公众祝福下结婚、生育，再把孩子送进同一个漩涡。</p>
<p>星期天，他们穿上最好的衣服，去过教堂或礼拜堂以后到公园散步；或者怀着与其说是道德罪恶、不如说是社会地位下降的感觉，穿旧衣服过星期天，做自己喜欢的事情。</p>
<p>几乎毫无例外，他们会赞美那些从小被教导应该唾弃的东西。</p>
<p>少数自称“独立”、敢于自发思考和感受的人，则会同时受到同伴和自身古老良心的骚扰。最后，他们不是撤回自己的想法，就是发展成夸张古怪的怪人。</p>
<p>但这样的人很少。压倒性多数都被群体习惯奴役。</p>
<p>现在，地球精神的命运竟然要取决于这样一群生物。</p>
<p>没有任何地方真正清楚看见眼前赌注是什么，也没有任何地方意识到：整个物种正在面对自身历史上最高级别的一次考验。</p>
<p>欧洲和美洲几乎没有一个男人或女人——更不用说遥远东方——真正明白，人类动物的大考已经到来；而且来得太早了。</p>
<p>面对战争，西方男女大致显露出几种相当鲜明的类型，虽然不同类型之间存在连续过渡。</p>
<p>事实上，几乎没有哪个个体完全属于一种类型。几乎每个人身上都能发现各种战争情绪的痕迹，许多人甚至几乎每天都会从一种心境摆到另一种。</p>
<p>不过，如果按照最典型的战争态度分类，西欧人大体可以分成下面几类。</p>
<p>第一类，在每个国家都有数量大得惊人的一群人。尽管他们嘴上爱国口号喊得极响，但绝大多数时候，他们根本无法真正严肃理解战争；战争对他们来说，最多只是可能危害自己或者让自己获利的来源。</p>
<p>这种生物存在于所有阶级，从体力工人到工业巨头，再到受人尊敬的政治家。</p>
<p>军队中也有许多这样的人，只不过没能逃掉兵役。</p>
<p>他们的智力水平可以从极愚蠢一直到极敏锐，但即使其中最聪明的人，也看不见私人利益——最多再加家庭利益——以外的世界。</p>
<p>他们几乎从不意识到自己有这种缺陷；可就像某些昆虫会在无意识中模仿周围环境一样，他们至少在言语上能够把该说的话说得完全正确，因此很少有人怀疑他们缺乏人性。</p>
<p>我们经常拿这些落后的动物做实验，试图把一点忘却自我的视角灌进某个大发军火财的人、受欢迎的煽动家、拼命向上爬的参谋军官，或者队伍里卑鄙逃责的人脑中。</p>
<p>大多数实验彻底失败。不过，有些案例会出现一种很奇怪的景象。</p>
<p>那个小小自我被“不以自我为中心”的幻想闯入后，感到受到侵犯，于是立刻召唤所谓“道德”或者“责任”来保护自己。</p>
<p>例如，一个野心勃勃的将军可能会因为某次华丽却毫无战略意义的进攻牺牲大量士兵，而短暂感到不安；随后他会告诉自己：“这是必要的。”却完全看不见，自己真正关心的不过是继续维持“果断统帅”的名声。</p>
<p>我们的观察者记录到的第二类，比第一类少一点可鄙，却几乎同样落后。</p>
<p>这些人往往拥有某种相当强的社会责任感，却仍天真接受战争和武士道德。他们的视野被“英雄理想”限制。</p>
<p>在他们眼里，战争仍是传统意义上的伟大机会：个人勇气和忠诚可以获得最高表达。</p>
<p>他们说，战争能够洗掉工业社会养成的自私，也能洗掉长期安全生活养成的软弱。</p>
<p>这些毫无罪感的战争拥护者，自己面对战争时可能表现得懦弱，也可能极其英勇；但他们从不怀疑战争本身。</p>
<p>他们完全真诚地把它当作上帝赐下的考验。</p>
<p>对他们来说，战争确实是一种宗教测试，是进入某个模糊“英雄神祇”共同体的机会。</p>
<p>反对战争，就是亵渎。</p>
<p>不过这种亵渎在他们看来荒唐得太厉害，所以与其说是邪恶，不如说像白痴行为。因此，尽管他们全心谴责和平主义，却往往并没有太强报复心。</p>
<p>我们的观察者一次又一次在这些简单心灵中做实验，试图植入一点怀疑，让他们意识到事情可能还有另一面。</p>
<p>可即使成功植入，这些怀疑对宿主本人来说也只像智力练习，而不是一个活生生的问题。</p>
<p>至于我们给他们展示的残酷、恶心与毁灭景象，也只会被他们理解成检验坚忍的考题。</p>
<p>奇怪的是，恰恰在这些古老心灵、这些“快乐的战士”当中，我们有时会遇见一种异常清澈的宗教经验。</p>
<p>他们是幸运而天真的人，没有像许多同伴那样被罪感和精神折磨摧毁。</p>
<p>对他们而言，问题非常简单：一边是自我保存，一边是忠于自己最珍视的一切。</p>
<p>那些有力量始终按照自身准则行动到底的人，也确实得到一种非常甜美、而且完全配得上的至福作为报偿。</p>
<p>尤其在战争初期，我们见过许多临终痛苦因此得到救赎。</p>
<p>很多老职业军人在这种心境中得到安息。许多极年轻的下级军官——他们摆在父母壁炉台上的照片，真实记录着一个明亮、洁白无瑕的少年军人——也在最后时刻进入一种超越其简单理解能力的平静。</p>
<p>但更多人没有这么幸运。死亡来得较慢，或者来得更加粗暴，他们便无法获得这种幸福。</p>
<p>我们看着成百上千个被自己信奉之神背叛的可怜生命滑入死亡深渊：他们抓扯、尖叫、困惑、愤怒，或者被痛苦彻底剥夺了人性。</p>
<p>比“快乐的战士”更常见的是第三类。</p>
<p>这些人在精神上已经越过骑士式天真，却仍拼命想保留它。</p>
<p>战争开始时，他们首先被彼此冲突的动机震动、撕裂：一边仍忠于旧有战争理想；另一边又有某种新东西在心里模糊活动，可他们不敢正面看它。</p>
<p>因为无论自己嘴上多么遗憾、多么同情受难者，他们在内心深处其实强烈渴望战争。</p>
<p>那种新东西恰好同这种欲望以及熟悉的道德准则冲突，所以他们竭力忽略它。</p>
<p>或者，他们会说服自己：战争虽然是恶，但“这一次战争”却是必要之恶。</p>
<p>他们发展出形形色色的论证，以证明自己国家的事业就是全人类事业；或者证明战争虽然悲惨，却会带来一场伟大道德净化。</p>
<p>他们急切接受关于敌人的每一条污蔑，因为这些被内在冲突折磨的人迫切需要相信：敌国人民几乎已经不算完整的人类。</p>
<p>只有这样，他们才有把握相信战争是正义的，也才能带着清白良心释放自己对战争的热情。</p>
<p>至于和平主义者，他们往往比真正敌人更痛恨。</p>
<p>因为在折磨和平主义者时，他们仿佛也在碾死自己心中那条蛇。</p>
<p>第四类在所有国家都未必构成绝对多数，却拥有最大影响，因为其他大多数类型身上也不同程度存在第四类人的感情。</p>
<p>这些人在战争开始时并没有受到太强爱国激情推动，战争对他们也只有很轻微的浪漫吸引力。</p>
<p>他们首先想到的是一个个具体生命和具体幸福，而离自己心最近的，自然是本国同胞的生命与幸福。</p>
<p>在各国政府用谎言宣传给整个民族精神下毒以后，他们真诚相信：敌方政府是错误的一方，并且正在用野蛮手段执行卑劣政策。</p>
<p>不过，他们多少还保留了一点理智，相信敌国普通民众总体上同自己没有多大差别。</p>
<p>作为个体，敌人也不过是“普通、体面的人”，只是因为某种意志薄弱，被带进错误政策。</p>
<p>因此——他们这样说——虽然每个敌人个体都无害，可这些人的“群体精神”却是不健康的。</p>
<p>一旦聚成整体，他们就会威胁文明，所以无论付出什么代价，也必须把他们击败。</p>
<p>这其实是交战双方绝大多数男人和许多女人的态度。</p>
<p>他们缺乏对于人性的信任。</p>
<p>而正因为不相信人性，他们最终背叛了人性。</p>
<p>其实，他们本来完全可以在所有国家以数百万计地站起来说：“这场战争必须停止；我们拒绝战斗。”</p>
<p>当然，从一种意义上说，这确实极其容易；可从另一种意义上说，对他们而言又根本不可能。</p>
<p>因为他们看不见人真正的目标，不怀疑现有世界，也不怀疑表面看上去如此的人性，所以不可避免地错过了那次巨大机会，并把整个物种判上衰亡道路。</p>
<p>这些可怜生物因为缺乏视野，或者缺乏勇气，把洪水般的痛苦、悲伤与绝望引到自己头上，也引到整个未来头上。</p>
<p>他们守满上千条战壕，忍受上千个昼夜的无聊或者恐怖，表现出你们所谓“崇高忠诚”。</p>
<p>这一切他们都做了。</p>
<p>而且全都白做。</p>
<p>他们把刺刀捅进彼此内脏，承受恐怖、恶心与疯狂悔恨构成的噩梦。</p>
<p>鲜血和污秽的记忆追逐他们，未来荒凉的景象也追逐他们。</p>
<p>其中有孩子的人交出自己的儿子；女人交出自己的男人。</p>
<p>而这一切仍然毫无意义；或者说，只是为了一个同“圆的正方形”一样不可能、无意义、自相矛盾的希望——疯狂地希望“用战争终结战争”。</p>
<p>他们相信，自己的痛苦一定会生出一个崭新、更加美好的世界。</p>
<p>可实际上，正因为他们彼此缺乏信任，整个物种的未来反而被阴云遮住。</p>
<p>我们发现的第五类，则真正反对战争。</p>
<p>和平主义者也分很多种。</p>
<p>其中少数是非常天真的人。他们无论在精神还是字面上都忠于基督教信仰，简单接受“不可杀人”这条诫命，然后不再继续思考。</p>
<p>但真正有行动力量的和平主义主体——包括那些公开把“宗教顾虑”当作理由的人——其实完全不同。</p>
<p>他们所有人虽然自己不知道，却都被我之前在保罗身上描述过的那股强烈而尚未形成明确概念的冲动支配：忠于正在黎明中苏醒的“人的精神”。</p>
<p>但因为真正驱动他们的源泉仍过于模糊，他们只好用各种方式把它理性化。</p>
<p>许多人以为，自己只是被基督教信仰推动。</p>
<p>然而，我们观察者发现，真实情况往往相反：不是基督教产生了他们的和平主义，而是和平主义给基督教赋予了新意义。</p>
<p>他们本能地忠于地球心灵那场伟大冒险，却把这种直觉解释成忠于基督教上帝。</p>
<p>他们说，自己努力按照耶稣命令去爱同类；同时也努力更加深爱耶稣本人。</p>
<p>对他们来说，耶稣虽然自己并不知道，却已经成了那个神圣精神的人格化形象——那个精神正通过他们摸索前进的物种一点点体现出来。</p>
<p>还有另一些人，同样的根本直觉在他们身上构造出不同的理性解释。</p>
<p>有些人相信自己只关心每个个体的幸福，于是宣称战争制造的痛苦必然远远超过一切可能好处。</p>
<p>国家荣誉、为了保护弱小民族而承担的条约义务、民族文化传播——这些他们一概不在乎。</p>
<p>他们说，这些全是幽灵，不值得牺牲哪怕一条生命。</p>
<p>与其把弱小民族的国土变成战场，不如让敌军和平占领；保护他们的方式不应是武力，而应依靠世界舆论压力。</p>
<p>至于文化，如果它必须靠刺刀和大炮维持，那就根本不值钱。</p>
<p>他们明确宣称，真正重要的只有个体幸福。</p>
<p>可是与此同时，这些享乐主义和平主义者之所以有勇气面对军事法庭、监狱，在少数情况下甚至面对行刑队，其激情来源其实并不是自由主义个人主义。</p>
<p>真正支撑他们的，是那个更深、更模糊的直觉：人类并不是一群有时快乐、有时不快乐的个体随便聚成的蜂群，而是一只仍未装满的容器，一件尚未完成粗加工的工具；有朝一日，它将被用于某种宇宙尺度的事业。</p>
<p>为了这一点，他们走进监狱；为了这一点，他们抵抗自己群体良心的嘲弄；也为了这一点，他们死亡。</p>
<p>因为他们心里隐约知道：西方各民族如今终于必须敢于彼此说——</p>
<p>“我们宁可让你们占领我们的土地，没收我们的财产，和我们的妻子睡觉，把我们的孩子教育成你们的生活方式，也不愿向你们发动战争。因为你们和我们，都是同一个精神同等的容器。”</p>
<p>战争爆发时，欧洲大体就是由这些人组成。</p>
<p>有一大群人几乎完全从个人利益角度理解战争；有规模较小的一批军事浪漫主义者；有被内部冲突折磨、憎恨敌人又憎恨和平主义者的人；还有无数缺乏想象力却忠诚的人，他们把战争接受成保存人类幸福唯一办法，却又被要求采取的野蛮手段深深困扰；最后，则有极少数人直觉看见：现代文明人彼此发动战争，乃是彻底的愚蠢和亵渎，不可能存在比它更糟的选择。</p>
<p>这最后一类中，意志最坚定、斗争性最强的人，会绝对拒绝参与这场巨大疯狂，因此遭到迫害、监禁，甚至枪决。</p>
<p>但还有另一些人，像保罗一样，没有那么英雄，也没有那么确信自己的判断，或者对整个社会正在承受的巨大痛苦过于同情，因而无法让自己站在一旁完全不参与。</p>
<p>于是，他们选择帮助伤员；允许自己在制度许可范围内尽可能暴露于危险；并试着同时承担两种痛苦：一边是所有人共同经历的巨大苦难，另一边则是无法同同伴分享最深激情者所承受的私人孤独。</p>
<p>对海王星观察者而言，这些困惑的人是最值得研究的对象。</p>
<p>因为在他们身上，古老与现代之间的平衡最微妙。</p>
<p>在大多数人那里已经被暴力解决——无论倒向哪一边——的冲突，在他们内部却始终存在，而且无法解决。</p>
</section>

<h2>ii. EUROPE CHOOSES WAR</h2>
<p>IN A UGUST 1914 it was said by the more thoughtful among you that a great war in Europe might well cause the’ downfall’ of civilization. They expected that the war would lead at once to complete economic and social confusion. In this prophecy they were wrong. They underestimated the recuperative powers of their material civilization. But the war was to cause a disaster more subtle and profound than any which was foreseen in 1914. It was to undermine man’s confidence in his own nature. Henceforth your species was to suffer a kind of racial neurosis blended of guilt, horror, inferiority, and hate. Not only civilization was to be undermined, but the integrity of a species.</p>
<p>In the fateful days when ultimatums and declarations of war were being bandied from capital to capital, the population of Europe was wholly unprepared to take the one line which could have saved it. It had neither the courage nor the imagination for a general to fight. On the other hand it could not accept the war innocently, as earlier generations had accepted wars. Hitherto men had fought with a clear conscience, however much they might personally loathe the distresses that war must bring. But, since the last war in Europe, a change had begun to come over men’s minds. Though it was not yet possible for the masses to reject war, it was no longer possible for them to accept it without guilt. Few, even of those who suffered no conscious heart-searching, were wholly immune from that unwitting shame and embitterment which was the characteristic mood of your war-tortured populations, and had never occurred at all widely in any earlier war.</p>
<p>It was extremely interesting to observe within minds of various types the different reactions of your species to the novel fact of war. Most were taken completely by surprise. In the manner characteristic of their species they had lived hitherto without serious thought ‘for matters of public concern. The rivalries of national states might indeed rouse in them some sentimental interest, but the life of the race lay almost wholly beyond their grasp. They were fully occupied in keeping themselves and their families afloat in the maelstrom of economic individualism. Inevitably their chief concern was private fulfilment, and its essential means, money. National affairs, racial affairs, cosmical events, were of interest to them only in their economic bearing, or at most as occasions of curiosity, wonder or ridicule. They produced and consumed, bought and sold, played ritual games with balls, and transported themselves hither and thither in mechanical vehicles in search of a goal which ever eluded them. They indulged in illicit sexual intercourse; or with public applause they married, propagated, launched their children upon the maelstrom. They put on their best clothes on Sunday, and after church or chapel they walked in the park. Or, with a sense less of moral guilt than of social degradation, they spent their Sundays in old clothes and upon congenial occupations. Almost invariably they applauded the things they had been taught to revile. Or, if they were’ original’ and dared to think and feel spontaneously, they found themselves harassed both by their fellows and by their own archaic consciences. They then either recanted or developed into extravagant cranks. But these were few. The overwhelming majority were enslaved by the custom of the herd.</p>
<p>Such were the beings on whom the fate of the Terrestrial spirit now depended. Nowhere was there any clear perception of the issues at stake, nowhere any recognition that the species was faced with the supreme crisis of its career. Scarcely a man or woman in Europe or America, still less in the remote East, realized that the great test of the human animal had come, and come, alas, too soon.</p>
<p>In their reaction to war, Western men and women revealed, themselves as falling into a few well-marked types, which nevertheless graded into one another. Indeed, scarcely any individuals could be said to belong wholly to any one type. In almost all there were traces of every kind of war sentiment, and in many there was an almost diurnal fluctuation of mood from one to another. Nevertheless Western Europeans may be significantly classified according to their most characteristic attitude to the war as’ follows:</p>
<p>First, in every nation there was the incredibly large swarm of’ persons who, in spite of their vociferous patriotism, were at most tinles incapable of taking the war seriously in any sense except as a source of possible danger or profit to themselves. Such creatures we found in all classes, from manual labourers to captains of industry and respected statesmen. In the armies also we found, many, who had failed to evade their military obligations. They were of all orders of intelligence, from the very stupid to the acute; but even the most brilliant of them lacked the power to see beyond the horizon of private, or at most family, interest. They were nearly always quite unconscious of their own deficiency; yet almost with the unwitting mimicry shown by some insects, they managed to behave, verbally at least, with impeccable correctness. They were seldom suspected of being inhuman. Often have we experinlented on these backward animals, striving to introduce into the mind of some munition-profiteer, some popular demagogue, some climbing staff-officer, or some abject shirker in the ranks, glimmers of a self-oblivious view. Most often the experiment has failed completely; but in some cases we have been rewarded by a curious spectacle. The little self, outraged by the incursion of unself-centred fantasies, has called ‘morality’ or ‘duty’ to its assistance. The ambitious general, for instance, troubled for a moment by the sacrifice of life entailed in some brilliant barren attack, has told himself that it is necessary, and could not see that he was caring only for his reputation as a resolute commander.</p>
<p>The second type recorded by our observers was less contemptible, but almost as backward. These were the persons who, though often strong in a kind of social sense, innocently accepted war and the martial code. Their vision was limited to the hero ideal. They saw the war in the good old way as a supreme opportunity of personal courage and devotion. It came, they said, to purge men of the selfishness bred of industrialism and of the softness bred of security. These guiltless champions of the war might personally behave toward it either with cowardice or heroism; but they never questioned it. With complete sincerity they faced it as a god-sent ordeal. For them it was indeed a religious test, an opportunity to enter into communion with some obscurely conceived heroic deity. To speak against it was sacrilege; but a sacrilege so gross and fantastic that it should be regarded as a sign rather of idiocy than of wickedness. Consequently, though they condemned pacifism whole-heartedly, there was no vindictiveness in their condemnation. Again and again our observers, experimenting in these simple minds, have tried to introduce some doubt, some apprehension that there might be another side to the matter. But such doubts as could be introduced appeared to the subject himself as merely an intellectual exercise, not as a live issue. Such images of brutality and disgust as were introduced were accepted simply as tests of fortitude.</p>
<p>Curiously it was among these archaic souls, these happy warriors, that we sometimes came upon a pellucid kind of religious experience. Fortunate innocents, they were exempt from the guilt and torture which wrecked so many of their fellows. For them the issue was a clear issue between self-regard and loyalty to all that they most cherished. And those who had the strength to bear themselves throughout according to their code had the reward of a very sweet and well-deserved beatitude. We attended many a death-agony that was thus redeemed, especially in the earliest phase of the war. Many an old regular thus found his rest. Many a very young subaltern, whose photograph on the parental mantelpiece truthfully commemorated a bright immaculate boy-soldier, found in his last moment that peace which passed his simple understanding. But many more, to whom death came less suddenly or more brutally, could not attain that bliss. Hundreds, thousands of these luckless beings, betrayed by their god, we have watched slipping down into the gulf of death, clutching, screaming, bewildered and indignant, or utterly dehumanized by pain.</p>
<p>More common than the ‘happy warriors’ was a third type, namely those who, having passed in spirit beyond this knightly innocence, still tried to retain it. These, when the war began, were first shocked and tom asunder by conflicting motives, by loyalty to the old idea of War and by the obscure stirrings of something new which they dared not clearly face. For, in spite of all their regrets and compassion, they very deeply lusted for war. And because this new thing that disturbed them ran counter to this lust and to the familiar code, they strove to ignore it. Or they persuaded themselves that though war was an evil, this war was a necessary evil. They elaborated all manner of arguments to convince themselves that their country’s cause was the cause of humanity, or that the War, though tragic, would result in a great moral purgation. They eagerly accepted every slander against the enemy, for it was very urgent for these distraught spirits to believe that the enemy peoples were almost sub-human. Only so could they feel confident that the War was right, and indulge their martial zeal with a clear conscience. The pacifists they condemned even more bitterly than the enemy, for in tormenting the pacifists they seemed to be crushing the snake in their own hearts.</p>
<p>The fourth type, though not actually a majority in all lands, had the greatest influence, because in most of the other types the sentiment of this fourth type was present in some considerable degree. These were at the outset little stirred by patriotism, and for them war had but a slight romantic appeal. They thought only of individual lives and happiness, and nearest their hearts were the lives and happiness of their fellow-countrymen. Under the influence of the lying propaganda with which the spirit of each nation was poisoned by its government, they sincerely believed that the enemy government was in the wrong, and was carrying out a base policy by brutal measures. But they preserved their sanity so far as to believe that the enemy peoples were on the whole not very different from themselves. As individuals the enemy were ‘just ordinary decent folk’ who, through some lack of resolution, had been led into a false policy. Consequently (so it was said) the ‘group spirit’ of these swarms of harmless enemy individuals was unhealthy. In the mass they were a danger to civilization, and so at all costs they must be beaten.</p>
<p>Such was the attitude of most men and many women in both the opposed groups of peoples. They lacked faith in human nature. And through their lack of faith in it they betrayed it. They might so easily have risen up in their millions in all lands to say, ‘This war must stop; we will not fight.’ Yet of course, though in a sense so easy, such a refusal was also utterly impossible to them. Because they were without any perception of man’s true end, because they accepted the world as it stood and human nature as it seemed, they inevitably missed the great opportunity, and condemned their species to decline. Pitiable beings, they brought upon their own heads, and upon the future, deluges of pain, grief, despair, all through lack of vision, or of courage. They manned a thousand trenches, endured a thousand days and nights of ennui or horror, displayed what in your kind is called superb devotion. All this they did, and all for nothing. They thrust bayonets into one another’s entrails, they suffered nightmares of terror, disgust and frantic remorse. They were haunted by bloody and filthy memories, and by prospects of desolation. Those of them who were parents gave up their sons, those who were women gave up their men, and all for nothing; or for a hope that was as impossible, as meaningless, as self-contradictory, as a round square, for the mad hope that war should end war. They believed that from their agony there must spring anew, fair world. But in fact through their lack of faith in one another the whole future of their species was overclouded.</p>
<p>The fifth type that we discovered was actually opposed to the war. There were many kinds of pacifists. A few were those naive beings who, loyal to the Christian faith both in the spirit and the letter, simply accepted the commandment ‘Thou shalt not kill’, and thought no further. But the main body of effective pacifists, even of those who gave as their motive ‘religious scruples’, were of a very different water. One and all, though they knew it not were ruled by that imperious, but still unformulated impulse which I have already noted in the case of Paul, the impulse of loyalty to the dawning spirit of man. But since the real spring of their conduct Was still so obscure, they had to rationalize it in various manners. Many supposed themselves to be moved simply by the Christian faith. But indeed in these, so our observers discovered, it was not Christianity that had bred pacifism, but pacifism that had given Christianity a new significance. Strong in the intuitive loyalty to the great adventure of the Terrestrial mind, they interpreted that intuition as loyalty to the Christian God. They strove, so they said, to love their fellows as Jesus had bidden them, but also they strove to love Jesus himself even more. And for them Jesus, though they knew it not, was the divine spirit embodied in their groping species.</p>
<p>Others there were, upon whom the basis of the same intuition constructed other rationalizations. Some, believing that they cared only for the happiness of individuals, declared that the misery of war must far outweigh all its good effects. They cared nothing for national honour, nothing for treaty obligations to defend weak peoples, nothing for the propagation of national culture. These, they said, were mere phantoms, for which not one life should be sacrificed. Better for the weak peoples to have their countries occupied peaceably than turned into a battlefield. Let them be defended not by force but by the pressure of world-opinion. As for culture, it was worth nothing if it depended on bayonets and guns. Nothing whatever mattered, so they affirmed, but the happiness of individuals. Yet all the while these hedonistic pacifists unwittingly drew the fervour which made them face tribunals, prison, and in some cases a firing party, not from their liberal individualism but from that deep and obscure intuition that the human race was no mere swarm of happy-unhappy individuals, but a vessel still unfilled, an instrument still roughly fashioned, and some day to be used for cosmical achievement. For this they went to prison, for this they resisted the taunts of their own herd-consciences. For this they died; and because they felt it in their hearts that the Western peoples must now at last dare to say, each to the other, ‘Rather than make war, we will let you overrun our lands, sequester our goods, sleep with our wives, educate our children to your way of living. For we are all equal vessels of the one spirit, we and you.’</p>
<p>Such was the composition of Europe when the war began. There was the great host of those who regarded it almost solely from the personal point of view; the smaller company of martial romantics; the conflict-racked enemy-haters and pacifist-baiters; the swarms of unimaginative loyal folk, who accepted the war as the only way to preserve human happiness, but were sorely perplexed by the savagery that was expected of them; the minute band of those who intuited that war between modern civilized men was utter folly and sacrilege, than which there could be no worse alternative.</p>
<p>Of these last the more resolute and the more pugnacious refused absolutely to have any part in the great madness, and were therefore persecuted, imprisoned, or even shot. But others, like Paul, less heroic, less confident of their own opinion, or more sympathetic to the great public agony, could not bring themselves to stand aside inactive. They chose therefore to help the wounded, to expose themselves so far as was permitted, to accept so far as possible on the one hand the great common agony, and on the other the private loneliness of those who cannot share the deepest passions of their fellows.</p>
<p>To Neptunian observers these perplexed beings were the most significant matter for study, for in them the balance between the archaic and the modern was most delicate. The conflict which in most had been violently solved, in one way or the other, was in these ever present and insoluble.</p>
