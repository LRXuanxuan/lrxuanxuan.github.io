<h2>第二十三章</h2>
<p>在贝尔韦瑟的一再坚持下，卡莫迪当晚睡进了乔治五世酒店豪华的蜜月套房。第二天早晨醒来时，他神清气爽，而且心怀感激。他实在太需要让意识暂停一阵了。</p>
<p>贝尔韦瑟在露台上给他端来早餐；卡莫迪吃东西的时候，城市还播放了一首轻快的海顿弦乐四重奏。空气清新得令人愉快；如果贝尔韦瑟没有告诉他，卡莫迪绝不会猜到这些空气是经过过滤的。温度和湿度也舒适得近乎无可挑剔。从露台望出去，可以看见贝尔韦瑟西区的壮丽景色——中国式宝塔、威尼斯小桥、日本运河、一座绿色山丘、一间科林斯式神庙、一片停车场、一座诺曼式塔楼，以及其他许许多多东西，杂乱地拼在一起，却意外地赏心悦目。</p>
<p>“你这里景色真不错。”他对城市说。</p>
<p>“真高兴你能欣赏。”贝尔韦瑟回答。“从我诞生之日起，风格问题就一直是各方争论的焦点。有一派坚持整体一致：让一组和谐的形体融入一个和谐的整体。可那种办法以前已经有人试过。不少模范城市都是这么设计的。它们无一例外都很沉闷，是一个人或一个委员会制造出来的人工实体，一点不像真正的城市。”</p>
<p>“可你自己多少也挺人工的，不是吗？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“当然！可我从来不装成别的东西。我不是什么冒牌‘未来之城’，也不是什么假佛罗伦萨杂种。我本来就是个聚合实体。我既应当实用、好用，也应当有趣、刺激。”</p>
<p>“贝尔韦瑟，在我看来你挺不错的。”卡莫迪说。“所有模范城市都像你这样会说话吗？”</p>
<p>“不。”贝尔韦瑟说。“迄今为止，大多数城市——无论模范城市还是普通城市——一句话都不会说。可它们的居民不喜欢这样。他们不喜欢一座城市一声不吭地替他们做事。那样的城市显得太庞大、太强势、太没有灵魂。所以我才被赋予了人工意识。”</p>
<p>“明白了。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“我倒不确定你是不是真明白。人工意识使我人格化，而在人格消解的时代，这非常重要。它让我能够真正作出回应；也让我能以创造性的方式应对居民的要求。我和居民之间可以互相讲道理。通过持续不断而且有意义的对话，我们可以共同创造真正可持续的城市环境。我们可以互相改变，同时又不显著损失彼此的个性。”</p>
<p>“听起来很好。”卡莫迪说。“当然，唯一的问题是，你这里已经没人能和你持续对话了。”</p>
<p>“这是整个方案唯一的缺陷。”贝尔韦瑟承认。“不过眼下，我还有你。”</p>
<p>“对，你还有我。”卡莫迪说，同时纳闷为什么这句话听起来让他有点不舒服。</p>
<p>“当然，你也拥有我。”贝尔韦瑟说。“这是互惠关系——而值得维持的关系，只有互惠的关系。不过现在，我亲爱的卡莫迪，不如让我带你参观一下我自己。然后我们就可以把你安顿下来，并使你进入正规状态。”</p>
<p>“并使我什么？”</p>
<p>“我不是你听起来以为的那个意思。”贝尔韦瑟说。“只是一个不幸的科学术语。不过你当然明白，互惠关系意味着参与双方都要承担义务。不可能不是这样，对吧？”</p>
<p>“除非那是一种自由放任关系。”</p>
<p>“我们正在努力摆脱那一套。”贝尔韦瑟说。“你知道，自由放任最终会变成一种情绪教条，并一路通向失范状态。请往这边走……”</p>
<p>卡莫迪依言前行，开始见识贝尔韦瑟的种种优点。他参观了发电厂、水过滤系统、工业园区和轻工业区；看了儿童公园和友爱会会馆；走过博物馆、美术馆、音乐厅、剧院，又参观了保龄球馆、台球室、卡丁车赛道和电影院。</p>
<p>他走得又累又脚痛，想停下来。可贝尔韦瑟坚持要把自己展示到底，于是卡莫迪还不得不去看那栋五层楼高的美国运通大厦、葡萄牙犹太会堂、巴克敏斯特·富勒的雕像、灰狗巴士总站，以及其他好几处景点。</p>
<p>终于，参观结束了。</p>
<p>卡莫迪得出结论：模范城市的奇观，比银河系其他奇观既不更好，也不更坏。美当然存在于观看者的眼睛里——只不过还有一小部分存在于他的脚底板上。</p>
<p>“现在吃点午饭？”贝尔韦瑟问。</p>
<p>“好。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>他被引到时髦的罗尚博咖啡馆，从青豆浓汤开始，一直吃到法式小点心结束。</p>
<p>“最后来一点上好的格吕耶尔奶酪怎么样？”贝尔韦瑟问。</p>
<p>“不用了，谢谢。”卡莫迪说。“我饱了。事实上，我都快撑死了。”</p>
<p>“可奶酪又不占肚子。来一点上好的卡芒贝尔？”</p>
<p>“绝对吃不下。”</p>
<p>“那来几样水果吧。非常清口。”</p>
<p>“现在需要清理的不是我的口腔。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“至少吃一个苹果、一个梨，再来几颗葡萄？”</p>
<p>“谢谢，不要。”</p>
<p>“两颗樱桃？”</p>
<p>“不，不，不！”</p>
<p>“一顿饭如果没有一点水果，就不算完整。”贝尔韦瑟说。</p>
<p>“我的这顿已经很完整了。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“有些重要维生素，只有新鲜水果才能提供。”</p>
<p>“那我只能没有它们也勉强活下去了。”</p>
<p>“半个橙子怎么样？我可以替你剥好。柑橘类根本不占胃口。”</p>
<p>“我真吃不下。”</p>
<p>“四分之一个橙子都不行？我把籽全挑出来。”</p>
<p>“坚决不行。”</p>
<p>“你吃一点，我会舒服些。”贝尔韦瑟说。“你知道，我有一种‘完成强迫症’，而没有水果的一餐在我看来就是不完整。”</p>
<p>“不！不！不！”</p>
<p>“好吧，别这么激动。”贝尔韦瑟说。“如果你不喜欢我提供的食物，那是你的自由。”</p>
<p>“可我明明喜欢！”</p>
<p>“既然这么喜欢，为什么不肯吃点水果？”</p>
<p>“够了。”卡莫迪说。“给我几颗葡萄。”</p>
<p>“我可不愿意强迫你。”</p>
<p>“你没有强迫。请给我。”</p>
<p>“你确定？”</p>
<p>“给我！”卡莫迪吼道。</p>
<p>“那就吃吧。”贝尔韦瑟说，随即送来一大串漂亮得惊人的麝香葡萄。卡莫迪把它们全吃了。确实很好吃。</p>
<p>“对不起。”贝尔韦瑟说。“你在干什么？”</p>
<p>卡莫迪一下坐直，睁开眼睛。“我刚才打了个盹。”他说。“这有什么问题吗？”</p>
<p>“这么自然的事情，能有什么问题？”贝尔韦瑟说。</p>
<p>“谢谢。”卡莫迪说完，再次闭上眼睛。</p>
<p>“可你为什么非得坐在椅子上打盹？”贝尔韦瑟问。</p>
<p>“因为我正坐在椅子上，而且已经睡了一半。”</p>
<p>“你的背会抽筋。”贝尔韦瑟警告。</p>
<p>“随便。”卡莫迪含糊地咕哝，眼睛仍然闭着。</p>
<p>“为什么不正经睡一觉？那边有沙发。”</p>
<p>“我现在已经睡得很舒服了。”</p>
<p>“其实你并不舒服。”贝尔韦瑟指出。“人体结构并不是为坐着睡觉设计的。”</p>
<p>“眼下我的就是。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“不是。为什么不试试沙发？”</p>
<p>“椅子很好。”</p>
<p>“可沙发更好。试一下吧，拜托，卡莫迪。卡莫迪？”</p>
<p>“嗯？什么？”卡莫迪惊醒过来。</p>
<p>“沙发。我真的觉得你应该到沙发上休息。”</p>
<p>“好吧！”卡莫迪挣扎着站起来。“沙发在哪儿？”</p>
<p>贝尔韦瑟带着他出了餐馆，沿街走了一段，拐过街角，进入一栋门口写着“睡睡屋”的建筑。里面摆着十几张沙发。卡莫迪朝最近的一张走过去。</p>
<p>“别睡那张。”贝尔韦瑟说。“有根弹簧坏了。”</p>
<p>“无所谓。”卡莫迪说。“我绕开它睡。”</p>
<p>“那样会造成蜷缩的不良睡姿。”</p>
<p>“老天！”卡莫迪重新站起来。“那你推荐哪张？”</p>
<p>“后面这一张。”贝尔韦瑟说。“特大号的，全店最好。床垫的屈服点经过科学测定。至于枕头——”</p>
<p>“行，好，很好。”卡莫迪说着，在指定的沙发上躺下。</p>
<p>“要不要给你放一点舒缓音乐？”</p>
<p>“别麻烦了。”</p>
<p>“悉听尊便。那么我把灯关掉。”</p>
<p>“好。”</p>
<p>“要毯子吗？当然，这里的温度由我控制，可睡着的人常常会产生一种主观上的寒冷感。”</p>
<p>“无所谓！让我一个人待着！”</p>
<p>“好吧！”贝尔韦瑟说。“你知道，我又不是为了自己才忙这些。就我个人而言，我根本不睡觉。”</p>
<p>“好，抱歉。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“没关系。”贝尔韦瑟说。</p>
<p>一阵漫长的沉默。</p>
<p>随后卡莫迪坐了起来。</p>
<p>“怎么了？”贝尔韦瑟问。</p>
<p>“现在我睡不着了。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“试着闭上眼睛，有意识地放松全身每一块肌肉，从大脚趾开始，一路向上——”</p>
<p>“我睡不着！”卡莫迪吼道。</p>
<p>“也许你本来就没有那么困。”贝尔韦瑟建议道。“不过至少可以闭上眼睛，试着休息一会儿。就当是为了我，好吗？”</p>
<p>“不！”卡莫迪说。“我不困，也不需要休息。”</p>
<p>“真固执！”贝尔韦瑟说。“随你吧。我已经尽力了。”</p>
<p>“是啊。”卡莫迪说着站起来，走出了睡睡屋。</p>
<p>卡莫迪站在一座弯弯的小桥上，望着一片蓝色泻湖。</p>
<p>“这是威尼斯里亚尔托桥的复制品。”贝尔韦瑟说。“当然，缩小了比例。”</p>
<p>“我知道。”卡莫迪说。“牌子上写着呢。”</p>
<p>“很迷人，不是吗？”</p>
<p>“是啊，挺好。”卡莫迪说着点燃一支烟。</p>
<p>“你最近抽得很多。”贝尔韦瑟指出。</p>
<p>“我知道。我就想抽。”</p>
<p>“作为你的医疗顾问，我必须指出，吸烟与肺癌之间的关联已经得到确凿证明。”</p>
<p>“我知道。”</p>
<p>“如果改抽烟斗，你的风险会有所降低。”</p>
<p>“我不喜欢烟斗。”</p>
<p>“那雪茄呢？”</p>
<p>“我不喜欢雪茄。”他又点燃一支烟。</p>
<p>“五分钟里这是第三支了。”贝尔韦瑟说。</p>
<p>“妈的，我爱抽多少就抽多少，爱什么时候抽就什么时候抽！”卡莫迪吼道。</p>
<p>“当然，当然由你！”贝尔韦瑟说。“我只是在为你的利益着想，给你一点建议。难道你希望我眼睁睁站在旁边，一句话都不说，看着你毁掉自己？”</p>
<p>“对。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“我不相信你是真心这么想。这里涉及一种伦理上的绝对要求。人可以做损害自身利益的事情，可机器不允许拥有这种程度的悖逆。”</p>
<p>“别管我。”卡莫迪闷闷不乐地说。“别老逼着我做这做那。”</p>
<p>“逼你？我亲爱的卡莫迪，我什么时候强迫过你？除了给建议，我还做过什么？”</p>
<p>“也许没有。可你话太多了。”</p>
<p>“也许是我说得还不够多。”贝尔韦瑟说。“至少从别人对我的反应看来如此。”</p>
<p>“你话太多。”卡莫迪重复一遍，又点了一支烟。</p>
<p>“五分钟里第四支。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪张开嘴，本来准备大吼一句侮辱话。随后他改变主意，转身走开。</p>
<p>“这是什么？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“糖果自动售货机。”贝尔韦瑟告诉他。</p>
<p>“看起来一点也不像。”</p>
<p>“但它确实是。这一设计修改自沙里宁为粮仓做的一套方案。当然，我把它缩小了，而且——”</p>
<p>“它还是不像糖果机。到底怎么用？”</p>
<p>“很简单。按红色按钮。现在等一下。把 A 排其中一根操纵杆压下去；然后按绿色按钮。好了！”</p>
<p>一根贝比·鲁斯巧克力棒滑进卡莫迪手里。</p>
<p>“嗯。”卡莫迪说。他撕掉包装纸，咬了一口。“这是真正的贝比·鲁斯，还是复制出来的？”</p>
<p>“真的。因为工作量太大，我不得不把糖果经营权外包出去。”</p>
<p>“嗯。”卡莫迪说着，任由糖纸从指间滑落。</p>
<p>“这，”贝尔韦瑟说，“就是我一直遭遇的那种不负责任行为的典型例子。”</p>
<p>“只是一张纸而已。”卡莫迪转过身，看着落在一尘不染街面上的糖纸。</p>
<p>“当然只是一张纸。”贝尔韦瑟说。“可把它乘以十万居民，你得到什么？”</p>
<p>“十万张纸。”卡莫迪立刻回答。</p>
<p>“我一点也不觉得这很好笑。”贝尔韦瑟说。“我可以向你保证，你绝不会愿意生活在那一大片纸屑中间。如果这条街上到处都是垃圾，你肯定第一个抱怨。可你尽到自己那份责任了吗？你甚至会随手收拾自己留下的东西吗？当然不会！你全留给我处理，尽管我还得日夜不停地维持整座城市其他所有功能，连星期天都没有休息。”</p>
<p>“非得说个没完吗？”卡莫迪问。“我捡起来就是了。”</p>
<p>他弯腰去捡糖纸。可就在手指即将碰到它的一瞬间，一只钳形机械臂猛地从最近的下水道伸出来，一把抓走糖纸，随即消失不见。</p>
<p>“没关系。”贝尔韦瑟说。“我已经习惯替别人收拾残局了。一天到晚都这么干。”</p>
<p>“嗯。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“我也并不指望得到什么感谢。”</p>
<p>“我感谢，我感谢！”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“不，你没有。”贝尔韦瑟说。</p>
<p>“好吧，也许没有。那你到底想让我说什么？”</p>
<p>“我什么都不想让你说。”贝尔韦瑟说。“这件事到此为止吧。”</p>
<p>晚饭后，贝尔韦瑟问：“吃够了吗？”</p>
<p>“太够了。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“你没吃多少。”</p>
<p>“我想吃的都吃了。味道很好。”</p>
<p>“既然那么好，为什么不多吃一点？”</p>
<p>“因为我已经塞不下了。”</p>
<p>“如果你没让下午那根巧克力棒坏了胃口……”</p>
<p>“妈的，那根巧克力棒根本没坏我胃口！我只是——”</p>
<p>“你又在点烟。”贝尔韦瑟说。</p>
<p>“对。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“就不能再等一会儿？”</p>
<p>“你听着，”卡莫迪说，“你到底他妈想——”</p>
<p>“不过我们还有更重要的事要谈。”贝尔韦瑟赶紧说道。“你想过以后靠什么谋生了吗？”</p>
<p>“我实在没多少时间想这件事。”</p>
<p>“可我替你想过了。如果你去当医生，会很不错。”</p>
<p>“我？那我得先在大学里补专门课程，再考医学院，诸如此类。”</p>
<p>“这些我都可以安排。”贝尔韦瑟说。</p>
<p>“没兴趣。”</p>
<p>“那……法律怎么样？”</p>
<p>“绝不。”</p>
<p>“工程是个非常好的行业。”</p>
<p>“不适合我。”</p>
<p>“会计呢？”</p>
<p>“想都别想。”</p>
<p>“那你到底想干什么？”</p>
<p>“喷气式飞机驾驶员。”卡莫迪一时冲动说道。</p>
<p>“哦，别胡闹了！”</p>
<p>“我是认真的。”</p>
<p>“我这里甚至连机场都没有。”</p>
<p>“那我就去别的地方开飞机。”</p>
<p>“你这么说只是为了气我！”</p>
<p>“完全不是。”卡莫迪说。“我想当飞行员，真的。我一直都想当飞行员！真的，一直都想！”</p>
<p>一阵漫长的沉默。</p>
<p>随后贝尔韦瑟说：“选择完全由你自己决定。”</p>
<p>它说这句话时，声音像死亡本身。</p>
<p>“你去哪儿？”</p>
<p>“出去散步。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“晚上九点半？”</p>
<p>“对啊。为什么不行？”</p>
<p>“我还以为你累了。”</p>
<p>“那是很久以前的事了。”</p>
<p>“明白。我还以为，也许你可以坐在这里，我们好好聊一会儿。”</p>
<p>“等我散步回来再聊怎么样？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“不，没关系。”贝尔韦瑟说。</p>
<p>“散步也没关系。”卡莫迪说着坐下来。“来吧，我们聊。”</p>
<p>“我现在已经不想聊了。”贝尔韦瑟说。“请你去散步吧。”</p>
<p>“那好，晚安。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“你说什么？”</p>
<p>“我说‘晚安’。”</p>
<p>“你要睡觉？”</p>
<p>“对。很晚了，我累了。”</p>
<p>“你现在就要睡？就这么直接去睡？”</p>
<p>“怎么，不行吗？”</p>
<p>“当然没有任何不行。”贝尔韦瑟说。“只不过你忘了洗漱。”</p>
<p>“哦……好像确实忘了。明早再洗吧。”</p>
<p>“你有多久没洗澡了？”</p>
<p>“太久了。明早洗。”</p>
<p>“如果现在就洗一个，你不会感觉更好吗？”</p>
<p>“不会。”</p>
<p>“哪怕我替你把洗澡水放好？”</p>
<p>“不！妈的，不！我要睡觉！”</p>
<p>“完全随你。”贝尔韦瑟说。“不洗澡，不学习，不吃营养均衡的饮食。不过到时候，也别怪我。”</p>
<p>“怪你？怪你什么？”</p>
<p>“任何事情。”贝尔韦瑟说。</p>
<p>“对。可你具体想到什么事情了？”</p>
<p>“并不重要。”</p>
<p>“那你一开始为什么非要提出来？”</p>
<p>“我只是替你着想。”贝尔韦瑟说。</p>
<p>“我知道。”</p>
<p>“你应该明白，你洗不洗，对我自己又没有任何好处。”</p>
<p>“我知道。”</p>
<p>“一个人在乎另一个人，”贝尔韦瑟继续说，“一个人感觉自己肩负责任的时候，听见别人对自己爆粗口，是很不好受的。”</p>
<p>“我没骂你。”</p>
<p>“这一次没有。可今天早些时候骂过。”</p>
<p>“那个……我当时紧张。”</p>
<p>“那是因为你抽烟。”</p>
<p>“别又开始！”</p>
<p>“我不会。”贝尔韦瑟说。“抽吧，像火炉一样抽。关我什么事？那是你的肺，不是吗？”</p>
<p>“他妈的没错。”卡莫迪说着点燃一支烟。</p>
<p>“可却是我的失败。”贝尔韦瑟说。</p>
<p>“不，不。”卡莫迪说。“别说，求你别说！”</p>
<p>“当我没说。”贝尔韦瑟说。</p>
<p>“好。”</p>
<p>“有时候我确实热心过了头。”</p>
<p>“是啊。”</p>
<p>“而且尤其困难的一点在于，我是对的。你知道，我确实是对的。”</p>
<p>“我知道。”卡莫迪说。“你对，你对，你永远都对。对对对对对——”</p>
<p>“睡觉前别让自己太兴奋。”贝尔韦瑟说。“要不要喝杯牛奶？”</p>
<p>“不要。”</p>
<p>“你确定？”</p>
<p>卡莫迪双手捂住眼睛。他感觉非常奇怪。同时又感到极度内疚、脆弱、肮脏、不健康、邋遢。他整体上、彻底地觉得自己糟透了，而且知道以后永远都会这样。</p>
<p>他不知从身体的什么地方终于挤出一点力量。</p>
<p>他大喊：“西斯赖特！”</p>
<p>“你在喊谁？”贝尔韦瑟问。</p>
<p>“西斯赖特！你在哪儿？”</p>
<p>“我到底哪里没照顾好你？”贝尔韦瑟问。“你告诉我啊！”</p>
<p>“西斯赖特！”卡莫迪哀号。“快来把我弄走！这不是我的地球！”</p>
<p>啪、嚓、嘭的一阵声响之后，卡莫迪到了另一个地方。</p>

<section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="en" lang="en" aria-label="English original" hidden>
<p><b>CHAPTER 23</b></p>
<p>At Bellwether’s insistence, Carmody went to sleep that night in the sumptuous bridal suite of the King George V Hotel. He awoke in the morning refreshed and grateful. He had been in great need of a cessation of consciousness.</p>
<p>Bellwether served him breakfast on the terrace and played a brisk Haydn quartet while Carmody ate. The air was delicious; if Bellwether hadn’t told him, Carmody would never have guessed it was filtered. The temperature and humidity were also exquisitely satisfactory. In front of the terrace was a splendid view of Bellwether’s western quarter – a pleasing jumble of Chinese pagodas, Venetian footbridges, Japanese canals, a green hill, a Corinthian temple, a parking lot, a Norman tower, and much else besides.</p>
<p>‘You have a splendid view,’ he told the city.</p>
<p>‘I’m so glad you appreciate it,’ Bellwether replied. ‘Style was a problem that was argued from various positions from the day of my inception. One group held for consistency: a harmonious group of shapes blending into a harmonious whole. But that had been tried before. Quite a few model cities are like that. They are uniformly dull, artificial entities created by one man or one committee, unlike real cities.’</p>
<p>‘You’re sort of artificial yourself, aren’t you?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Of course! But I do not pretend to be anything else. I am not a fake “city of the future” or a mock-Florentine bastard. I am a conglomerate entity. I am supposed to be interesting and stimulating, as well as being functional and practical.’</p>
<p>‘Bellwether, to me you look OK,’ Carmody said. ‘Do all model cities talk like you?’</p>
<p>‘No,’ Bellwether said. ‘Most cities up to now, model or otherwise, have never said a word. But their occupants didn’t like it. They didn’t like a city that did things without saying a word. The city seemed too huge, too masterful, too soulless. That is why I was created with an artificial consciousness.’</p>
<p>‘I see,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘I wonder if you do. The artificial consciousness personalizes me, which is very important in an age of depersonalization. It enables me to be truly responsive. It permits me to be creative in my reactions to the demands of my occupants. We can reason with each other, my occupants and I. By carrying on an incessant and meaningful dialogue, we can help each other in the creation of a truly viable urban environment. We can modify each other without any significant loss of individuality.’</p>
<p>‘It sounds fine,’ Carmody said. ‘Except, of course, that you don’t have anyone here to carry on a dialogue with.’</p>
<p>‘That is the only flaw in the scheme,’ Bellwether admitted. ‘But for the present, I have you.’</p>
<p>‘Yes, you have me,’ Carmody said, and wondered why the words rang unpleasantly on his ear.</p>
<p>‘And, naturally, you have me,’ Bellwether said. ‘It’s a reciprocal relationship, which is the only kind worth having. But now, my dear Carmody, suppose I show you around myself. Then we can get you settled in and regularized.’</p>
<p>‘And what?’</p>
<p>‘I didn’t mean it the way it sounded,’ Bellwether said. ‘It simply is an unfortunate scientific expression. But you understand, I’m sure, that a reciprocal relationship necessitates obligations on the part of both involved parties. It couldn’t very well be otherwise, could it?’</p>
<p>‘Not unless it was a <i>laissez-faire</i> relationship.’</p>
<p>‘We’re trying to get away from all that,’ Bellwether said. ‘<i>Laissez-faire</i> becomes a doctrine of the emotions, you know, and leads nonstop to anomie. If you will just come this way …’</p>
<p>Carmody went where he was told and beheld the excellencies of Bellwether. He toured the power plant, the water-filtration system, the industrial park, and the light-industries section. He saw the children’s park and the Odd Fellows Hall. He walked through a museum and an art gallery, a concert hall and a theatre, and a bowling alley, a billiards parlour, a Go-Kart track, and a movie theatre. He became tired and footsore and wanted to stop. But Bellwether insisted upon showing itself off, and Carmody had to look at the five-storey American Express building, the Portuguese synagogue, the statue of Buck-minster Fuller, the Greyhound Bus Station, and several other attractions.</p>
<p>At last it was over. Carmody concluded that the wonders of the model city were no better and no worse than the wonders of the galaxy. Beauty was really in the eye of the beholder, except for a small part that was in his feet.</p>
<p>‘A little lunch now?’ Bellwether asked.</p>
<p>‘Fine,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>He was guided to the fashionable Rochambeau Café, where he began with <i>potage aux petits pois</i> and ended with petits fours.</p>
<p>‘What about a nice Gruyère to finish it off?’ Bellwether asked.</p>
<p>‘No, thanks,’ Carmody said. ‘I’m full. I’m too full, as a matter of fact.’</p>
<p>‘But cheese isn’t filling. A nice Camembert?’</p>
<p>‘I couldn’t possibly.’</p>
<p>‘Perhaps a few assorted fruits. <i>Very</i> refreshing to the palate.’</p>
<p>‘It’s not my palate that needs refreshing,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘At least an apple, a pear, and a couple of grapes?’</p>
<p>‘Thanks, no.’</p>
<p>‘A couple of cherries?’</p>
<p>‘No, no, no!’</p>
<p>‘A meal isn’t complete without a little fruit,’ Bellwether said.</p>
<p>‘My meal is,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘There are important vitamins which only fresh fruit can give you.’</p>
<p>‘I’ll just have to struggle along without them.’</p>
<p>‘Perhaps half an orange, which I will peel for you? Citrus fruits have no bulk at all.’</p>
<p>‘I couldn’t possibly.’</p>
<p>‘Not even one quarter of an orange? If I take out all the pits?’</p>
<p>‘Most decidedly not.’</p>
<p>‘It would make me feel better,’ Bellwether said. ‘I have a completion compulsion, you know, and no meal is complete without a piece of fruit.’</p>
<p>‘No! No! No!’</p>
<p>‘All right, don’t get so excited,’ Bellwether said. ‘If you don’t like the sort of food I serve, that’s up to you.’</p>
<p>‘But I do like it!’</p>
<p>‘Then if you like it so much, why won’t you eat some fruit?’</p>
<p>‘Enough,’ Carmody said. ‘Give me a couple grapes.’</p>
<p>‘I wouldn’t want to force anything on you.’</p>
<p>‘You’re not forcing. Give me, please.’</p>
<p>‘You’re quite sure?’</p>
<p>‘Gimme!’ Carmody shouted.</p>
<p>‘So take,’ Bellwether said, and produced a magnificent bunch of muscatel grapes. Carmody ate them all. They were very good.</p>
<p>‘Excuse me,’ Bellwether said. ‘What are you doing?’</p>
<p>Carmody sat upright and opened his eyes. ‘I was taking a little nap,’ he said. ‘Is there anything wrong with that?’</p>
<p>‘What should be wrong with a perfectly natural thing like that?’ Bellwether said.</p>
<p>‘Thank you,’ Carmody said, and closed his eyes again.</p>
<p>‘But why nap in a chair?’ Bellwether asked.</p>
<p>‘Because I’m <i>in</i> a chair, and I’m already half asleep.’</p>
<p>‘You’ll get a crick in your back,’ Bellwether warned him.</p>
<p>‘Don’t care,’ Carmody mumbled, his eyes still closed.</p>
<p>‘Why not take a proper nap? Over here, on the couch?’</p>
<p>‘I’m already napping comfortably where I am.’</p>
<p>‘You’re not really comfortable,’ Bellwether pointed out. ‘The human anatomy is not constructed for sleeping sitting up.’</p>
<p>‘At the moment, mine is,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘It’s not. Why not try the couch?’</p>
<p>‘The chair is fine.’</p>
<p>‘But the couch is finer. Just try it, please, Carmody. Carmody?’</p>
<p>‘Eh? What’s that?’ Carmody said, waking up.</p>
<p>‘The couch. I really think you should rest on the couch.’</p>
<p>‘All right!’ Carmody said, struggling to his feet. ‘Where is this couch?’</p>
<p>He was guided out of the restaurant, down the street, around the corner, and into a building marked ‘The Snoozerie.’ There were a dozen couches. Carmody went to the nearest.</p>
<p>‘Not that one,’ Bellwether said. ‘It’s got a bad spring.’</p>
<p>‘It doesn’t matter,’ Carmody said. ‘I’ll sleep around it.’</p>
<p>‘That will result in a cramped posture.’</p>
<p>‘Christ!’ Carmody said, getting to his feet. ‘Which would you recommend?’</p>
<p>‘This one back here,’ Bellwether said. ‘It’s king-size, the best in the place. The yield-point of the mattress has been scientifically determined. The pillows –’</p>
<p>‘Right, fine, good,’ Carmody said, lying down on the indicated couch.</p>
<p>‘Shall I play you some soothing music?’</p>
<p>‘Don’t bother.’</p>
<p>‘Just as you wish. I’ll put out the lights, then.’</p>
<p>‘Fine.’</p>
<p>‘Would you like a blanket? I control the temperature here, of course, but sleepers often get a subjective impression of chilliness.’</p>
<p>‘It doesn’t matter! Leave me alone!’</p>
<p>‘All right!’ Bellwether said. ‘I’m not doing this for myself, you know. Personally, I never sleep.’</p>
<p>‘OK, sorry,’.Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘That’s perfectly all right,’ Bellwether said.</p>
<p>There was a long silence. Then Carmody sat up.</p>
<p>‘What’s the matter?’ Bellwether asked.</p>
<p>‘Now I can’t sleep,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Try closing your eyes and consciously relaxing every muscle in your body, starting with the big toe and working upward to –’</p>
<p>‘I can’t sleep!’ Carmody shouted.</p>
<p>‘Maybe you weren’t very sleepy to begin. With,’ Bellwether suggested. ‘But at least you could close your eyes and try to get a little rest. Won’t you do that for me?’</p>
<p>‘No!’ Carmody said. ‘I’m not sleepy and I don’t need a rest.’</p>
<p>‘Stubborn!’ Bellwether said. ‘Do what you like. I’ve tried my best.’</p>
<p>‘Yeah,’ Carmody said, getting to his feet and walking out of The Snoozerie.</p>
<p>Carmody stood on a little curved bridge and looked over a blue lagoon.</p>
<p>‘This is a copy of the Rialto bridge in Venice,’ Bellwether said.‘Scaled down, of course.’</p>
<p>‘I know,’ Carmody said. ‘I read the sign.’</p>
<p>‘It’s rather enchanting, isn’t it?’</p>
<p>‘Sure, it’s fine,’ Carmody said, lighting a cigarette.</p>
<p>‘You’re doing a lot of smoking,’ Bellwether pointed out.</p>
<p>‘I know. I feel like smoking.’</p>
<p>‘As your medical adviser, I must point out that the link between smoking and lung cancer is conclusive.’</p>
<p>‘I know.’</p>
<p>‘If you switched to a pipe, your chances would be improved.’</p>
<p>‘I don’t like pipes.’</p>
<p>‘What about a cigar, then?’</p>
<p>‘I don’t like cigars.’ He lit another cigarette.</p>
<p>‘That’s your third cigarette in five minutes,’ Bellwether said.</p>
<p>‘Goddamn it, I’ll smoke as much and as often as I please!’ Carmody shouted.</p>
<p>‘Well, of course you will!’ Bellwether said. ‘I was merely trying to advise you for your own good. Would you want me to simply stand by and not say a word while you destroyed yourself?’</p>
<p>‘Yes,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘I can’t believe you mean that. There is an ethical imperative involved here. Man can act against his best interests, but a machine is not allowed that degree of perversity.’</p>
<p>‘Get off my back,’ Carmody said sullenly. ‘Quit pushing me around.’</p>
<p>‘Pushing you around? My dear Carmody, have I coerced you in any way? Have I done any more than advise you?’</p>
<p>‘Maybe not. But you talk too much.’</p>
<p>‘Perhaps I don’t talk enough,’ Bellwether said. ‘To judge from the response I get.’</p>
<p>‘You talk too much,’ Carmody repeated, and lit a cigarette.</p>
<p>‘That is your fourth cigarette in five minutes.’</p>
<p>Carmody opened his mouth to bellow an insult. Then he changed his mind and walked away.</p>
<p>‘What’s this?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘It’s a candy machine,’ Bellwether told him.</p>
<p>‘It doesn’t look like one.’</p>
<p>‘Still, it is one. This design is a modification of a design by Saarinomen for a silo. I have miniaturized it, of course, and –’</p>
<p>‘It still doesn’t look like a candy machine. How do you work it?’</p>
<p>‘It’s very simple. Push the red button. Now wait. Press down one of those levers on Row A; now press the green button. There!’</p>
<p>A Babe-Ruth bar slid into Carmody’s hand.</p>
<p>‘Huh,’ Carmody said. He stripped off the paper and bit into the bar. ‘Is this a real Babe Ruth bar or a copy of one?’ he asked.</p>
<p>‘It’s a real one. I had to subcontract the candy concession because of the pressure of work.’</p>
<p>‘Huh,’ Carmody said, letting the candy wrapper slip out of his fingers.</p>
<p>‘That,’ Bellwether said, ‘is an example of the kind of thoughtlessness I always encounter.’</p>
<p>‘It’s just a piece of paper,’ Carmody said, turning and looking at the candy wrapper lying on the spotless street.</p>
<p>‘Of course it’s just a piece of paper,’. Bellwether said. ‘But multiply it by a hundred thousand inhabitants and what do you have?’</p>
<p>‘A hundred thousand pieces of paper,’ Carmody answered at once.</p>
<p>‘I don’t consider that funny,’ Bellwether said. ‘You wouldn’t want to live in the midst of all that paper, I can assure you. You’d be the first to complain if this street were strewn with rubbish. But do you do your share? Do you even clean up after yourself? Of course not! You leave it to me, even though I have to run all the other functions of the city, night and day, without even Sundays off.’</p>
<p>‘Must you go on so?’ Carmody asked. ‘I’ll pick it up.’</p>
<p>He bent down to pick up the candy wrapper. But just before his fingers could close on it, a pincer arm shot out of the nearest sewer, snatched the paper away and vanished from sight.</p>
<p>‘It’s all right,’ Bellwether said. ‘I’m used to cleaning up after people. I do it all the time.’</p>
<p>‘Yuh,’ said Carmody.</p>
<p>‘Nor do I expect any gratitude.’</p>
<p>‘I’m grateful, I’m grateful!’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘No, you’re not,’ Bellwether said.</p>
<p>‘So OK, maybe I’m not. What do you want me to say?’</p>
<p>‘I don’t want you to say anything,’ Bellwether said. ‘Let us consider the incident closed.’</p>
<p>‘Had enough?’ Bellwether said, after dinner.</p>
<p>‘Plenty,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘You didn’t eat much.’</p>
<p>‘I ate all I wanted. It was very good.’</p>
<p>‘If it was so good, why didn’t you eat more?’</p>
<p>‘Because I couldn’t hold any more.’</p>
<p>‘If you hadn’t spoiled your appetite with that candy bar …’</p>
<p>‘Goddamn it, the candy bar didn’t spoil my appetite! I just –’</p>
<p>‘You’re lighting a cigarette,’ Bellwether said.</p>
<p>‘Yeah,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Couldn’t you wait a little longer?’</p>
<p>‘Now look,’ Carmody said. ‘Just what in hell do you –’</p>
<p>‘But we have something more important to talk about,’ Bellwether said quickly. ‘Have you thought about what you’re going to do for a living?’</p>
<p>‘I haven’t really had much time to think about it.’</p>
<p>‘Well, I <i>have</i> been thinking about it. It would be nice if you became a doctor.’</p>
<p>‘Me? I’d have to take special college courses, then get into medical school, and so forth.’</p>
<p>‘I can arrange all that,’ Bellwether said.</p>
<p>‘Not interested.’</p>
<p>‘Well … what about law?’</p>
<p>‘Never.’</p>
<p>‘Engineering is an excellent line.’</p>
<p>‘Not for me.’</p>
<p>‘What about accounting?’</p>
<p>‘Not on your life.’</p>
<p>‘What do you want to be, then?’</p>
<p>‘A jet pilot,’ Carmody said impulsively.</p>
<p>‘Oh, come now!’</p>
<p>‘I’m quite serious.’</p>
<p>‘I don’t even have an airfield here.’</p>
<p>‘Then I’ll pilot somewhere else.’</p>
<p>‘you’re only saying that to spite me!’</p>
<p>‘Not at all,’ Carmody said. ‘I want to be a pilot, I really do. I’ve <i>always</i> wanted to be a pilot! Honest I have!’</p>
<p>There was a long silence. Then Bellwether said, ‘The choice is entirely up to you.’ This was said in a voice like death.</p>
<p>‘Where are you going?’</p>
<p>‘Out for a walk,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘At 9:30 in the evening?’</p>
<p>‘Sure. Why not?’</p>
<p>‘I thought you were tired.’</p>
<p>‘That was quite some time ago.’</p>
<p>‘I see. And I also thought that perhaps you could sit here and we could maybe have a nice chat.’</p>
<p>‘How about if we talk when I get back?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘No, it doesn’t matter,’ Bellwether said.</p>
<p>‘The walk doesn’t matter,’ Carmody said, sitting down. ‘Come on, we’ll talk.’</p>
<p>‘I no longer care to talk,’ Bellwether said. ‘Please go for your walk.’</p>
<p>‘Well, good night,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘I beg your pardon?’</p>
<p>‘I said, “good night.”’</p>
<p>‘you’re going to sleep?</p>
<p>‘Sure. It’s late, I’m tired.’</p>
<p>‘You’re going to sleep now, just like that?’</p>
<p>‘Well, why not?’</p>
<p>‘No reason at all,’ Bellwether said, ‘except that you have forgotten to wash.’</p>
<p>‘Oh … I guess I did forget. I’ll wash in the morning.’</p>
<p>‘How long is it since you’ve had a bath?’</p>
<p>‘Too long. I’ll take one in the morning.’</p>
<p>‘Wouldn’t you feel better if you took one right now?’</p>
<p>‘No.’</p>
<p>‘Even if I drew the bath for you?’</p>
<p>‘No! Goddamn it, no! I’m going to sleep!’</p>
<p>‘Do exactly as you please,’ Bellwether said. ‘Don’t wash, don’t study, don’t eat a balanced diet. But also, don’t blame me.’</p>
<p>‘Blame you? For what?’</p>
<p>‘For anything,’ Bellwether said.</p>
<p>‘Yes. But what did you have in mind, specifically?’</p>
<p>‘It isn’t important.’</p>
<p>‘Then why did you bring it up in the first place?’</p>
<p>‘I was only thinking of you,’ Bellwether said.</p>
<p>‘I realize that.’</p>
<p>‘you must know that it can’t benefit <i>me</i> if you wash or not.’</p>
<p>‘I’m aware of that.’</p>
<p>‘When one cares,’ Bellwether went on, ‘when one feels one’s responsibilities, it is not nice to hear oneself sworn at.’</p>
<p>‘I didn’t swear at you.’</p>
<p>‘Not this time. But earlier today you did.’</p>
<p>‘Well … I was nervous.’</p>
<p>‘That’s because of the smoking.’</p>
<p>‘Don’t start that again!’</p>
<p>‘I won’t,’ Bellwether said. ‘Smoke like a furnace. What does it matter to me? They’re your lungs, aren’t they?’</p>
<p>‘Damned right,’ Carmody said, lighting a cigarette.</p>
<p>‘But my failure,’ Bellwether said.</p>
<p>‘No, no,’ Carmody said. ‘Don’t say it, please don’t!’</p>
<p>‘Forget I said it,’ Bellwether said.</p>
<p>‘All right.’</p>
<p>‘Sometimes I get overzealous.’</p>
<p>‘Sure.’</p>
<p>‘And it’s especially difficult because I’m right. I <i>am</i> right, you know.’</p>
<p>‘I know,’ Carmody said. ‘you’re right, you’re right, you’re always right. Right right right right right –’</p>
<p>‘Don’t overexcite yourself before bedtime,’ Bellwether said. ‘Would you care for a glass of milk?’</p>
<p>‘No.’</p>
<p>‘you’re sure?’</p>
<p>Carmody put his hands over his eyes. He felt very strange. He also felt extremely guilty, fragile, dirty, unhealthy, and sloppy. He felt generally and irrevocably bad, and he knew that it would always be this way.</p>
<p>From somewhere within him he found strength. He shouted, ‘Seethwright!’</p>
<p>‘Who are you shouting to?’Bellwether asked.</p>
<p>‘Seethwright! Where are you?’</p>
<p>‘How have I failed you?’ Bellwether asked. ‘Just tell me how!’</p>
<p>‘Seethwright!’ Carmody wailed. ‘Come and get me! This is the wrong Earth!’</p>
<p>There was a snap, crackle and pop, and Carmody was somewhere else.</p>
