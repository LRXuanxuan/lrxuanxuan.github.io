<h2>三、西藏人的自卫</h2>
<p>显而易见，西藏人口稀少、相对贫穷，也没有为战争作好装备，根本不可能抵挡那两个几乎覆盖整个世界的帝国合力进攻。唯一的希望，是各国的光明仆人能够发动规模如此巨大的消极抵抗与积极破坏，以至于这场进攻最终根本无法发动。</p>
<p>西藏复兴运动本来具有强烈的和平主义气质，不过从未承诺绝对非暴力。印度影响又同中国影响复杂地交织在一起。新危机到来后，一个声势浩大的派别主张：既然反抗无论如何都毫无希望，那么现在就应当以英雄式的不抵抗来面对入侵；同时，也不应鼓励两个帝国内部的破坏活动。反对者则指出，面对那些受过训练、把温和视为可鄙软弱的侵略者，不抵抗注定失败；唯一可能奏效的政策，必须把三件事结合起来：在帝国领土内发动全面革命行动，对入侵进行绝望而坚决的抵抗，以及对精神本身保持绝对忠诚。</p>
<p>这最终成为官方政策。但随着战争发展，彻底的和平主义者势力逐渐强大，已经足以磨钝整个民族决断的锋刃。尤其在如何应对俄罗斯和中国对西藏的宣传方面，国内纯粹和平主义的强势产生了不幸影响。大批智力较低的西藏人清楚看出，面对无情敌人，绝对和平主义根本不起作用，于是对一切多少同和平主义抱有同情的人都产生怀疑和厌恶。这恰好使他们暴露在黑暗仆人的宣传之下；而后者很快发现，自己试图动摇西藏信仰的努力并非全无成效。</p>
<p>但战斗还没有失败。两个帝国境内的光明仆人确实成功唤起许多民族，为保卫西藏组织罢工和起义。在中国西部一些地区、新疆以及克什米尔——这些地方都深受新西藏影响——帝国政府遭到击败，光明政府由此建立。甚至远在欧洲，以及更遥远的美洲，俄罗斯的统治也受到严重威胁。各地起义者都知道，自己正在为一个几乎绝望的事业作战；一旦失败，暴君的报复必将如恶魔一般残酷。但对于全世界数百万人而言，西藏已经成为圣地，西藏人也成为无论付出什么代价都必须保存下来的“选民”。因为人们把西藏看作一枚胚种，一个新的世界有机体终有一天将从中发育出来。如果这枚胚种被毁灭，一切希望便会永远消失。</p>
<p>就在这些起义此起彼伏之时，整个亚洲的军火工厂也不断神秘爆炸，飞机则表现出一种奇怪的“无法离开地面”的毛病。与此同时，西藏人正在仓促组织一场明知希望渺茫的防御。北部边境之外的起义，使他们得以不受干扰地把喀喇昆仑山脉和唐古拉山脉改造成一条连续要塞。南面，喜马拉雅山本身就是天然屏障。西面，已经成功起义的克什米尔人愿意为保卫他们战斗至死。向东，川边峡谷仍在坚守。</p>
<p>然而，对地面入侵最重要的防御手段——虽然它并不能防御空袭——却是一项不久前由某座大型改革派寺院中的遗传学家和生物化学家发明的新技术。这项发明本身就显示出，在光明意志影响下，科学的发展已经变得何等奇特。在工事前方数英里处，一条约两英里宽的防御带环绕着几乎整个西藏领土，只有河流进出口除外。在这条地带中，地面以下数英尺深处都被一种微生物浸染；它是由一种天然病毒人工培育而来，具有极其奇特的性质。在生命周期的一个阶段，这种超显微物体会停留在地下深处，同某些植物分解产物发生化学反应；到了另一个阶段，它又会逐渐渗向地表，最后飘入空气，在那里繁殖并参与其他化学反应，之后重新落回地面，沉入地下土层。在空气中，这种病毒形成一种超显微尘埃，任何进入感染区域的动物都不可避免地会把它吸入体内。病毒由呼吸器官进入大脑，对高级脑中枢产生惊人影响：它会造成彻底但暂时性的记忆丧失，以及几乎全部后天技能的丧失。即使那些最根深蒂固、最熟悉的习惯也会受到严重破坏。说话与行走退化到婴儿状态，知觉大体失去意义。智力本身仍然存在；但一旦剥离全部后天经验，就只剩下一个聪明却无知的孩子那样的智力。不过，这种病毒最惊人的特点在于：那些智力高、人格完整，并接受过彻底精神训练的心灵，几乎可以完全抵抗它的影响。因此，许多西藏人只要在穿越防御带时始终让自己的心神专注于冥想，并抵抗那种作为心智解体最初征兆的沉重困意，就能够安全通过。</p>
<p>等到俄罗斯和中国那些头脑迟钝的军队，带着不可阻挡的战争机器终于试图穿过这条防御带时，军中人员却神秘地退化成了婴儿般的状态。许多人误操作自己的机器，把自己杀死。整支军队变成一个踉跄、无助的人群。西藏警察像赶羊一样把他们赶回各自领土。许多人随后又被自己的俄罗斯或中国同胞当作无用废物屠杀；另一些被保留下来观察，数周之后便完全恢复。后来再次发动的入侵也遭遇同样命运。防毒面具毫无用处，因为那些超显微孢子能够穿透任何过滤装置；而能够杀死它们的任何毒物，也同样会毒死人类。</p>
<p>可是，虽然陆地边境因此不可侵犯，这种病毒却无法抵挡空中攻击。西藏拥有一支规模很小却极为出色的空军。过去人们一直认为，如果两个帝国中任何一方发动进攻，另一方必然乐于遏制竞争对手的侵略。在这种情况下，西藏这样一支小型空军可能极有价值。但若同时面对俄罗斯和中国的联合空军，它显然必定无能为力——至少两个帝国的领导者是这样想的。这个计算漏掉了精神因素。西藏飞行员不仅受过最高水平的技术训练，而且无一例外都是自觉的光明仆人。他们虽然都还只是年轻人，因此尚不可能获得最深层的精神洞见，却从小便被培养成能够不受歪曲地体验西藏所捍卫的那些根本价值。他们十分清楚，西藏共同体是一个疯狂世界中唯一健全而喜悦的共同体，也确实是地球上第一个自觉按照“让精神得到充分表达”这一目的设计的社会。他们同样知道，如果自己让西藏被征服，就等于把整个人类种族交给黑暗意志奴役；从此以后，人类一切美好都将枯萎、消失。他们还深信，对自己而言，生命的实现就在于在天空中进行完美的服务。于是，这些年轻人以一种比任何狂热都更加可怕的、平静而绝对的勇气升空迎击入侵轰炸机，并成千上万地把它们击落。</p>
<p>顺便一提，西藏政府还对这些年轻飞行员规定了一项不同寻常的资格条件：他们必须是已婚男子。除此之外，任何人若尚无子女，而且妻子也没有怀孕，就不得出击迎战敌人。后来，这些奇特的“王牌飞行员”之间甚至形成一种荣誉准则：在至少已经有三个孩子以前，不应冒极端危险。</p>
<p>西藏空军的防御如此有效，以至于一波又一波空袭逐渐变得越来越稀疏，最终甚至停顿了好几年。在这段时期里，西藏人几乎完全同世界其余部分隔绝，除了无线电联系，以及偶尔由飞机执行的大胆远征——要么去煽动革命，要么夺取某种极其需要的物资。与此同时，帝国主义者则在准备一支规模如此庞大的空军，以及数量如此众多的飞行员，以至于已经大幅缩水的西藏空军再也不可能进行有效抵抗。</p>
<p>大进攻终于发动时，西藏上空被入侵轰炸机遮得昏暗。所有城市、村庄，以及那些孤立在山野中的大型寺院，很快便全部被毁。国家的精神心脏拉萨，则被彻底从地面上抹去。</p>
<p>我从遥远未来的观察点观看这些事件，身边还有超越人类的智慧者一同旁观，照理说，本应能够免于普通人类的怜悯。但事实上，我仍被同情与敬佩彻底淹没。因为眼前这个民族异常敏锐、异常清醒，是一种罕见而辉煌的社会结构的继承者与守护者；他们也有充分理由相信，自己正是黑暗世界中唯一仍真正有效地捍卫光明的力量。可如今，他们建立的一切都在被摧毁。被牺牲的不只是他们所热爱的信仰圣殿，不只是珍贵的学术殿堂和全部经济生产工具；比这些更宝贵得多的，还有他们的青年——过去全部努力所结出的最完美果实。家庭永远破碎，父母失去子女，孩子沦为孤儿；恋人们甚至在死亡羽翼笼罩下仍抓住欢乐，却突然在一种可怖而并非他们所愿的“结合”中被炸得血肉混杂。夜晚，高空云层不断被炮火与炸弹的闪光，以及巨大火灾那阴森却又美丽的光芒照亮。白天也好，夜晚也好，炸弹仍在尖啸、爆裂，而人们则在瓦砾中搜寻伙伴。西藏人没有陷入自怜。支配全民族的，是一种献身式的爱国主义；它像以往许多爱国主义一样，把保存本民族及其文化看作全人类福祉的迫切需要——只不过这一次，它这样想确实有充分理由。在战争这个阶段，人们带着一种由幽默调和过的昂扬状态继续工作。他们觉得，这是人类历史中最关键的时刻，是一场无限值得去打的战斗；可与此同时，他们又以一种令人惊讶的超然态度，品味着西藏处境中的反讽。</p>
<p>此时，人们开始设法适应新的生存条件。</p>
<p>西藏国土辽阔，而人口稀少。新政权曾经如此精心扶植的农业如今已经无法继续：农庄遭到轰炸和机枪扫射，大型水库的堤坝也被摧毁。但牦牛还在，于是人口重新退回游牧牧业生活。人们分成小群不断迁徙，每晚把经过伪装的帐篷搭在新的地方，因此敌人很难找到值得攻击的目标。幸运的是，帝国主义者起初并没有尝试空降部队，因为他们相信整个国家都已经感染了那种挫败最初地面进攻的奇怪疾病。与此同时，西藏人正匆忙把宝贵的病毒扩散到全部领土。它的作用，是淘汰所有清醒程度不足、无法抵抗感染的人。真正因此完全失去行动能力的只是一小部分，这些人被安置到专门机构照料。另有更多人——但仍只是少数——遭受暂时性的轻微发作。此时，病毒也开始自行越过边境扩散。边境之外的后果当然严重得无可比拟：所有受感染地区的组织秩序都完全消失了。</p>

<h2>iii. THE TIBETANS DEFEND THEMSELVES</h2>
<p>IT WAS OBVIOUS that the Tibetans, few, relatively poor, and unequipped for war, could not resist the combined forces of the two empires that covered the world. There was only one hope, namely that the servants of the light in all countries would be able to carry out so great a campaign of passive resistance and active sabotage that the attack would never be launched.</p>
<p>The Tibetan renaissance had been strongly pacifist in temper, though never pledged to absolute non-violence. The Indian influence had been complicated by the influence of China. In the new crisis a vociferous party urged that, since resistance was anyhow hopeless, the time had come for heroic non-resistance to invasion; and that sabotage in the two empires must not be encouraged. Against this view it was pointed out that non-resistance was doomed to fail against invaders schooled to despise gentleness, and that no policy could succeed but one which combined total revolutionary action in the imperial territories, desperate resistance to invasion, and absolute loyalty to the spirit.</p>
<p>This became the official policy, but as the war proceeded the pure pacifists became strong enough to blunt the edge of resolution. In relation to Russian and Chinese propaganda in Tibet the strength of pure pacifism in the country had an unfortunate influence. Large numbers of the less intelligent Tibetans, seeing clearly enough that pure pacifism would not work against the ruthless enemy, conceived suspicion and disgust against all those who were in any way sympathetic to pacifism. They thus laid themselves open to the propaganda of the servants of darkness, who soon discovered that their efforts to undermine Tibetan faith were not wholly unsuccessful.</p>
<p>But the battle was not yet lost. The servants of light throughout the empires did succeed in rousing many peoples to organize strikes and rebellions in defence of Tibet. In parts of Western China, in Sinkiang, and in Kashmir, all of which had been greatly influenced by the new Tibet, the imperial governments were defeated, and governments of the light were created. Even in far Europe and in farther America the Russian power was seriously threatened. Everywhere the rebels knew that they were fighting in a desperate cause, and that if they were defeated the vengeance of the tyrants would be diabolic. But Tibet had become for millions throughout the world a holy land, and its people the chosen people who must be preserved at all costs. For Tibet was thought of as the germ from which a new world-organism would in due season develop. If the germ was destroyed, all hope would be for ever lost. .</p>
<p>While these rebellions were in progress, and while throughout Asia munition factories were mysteriously blowing up and aeroplanes showing a strange inability to leave the ground, the Tibetans were hastily organizing a forlorn defence. Rebellions beyond their northern frontiers made it possible to work unhindered to turn the Karakorum and Dangla Ranges into a continuous fortress. To thesouth the Himalayas were a natural barrier. To the west the successful Kashmiri rebels would defend them to the death. Eastward the Chwanben gorges were still being held.</p>
<p>But the main defence against invasion, though not against attack from the air, was a device recently invented by geneticists and biochemists in one of the great reformed Lamasseries. The character of this invention shows how strangely science was developing under the influence of will for the light. Some miles in front of the fortifications the new defences formed a belt about two miles wide and completely surrounding Tibetan territory, save for the exits and entrances of rivers. Througout this belt the ground was impregnated to a depth of several feet with a micro-organism which had been artificially bred from a natural virus. It had a strange property. Though in one stage of’ its life-cycle this ultra-microscopic object remained deep underground in chemical reaction with certain products of vegetable decomposition, in another stage it gradually percolated towards the surface and finally drifted off into the air, to reproduce and take part in other chemical reactions before settling once more on the ground and sinking into the subsoil. In the air this virus formed an ultra-microscopic dust which would inevitably be inhaled by all animals in the infected area. From the respiratory organs it travelled to the brain. It had a startling effect on the higher brain centres. It produced a complete but temporary loss of memory and of nearly all acquired skills. Even those habits that were most long-established and familiar were seriously disturbed. Speech and walking became infantile, perception largely meaningless. Intelligence remained; but, shorn of all its acquired experience, it was like the intelligence of a bright and ignorant child. But the most striking aspect of the virus was that its influence could be almost completely resisted by minds of high intelligence and integrity that had undergone a thorough spiritual discipline. Many Tibetans, therefore, could cross the defence belt in safety so long as they kept their minds occupied with meditation, while on the journey and resisted the oppressive drowsiness which was the first symptom of disintegration.</p>
<p>When at last the dull-witted armies of Russia and China with their irresistible war machines attempted to cross the belt, their personnel was mysteriously reduced to infantilism. Many accidentally killed themselves with their own machinery. The army became a stumbling, helpless mob. They were shepherded back into their own territory by Tibetan police. Many were then slaughtered by their Russian or Chinese compatriots as worthless goods. Some were preserved for observation, and after a few weeks they completely recovered. Fresh attempts at invasion met with the same fate. Respirators were of no avail, for the ultra-microscopic spores could pass through any filter, and nothing would poison them that was not also poisonous to human beings.</p>
<p>But though on the ground the frontier was inviolate, the virus provided no defence against attack by air. The Tibetans had a small but brilliant air force. It had been assumed that in any attack by one of the two empires the other would be eager to check aggression by its rival. In such circumstances such an air force as Tibet possessed might prove invaluable. But against the combined air forces of Russia and China, it must surely (thought the leaders of those empires) prove impotent. This calculation omitted the spiritual factor. Not only had the Tibetan airmen been trained to the highest technical proficiency. They were also, one and all, conscious servants of the light. Boys though they were, and therefore as yet incapable of the deeper spiritual insight, they had been brought up to experience without perversion the fundamental values for which Tibet was standing. Full well they knew that the Tibetan community was the one sane and joyful community in a crazy world, and indeed the first terrestrial society to be consciously planned for the full expression of the spirit. They also knew that if they allowed Tibet to be conquered they would doom the human race to servitude under the will for darkness. They knew that henceforth all human loveliness would wither and vanish. And they were convinced that for themselves fulfilment must lie in perfect service in the air. With a calm and absolute courage more formidable than any fanaticism these young men soared against the invading bombers, and brought them down in thousands.</p>
<p>In passing I record one unusual qualification which the Tibetan government exacted of its young airmen. They must be married men. Further, none might go into action against the enemy unless he had a child, or his wife was pregnant. It even became a point of honour with these strange ‘aces’ not to take extreme risks until they had at least three children to their credit.</p>
<p>So effective was the defence put up by the Tibetan air force that the repeated waves of attack became more and more infrequent and finally ceased for several years. During this period the Tibetans maintained themselves in complete isolation from the rest of the world, save by radio and occasional daring excursions by planes to foment revolution or seize some much needed commodity. Meanwhile the imperialists were preparing so great an air-fleet and so numerous a population of pilots that effective resistance by the shrunken Tibetan air force would be impossible.</p>
<p>When the great attack was launched, the sky over Tibet was darkened by the invading bombers. Every town and village and all the great isolated monasteries were very soon destroyed. Lhasa, the spiritual heart of the country, was completely obliterated.</p>
<p>Watching these events from my look-out in the remote future, with superhuman intelligences as my fellow spectators, I might surely have been immune from human pity. But in fact compassion and admiration overwhelmed me. For here was a people most sensitive, most aware, the heirs and upholders of a most rare and glorious social fabric, a people rightly believing themselves to be the sole effective champions of the light in a darkened world. And all that they had built was being destroyed. Not only the loved temples of their faith, not only their precious houses of learning and all their instruments of economic production, were now being sacrificed, but also, and far more precious, their young people, the perfect fruit of all their past endeavour. Homes were broken up for ever, parents bereft, children orphaned, and lovers, seizing delight even under the wings of death, were suddenly mingled in a hideous and undesired union. By night the high clouds were lit up continuously by the flashes of guns and bombs and the sinister but lovely glow of the great fires. By night and by day the bombs still screamed and crashed, while men searched the wreckage for their companions. The Tibetans did not give way to self- pity. The prevailing temper was a devoted patriotism, which, like so many earlier patriotisms, but this time with justice, regarded the preservation of this nation and its culture as urgent for the well- being of humanity. At this stage of the war the population went about its work in a state of exaltation tempered by humour; with a sense that this was the supreme moment of mankind and a battle infinitely worth fighting, yet with surprisingly detached relish of the irony of Tibet’s plight.</p>
<p>The people now set about adapting themselves to their new conditions.</p>
<p>The country was large, and the population small. Agriculture, which had been so carefully fostered by the new régime, now ceased to be possible, for the homesteads were bombed and machine-gunned, and the dams of the great reservoirs were destroyed. But the yak remained; the population reverted to a nomad pastoral life. Wandering in small groups, pitching their camouflaged tents in fresh places every night, they offered a poor target to the enemy. Fortunately the imperialists at first made no attempt to land troops by plane, for they believed that the whole country was infected with the strange disease that had frustrated the first land attacks. The Tibetans, meanwhile, were hastily spreading the precious virus throughout their territory. Its effect was to eliminate all who did not attain the necessary standard of lucidity to resist infection. Only a small minority were thus put out of action. These were cared for in special homes. A much larger number, but still only a minority, suffered from temporary mild attacks of the disease. The virus was now also spreading itself beyond the frontiers. There, of course, its effects were incomparably worse. Organization in the infected areas completely vanished.</p>
