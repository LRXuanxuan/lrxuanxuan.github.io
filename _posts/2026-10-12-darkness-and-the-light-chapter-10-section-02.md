<h2>二、帷幕后面</h2>
<p>然而，这个和平发展、充满信心的时代并不会永远延续。最初的征兆，是前行者内部发生了一场危机。危机起初一直保密，但人们后来终于看出，一定发生了非常严重的事情。前行者显然受到了深深震动。原本留在静修所和静观院中的人纷纷涌入世界各地。他们照常旅行，也参加劳动，却始终处于一种焦虑而心不在焉的状态。偶然相遇时，他们不断进行私下讨论，也频繁召开事先安排好的会议，可讨论内容始终没有公开。最后，拉萨决定举行一次全球大会。几个月里，来自每座城市的大批前行者拥进神圣之城，并在周围乡野扎营。全体与会者花了数月讨论那个秘密问题，同时进行严酷的精神操练，以使自己具有作出正确判断的资格。在这期间，世界其余人口并没有表现出多大好奇心。生活中还有太多更有趣的事情。直到大会终于结束、前行者各自返回祖国以后，他们才向全世界人民发表一份宣言。普通世界公民读到它时极其震惊：前行者的朋友多半感到恐惧和沮丧，怀疑派则发出欢快的大笑，根本不肯相信。</p>
<p>像我这样来自较早时代、文化发展程度较低的人，只能以最表面的方式理解前行者究竟把经验领域拓展到了何等惊人的程度，也只能模糊理解如今即将强加给全人类的那个可怕选择。然而，这件事对人类生活产生了极其深远的影响。前行者的声明起初只被当作一种遥远而耸动的奇谈；可他们遍布每一个村庄，不断以自己的存在为其真实性作证，又持续把人们的注意力拉回它对于整个人类那种骇人的意义。于是，最初的不可置信一点一点转变成沉重而痛苦的接受。</p>
<p>这一新发现——如果它真的是发现——使人类意识越过了熟悉的物质现实，一步进入一种性质完全不同的存在领域。</p>
<p>很久以来，人类对物质宇宙以及物质宇宙内部心灵现象的认识都已经极其深入。例如，人们早已知道，其他太阳系的行星上也存在智慧种族。地球科学家已经开始探索太阳系的其他行星，相信开发这些世界会成为下一片伟大的人类事业疆域。他们说，总有一天，人类甚至可以尝试那场不可思议的长途航行，前往距离太阳最近的恒星；而如今已知，那颗恒星也有自己的行星。事实上，人类内部已经出现争论：一边是主张以地外冒险作为“人类进步”形式的浪漫派科学家；另一边则是“古典派”，他们坚持认为这种事业会使人类偏离真正任务，因为仅仅地球本身，就已经有多得足够整个人类永远忙下去的事情。古典派认为，无止境地提高感受力和智力，才是远比少年式星际旅行兴奋感、远比多余地去攫取遥远世界资源更配得上人类精神的事业。倘若可能，当然应该改善心灵感应，使人类能够轻松而有益地同远方智慧交流；但星际航行这种孩子气的梦想必须放弃。</p>
<p>另一场重大争论也正在浮现。一边仍是古典人文主义者，另一边则是优生主义者。后者主张，人类已经到了“接管自身进化”的时刻，应主动创造一种发展程度更高的新人类类型。他们相信，通过遗传控制，可以大幅扩大智力与感受力的范围。古典派对此回答说，这种鲁莽冒险可能会破坏整个物种的基本结构，并把世界那套已经久经检验的秩序拖入混乱。为了增强健康、延长寿命、延长心智成熟期，当然可以进行有限的优生学研究；但把人性改造成某种超人存在的幻想，绝不应当鼓励。</p>
<p>就在这两场大争论不再只是学术问题、而开始出现在实际政治地平线上时，前行者偶然撞见了那个发现——或者看似发现。如果它是真的，人类不仅必须放弃星际冒险和优生改造，甚至必须放弃古典人文主义本身。就我能够理解的范围而言，他们宣布的内容大致如下。</p>
<p>他们说，他们已经发现：我们熟悉的时空宇宙虽然绝不是幻觉或梦，却只是一个更深现实的表面。我们熟悉的自然法则，无论物理的还是心理的，都根本不是终极法则，只不过是在某个“局部”范围内，对更深层、此前从未被猜想到的法则如何表现出来的一种表面描述。柏拉图关于洞穴中影子的寓言，在这一点上竟有深刻的真实性。整个由群星与星系构成的宇宙，虽然完全真实、绝不是人类心灵的虚构，却也不过是被隐秘之风卷起、沿一片隐秘存在之海表面吹动的飞沫。科学曾经如此彻底探索过这个“飞沫宇宙”的规律，而这些规律在一定范围内确实具有一致性；但有些事物仅靠这些法则永远不可能得到充分描述，例如心灵，以及善与恶。前行者此前几个世纪持续工作，正是希望理解这些问题，而最重要的目标，则是接触那种隐秘的现实。</p>
<p>他们说，终于有一次，他们在短暂瞬间真正穿透到了更深的真相。人类第一次与那个更巨大、更真实的存在面对面。</p>
<p>然而，那次经验非但没有带来至福，反而恐怖至极。他们从那些无法言说的事实面前惊骇后退。作为光明的仆从、光明的孩子，他们竟发现：在自己的眼睛里，“光明”本身不过是主观幻象，就像一个人在黑暗中、或者闭上眼睛时视网膜自行产生的光点。他们曾有一瞬真正睁开眼睛，结果却只看见一层更深、更可怕的黑暗。或者，那甚至是某种比黑暗更糟糕的东西？</p>
<p>此前他们一路前进，从未怀疑这场冒险最终会让挣扎中的人类精神同一个更宏大、但本质上与之同类的精神现实发生交流，从而获得力量。许多代以来，大量伟大圣者和数以千计献身其中的追随者，都受这种希望驱使，经历严酷纪律的试炼之火，冒险进入陌生而冰冷的精神经验领域，收集着某种尚未显现的荣耀留下的迹象和暗示，并向自己的同类作证。如今，这笔巨大财富和更巨大希望的继承者，终于集中全部力量，向那扇紧锁的神秘之门发动最后进攻；他们撬开了门，却只来得及窥见一种不可理解的恐怖，随即惊惶后退。</p>
<p>在拉萨那场漫长会议中，前行者全体成员在精神领袖带领下，再一次鼓起勇气面对这个可怕真相，以确认其中是否存在错误。然而，他们再次遭遇了那个似乎已经终极的恐怖。经过长时间静观与讨论，他们终于作出决定，随后散向世界各地，把真相告诉这个渺小的人类种族，同时提出一种行动方针。</p>
<p>他们坚持说，自己的发现超出了人类语言的能力。它不可言说，只能借隐喻描述。他们原本寻找的是一种证据，想证明人类为光明而奋斗，同宇宙的根本精神彼此和谐；然而找到的却是一片巨大而幽暗的力量混沌，它不仅毫不在意人类的命运，也毫不在意人类经历如此痛苦才学会奉为神圣的一切事物。为了传达这项发现，他们创造了一个神话。这个神话本身虽然荒诞而渺小，他们却声称它确实传达了那种陌生、荒凉真相的本质。他们说，这个由星系和原子、爱情和仇恨以及种种斗争组成的宇宙，不过是一片正在融化的雪花，随时可能被彼此斗殴、对一切漠不关心的泰坦巨人一脚踩进泥浆。只有这样遥远而牵强的形象，才能多少表达他们所看到的事实。当然，这个比喻远远不够。那些从无形而不可穿透的黑夜中落下的“雪花”，并不是冰冷死物，而是温暖的，怀着生命与精神的潜能；它们融化，实际上就是死亡，就是生命能量的耗散。无穷无尽的雪花，每一片本身都是一个庞大的物质宇宙，它们摇晃着向下飘落，最后停在雪原上。前行者继续展开这个古怪神话，说那些“泰坦”的脚印，就是成千上万个宇宙被践踏、压碎、混成泥泞混沌的区域。毫无意义的斗殴每持续一刻，就造成新的毁灭。由宇宙组成的雪原越来越密集地被踩烂，就像一座城市一个月又一个月承受越来越猛烈的轰炸。就在任何一刻，我们自己这个由无数星系构成的宇宙，其基本物理结构和物质本身都可能被打成混沌，于是其中所有脆弱的智慧世界都将在一瞬间消失。他们强调，这件事任何时候都可能发生。事实上，它居然直到现在还没有发生，本身就近乎奇迹。</p>
<p>前行者说，他们曾不断向上窥望——姑且这样形容——试图从那些狂乱挥舞的泰坦肢体之间找到天上的光，可唯一的亮光却只在地面。仿佛正是那些雪花本身，拥挤成一片正在消融的雪原，并在持续死亡的过程中发出微弱磷光。他们继续沿用这个怪异比喻——并反复声明，比喻几乎完全不足以描述那些不可说的事实——说每一个雪花宇宙散发出的微弱而弥漫的光，如果放大到显微尺度观察，就会分解成无数瞬间闪烁的亮点；每一个亮点，都是某个短命世界在精神清明达到顶峰时发出的一次辉煌闪光。头顶上却只有足以令人失明的黑暗；所有雪花都从那里摇摆着落向地面。</p>
<p>前行者只能借这幅荒凉图景表达自己新的、令人恐惧的视野。他们也讨论这个令人厌恶的真相意味着什么，以及人类应采取怎样的政策。所有人一致宣称，他们仍然忠于精神。他们说：“每一个人都能在自己的经验里知道，爱与智慧的生活是绝对的善，是任何觉醒存在唯一真正令人满足的生活。无论我们对终极现实的本质作出多么毁灭性的发现，也无法动摇这种直接知觉。因此，无论前景如何，人类都将继续在地球上为爱与智慧而奋斗。但若假装我们的形而上发现什么都没有改变，那就是愚蠢。过去，我们似乎即将接触一切精神那赋予生命、鼓舞心灵的源泉；如今我们找到的只有荒凉。”</p>
<p>不过，前行者并没有就此结束。他们还提出了一种希望，以及相应的行动方针。希望首先来自一个事实：无论如何，这场由物质宇宙、也就是潜在精神宇宙组成的“暴雪”总得来自某个地方。“泰坦”并不是全部终极现实。因此，也许进一步的纪律与静观终究能够让人穿透天空彻底的黑暗，最后真正与光明面对面。</p>
<p>他们说，希望甚至可以进行一次更高、虽也更危险的飞跃。因为最娴熟的少数前行者声称，在自己最清澈的时刻，他们似乎看见了更多东西。他们看到：雪花宇宙以及其中意识生命的存在虽然极其不稳定，可一旦这些有意识的存在达到成熟的精神高度，便会获得极其强大的力量。那些最先深入的前行者声称，用这个并不充分的比喻来说，他们有时看见某一片雪花内部突然爆发出短暂却耀眼的辉光，像一颗新星闪耀。那场精神之火有时会明亮到照亮整个辽阔雪原。每当如此，那些“泰坦”仿佛被突如其来的光吓坏，纷纷朝远离光源的方向逃散。其中有些甚至被光辉直接消灭，就像夜的阴影在日出前消失一样。于是，任何智慧世界正确的道路显然都是努力达到这种精神光辉。也显然，唯有它能够战胜“泰坦”。最可爱、最珍贵的事物平日看起来如此脆弱，可一旦发展成熟，竟也是一切力量中最强大的。但这种强到能够毁灭“泰坦”的力量，不可能来自少数孤立探索的个人；它必须来自整个种族、整个有意识的世界，甚至整个宇宙，在最亲密的精神共同体中联合起来。而如果没有整个种族最彻底的献身，这种力量便绝无可能达到。</p>
<p>于是，前行者向人类提出了挑战。这是一声行动号召。它要求全世界每一个人都完全为了共同任务而活，放弃除精神之外的一切；不仅要抛弃世俗目标，还要抛弃科学、艺术和智力探索的虚荣；甚至必须使自己彻底摆脱个人爱情那温柔的束缚；必须停止生育；必须把整个人类种族的能量一滴不剩地榨出，全部投入这项至高的精神任务。</p>
<p>在此以前，人类面前曾有两条可能的道路。一条是浪漫派科学家的理想：发展各行星系统之间的联系，最终建立遍及整个银河的智慧世界共同体。另一条则坚持认为人的真正事业永远应该围绕人本身，目标是按照古典理想深入发展人类现有的家园和文化。</p>
<p>如今，第三条而且更加革命性的道路打开了。对于一片身处斗殴“泰坦”之间的雪花里的居民而言，这似乎是唯一合理的政策：进行一场英雄式冒险，牺牲一切，尝试以人类精神的清明摧毁那些“泰坦”。</p>
</section>

<h2>ii. BEHIND THE VEIL</h2>
<p>BUT THIS AGE of peaceful development and confidence was not to last for ever. The first symptom was a crisis among the forwards. This crisis was at first kept secret, but in time it became clear that something grave was afoot. The forwards were evidently deeply disturbed. Those that were in the hostels and houses of contemplation came pouring out into the world. They travelled and took up work, but they lived in a state of anxious abstraction. There were endless private discussions during casual encounters, and many prearranged conferences, the subject of which was never disclosed. At last a world conference was arranged at Lhasa. For many months hosts of forwards from every city crowded the sacred city, and camped in the surrounding country. Several months were spent by the assembled forwards in discussing their secret problem and performing severe spiritual exercises in order to fit themselves for right judgment. During this period the rest of the world showed little curiosity. Life was far too full of more interesting matters. When at last the conference had ended and the forwards had returned to their home countries, a manifesto was issued to the peoples of the world. Its content was greeted by ordinary world-citizens with astonishment, varying from dismay among the friends of the forwards to hilarious incredulity among the sceptics.</p>
<p>It was not possible to me, a creature of an earlier age and a less developed culture, to understand save in the most superficial way the immense expansion of experience which the forwards had achieved, and the terrible choice which was now to be forced upon the human race. But the effect on the life of the race was far-reaching. Although the statement of the forwards was at first treated as merely remote sensationalism, their presence in every village, bearing witness to its truth and constantly directing men’s attention to its dreadful significance for the human race, gradually turned incredulity into heavy-hearted acceptance.</p>
<p>The new discovery, if such it was, carried human consciousness beyond the familiar physical actuality, and opened up in one stride a sphere of existence which was of an entirely different order.</p>
<p>Man’s knowledge both of the physical cosmos and of mentality within the physical cosmos had for long been very far-reaching. It was known, for instance, that there were other intelligent races on planets belonging to other solar systems. Already the scientists of the earth had turned their attention to exploring our own sun’s other planets, believing that in the exploitation of these globes lay the next great field of human enterprise. Some day, they said, it would be possible even to attempt the immense journey to the sun’s nearest stellar neighbour, which was now known to have attendant planets. Indeed there was already a dispute between the romantic enthusiasts for ‘human advancement’ in the form of extraterrestrial ventures and the ‘classicists’ who insisted that any such enterprise would distract man from his proper task, since here on earth there was far more than enough to occupy the race. The endless refinement of sensibility and intelligence, they affirmed, offered a task far more worthy of the human spirit than the schoolboy’s excitement of interplanetary travel, and the unnecessary attempt to tap the resources of remote worlds. By all means let telepathic communication be improved, if possible, so that man could communicate easily and profitably with remote intelligences, but the childish dream of interstellar travel must be abandoned.</p>
<p>Another great dispute was also coming to the fore, namely between the classical humanists and the eugenists, who urged that the time had come for man to ‘take charge of his own evolution’ and create a new and more highly developed human type. They believed that by genetic control the range of intelligence and sensibility could be immensely increased. To this the classicists replied that any such rash adventure might undermine the constitution of the race and bring chaos into the well-tried order of the world. By all means let minor eugenical researches be carried out for increased health, longevity, and the prolongation of mental maturity, but the hope of transforming human nature into something superhuman must not be entertained.</p>
<p>At the time when these two great disputes were ceasing to be merely academic, and were actually appearing over the horizon of practical politics, the forwards stumbled upon the discovery, or seeming discovery, which, if true, must force the abandonment, not only of interstellar adventure and of eugenical improvement, but also of classical humanism itself. The announcement which they made, so far as I could comprehend it, was to this effect.</p>
<p>They had discovered, they said, that the universe of familiar space and time, though no mere illusion or dream, was but the surface of a deeper reality. The familiar natural laws, both physical and psychological, were not fundamental laws at all, but superficial descriptions of the ‘local’ incidence of deeper and hitherto unguessed laws. Plato’s parable of the shadow figures cast by unseen persons and an unseen source of light was to this extent profoundly true. The whole universe of stars, of galaxies, though fully actual and no mere figment of man’s mind, was but spindrift caught up by occult winds and driven along the surface of an occult ocean of existence. The laws of this spindrift universe, which science had so thoroughly explored, were up to a point coherent; but certain things could never be adequately described in terms of these laws alone, for instance mind, and good and evil. It was in the hope of gaining insight into these matters, but above all in order to have access to the occult reality, that the forwards had been working during the preceding centuries.</p>
<p>At last, they said, they had momentarily penetrated to the deeper truth. They had for the first time come face to face with the vaster real.</p>
<p>But the experience, far from being beatific, had been terrible. They had recoiled in horror from the unspeakable facts. Servants of the light, children of the light, they had discovered that the light itself in their own eyes was but a subjective figment, like the retinal lights that a man sees in the dark, or when his eyes are closed. For a moment they had succeeded in opening their eyes, but only to discover a deeper and more formidable darkness. Or was it something worse than darkness?</p>
<p>They had pressed forward thus far without any doubt that their venture would lead to a fortifying of the struggling human spirit by intercourse with a vaster but essentially kindred spiritual reality. Over a period of many generations many great saints and thousands of devoted followers, spurred by this hope, had passed through the testing fires of discipline, had ventured into strange and icy spheres of spiritual experience, had gathered signs and intimations of a glory still to be revealed, had borne witness to their fellow men. And now at last the heirs to all this great treasure and greater promise, having gathered all their strength for the final assault on the locked door of mystery, had prized it open only to glimpse an incomprehensible horror, and to fall back in dismay.</p>
<p>During the long conference in Lhasa the whole population of forwards, assembled under their spiritual leaders, dared once more to face the terrible truth, lest there should have been some mistake. But once more they encountered the seemingly ultimate horror. After long contemplation and discussion they came to a decision, and then dispersed to tell the little human race the truth, and to suggest a course of action.</p>
<p>Their discovery, they insisted, transcended the Powers of human language. It was ineffable. It could be described only in metaphor. They had been seeking, they said, evidence that man’s struggle for the light was in harmony with the essential spirit of the universe They had found instead a vast and obscure confusion of powers, careless not only of man’s fate but of all that he had so painfully learned to hold sacred. To communicate their discovery they conceived a myth which, though fantastic and petty, did, they affirmed, convey the essence of the strange and desolate truth. This universe, they said, of galaxies and atoms, of loves and hates and strifes, is no more than a melting snowflake which at any moment may be trampled into the slush by indifferent and brawling titans. Not otherwise than in this far- fetched image, they said, could they express the truth that they had seen. It was an inadequate image; for these snowflakes, descending from the formless and impenetrable blackness of the night sky, were indeed not frozen but warm with the potentiality of life and of spirit, and their thawing was in truth a dying, a dissipation of their vital energy. Myriad upon myriad of these snowflakes, each one a great physical cosmos, faltered downwards and rested on the field of snow. The footmarks of the ‘titans’, the forwards affirmed, developing the strange myth, were areas where thousands of these universes had been crushed together into a muddy chaos. Every moment, as the meaningless brawl continued, new devastations were inflicted. The snowfield of. universes was more and more closely trampled, like a city more and more bombed, month by month. At any moment the fundamental physical structure and substance of our own many-galaxied cosmos might be reduced to chaos, so that in a flash all its frail intelligent worlds would vanish. At any moment, they insisted, this might happen. Indeed, that it had not already happened, seemed to be a miracle.</p>
<p>The forwards affirmed that they had peered and peered upwards (so to speak) between the rioting titanic limbs in search of the celestial light; but the only luminosity was on the ground. It was all though the flakes themselves, congested into a thawing snowfield, created in their constant dying a dim phosphorescence. Pursuing this strange metaphor, which (they reiterated) was almost wholly inadequate to the unspeakable facts, they declared that the faint, diffused glow emitted by each separate snowflake universe, resolved itself in closer, microscopic inspection, into a myriad instantaneous scintillations, each one a short-lived world’s bright climax of spiritual lucidity. Overhead there was nothing but the blinding darkness, whence ‘the flakes vacillated groundwards.</p>
<p>Such was the bleak image by which the forwards tried to express their new and dreadful vision. They also discussed the implications of the repugnant truth, and the policy which the human race should adopt towards it. One and all, they affirmed their continued loyalty to the spirit. ‘Every man,’ they said, ‘knows in his own experience that the life of love and of intelligence is good absolutely, is the only satisfying life for awakened beings. No devastating discovery about the nature of the ultimate reality can shake that immediate perception. Therefore, whatever the prospect, the human race will continue the struggle for love and intelligence here on earth. But it would be foolish to pretend that our metaphysical discovery makes no difference. Formerly it seemed that man would soon make contact with the life-giving and enheartcning source of all spirit. We have found only desolation.’</p>
<p>But the forwards did not leave matters thus. They suggested also a hope and a policy. The hope lay in the fact that, after all, the snowstorm of physical and potentially spiritual universes must come from somewhere. The ‘titans’ were not the whole ultimate reality. And so it might after all be that further discipline and contemplation might enable man to penetrate the utter blackness of the sky and come at last face to face with the true light.</p>
<p>Hope, they said, might even permit itself a higher though a precarious flight. For some of the most adept forwards had claimed that in their most lucid moments they had seen something more. They had seen that in spite of the precarious existence of the snowflake universes and of the conscious beings within them, these beings themselves, when they attained mature spiritual stature, acquired very formidable powers. The pioneering forwards claimed that, in terms of the inadequate image, they had sometimes seen a brief but dazzling effulgence blaze up within some snowflake, like the brilliance of a new star. So brilliant might this conflagration be that it illuminated the whole wide snowfield. When this happened, the ‘titans’, seemingly terrified by the sudden light, fled in all directions, away from its source. Some of them were even annihilated by the radiance, like the shades of night at sunrise. Clearly, then, the right course for every intelligent world was to strive for that brilliance of the spirit. Clearly this alone could overcome the ‘titans’. Clearly what was most lovely and precious, though commonly so frail, was also, in the fullness of its growth, the mightiest power of all. But this power, intensified to such a pitch that it could destroy the ‘titans’, was not the power of a few individuals exploring in isolation; it was the power of a whole race, of a whole conscious world, perhaps of a whole cosmos, united in most intimate spiritual communion. And such power was not to be attained without the utmost racial dedication.</p>
<p>Hence arose the challenge which the forwards laid before mankind. It was a call to action. It was a call to all individuals throughout the world to live wholly for the common task, to give up everything but the spirit, to discard not only mundane ends but also the vanity of science and art and intellectual exploration, to detach themselves absolutely even from the gentle bondage of personal love, to refrain from procreation, to drain the whole energy of the race to the last drop for the supreme spiritual task.</p>
<p>Hitherto there had been two possible ventures open to the human race. One was the romantic scientist’s ideal of developing communication between the planetary systems, so as to create a galaxy-wide community of intelligent worlds. The other, which assumed that man’s proper business must always be with man, was the classical aim of the intensive development of man’s present home and culture.</p>
<p>A third and more revolutionary policy was now open. For the inhabitants of a snowflake among brawling ‘titans’, it was the sole reasonable policy. This was the heroic venture of sacrificing everything in the attempt to destroy the ‘titans’ with the lucidity of the human spirit.</p>
