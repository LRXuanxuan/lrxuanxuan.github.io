<h2>v. 降入第一人类之中</h2>
<p>在这次冒险的整个早期阶段，我已经不断试图从混乱经验中筛选出某种东西，好把自己引向人类历程中属于你们的那个特定阶段。我前面已经说过，我让自己朝你们时代靠近的主要方法，是在想象中尽可能精确地采用：首先，你们物种最基本的气质模式；其次，你们这个时期所特有的那种具体形式；最后，是我希望研究的那个独一无二的个体，在某一特定地点与时刻所具有的精神形态。我当时已经开始做这项工作；但和大多数时候一样，那次也受到我前面试图描述的那股感知、思想与情绪洪流的严重干扰。我像一个被巨浪反复卷走的游泳者，明明知道自己的避风港就在某个岩石小湾里，却几乎怎么也游不到那里。</p>
<p>首先，我必须设想并在情感上采取所有原始人类心灵的一般形式，从视野中排除我自己的整个物种，也排除人类离开地球前夕曾短暂出现、却最终夭折的另一次精神成熟阶段。我必须在想象中把自己变成一个困惑而受限的存在：不了解自己的本性；不断遭到自身无法理解的内部冲突折磨；永远渴望成为一个完整的人，却又不断被眼前冲动卷走，而那些冲动甚至在它自己看来都显得可笑。</p>
<p>经过耐心练习，我终于把这种内在“角色扮演”做到足够成功，于是开始短暂而清晰地看见原始人类各个类型中的个体与事件：有时，我是一名有翼的金星人，掠过狂野海洋；有时，我看见一个红润而灵活的第三人类女子给自己的兽类喂食；又有时，我看见海王星上一名几乎已经称不上人类的早期居民。当然，我每次都是透过某个同时代人的眼睛看见这些个体；而只要我在这些经验中偶尔低头看一眼“自己”的身体，映入眼中的便绝不是我熟悉的身体，而是与被观察者属于同一物种的身体。透过过去存在的眼睛去感知时，我也会把他们的身体一个接一个当成自己的身体来感知。</p>
<p>接下来，我必须从所有原始形态中挑出你们物种独有的那一种。它的特征包括：受到压抑的性欲，过度膨胀的自我关切，以及一种既处于初级阶段、又受无序欲望奴役的智力。你们这个物种无论处在哪个时期、属于哪个种族，虽然彼此差别很大，却都体现出这些因素的一种独特混合。不过，在想象中采用这种心灵模式是危险的，因为在海王星上、我们之前的某个人类物种，在这方面竟与你们异常相似。</p>
<p>于是，我像从前许多次那样，开始痛苦地在这两个人类阶段之间来回摇摆。我一会儿看见地球的山丘和高大树木，一会儿又看见海王星的平原与灌木；一会儿拥有地球人蜘蛛般细长的四肢和轻快步态，一会儿又换上海王星早期人类象腿般粗壮的双腿、袋鼠似的手臂与庄重姿态。我刚听了一会儿阿提卡式的辩论，下一瞬便突然只剩原始海王星人的咕哝和咔哒声。这就像用一台选择性很差的无线电收音机调台。</p>
<p>或者换一个比喻：我像一只被暴风吹得东倒西歪的鵟，盘旋在群山上空；脚下是一道分水岭，两边各有一座山谷。我本来打算降进其中一座，可风总把我推回山脊另一侧。某一刻，想去的山谷就在脚下；下一刻，白费一番挣扎后，眼前又变成另一座。最后，我终于以极强的注意力强迫自己集中于你们这个物种，把另一个排除出去。渐渐地，灌入我的全部意象与思想，都只来自第一人类的各个历史阶段。</p>
<p>但我的经验依然十分混乱。我确实已经形成一种强烈却模糊的整体感，仿佛把你们这个物种看成一个历史整体：其中有许多漫长的沉睡期，只有极少数短暂耀眼的时刻。仔细观察后，我还察觉到一种宇宙悲剧感；当你们更有智慧的后代终于意识到自己的物种不可避免地开始衰退时，这种感觉将把他们压倒。不过在绝大多数时候，我遭遇的仍只是来自你们全部时代的随机样本，令人眩晕地混杂在一起。</p>
<p>于是，某一刻我成了一个奥里尼亚克时代的人，正在洞壁上刻画鹿与野牛的鲜活形象，同伴猎人从烟雾后面赞叹地看着。下一刻，我又成了未来那个已经被美国化的世界国家中的一名中国公民，用一种磨损、污染后的英语和苏丹裔妻子说话。接着我又成了伊丽莎白时代的一位贵妇，腰被紧身衣勒得极细，浑身堆满珠宝。突然之间，我又闪到了几万年以后的巴塔哥尼亚文明——那是你们这个物种最后一次疲惫不堪的努力。我成了一个过早衰老的男孩，俯伏在神庙里，拜倒在一尊怪诞的“权力”偶像前。</p>
<p>直到又有许多这样的幻象出现并被我舍弃，我才终于大致进入你们的时代。我成了一个印度人，面对一个戴遮阳帽、露着膝盖的英国人。我的心里同时有愤怒、恐惧、轻蔑与伪装；而我对他的理解，就和他对我的理解一样少得可怜。</p>
<p>我已经疲惫不堪，却终于更有希望，于是严格强迫自己进入一种在原始工业社会大多数成员中共同存在的心态。对你们而言，这种奇特经验实在太熟悉，所以你们很难意识到它其实多么罕见、多么古怪，虽然你们中也有许多人隐约觉得，这种状态既不健康，也绝非不可避免。为了让你们明白，我当时必须付出多么猛烈的想象努力和情绪控制，我要列出几项使“原始工业心态”对探索者格外难以进入的主要特征。</p>
<p>继续使用前面的比喻：那只鵟虽然终于成功降进自己选中的山谷，却又必须钻进一座废弃老矿井的通风竖井，在黑暗和腐臭空气中不断下降，狭窄得几乎连翅膀都展不开。难怪在最后落入矿井底部那些苍白、无眼的动物之间以前，它会遭受严重痛苦，也会冒相当大的风险；难怪它自己的眼睛也需要很长时间，才能适应那个世界唯一的微弱磷光。</p>
<p>探索者若想把自己安置进你们的社会，首先就必须采用一种震颤不安、永远无法安定的情绪状态；凡是生活环境与自身天生能力不相适应的生物，都容易具有这种状态。更具体地说，他必须在想象中制造出一种伴随终身的紧张：眼睛每天看见的是街道、交通和空气污浊的室内空间，而不是最初塑造人类感官能力的自然景观。他还必须在自己内部重新建造一种无意识的“物质迷恋”——或者更准确地说，是对通过机械与化学手段控制物质的迷恋。他也必须设想，人类心灵如何在自己制造出来的机器人后代面前不自觉地低头，并对自身产生怀疑。</p>
<p>此外，他还必须让原本自由游荡的精神承受狭窄专业化对身体和心灵造成的可怕后果；必须设想一种几乎难以想象的无知与摸索式仇恨——一个人的整个生命若被塑造成极狭窄的一圈职责与感知，他便可能变成这样。同样，他还必须设想原始工业社会的主人阶层那种深刻却不自知的罪责感，也必须设想漂浮在一切原始工业社会表面的那层无所事事的浮沫。</p>
<p>一点一点地，我终于在自己内部复制出这种逼仄、吝啬的心灵模式。与此同时，你们世界的景象越来越清晰地蜂拥而来。我成了你们工业革命时期煤矿里的一个半裸女人，四肢着地爬过低矮矿道，像牲口一样用项圈拖着一辆矿车，牵引带从两腿之间穿过。我低声呜咽，却不是因为眼前身体有什么特别痛苦，而只是因为丢了一件纪念品。这个片段显然比我想找的时代早了至少一个世纪；但更多景象随即出现，而且越来越多确实属于你们的时期。</p>
<p>接下来，我必须从你们之中寻找某一个具体的人，并找到他生命中的某一个具体时刻。可就在这个过程中，我差点遭遇灾难。金星上曾经有过——从你们的视角说，是将来会有——一个工业阶段，在某些方面与你们非常相似，虽然创造那个社会的物种与你们完全不同。也不知道究竟是哪个细节的相似触发了联想，我突然被一下抛进那个时代。一个显然不属于第一人类的面孔缓慢朝我做出怪相：它苍白、频频眨眼，像一只青蛙；左眼下面有一道不断渗液的溃疡；头上还歪戴着一顶向后翘起的荒诞帽子。</p>
<p>那一刻我极其不安，生怕自己会被困在金星人类的那个阶段里，而我的心灵却仍按照第一人类的模式塑形。于是我拼命把注意力集中在地球式经验上，终于从这条危险的岔路里脱身。下一瞬，我猛地一跃，又回到了你们历史中我真正想要抵达的那个点上。</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" class="stapledon-body" lang="en" hidden>
<h2>v. DESCENT AMONG THE FIRST MEN</h2>
<p>DURING THE WHOLE of this early stage of my adventure I was already trying to select from the confusion of experiences something which might serve to bring me nearer to your own particular phase of the human process. My chief method of directing myself toward your age is, as I have already said, to assume imaginatively, and as precisely as possible, first the basic temperamental pattern of your species, and then the particular form of it which is characteristic of your period, and of that unique point and moment of individuality which I desire to study. This work I was already attempting; but it was hampered on this occasion, and on most others, by the prodigious influx of perceptions, thoughts and emotions which I have attempted to describe. I was like a swimmer swept hither and thither by great waves, and scarcely able to make the particular rocky creek which is his haven.</p>
<p>I had first to conceive, and emotionally assume, the general form of all primitive human mentality, so as to exclude from my view the whole of my own species, and also that other, abortive, phase of spiritual maturity, which took place just before man left the earth. I had to become in imagination a bewildered limited being, ignorant of its own nature, ever racked by inner conflicts which eluded its apprehension, ever yearning to be whole, yet ever carried away by momentary cravings which were ludicrous even in its own estimation. With patience I was able to achieve this piece of inner play-acting with such success that I began to have definite but fleeting glimpses of individuals and incidents among the primitive kinds of men, now a winged Venerian, skimming his wild ocean, now a woman of the Third Men, ruddy and lithe, feeding her beasts, now a scarcely human early inhabitant of Neptune. These individuals I perceived, of course, through the eyes of some contemporary; and if ever I troubled to look at ‘my own’ body during these experiences, I perceived it to be not my familiar body at all, but of the same species as the observed individual. Perceiving through the eyes of past beings, I perceived their bodies as though they were each in turn my own body.</p>
<p>I had now to select that mode of the primitive which is distinctive of your own species, a mode characterized by repressed sexuality, excessive self-regard, and an intelligence which is both rudimentary and in bondage to unruly cravings. Now though all the races of your species in all its stages manifest, in spite of their differences, one unique blend of these factors, yet the process of imaginatively assuming this mode of mentality is dangerous; for there is another species, one of our fore-runners on Neptune, which is strangely like you in this respect. I now found myself, as often before, oscillating painfully between these two phases of man’s career. I saw now the hills and tall trees of Earth, now the plains and bushes of Neptune. I assumed now the spidery limbs and jaunty gait of a Terrestrial, now the elephantine legs, kangaroo arms and grave demeanour of an early Neptunian. I listened for a moment to Attic disputation, then suddenly to primitive Neptunian grunts and clicks. It was as though I were listening-in with a radio set of bad selectivity. Or, to change the image, I was a storm-tossed buzzard among the hills, beneath me a dividing range, on either side of it a valley. Into one of these valleys I purposed to descend, but the wind kept thrusting me back over the ridge into the other. At one moment I saw beneath me the desired valley; then, after a vain struggle, the other. At length, however, by a violent effort of attention I succeeded in concentrating upon your species and avoiding the other. Gradually the imagery and thought which flooded in on me came to be entirely derived from the First Men, in one or other of their phases.</p>
<p>But my experience was still chaotic. I had indeed a strong but vague apprehension of your species as a historic whole, of its many long phases of somnolence and its few brief effulgences. And after careful inspection I detected that sense of cosmical tragedy which overwhelmed, or, from your point of view, will overwhelm, the more intelligent of your descendants, when they realize that their species is setting inevitably into decline. But for the most part I had merely a sickening confusion of random samples from all your ages. Thus, at one moment I was an Aurignacian, engraving his cave wall with vivid shapes of deer and bison, while my fellow hunters peered with admiration through the smoke. Now I was a Chinese citizen of that Americanized world-state which lies future to you; and I was speaking in worn and polluted English to my Soudanese wife. Now I was an Elizabethan dame, tight-laced and plastered with jewellery. Suddenly I flashed many thousands of years ahead into that Patagonian civilization which is the last weary effort of your species. I was a prematurely aged boy, prostrate in a temple before the grotesque image of Power. Not until many more of these visions had appeared, and been dismissed, did I find myself approximately in your age. I was an Indian confronting a topeed, bare-kneed Englishman. Rage was in my heart, fear, contempt and subterfuge, and as little understanding of the other as he had of me.</p>
<p>Fatigued but now more hopeful, I forced myself rigorously into the mentality which is common to most members of a primitive industrialistic community. To you this curious mode of experience is so familiar that you do not suspect its rarity and oddity, though many of you feel that it is neither wholesome nor inevitable. That you may realize the violent effort of imagination and emotional control that was now incumbent on me, I will set down the main features which render the primitive industrial mentality so difficult for the explorer. To revert to the image which I have already used, it is as though the buzzard, having successfully descended into the valley of his choice, were now to enter the ventilation shaft of an old disused mine, and were to sink downwards in the dark through fetid air, with scarcely room to keep his wings outspread. No wonder that he suffers considerable distress and incurs serious dangers, before he finally alights amongst the pale and eyeless fauna of the pit. No wonder that his own eyes take time to accustom themselves to the dim phosphorescence which is the sole illumination of that world. When the explorer seeks to establish himself in your society, he has first to assume that jarred and restless mood which is common to all those whose habitual environment is alien to their native capacities. In particular he must imaginatively produce in himself the lifelong strain of perceiving streets, traffic, and stuffy interiors, instead of the landscapes which originally moulded his capacities. He must also reconstruct in himself the unconscious obsession with matter, or rather with the control of matter by machinery and chemical manipulation. He must conceive also the mind’s unwitting obeisance and self-distrust before its robot offspring. Further, he must subject his own free-roaming spirit to the hideous effects of a narrow specialism, both of body and mind. He must conceive the almost inconceivable ignorance and groping hate of the being whose life is moulded to one narrow round of duties and perceptions. Equally he must conceive the profound unwitting guiltiness of the primitive industrial master, and also of that idle scum that floats on the surface of all primitive industrial societies.</p>
<p>Little by little I reproduced in myself this cramped and ungenerous mode of the mind; and, as I did so, visions of your world came crowding in upon me with increasing vividness. I became a half-naked woman in a coal-mine of your Industrial Revolution. I crept on hands and knees along a low gallery. Tugging at the collar like a beast, I dragged a truck. The trace passed between my legs. I was whimpering, not from any present discomfort, but because I had lost a keepsake. This incident was obviously a century or more too early, but others followed, and more and more of them were of your period. I had now to seek out a particular individual among you, and a particular moment of his life. But in doing so, I nearly suffered catastrophe. On Venus there was once, or rather from your point of view there will be, an industrial phase much like your own in certain respects, although the species which produced it was very different from yours. Into that epoch I was suddenly flung by I know not what detail of identity. A face which was obviously not of the First Men, a pale and blinking frog-like face, with a running sore beneath the left eye and a fantastic hat cocked backwards, slowly grimaced at me. For one anxious moment I feared that I should be trapped in that Venerian phase of man, with my mind still moulded to the mentality of the First Men. But by a desperate effort of concentration upon the Terrestrial mode of experience I managed to disengage myself from this dangerous irrelevance. And with one leap I found myself back again at the desired point of your history.</p>
