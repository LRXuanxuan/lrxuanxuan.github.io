<p>我在山坡上醒来。郊区的街灯比群星更加明亮。</p>
<p>钟声余响未散，随后又连续响了十一下。我认出了我们家的那扇窗。一阵喜悦——狂烈的喜悦——像浪潮一样席卷全身。然后，一切归于平静。</p>
<p>地球上的事件如此渺小，却又如此强烈！那超宇宙的实在、那喷涌出一切创造的狂野泉源、那漫天飞溅的世界水珠，全都消失了，在一瞬间被彻底抹去；它们消失，转化成幻想，也转化成一种崇高的无关紧要。</p>
<p>这整粒岩石是如此渺小，却又如此强烈：表面覆着海洋与空气的薄膜，又覆着一层断断续续、斑斓多样、不断颤动的生命薄膜；还有幽暗的丘陵、朦胧而没有地平线的大海、像造父变星一样脉动的灯塔、铿锵作响的铁路货车。我的手抚过石南粗糙而令人愉快的触感。</p>
<p>超宇宙的幻象消失了。真正的实在并不像我曾梦想的那样，而是无限地更加精微、更加可怖、更加卓越，也无限地更贴近我们的家园。</p>
<p>然而，无论那幅异象在结构细节上有多么虚假，甚至无论它的整体形式是否也是虚假的，它的精神气质至少必定与真实有关；在气质上，它也许甚至就是真实的。真正的实在本身，想必正是推动我构想出那幅形象的力量；那形象的每一个主题、每一个侧面也许都错了，可它的精神却是真的。</p>
<p>群星在街灯上方苍白地颤动。它们是巨大的太阳吗？还是夜空中微弱的火星？据模糊的传闻说，那是太阳。至少，它们是可以为航向指路的光，也是在尘世纷扰之外召唤心灵的光；然而，它们冰冷的长矛也刺穿人的心。</p>
<p>我坐在石南丛中，坐在我们这粒行星微尘上，面对四面八方以及未来敞开的深渊，不由得退缩。沉默的黑暗、毫无特征的未知，比想象曾召集起来的一切恐怖都更加可怕。心灵竭力窥视，却看不见任何确凿之物；全部人类经验中，除了不确定本身，竟没有任何东西可以被握住、被认作确定。四周只有浓厚理论迷雾制造出的晦暗。人的科学，不过是一团数字之雾；人的哲学，也不过是一片词语之雾。就连人对这颗岩石微粒以及其上一切奇迹的感知，也只是一个不断变化、不断欺骗的幻象。甚至“自己”——这个看似位于一切中心的事实——也不过是幽灵：它如此善于欺骗，以至于最诚实的人也必须怀疑自己的诚实；它又如此缺乏实体，以至于人甚至必须怀疑自身是否真正存在。还有我们的忠诚！如此自欺，如此建立在错误信息和错误理解上；又被追求得如此野蛮，被仇恨扭曲得如此严重！甚至我们的爱，即使它充分而慷慨地亲密，也必须被指责为盲目、自我中心、自我陶醉。然而——真的如此吗？我重新认出我们家的窗。我们曾经一起幸福过！我们已经发现，或者已经创造了，属于我们自己的那一点共同体珍宝。在全部经验翻涌的混乱中，这就是唯一一块岩石。不是天文学和超宇宙尺度的浩瀚，也甚至不是这粒行星微尘；只有这个，这个本身，才是存在的坚实地面。四面八方都是混乱，一场风暴正在升起，巨浪已经开始扑湿我们的岩石。而在周围黑暗翻腾的浪涛里，是一张张若隐若现、转瞬消失的脸，是一只只伸来求援、又很快消失的手。</p>
<p>那么未来呢？它被这个世界不断升起的疯狂风暴染得漆黑，却又被一种崭新而猛烈的希望不时照亮——对一个健全、理性、更加幸福世界的希望。从我们的时代通向那个未来，中间还埋伏着多少恐怖？压迫者不会温顺退让。而我们两个人，只习惯了安全与温和，只适合生活在一个仁慈的世界里：在那里，没有人遭受折磨，也就没有人被逼到绝望。我们只适应晴朗天气，只适合在一个安全而公正的社会中践行那些友善、却并不艰难、更谈不上英雄主义的德性。可我们偏偏置身于一个巨人冲突的时代：无情的黑暗力量，与由于绝望而同样无情的光明力量，正在这个被撕裂世界的心脏里彼此扭打，展开一场你死我活的斗争；一场危机接着一场危机，每一次都必须作出严峻选择，而任何简单、熟悉的原则都已经不足够。</p>
<p>在我们的河口之外，一座铸造厂喷起一丛红色火焰。近处，荆豆丛黑暗的轮廓给这片被郊区居民踩出无数足迹的荒原增添了神秘。</p>
<p>在想象中，我看见我们所在山丘顶部后方那些更远、肉眼看不见的山。我看见平原和树林，看见所有田地，而每一块田里又有亿万片独一无二的草叶。我看见整片陆地从脚下向远方弯曲，沿行星的肩膀沉落下去。村庄被道路、钢铁轨道与嗡鸣电线织成的网彼此串联，像蛛网上的一颗颗雾珠。这里那里，一座城镇显现为一片光的铺展，一团星云般的亮雾，其中又散落着星星点点的灯火。</p>
<p>越过平原，霓虹灯照亮、沸腾不休的伦敦，像一块从污水中取样制成的显微镜玻片，挤满四处探寻的微小生物。微小生物！从群星看来，这些生命无疑只是害虫；然而对每一个自身而言，而且有时对彼此而言，它们都比所有星星更加真实。</p>
<p>越过伦敦向远方凝望，想象辨认出海峡朦胧的水带，随后是整个欧洲——一幅耕地与沉睡工业拼成的斑驳图案。越过遍布白杨的诺曼底，巴黎铺展开来；由于地球表面的弯曲，圣母院的双塔仿佛略微倾斜。更远处，西班牙的夜空正被城市遭到屠杀的火光照亮。左边远方是德国，有森林和工厂，有音乐，也有钢盔。在大教堂广场上，我仿佛看见成千上万青年列队而立，亢奋、着魔，对着探照灯照亮的元首举手致意。在意大利，这个充满记忆与幻象的国家，大众崇拜的偶像同样迷住了青年。</p>
<p>再向遥远的左方望去，俄罗斯像地球上一块明显凸起的弧面，在黑暗中因积雪而苍白，铺展在群星与云带之下。我不可避免地看见克里姆林宫的尖塔面对红场；列宁就躺在那里，以胜利者的姿态长眠。远方乌拉尔山脚下，想象看见马格尼托斯特罗伊泛红的烟柱与笼罩天空的烟幕。群山另一侧已经泛起一丝黎明，因为在我的午夜时分，白昼早已从亚洲向西倾泻；金色与玫瑰色的晨光前沿，正追赶西伯利亚大铁路列车那条微小的烟雾毛虫。北方，坚硬如铁的北极压迫着集中营里的流放者。遥远南方，是曾经孕育我们物种的富饶河谷与平原；可我此时看见铁路线已经像尺子画出的直线一样穿过积雪。亚洲每一个村庄里，孩子们正在醒来，准备开始又一个上学日，也准备再次听到列宁的传奇。再向南，喜马拉雅山从山腰到顶峰披着积雪，越过杂乱的山麓俯瞰拥挤的印度。我看见棉株随风摇摆，看见小麦，也看见那条神圣河流，把来自卡梅特峰的水带过稻田与鳄鱼出没的浅滩，经过拥有船运与写字楼的加尔各答，最后流向大海。我从自己的午夜望进中国。晨光从水田上闪过，为祖坟镀上金色。长江像一根闪亮而皱曲的细线，奔腾穿过峡谷。越过朝鲜半岛的山脉和海面，富士山矗立在那里，死寂而端正。一个火山般的人口群体在这狭窄国土上涌动翻腾，如同火山口中的熔岩；它已经把军队与贸易的洪流溢向亚洲大陆。想象收回目光，又转向非洲。我看见那条把西方与东方连接起来的人造水道；随后是宣礼塔、金字塔，以及永远等待着的斯芬克斯。就连古老的孟菲斯，如今也回响着马格尼托斯特罗伊的传闻。再向遥远南方，黑人在大湖岸边沉睡，大象践踏庄稼。更远处，在荷兰人与英国人从数百万黑人身上获取利益的地方，那些人群也被模糊的自由之梦搅动。目光越过非洲整个隆起的大陆，越过云层覆盖的桌山，我看见被风暴染黑的南大洋；随后是有海豹和企鹅栖息的冰崖，以及这个唯一无人居住大陆高处的雪原。想象迎着午夜太阳，跨过南极，经过埃里伯斯火山；它正把炽热熔岩吐在自己洁白的雪袍上。随后想象向北疾驰，越过夏日海洋，掠过新西兰——那个更加自由、却较少自觉的英国——抵达澳大利亚，在那里，目光清澈的骑手正在收拢羊群。</p>
<p>我仍从山坡向东方凝望，看见散布着岛屿的太平洋，随后看见美洲。在那里，欧洲人的后代很久以前凭借更早使用枪炮，以及枪炮滋养出的傲慢，征服了亚洲人的后代。在更远一片大洋沿岸，从北到南，铺展着这个古老的“新世界”：拉普拉塔河和里约热内卢，新英格兰的城市群——那种既古老又新颖的生活与思想方式向外辐射的中心。纽约背对午后阳光显得漆黑，像一簇高耸晶体，又像由现代巨石构成的巨石阵。巨型远洋客轮拥挤在这些高楼周围，仿佛鱼儿在涉水者脚边啄食。我也看见海面上的客轮，以及颠簸前行的货船；它们穿过落日航行，舷窗与甲板都亮着灯。司炉工在炉火边汗流浃背，瞭望台上的水手冻得发抖，从敞开的舱门传出的舞曲又被海风淹没。</p>
<p>此刻，整颗行星、整粒岩石微尘连同其上忙碌的亿万群体，在我眼中都成了一座竞技场：两个宇宙性的对手、两种精神，已经在那里为一场关键斗争作准备；它们已经穿上尘世和地方性的外衣，并在我们这些半觉醒心灵中彼此短兵相接。一个城市接一个城市，一个村庄接一个村庄，以及无数孤独的农庄、村舍、陋屋、棚屋和茅舍；在人类专注于一点点舒适、一点点胜利和一点点逃避的所有缝隙里，我们这个时代的伟大斗争都正在酝酿。</p>
<p>其中一个对手表现为一种敢于冒险的意志，为了那个崭新的、渴望已久的、理性而欢乐的世界去冒险；在那里，每一个男人和女人都能得到充分生活的空间，也能够在服务人类中生活。另一个对手的本质似乎是对未知的短视恐惧；或者，它其实更加阴险？它是否是一种狡猾的私人支配意志，为达到自己的目的，故意煽动那种古老、仇视理性、充满报复性的部族激情？</p>
<p>仿佛在即将到来的风暴中，一切最珍爱的东西都必须被摧毁：所有私人幸福，所有爱，所有在艺术、科学与哲学中的创造性工作，所有理智审视与思辨想象，所有创造性的社会建设；事实上，所有一个人在正常时代本应为之而活的东西，在公共灾难面前都仿佛变成愚蠢、讽刺和纯粹的自我放纵。可是，如果我们不设法保存这些东西，它们又要到什么时候才能重新活过来？</p>
<p>该怎样面对这样的时代？如果自己只会那些朴素日常的德性，又该怎样召集勇气？又怎样在做到这一切的同时，仍保存心灵自身的完整，永远不让斗争在自己内心摧毁自己试图在世界上服务的东西——精神的完整性？</p>
<p>有两盏灯可以指引方向。第一盏，是我们这个小小而发光的共同体原子，以及它所代表的一切。第二盏，是群星冰冷的光，它象征超宇宙的实在以及那种晶体般的狂喜。奇异的是，在这样的光中，即使最珍爱的爱也会被冰冷地评估，即使我们这个半觉醒世界可能遭遇失败，也会在不停止赞美的情况下被凝视；然而，人类眼前的危机并没有因此失去意义，反而获得了更大的意义。奇异的是，在这种光照下，参与这场斗争似乎不是变得不那么迫切，而是更加迫切：这不过是一群微小生物短暂的努力，要在最终黑暗降临之前，为自己的种族争取多一点清明。</p>
<p>——全书完——</p>

<h3>XVI. EPILOGUE: BACK TO EARTH</h3>
<p>I WOKE ON the hill. The street lamps of our suburb outshone the stars. The reverberation of the clock’s stroke was followed by eleven strokes more. I singled out our window. A surge of joy, of wild joy, swept me like a wave. Then peace.</p>
<p>The littleness, but the intensity, of earthly events! Gone, abolished in an instant, was the hypercosmical reality, the wild fountain of creations, and all the spray of worlds. Vanished, transmuted into fantasy, and into sublime irrelevance.</p>
<p>The littleness, but the intensity, of this whole grain of rock, with its film of ocean and of air, and its discontinuous, variegated, tremulous film of life; of the shadowy hills, of the sea, vague, horizonless; of the pulsating, cepheid, lighthouse; of the clanking railway trucks. My hand caressed the pleasant harshness of the heather.</p>
<p>Vanished, the hypercosmical apparition. Not such as I had dreamed must the real be, but infinitely more subtle, more dread, more excellent. And infinitely nearer home.</p>
<p>Yet, however false the vision in detail of structure, even perhaps in its whole form, in temper surely it was relevant; in temper perhaps it was even true. The real itself, surely, had impelled me to conceive that image, false in every theme and facet, yet in spirit true.</p>
<p>The stars wanly trembled above the street lamps. Great suns? Or feeble sparks in the night sky? Suns, it was vaguely rumoured. Lights at least to steer by, and to beckon the mind from the terrestrial flurry; but piercing the heart with their cold spears.</p>
<p>Sitting there on the heather, on our planetary grain, I shrank from the abysses that opened up on every side, and in the future. The silent darkness, the featureless unknown, were more dread than all the terrors that imagination had mustered. Peering, the mind could see nothing sure, nothing in all human experience to be grasped as certain, except uncertainty itself; nothing but obscurity gendered by a thick haze of theories. Man’s science was a mere mist of numbers; his philosophy but a fog of words. His very perception of this rocky grain and all its wonders was but a shifting and a lying apparition. Even oneself, that seeming-central fact, was a mere phantom, so deceptive, that the most honest of men must question his own honesty, so insubstantial that he must even doubt his very existence. And our loyalties! so self-deceiving, so mis-informed and misconceived. So savagely pursued and hate-warped! Our very loves, and these in full and generous intimacy, must be condemned as unseeing, self-regarding, and self-gratulatory.</p>
<p>And yet? I singled out our window. We had been happy together! We had found, or we had created, our little treasure of community. This was the one rock in all the welter of experience. This, not the astronomical and hypercosmical immensity, nor even the planetary grain, this, this alone, was the solid ground of existence.</p>
<p>On every side was confusion, a rising storm, great waves already drenching our rock. And all around, in the dark welter, faces and appealing hands, half-seen and vanishing.</p>
<p>And the future? Black with the rising storm of this world’s madness, though shot through with flashes of a new and violent hope, the hope of a sane, a reasonable, a happier world. Between our time and that future, what horror lay in store? Oppressors would not meekly give way. And we two, accustomed only to security and mildness, were fit only for a kindly world; wherein, none being tormented, none turns desperate. We were adapted only to fair weather, for the practice of the friendly but not too difficult, not heroical virtues, in a society both secure and just. Instead, we found ourselves in an age of titanic conflict, when the relentless powers of darkness and the ruthless because desperate powers of light were coming to grips for a death struggle in the world’s torn heart, when grave choices must be made in crisis after crisis, and no simple or familiar principles were adequate.</p>
<p>Beyond our estuary a red growth of fire sprang from a foundry. At hand, the dark forms of the gorse lent mystery to the suburb’s foot-worn moor.</p>
<p>In imagination I saw, behind our own hill’s top, the further and unseen hills. I saw the plains and woods and all the fields, each with its myriads of particular blades. I saw the whole land curving down from me, over the planet’s shoulder. The villages were strung together on a mesh of roads, steel lines, and humming wires. Mist-drops on a cobweb. Here and there a town displayed itself as an expanse of light, a nebulous luminosity, sprinkled with stars.</p>
<p>Beyond the plains, London, neon-lit, seething, was a microscope-slide drawn from foul water, and crowded with nosing animalcules. Animalcules! In the stars’ view, no doubt, these creatures were mere vermin; yet each to itself, and sometimes one to another, was more real than all the stars.</p>
<p>Gazing beyond London, imagination detected the dim stretch of the Channel, and then the whole of Europe, a patch-work of tillage and sleeping industrialism. Beyond poplared Normandy spread Paris, with the towers of Notre-Dame tipped slightly, by reason of Earth’s curvature. Further on, the Spanish night was ablaze with the murder of cities. Away to the left lay Germany, with its forests and factories, its music, its steel helmets. In cathedral squares I seemed to see the young men ranked together in thousands, exalted, possessed, saluting the flood-lit Führer. In Italy too, land of memories and illusions, the mob’s idol spell-bound the young.</p>
<p>Far left-wards again, Russia, an appreciably convex segment of our globe, snow-pale in the darkness, spread out under the stars and cloud-tracts. Inevitably I saw the spires of the Kremlin, confronting the Red Square. There Lenin lay, victorious. Far off, at the foot of the Urals, imagination detected the ruddy plumes and smoke-pall of Magnetostroy. Beyond the hills there gleamed a hint of dawn; for day, at my midnight, was already pouring westward across Asia, overtaking with its advancing front of gold and rose the tiny smoke-caterpillar of the Trans-Siberian Express. To the north, the iron-hard Arctic oppressed the exiles in their camps. Far southward lay the rich valleys and plains that once cradled our species. But there I now saw railway lines ruled across the snow. In every village Asiatic children were waking to another school-day, and to the legend of Lenin. South again the Himalayas, snow-clad from waist to crest, looked over the rabble of their foot-hills into crowded India. I saw the dancing cotton plants, and the wheat, and the sacred river that bore the waters of Kamet past rice-fields and crocodile-shallows, past Calcutta with its shipping and its offices, down to the sea. From my midnight I looked into China. The morning sun glanced from the flooded fields and gilded the ancestral graves. The Yang Tse, a gleaming, crumpled thread, rushed through its gorge. Beyond the Korean ranges, and across the sea, stood Fujiyama, extinct and formal. Around it a volcanic population welled and seethed in that narrow land, like lava in a crater. Already it spilled over Asia a flood of armies and of trade.</p>
<p>Imagination withdrew and turned to Africa. I saw the man-made thread of water that joins West to East; then minarets, pyramids, the ever-waiting Sphinx. Ancient Memphis itself now echoed with the rumour of Magnetostroy. Far southward, black men slept beside the great lakes. Elephants trampled the crops. Further still, where Dutch and English profit by the Negro millions, those hosts were stirred by vague dreams of freedom.</p>
<p>Peering beyond the whole bulge of Africa, beyond cloud-spread Table Mountain, I saw the Southern Ocean, black with storms, and then the ice-cliffs with their seals and penguins, and the high snow-fields of the one unpeopled continent. Imagination faced the midnight sun, crossed the Pole, and passed Erebus, vomiting hot lava down his ermine. Northward it sped over the summer sea, past New Zealand, that freer but less conscious Britain, to Australia, where clear-eyed horsemen collect their flocks.</p>
<p>Still peering eastward from my hill, I saw the Pacific, strewn with islands; and then the Americas, where the descendants of Europe long ago mastered the descendants of Asia, through priority in the use of guns, and the arrogance that guns breed. Beside the further ocean, north and south, lay the old New World; the River Plate and Rio, the New England cities, radiating centre of the old new style of life and thought. New York, dark against the afternoon sun, was a cluster of tall crystals, a Stonehenge of modern megaliths. Round these, like fishes nibbling at the feet of waders, the great liners crowded. Out at sea also I saw them, and the plunging freighters, forging through the sunset, port holes and decks aglow. Stokers sweated at furnaces, look-out men in crow’s-nests shivered, dance music, issuing from opened doors, was drowned by the wind.</p>
<p>The whole planet, the whole rock-grain, with its busy swarms, I now saw as an arena where two cosmical antagonists, two spirits, were already preparing for a critical struggle, already assuming terrestrial and local guise, and coming to grips in our half-awakened minds. In city upon city, in village after village, and in innumerable lonely farmsteads, cottages, hovels, shacks, huts, in all the crevices where human creatures were intent on their little comforts and triumphs and escapes, the great struggle of our age was brewing.</p>
<p>One antagonist appeared as the will to dare for the sake of the new, the longed for, the reasonable and joyful, world, in which every man and woman may have scope to live fully, and live in service of mankind. The other seemed essentially the myopic fear of the unknown; or was it more sinister? Was it the cunning will for private mastery, which fomented for its own ends the archaic, reason-hating, and vindictive, passion of the tribe.</p>
<p>It seemed that in the coming storm all the dearest things must be destroyed. All private happiness, all loving, all creative work in art, science, and philosophy, all intellectual scrutiny and speculative imagination, and all creative social building; all, indeed, that man should normally live for, seemed folly and mockery and mere self-indulgence in the presence of public calamity. But if we failed to preserve them, when would they live again?</p>
<p>How to face such an age? How to muster courage, being capable only of homely virtues? How to do this, yet preserve the mind’s integrity, never to let the struggle destroy in one’s own heart what one tried to serve in the world, the spirit’s integrity.</p>
<p>Two lights for guidance. The first, our little glowing atom of community, with all that it signifies. The second, the cold light of the stars, symbol of the hypercosmical reality, with its crystal ecstasy. Strange that in this light, in which even the dearest love is frostily assessed, and even the possible defeat of our half-waking world is contemplated without remission of praise, the human crisis does not lose but gains significance. Strange, that it seems more, not less, urgent to play some part in this struggle, this brief effort of animalcules striving to win for their race some increase of lucidity before the ultimate darkness.</p>
