<h2>二、合成信仰</h2>
<p>俄罗斯帝国边缘这段小插曲本身并没有多少普遍意义。真正吸引世人注意的始终是西藏本身。两个帝国强权当然一直试图阻挠西藏革命，但起初，双方都把“世界屋脊”上这场奇异骚动看成一出滑稽的附带节目。它们更关心的是如何在西藏这片无人地带取得对竞争对手的外交胜利，而不是如何维护旧有的西藏政权。然而，等到革命真正成功，俄罗斯和中国的寡头们便开始感到不安。再后来，人们发现这个微不足道的西藏国家正在煽动境外的颠覆力量，并筹划一场世界革命，两个帝国政府便都认真行动起来。双方各自在本国境内发动的恐怖运动，并没有取得预想中的效果。由西藏领导者训练起来的进步少数派表现出狂热般的勇气。更何况，两国政府起初都犯了同样的错误：暗中扶植对方领土上的颠覆运动。直到局势已经极其严重，两个强权才以一种心照不宣的协议放弃这套政策，决定在“叛乱瘟疫”被彻底扑灭以前，暂缓彼此之间的一切行动。即便如此，双方仍不能相信对方不会借摧毁西藏实验之机吞并这个国家。每当其中一国威胁说，如果西藏不停止宣传就要入侵时，拉萨政府总可以指望另一国以外交、甚至军事方式进行干预。</p>
<p>然而终于有一天，对西藏思想的恐惧压倒了帝国之间的竞争。两个寡头集团都发现，本国境内日益高涨的宗教狂热已经越来越难以控制。如今每座城市都设有拥挤不堪的集中营；集中营一次又一次被“清空”，以便举行公开的大屠杀：在嚎叫着、陷入狂喜的人群眼前，成千上万人被活活烤死，或者被专门设计来制造最大痛苦的机器进行活体解剖。可是运动仍在扩散，甚至感染了军队。在这种情况下，两个寡头政权被迫暂时放下争斗。双方领导人在贝加尔湖森林覆盖的岸边、伊尔库茨克最新也最富裕的郊区举行会议，共同制定政策。会议实际上由一名年轻的中国官方心理学家主导；他声称自己掌握一种绝不会失败的方法，可以治愈整个世界的疯狂。</p>
<p>若要理解他的主张，就必须先理解这些寡头的心理。大体说来，他们确实真诚地相信各自的帝国，也相信帝国主义本身。他们清醒的自我意识属于那种忠诚、办事极其精确的公务员：他们觉得自己的社会正因为一种自己无法理解的热情而面临瓦解。总体而言，他们并不喜欢为了摧毁这场运动而发动的酷刑狂欢，但他们相信这是必要的。更何况，其中多数人自己也在不知不觉中从酷刑里得到满足。因为他们大多是受挫的心灵，被一种自己并未承认的怨恨折磨着——怨恨那些敢于反抗既成野蛮秩序，从而保存了精神自由与完整性的人。此外，俄罗斯与中国文化中都存在某些有利于残酷行为的因素。俄罗斯人本性善良，并非残酷民族；但在堕落俄罗斯的伪神秘主义里，某些革命前的观念又死灰复燃。痛苦被看作最高的净化手段，也是最高的启示来源。于是，把痛苦施加给别人有时反而可以被认为值得赞扬。另一方面，中国人虽然如此讲究、如此友善，却历来既容易表现出冷酷，也容易爆发激情式的报复。一个“发了狂”的中国人，不过是把某种潜藏在整个民族、其实也潜藏在全人类中的冲动，以更戏剧化的方式表现出来而已。</p>
<p>年轻心理学家的论证大致如下。西藏已经被一个观念迷住，并正在把这种迷狂传染给各民族。要抵抗这种既富于感情又充满行动力的观念，就必须提出另一个与之相反、而且力量更大的观念。必须给人民某种值得为之生、为之死、也为之杀人的东西。西藏的观念，是那个不可思议的理想：人在为共同福祉进行喜悦的服务中，充分实现自己的能力。要对付这种阴险教义，就必须宣扬牺牲、自我献祭、在苦难中获得启示，以及服从那神圣而无情的“意志”——而这个意志当然具体体现为国家的命令。心理学家坚持认为，有两个观念必须在一切可能的场合反复灌输，并赋予某种具体象征。第一，必须不断指出：西藏人虽然也强调服从神意，但他们所理解的神意是软弱、女人气的。更何况，西藏一面强调服从，一面又鼓动人民为革命变化而斗争，这两者根本彼此矛盾。真正的服从必须是绝对的、炽烈的、狂喜的。只有当国家命令时，服从才应转化为斗争；而这时，斗争本身也必须源自对神圣国家彻底的服从。当然，如果国家显然并不神圣——例如那个彻底堕落的西藏国家——那么人们就必须持续而坚决地斗争，直到真正的国家建立起来。但一旦处于神圣国家之下，最高美德就是服从；因为国家凭借自己的智慧，会为每个人决定其恰当职能。至于受教育的权利，根本不存在这种东西。取而代之的应当是“无知的权利与义务”。每个人只应知道履行自身职能所必需知道的东西。知道得更多就是邪恶，而且真正有灵性的人会对此感到厌恶。服从还意味着虔诚地接受痛苦——自己的，也包括邻人的。然而痛苦不应只是勉强忍受；人还必须欢迎它。因为心理学家强调的第二个伟大观念，正是痛苦与残酷本身的卓越价值。西藏人在赞美善意与相互尊重时，漏掉了另一种重要价值。善意当然自有其位置。在一个家庭的成员之间，在神圣国家忠诚的成员之间，只要善意不损害忠诚，它就是必要的。但从精神角度看，还有一种美德比善意更加重要，也更加能够带来启示，那就是残酷。因为按他的说法，残酷与痛苦互为补充。在酷刑中，受害者与施刑者都应体验到一种不可言说的启示。正如爱的结合一样——而且要强烈得多——受刑者与施刑者的结合是一种创造性的综合，在其中，一种崭新而辉煌的现实被创造出来。证明就在体验本身。施刑者很清楚那种狂喜；至于受害者，如果事先接受过充分的精神训练，则应体验到一种更加精妙、更加撕裂人的欢乐。</p>
<p>心理学家主张，两国政府应秘密挑选并训练未来这种信仰的先知，然后把他们放到两个帝国各处，让他们以自发的宗教狂热者身份出现。最好让这些煽动者批判现有帝国政府，谴责它们不过是真理软弱无力的体现。事实上，这些由国家扶植的革命者还应该被鼓励去要求一个新政权。甚至可以让他们走得足够远，以至于遭到现政府迫害。这样一来，其中一部分人当然必须被牺牲掉；但幸存者则必须得到大量资金支持，让他们在民众中煽起革命热情。这种革命追求的目标，不应是西藏已经实现的那种软弱无力的自由主义—社会主义乌托邦，而应是充分实现现存秩序本身的潜能。只有真正的神圣国家建立以后，绝对顺从这种美德才可能真正实现。</p>
<p>心理学家预言，这样一场运动将横扫世界。它既会迎合人们在别人要求帮助时“从另一边绕过去”的普遍冲动，也会迎合几乎同样普遍的毁灭与残酷欲望。他建议，按照两个民族不同的气质，在俄罗斯应重点强调顺从，在中国则应重点强调残酷。他还补充说，将来到了适当时候——而这个时候必然会到来——世界统治阶级若要借一场世界大战进一步收紧对人民的控制，这种差异便可以成为制造俄中民族仇恨的基础。这个年轻人究竟真心信奉自己所宣扬的信仰，还是只把它当作必要的治国术，始终无人能够确定。会议接受这套政策时，则明确只是把它当作治国手段。</p>
<p>不久，西藏传教者便发现自己面对着一场竞争性的传教运动，而他们根本无法应付。这场新运动的煽动者是一群近似狂野托钵僧的人。他们把听众煽入狂怒，宣扬神圣的残酷，要求让帝国国家重新焕发生命。每次集会结束后，总会发生私刑，有时甚至会把被俘的光明仆人大批献祭。这场运动从广州一直蔓延到列宁格勒。两个政府都向这场风暴低头：统治人员发生了一些变动，政策则变得更清晰，也完全调整到与新信仰一致。民族差异暂时被压到次要位置，共同的意志只有一个——摧毁西藏。</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" class="stapledon-body" lang="en" hidden>
<h2>ii. A SYNTHETIC FAITH</h2>
<p>THIS LITTLE EPISODE on the fringe of the Russian Empire was of no general significance. The focus of interest was always Tibet itself. The two imperial powers had, of course tried to frustrate the Tibetan revolution, but at first each had regarded the strange commotion on ‘the Roof of the World’ as a comic side-show. Each had been concerned to gain a diplomatic victory over its rival in the Tibetan no-man’s- land rather than to preserve the old Tibetan régime. But when the revolution was actually accomplished, the Russian and Chinese oligarchs began to be alarmed. And when it became evident that the insignificant Tibetan state was fomenting the subversive forces beyond its frontiers and planning a world-wide revolution, both the imperial governments began to take serious action. The campaign of terrorism which each undertook within its own frontiers was not as successful as had been hoped. The progressive minority, disciplined by Tibetan leaders, showed fanatical courage. Moreover each imperial government at first made the mistake of fostering the subversive movement in its rival’s territory. Not till matters had become very grave was this policy abandoned by a tacit agreement between the two great powers to postpone all action against one another till the epidemic of sedition had been crushed. Even so, neither could trust the other not to use the crushing of the Tibetan experiment as a pretext for annexing the country. Whenever one of the two powers threatened invasion if Tibetan propaganda did not cease, the government at Lhasa was able to count on diplomatic or even military intervention by the other.</p>
<p>There came a time, however, when fear of Tibetan ideas overcame imperial rivalry. Both oligarchies were finding it impossible to cope with the rising tide of religious fanaticism within their own frontiers. Though every city had now its own congested concentration camp, though time after time these camps were emptied to provide a public holocaust in which, before the eyes of a howling and ecstatic mob, thousands were roasted alive or vivisected by machinery devised to produce maximal pain, the movement continued to spread. It even infected the troops. In these circumstances the two oligarchies were forced to put aside their rivalry. Their leaders met in conference in the newest and wealthiest suburb of Irkutsk, on the forest-clad shores of Lake Baikal. There they worked out a common policy. The conference was dominated by a young Chinese official psychologist who claimed to have an infallible cure for the world’s madness.</p>
<p>To appreciate his contentions it is necessary to understand the mentality of the oligarchs. They were in the main sincere believers in their respective empires, and in imperialism itself. Their conscious minds were those of devoted, meticulously accurate civil servants who felt that their society was in danger of disintegration through an enthusiasm beyond their comprehension. On the whole they disliked the orgy of torture with which it was hoped to break the movement, but they believed it necessary; Moreover most of them unwittingly derived satisfaction from it, for most were frustrated spirits, teased by an unrecognized itch of resentment against those who had maintained spiritual liberty and integrity by rebelling against the established barbarism. Moreover in the Russian and the Chinese cultures there were elements which favoured cruelty. The Russians were a kindly not a cruel people, but in the pseudo-mysticism of degenerate Russia there was in some respects a return to pre-revolutionary ideas. Suffering was conceived of as the supreme purifier and the supreme source of illumination. Consequently the infliction of suffering on others might sometimes be laudable. The Chinese, on the other hand, though so fastidious and so friendly, had always been liable both to cold cruelty and to passionate vindictiveness. The Chinaman who had ‘run amok’ did but manifest an impulse which was latent in all his race, and indeed in all mankind, though with less dramatic expression.</p>
<p>The argument of the young psychologist was briefly this. Tibet had become obsessed with an idea, and was infecting every people. To resist such an emotional and dynamic idea it was necessary to have another idea, contrary and even more potent. It was necessary to give the people something to live for, die for, and kill for. The Tibetan idea was the incredible ideal of a world in which men would fulfil their powers in joyful service of the common weal. To counter this insidious doctrine it was necessary to preach sacrifice, self- immolation, enlightenment in suffering, obedience to the divine and ruthless Will, embodied, of course, in the fiat of the state. Two ideas, the psychologist insisted, must be reiterated on all possible occasions and given some kind of concrete symbolization. In the first place it must be constantly pointed out that though the Tibetans themselves insisted on submission to the divine will, their conception of that will was effeminate. Moreover the Tibetan emphasis on submission was incompatible with the contrary exhortation to strive for revolutionary change. Submission must be absolute, fervent, ecstatic. Only at the command of the state must it give place to struggle, and then struggle itself must spring from utter submission to the divine state. Of course if the state was palpably not divine, if it was, for instance, the utterly perverted Tibetan state, struggle must be constant and resolute until the true state was founded. But under the divine state the supreme virtue was obedience. For the state in its wisdom would decide what was the right function of everyone. As for the right to education, there was no such thing. In its place must be set the right and duty of ignorance. Let each man know merely whatever was needed for the fulfilling of his function. To know more was wicked, and to the truly spiritual mind repugnant. Obedience involved also the pious acceptance of suffering, one’s own and one’s neighbour’s. But indeed suffering was not only to be reluctantly accepted; it must be welcomed. For the second great idea which the psychologist stressed was the excellence both of suffering and of cruelty. In praising kindliness and mutual respect the Tibetans had overlooked another important value. No doubt there was a place for kindliness. Between members of one family, and between loyal members of the divine state, kindliness was necessary so long as it did not infringe against loyalty, But from the spiritual point of view there was a virtue more important and more illuminating than kindliness, namely cruelty. For cruelty, he said, was complementary to suffering. In torture, both victim and agent should experience an ineffable illumination. Like the union of love, and in a far more vivid manner, the union of victim and torturer was a creative synthesis in which a new and splendid reality was brought into being. The proof of this was in the experience itself. The torturer knew well that ecstasy. The victim, if he was spiritually disciplined beforehand, should experience an even more exquisite, excruciating joy.</p>
<p>The psychologist urged that the two governments should secretly select and train the future prophets of this faith, and launch them out as spontaneous religious enthusiasts throughout the two empires. It would be well that these agitators should be critical of the existing imperial governments, condemning them as but feeble embodiments of the truth. Indeed these state-aided revolutionaries should be encouraged to demand a new regime. Let them go so far as to incur persecution by the existing governments. Some of them would then have to be sacrificed, but the survivors must be heavily financed to rouse a revolutionary fervour among the populace, the object of which would be not the milk-sop liberal-socialist Utopia achieved by Tibet but the fulfilment of the potentialities of the existing order. Only when the true divine state had been established would the virtue of absolute acquiescence be possible.</p>
<p>Such a movement, the psychologist prophesied, would sweep the world. It would appeal both to the universal impulse to ‘pass by on the other side’ when help was demanded and to the no less ‘widespread need for destruction and cruelty. He suggested that, in consonance with the two national temperaments, acquiescence should be stressed in Russia, cruelty in China. This difference, he added, could be used as a basis on which to build Russo-Chinese national hatred when the time came (as it surely would) for the world-wide ruling class to tighten its grip on the people by means of a world war. It was never clear whether the young man believed in the faith that he was preaching or whether he advocated it merely as a piece of necessary statecraft. It was as statecraft that the conference accepted the policy.</p>
<p>Presently the Tibetan missionaries found themselves confronted by a rival missionary movement, with which they could not cope. The instigators of this new movement were a kind of wild dervish. They lashed their audiences into fury, preaching sacred cruelty and demanding a revitalization of the imperial state. After their meetings there was always a lynching, sometimes a mass sacrifice of captive servants of the light. The movement spread from Canton to Leningrad. The two governments bowed before the storm. Their personnel was somewhat changed, their policy clarified and brought into line with the new faith. National differences were for the time submerged under the common will to destroy Tibet.</p>
