<h2>一、经济衰退</h2>
<p>我无法确定“世界天朝帝国”究竟延续了多久。它的寿命无疑要以世纪计算，甚至可能延续了一两千年。尽管这个世界帝国从根本上说是一个病态社会，注定终将瓦解，它毕竟从较早的社会继承了某种坚韧性；而它的结构又使它只要外部条件不发生变化，就能够以一种活死人般的状态继续存在下去。在物质资源尚未受损的时候，它几乎可以完全自动地、不发生任何变化地运转。</p>
<p>事实上，人类已经获得了某种类似昆虫物种的稳定性；后者可以把同一套生活方式维持数百万年。帝国的整个经济体系，早在较早时代就由技术人员经过几十年的工作，把每个细节都安排妥当，最后终于彻底定型。各种原料在规定地区、按固定的年度数量生产出来，再依照一套历史悠久的计划分配给制造业地区，随后又按照同样古老的比例分给各个民族与社会阶层。整个工业技术体系已经获得了一种近乎宗教的神圣性。除了那些本身也已被神圣化的季节性变化之外，任何偏离都不被允许。</p>
<p>在这种条件下，技术人员——这些不被承认为统治者、却实际上掌管整个行星的人——其职能发生了根本变化。他们不再主要负责发明新工艺、作出新的调整，而只是成为神圣传统知识的正统载体。因此，智力——哪怕只是被束缚的智力——所能发挥的作用也越来越狭窄。在衰退开始以前，社会规划曾变得越来越全面、越来越有远见。人们会为几百年后的未来、为整个大型社会进行规划，甚至还曾试探性地考虑过物种本身的前途。但世界帝国一旦牢固建立并彻底定型，大规模规划便不再被认为有必要。只有安排个人生活时，智力还剩下一点用武之地。可随着个人生活越来越受国家强制规定的种种固定模式支配，智力在这里的职能也继续缩小。每当某个大胆人物试图改进正统程序时，他的智力往往又确实显得薄弱，行动也常常走向错误。他的失败只会进一步巩固社会对创新的普遍不信任。</p>
<p>在相当漫长的时期里，物质资源以及人类的生物学状况，实际上都保持着大体恒定。对世界帝国的臣民而言，现存秩序似乎显然会永远存在。无论物质还是精神方面，“进步”这个观念早已失去可信度，因为人人都认为社会已经完美。另一方面，也从来没有人认真想过种族可能衰退。然而，在这种稳定的表象背后，无论物质环境还是人类自身的构成，都已经发生着巨大的变化。</p>
<p>虽然火山能量几乎取之不尽，某些不可缺少的原材料却不是。煤与石油作为能源早已被淘汰，但它们仍是许多合成产品的重要原料，而且变得越来越难以取得。农业不可或缺的世界磷酸盐矿藏也在持续减少。早已被放弃的鸟粪肥，如今又重新被人们极其勤勉地收集起来。钾盐矿藏经过长期高强度开采，也已经严重枯竭。较早的时代曾知道，如果必要，可以从海水中取得无限供应的钾盐；只是当时没有迫切需求，所以从未把分离提取技术真正发展成熟。如今钾盐已经稀缺，人类却再也没有足够的创造能力来攻克这种困难技术。氮素长期以来一直从空气中提取，用于肥料和烈性炸药。不过这一技术已经充分定型，因此暂时没有失传的危险。铁虽然是最常见的元素之一，却也越来越难以开采。所有通常容易达到的矿藏都已严重消耗，而进行更深层采矿所需要的技术能力此时也已经丧失。</p>
<p>世界帝国晚期的林业状况，以一种极其奇特的方式暴露出这个物种的精神衰退。木浆曾经是许多合成产品的主要原料。在早期，当技术人员的智力仍然有效时，人们设计了造林计划，使森林生产与消耗能够保持平衡。但到了后来，种植远远落后于砍伐。乍看之下这似乎很奇怪，因为植树与采伐之间的平衡，本来正是那套僵硬而神圣的社会组织技术的一部分。造成差距不断扩大的原因其实极其简单，却完全躲过了帝国晚期那些迟钝管理者的注意。原来的制度，是在这样一个假设上计算出来的：林业技术将始终由反应迅速、具有实际判断力的人来执行。计划虽然为事故和错误留出了余量，却并不是一种连傻瓜操作也绝不会出问题的制度。当智力下降后，错误越来越频繁，纠正错误的能力又越来越差，于是那套古老神圣的公式开始失效。森林便缓慢却不可阻挡地缩减。然而按照神圣的造林经典，只要那些公式确实被遵守，森林缩减就是“不可能”的。因此，声称森林正在减少便成了不虔诚的言论。于是，凡是开始怀疑这种事情正在发生的人，都主动对事实视而不见。腐坏就这样不断继续，而没有任何人真正试图阻止它。</p>
<p>农业中也发生了同样灾难性的衰败。帝国农业制度最初的组织者曾设计出一套十分精细、保持微妙平衡的耕作体系；理论上，它既能够生产足够的粮食，又不会使土地贫瘠。但是这套体系必须依赖有智慧的灵活调整，它同样不是一套“傻瓜也不会搞坏”的制度。当迟钝的头脑接掌一切以后，整个系统每一个环节的浪费都显著增加，旧有公式于是逐渐失去效力。可是，任何修改又都会被视作亵渎。结果一世纪又一世纪，人们投入土地中的东西总是比从土地中取出的略少一些。于是，持续的土地剥蚀过程开始了。所有主要农业地区的生产力都缓慢却稳定地下降：北美与俄罗斯产粮的广大平原、中国和印度的稻作平原、曾为世界提供蔬菜的各处分散区域、加利福尼亚、澳大利亚和南非的果园地带，无一例外地逐渐恶化。它们一点一点化作沙质荒地，如同昔日肥沃的撒哈拉。森林缩减引发的气候变化，又使这一过程更加严重。</p>
<p>当然，农业的逐步失败是一个极其缓慢的过程。帝国普通公民并没有察觉。确实，人们到处可以看到大片沙漠，里面还残留着往昔农庄的废墟；但迟钝的人口从未想到，这竟是一个不断扩张的灾难的征兆。只有把当前产量与过去的记录作比较，问题才会显现出来。然而这些档案，以及农业生产的神圣比例，只掌握在农业这个“秘仪集团”手里——实际上也就是全球农业体系的最高负责人。这些权贵模模糊糊知道有哪里不对；但由于种种原因，他们判断麻烦大概不会在自己有生之年真正爆发，于是全都保持沉默。事实上，这种衰退很容易被掩盖，因为当粮食供给日益减少时，世界人口也正在迅速下降。</p>
</section>

<h2>i. ECONOMIC DECLINE</h2>
<p>ICANNOT BE sure how long the Celestial World Empire endured. Its life must certainly be counted in centuries, and possibly it lasted for a couple of thousand years. Though the world empire was at heart a diseased society and bound to disintegrate, it inherited from earlier societies a certain toughness of fibre, and its structure was such that it could carryon in a sort of living death so long as conditions remained unchanged. While its material resources were unimpaired it functioned automatically and without change.</p>
<p>The human race had in fact attained the kind of stability which insect species have maintained for many million years. Its whole economy had been worked out in intricate detail by the technicians of an earlier age through a period of many decades, and had at last become absolutely stereotyped. Raw materials, produced in appropriate regions and in regular annual quantities, were assigned to manufacturing districts according to a time-honoured plan, to be distributed in time-honoured proportions to the various nations and social classes. The whole industrial technique had acquired a kind of religious sanctity. No variations were to be tolerated, except the seasonal variations which were themselves sanctified.</p>
<p>In these circumstances the function of the technicians, the unacknowledged but effective rulers of the planet, was radically altered. From being primarily inventors of new processes and new adjustments they became simply orthodox vehicles of the sacred lore. Intelligence, therefore, even bound intelligence, came to have an increasingly restricted function. Before the onset of decline, planning had been becoming more and more comprehensive and far-seeing. Men had planned for centuries ahead and for great societies, even tentatively for the future of the species. But after the world empire had become firmly established and stereotyped, large planning was no longer necessary. Only in the ordering of individual lives was there any scope for intelligence. Even here, as individual lives became more and more dominated by the regularities imposed by the state, the office of intelligence became more restricted. Whenever any daring spirit did try to improve upon the orthodox procedure, his intelligence proved feeble and his action misguided. His failure merely strengthened the general distrust of innovation.</p>
<p>For a very long while the material resources and the biological condition of the race did remain in effect constant. To the subjects of the world empire it seemed certain that the existing order was eternal. The idea of progress, material or mental, had long since ceased to seem plausible, for society was universally regarded as perfect. On the other hand the idea of racial decline was never contemplated. But behind the appearance of stability great changes were already at work, both in the physical environment and in the constitution of the human race itself.</p>
<p>Though volcanic power was inexhaustible, certain essential raw materials were not. Coal and oil had long ago been superseded as sources of power, but as raw materials for many synthetic products they were valuable, and becoming ever more difficult to procure. The world’s phosphate deposits, so necessary for agriculture, were being steadily reduced. Guano, long ago abandoned, was once more assiduously collected. Potash deposits had been heavily worked and were seriously depleted. An earlier age had known that an unlimited supply of potash could, when necessary, be obtained from sea water, but there had been no need to work out a technique for isolating it. Now, when potash was scarce, there was no longer the inventive capacity to tackle so difficult a task. Nitrogen had for long been derived from the air for use in fertilizers and high explosives. In this case, however, the technique was well established, and so there was no immediate danger of its loss. Iron, though one of the commonest of all elements, was becoming steadily more difficult to reach. All ordinarily accessible deposits were seriously depleted, and the skill for much deeper mining was by now lacking.</p>
<p>The condition of forestry in the latter days of the world-empire throws a strange light on the mental decay of the race. Wood-pulp had been the main raw material for many synthetic products. In early days, when the intelligence of the technicians was still effective, afforestation schemes had been organized so as to keep the balance of production and consumption. But latterly planting had seriously lagged behind felling. This may seem surprising, since the balance of planting and felling was part of the rigid and sacred technique of social organization. The cause of the ever-increasing discrepancy was very simple but completely hidden from the sluggish minds of the latter-day empire controllers. The original scheme had been calculated on the assumption that the art of forestry would continue to be practised with quick intelligence. Some margin had been allowed for accidents and errors, but not a fool-proof margin. When intelligence had declined, mistakes became more frequent, and less successfully repaired. Consequently the old sacred formulae failed. The forests slowly but surely dwindled. But according to the sacred scriptures of afforestation this was impossible, if the formulae had indeed been followed. Therefore it was impious to suggest that the forests were dwindling. Therefore anyone who began to suspect that this was happening turned a blind eye on the facts. Thus the rot continued without any attempt being made to stop it.</p>
<p>The same disastrous decay took place in agriculture. The original organizers of the empire’s tillage had worked out a delicately balanced agricultural system which should yield an adequate crop of food-stuffs without impoverishing the land. But this system had depended on intelligent adjustment. It was not fool-proof. When sluggish minds took charge, there was a far greater wastage at every point in the system. The old formulae therefore became inadequate. But since any alteration would have been impious, the upshot was that century by century rather less was put into the ground than was taken from it. Thus there set in a steady process of denudation. Slowly but surely all the great agricultural districts became less productive. The corn-bearing plains of North America and Russia, the rice plains of China and India, the great scattered areas that had provided the world’s greens, the fruit lands of California, Australia, South Africa, one and all deteriorated. Little by little they turned into wastes of sand, like the once fertile Sahara. The process was made all the worse by climatic changes caused by the shrinking of the forests.</p>
<p>The gradual failure of agriculture was of course a very slow process. Ordinary citizens of the empire did not notice it. True, there were great desert tracts in which the ruins of former farmsteads might be observed; but the slow-witted populace never dreamed that this was a symptom of an ever-spreading disaster. Only by comparing the present output with past records could the trouble be realized. But the records and the sacred proportions of agricultural production were known only to the ‘mystery’ of agriculture, in fact to the heads of the world agricultural system. These magnates knew vaguely that something was wrong; but since for sundry reasons it was unlikely that there would be trouble in their day, they held their tongues. The decline was in fact easily concealed, because, while supplies were dwindling, the population of the world was also rapidly decreasing.</p>
