<p class="story-note">初刊于《Hotch Potch》</p>
<p>房间里挤得水泄不通，空气也闷得令人难受。那音乐似乎没有任何可以理解的形式，不过是一片声音的丛林。这个乐器刚吼出半截旋律，另一个乐器又接着冒出来；可这些夭折的音乐生物，一个还没来得及站稳脚跟就被杀死了。不是某头敌对的野兽扑上来把它吞掉，就是整片丛林把它活活闷死。</p>
<p>追踪这场生存竞争令我疲惫。我闭上眼睛，大概睡着了；因为忽然间，我猛地惊醒——或者说，仿佛醒了。有什么古怪的事发生了。音乐还在继续，可我全身瘫痪。我睁不开眼，喊不出声，身体也动不了，甚至感觉不到它。我没有身体了。</p>
<p>音乐本身也发生了某种变化，我的听觉也是。可究竟是什么？声音交织成的组织似乎突然变得无可比拟地庞大而复杂。我不懂音乐；但我忽然意识到，这音乐仿佛溢出了正常半音之间的一切缝隙。它使用的不只是四分之一音，而是“百分之一音”“千分之一音”；对正常人的耳朵来说，这种效果想必会是一种折磨。可在我改变后的状态里，它却带来一种普通音乐完全欠缺的丰厚、坚实和生命力。而且这奇异的音乐还有另一种丰富的来源：它向高处和低处延伸，越过正常听觉范围，跨越几十个八度。可我全都听得见。</p>
<p>我继续听着，竟很快习惯了这种新的语言。我发现自己能够轻易分辨出这个声音世界里形形色色、彼此连贯的音乐形式。在某种晦暗而异域的背景之上——那里有相对恒定的和弦，也有仿佛“叶丛”般颤动的音响——几个突出而不断变化的声音形体正在活动。每一个都是持续存在的音乐对象，虽然它的细部姿态不断波动，有时整个形体还会沿音阶上下移动。</p>
<p>忽然，我有了一个本应荒诞到难以置信的发现；可在当时，它对我来说却又熟悉而理所当然。我认出了这些活跃的声音形体：它们是活物，甚至具有智能。在正常世界里，我们把活物感知为由视觉和触觉性质组成、不断变化的图样；可在这个疯狂的世界——此时它已经开始让我感到相当亲切——构成生命可感身体的，不是颜色和形状的图样，而是声音的图样。想到自己竟掉进了一个“标题音乐”的国度，我一度颇为反感。这里竟有整个世界公然违背真正的音乐艺术准则！随后我提醒自己，这音乐并非只是在讲述一个故事，而是真的在活出它的故事。事实上，它不是艺术，而是生命。于是我放任自己的兴趣继续发展。</p>
<p>我观察着这些在面前嬉游的生物，发现——或者更准确地说，重新发现——这个世界虽然没有我们凭视觉和触觉认识的那种真正空间，却仍有某种空间。因为这些生命无论相对于我，还是彼此之间，都确实在某种意义上移动。这个世界的“空间”显然只有两个维度，而且两者在性质上截然不同。一个自然就是音调、也就是音高的维度，沿着这个世界精细得不可思议的“键盘”延伸。另一个维度只能间接感知。它对应于正常世界中，同一件乐器听起来离我们远或近的感觉。正如我们根据色彩和透视来判断视觉对象的远近，在这个奇异世界里，某些音色、泛音和谐波特征会传达“接近”的感觉；另一些则会传达“遥远”的感觉。一种奇特的刺耳、耀眼般的音质——常常还伴随着较大的响度——意味着“近”；某种扁平、幽灵般的音色——通常也更微弱——意味着“远”。一个对象若沿着我所谓的“层级”维度远离，就会逐渐失去饱满的音色，也失去细节和精确性；同时它会越来越轻，最终完全听不见。</p>
<p>还应补充一点：每个声音对象也都有自己的特征音色，几乎就像这个世界里的每一种东西，都是由同一种乐器演奏的一个主题。不过我很快发现，对生命而言，每个个体所能覆盖的音色范围极广；因为情绪变化可能伴随音色的剧烈改变，其幅度甚至超过正常世界中不同乐器之间的差别。</p>
<p>与那色彩斑斓却几乎不变的背景——或者说景观——相反，这些生命始终处于运动之中。它们总能保持自己的个体性，也就是基本的音调结构身份；可它们会沿“层级”维度退远或靠近，也会在音阶上上下奔走。与此同时，它们还不停做出一种波纹般的音乐姿态游戏。常常有一个生物沿音阶向上或向下移动，途中遇见另一个。此时二者要么干脆彼此穿透、交叉而过，就像池塘上两列横向水波穿过彼此；要么双方都会稍稍调整自己的形式，显然是为了从对方身边挤过去，避免发生“碰撞”。而这个世界里的碰撞，似乎很像我们音乐中的不协和。有时，为避免碰撞，一个生物只需要略微改变自身的音调形态；可有时它必须在另一个维度——也就是我所谓的“层级”维度——远远让开。于是它会暂时变得听不见。</p>
<p>又一个发现突然在我心中闪现，同样带着一种古怪的熟悉感：我自己在这个世界也有一个“身体”。它是所有声音对象里离我“最近”的那个。它近得如此彻底、如此显而易见，以至于我从来没有注意到它，直到它不得不行动起来。事情发生得很突然。一个正在移动的生物无意间撞上了我音乐身体上的一个小部位。那一点轻微的侵犯像针刺般带来尖锐的疼痛。我先是本能地、随即又有意识地重新调整了自己的音乐形状，以避免进一步冲突。就这样，我发现——或者重新发现——自己在这个世界里拥有自主行动的能力。</p>
<p>同时，我还迸发出一道响亮而闪耀的音乐姿态；我立刻知道，这是一句有意义的话。事实上，我用那个世界的语言说的是：“见鬼，那是我的脚趾头！”</p>
<p>对方发出一阵回应的低语，满含歉意。</p>
<p>这时，一个新来者从寂静的远方靠近，加入那群在我周围嬉戏的同伴。这个生物对我有极强的吸引力，而且熟悉得令人心痛。她轻盈柔韧的形体，她抒情却又带一丝讥诮的动作，使整片丛林忽然变成了阿卡狄亚乐土。令我欣喜的是，她似乎也认识我，而且并非全然不喜欢我。她做了一个快活的姿势，招呼我加入游戏。</p>
<p>我第一次不只是改变音乐肢体的姿态，而是让整个身体移动起来，同时沿音高维度和“层级”维度前进。我一靠近，她便笑着从我身边溜走。我追上去；可没多久，她就消失在丛林和遥远的寂静之中。自然，我决定追她。我已经无法想象没有她的生活。而在我们两种本性的精妙和谐里，我仿佛看见了奇妙的创造可能。</p>
<p>请容我简短说明一下这个世界中移动的方法与感受。我发现，只要伸出一条音乐肢体，把它的末端编织进远处某个固定对象的声音图样——无论那个对象是在一个维度上，还是同时横跨两个维度——我便能在它上面获得一个支点，把整个身体拉向那里。随后，我又可以伸出另一条肢体，抓住更远的地方。这样，我便能像长臂猿一样，以极快而准确的速度在声音森林中攀行。每当我在任一维度上移动时，我对自身运动的体验，都只是周围世界朝相反方向移动。近处的对象变得更近，或者不那么近；远处的对象变得不那么远，或者进一步滑向远方，最终消失。同样，我沿音乐音阶向上或向下移动时，体验到的却是其他一切对象的音高整体变低或变高。</p>
<p>移动时，其他对象几乎不会给我造成阻力，除非发生不协和的碰撞，而这种碰撞通常可以通过改变自己的形状来避免。我还发现，我和另一个对象之间存在一定程度的不协和时，只会产生很轻微的阻力，而且并不疼痛。事实上，这种接触有时甚至很愉快。但尖锐刺耳的和弦冲突却是一种酷刑，根本无法长久维持。</p>
<p>我很快发现，自己沿音阶上下移动的范围是有限的。当我下降到比正常位置低许多个八度之处时，便开始感到压抑而迟钝。我越是费力向下，身体越不舒服；最终，我会陷入一种近似昏厥的状态，自行漂浮回原本的音乐平面。反过来，当我升到正常平面以上时，最初会感到兴奋振奋；可经过许多个八度，一种头重脚轻和眩晕便会袭来，片刻后我就摇摇晃晃地坠回自己通常栖居的那几个八度之内。</p>
<p>至于“层级”维度，我的移动能力似乎没有边界；而我寻找那位消失的女仙，主要也是沿这个维度前进。我不断穿越变化无穷的音调景观。有时，景观会向“层级”方向敞开成一片辽阔视野，远处是模糊的音乐对象；有时又向“音调”方向上下展开，显露出我之上和之下几百个八度的深渊与高空。有时，由于茂密的音乐“植被”，视野会收窄成一条隧道，高度不过两三个八度，我只能十分艰难地从中穿行。有时为了绕开无法穿透的物体，我不得不远远攀入高音区或低音区；有时在空旷地带，我只能从一个落脚点跳向另一个。</p>
<p>终于，我开始疲惫。移动变得令人厌恶，感知也开始不确定。甚至连我身体本身的形式，也失去了一部分原本令人愉悦的饱满。某种本能忽然驱使我做了一件令理智惊讶的事，尽管行动时我毫不犹豫。我靠近一些鲜美诱人的小小音乐对象——它们是非常简单、却又强健有力并能长期维持的音色与和声图样——然后把它们吃掉。也就是说，我把每一个对象的声音图样分解成更简单的图样，再把这些部分纳入自己和谐的形体之中。随后，我恢复了精神，继续上路。</p>
<p>不久，一群有智能的生物突然出现在前方，乱七八糟地朝我冲来。它们在惊慌中彼此推撞，拼命奔逃。它们的情绪音色表达出如此强烈的恐惧和惊骇，以至于我的音乐形体也被传染了。我连忙把自己向低音区移开几个八度，以躲开它们疯狂的路线——它们大多从高音区奔来——同时高声问出了什么事。它们从我身边逃过时，我只听清一声喊叫，大意可以译作：“大坏狼！”</p>
<p>我的恐惧消失了，因为这时我认出，这不过是一群非常年幼的生物。于是我安慰地笑起来，又问它们有没有碰见我正在寻找的那位可爱生物。需要说出她的名字时，我惊喜地发现，她那个音乐名字竟如此自然而甜美地从我心中涌出；我自己也为此偷偷笑了。可那些幼小生物只是爆发出更响亮、更悲切的尖叫，一路消失在远方。</p>
<p>我心中不安，却仍继续前行。没多久，我进入一大片空旷地带，在极远处听见一种不祥的低吼。我停下来，想把它听得更清楚。那声音正在靠近。它的形体从远方浮现，细节也渐渐可闻。很快我便认出，这绝不是什么吓唬孩子的妖怪，而是一头巨大凶猛的野兽。它的身体在低音区笨重地运动，四肢却推动着它以惊人的速度前进。尖锐刺耳的声音触手四下闪动，远远探入高音区，嗅探猎物的踪迹。</p>
<p>我终于意识到，自己亲爱的同伴很可能遭遇了什么命运，顿时因恐怖而恶心欲呕。我的整个音乐身体都发起抖来，因眩晕而摇摆不定。</p>
<p>我还没决定该怎么办，那野兽便发现了我——或者更准确地说，是“听见”了我——随即朝我猛冲过来，发出的轰鸣和尖啸仿佛一列疾驰的火车，又像一发正在逼近的炮弹。我转身逃跑。可很快我意识到，双方距离仍在缩短，于是扑进前方一片混乱声音构成的灌木丛，那地方位于较高的音域。我尽力调整自己的音乐形体和音色，让它与周围这片荒野融为一体，同时继续往上攀爬。我希望既能藏住自己，又能逃出那怪物触手可及的范围。高处让我几乎昏厥，我终于选中一个落脚处，把音乐肢体编入当地固定对象的图样之中。就这样锚定自己以后，我一动不动地等待。</p>
<p>那野兽现在放慢了速度，一边靠近，一边四处嗅探我的踪迹。不久，它来到我正下方，远在低音区深处。此时它的身体听得再清楚不过：那是一团阴森可怖的嘈杂，由咆哮和打嗝般的声音混成。它刺耳的触手在我下面挥舞，就像一个攀在悬崖上的人俯视脚下随风摇曳的树梢。它依旧在寻找，最后从我下方经过。我的宽慰如此强烈，以至于一瞬间失去了意识，身体也滑落了好几个八度，等恢复过来时已经太晚。这一下移动暴露了我的位置。猛兽折返回来，笨拙地朝我攀爬。高音区的高度很快阻止了它继续上升，可它还是伸出一条触手够到了我——那是一串尖叫般的琶音。我拼命想把自己进一步撤向高音区，可怪物的肢体已经编入我肉体的声音图样。无论怎样疯狂挣扎，我还是被一点点拖下去，拖下去，拖进令人窒息的低音深处。在那里，由声音组成的獠牙和利爪把我痛苦地撕得四分五裂。</p>
<p>随后，我忽然在音乐厅里醒来。四周一片椅子拖动的刺耳混响，观众正在准备离场。</p>

<p>THE ROOM WAS OVERCROWDED AND STUFFY. THE music seemed to have no intelligible form. It was a mere jungle of noise. Now one instrument and now another blared out half a tune, but every one of these abortive musical creatures was killed before it had found its legs. Some other and hostile beast fell upon it and devoured it, or the whole jungle suffocated it.</p>
<p>The strain of following this struggle for existence wearied me. I closed my eyes, and must have fallen asleep; for suddenly I woke with a start. Or seemed to wake. Something queer had happened. The music was still going on, but I was paralysed. I could not open my eyes. I could not shout for help. I could not move my body, nor feel it. I had no body.</p>
<p>Something had happened to the music, too, and to my hearing. But what? The tissue of sounds seemed to have become incomparably more voluminous and involved. I am not musical; but suddenly I realized that this music had overflowed, so to speak, into all the intervals between the normal semitones, that it was using not merely quarter-tones but “centitones” and “millitones,” with an effect that would surely have been a torture to the normal ear. To me, in my changed state, it gave a sense of richness, solidity, and vitality quite lacking in ordinary music. This queer music, moreover, had another source of wealth. It reached up and down over scores of octaves beyond the range of normal hearing. Yet I could hear it.</p>
<p>As I listened, I grew surprisingly accustomed to this new jargon. I found myself easily distinguishing all sorts of coherent musical forms in this world of sound. Against an obscure, exotic background of more or less constant chords and fluttering “leafage,” so to speak, several prominent and ever-changing sound-figures were playing. Each was a persistent musical object, though fluctuating in detail of gesture and sometimes ranging bodily up or down the scale.</p>
<p>Suddenly I made a discovery which should have been incredible, yet it seemed to me at the time quite familiar and obvious. I found myself recognizing that these active sound-figures were alive, even intelligent. In the normal world, living things are perceived as changing patterns of visible and tangible characters. In this mad world, which was coming to seem to me quite homely, patterns not of colour and shape but of sound formed the perceptible bodies of living things. When it occurred to me that I had fallen into a land of “program music” I was momentarily disgusted. Here was a whole world that violated the true canons of musical art! Then I reminded myself that this music was not merely telling but actually living its story. In fact it was not art but life. So I gave rein to my interest.</p>
<p>Observing these creatures that disported themselves before me, I discovered, or rather rediscovered, that though this world had no true space, such as we perceive by sight and touch, yet it did have a sort of space. For in some sense these living things were moving in relation to me and in relation to one another. Apparently the “space” of this world consisted of two dimensions only, and these differed completely in quality. One was the obvious dimension of tonality, or pitch, on the subtle “keyboard” of this world. The other was perceived only indirectly. It corresponded to the heard nearness or remoteness of one and the same instrument in the normal world. Just as we see things as near and far through the signification of colour and perspective, so in this strange world, certain characters of timbre, of harmonics, of overtones, conveyed a sense of “nearness”; others a sense of “distance.” A peculiar blatancy, often combined with loudness, meant “near”; a certain flatness, or ghostliness of timbre, generally combined with faintness, meant “far.” An object receding in this “level” dimension (as I called it) would gradually lose its full-bodied timbre, and its detail and preciseness. At the same time it would become fainter, and at last inaudible.</p>
<p>I should add that each sound-object had also its own characteristic timbre, almost as though each thing in this world were a theme played by one and the same instrument. But I soon discovered that in the case of living things the timbre-range of each individual was very wide; for emotional changes might be accompanied by changes of timbre even greater than those which distinguish our instruments.</p>
<p>In contrast with the variegated but almost changeless background or landscape, the living things were in constant movement. Always preserving their individuality, their basic identity of tonal pattern, they would withdraw or approach in the “level” dimension or run up and down the scale. They also indulged in a ceaseless rippling play of musical gesture. Very often one of these creatures, travelling up or down the scale, would encounter another. Then either the two would simply interpenetrate and cross one another, as transverse trains of waves on a pond; or there would be some sort of mutual readjustment of form, apparently so as to enable them to squeeze past one another without “collision.” And collision in this world seemed to be much like dissonance in our music. Sometimes, to avoid collision, a creature needed merely to effect a slight alteration in its tonal form, but sometimes it had to move far aside, so to speak, in the other dimension, which I have called the “level” dimension. Thus it became for a while inaudible.</p>
<p>Another discovery now flashed upon me, again with curious familiarity. I myself had a “body” in this world. This was the “nearest” of all the sound-objects. It was so “near” and so obvious that I never noticed it till it was brought into action. This happened unexpectedly. One of the moving creatures inadvertently came into collision with a minor part of my musical body. The slight violation of my substance stabbed me with a little sharp pain. Immediately, by reflex action and then purposefully, I readjusted my musical shape, so as to avoid further conflict. Thus it was that I discovered or rediscovered the power of voluntary action in this world.</p>
<p>I also emitted a loud coruscation of musical gesture, which I at once knew to be significant speech. In fact I said in the language of that world, “Damn you, that’s my toe, that was.” There came from the other an answering and apologetic murmur.</p>
<p>A newcomer now approached from the silent distance to join my frolicking companions. This being was extremely attractive to me, and poignantly familiar. Her lithe figure, her lyrical yet faintly satirical movement, turned the jungle into Arcadia. To my delight I found that I was not unknown to her, and not wholly unpleasing. With a gay gesture she beckoned me into the game.</p>
<p>For the first time I not only changed the posture of my musical limbs but moved bodily, both in the dimension of pitch and the “level” dimension. As soon as I approached, she slipped with laughter away from me. I followed her; but very soon she vanished into the jungle and into the remoteness of silence. Naturally I determined to pursue her. I could no longer live without her. And in the exquisite harmony of our two natures I imagined wonderful creative potentialities.</p>
<p>Let me explain briefly the method and experience of locomotion in this world. I found that, by reaching out a musical limb and knitting its extremity into the sound-pattern of some fixed object at a distance, in either dimension or both, I obtained a purchase on the object, and could draw my whole body toward it. I could then reach out another limb to a still farther point. Thus I was able to climb about the forest of sound with the speed and accuracy of a gibbon. Whenever I moved, in either dimension, I experienced my movement merely as a contrary movement of the world around me. Near objects became nearer, or less near; remote objects became less remote, or slipped further into the distance and vanished. Similarly my movement up or down the musical scale appeared to me as a deepening or heightening of the pitch of all other objects.</p>
<p>In locomotion I experienced no resistance from other objects save in the collision of dissonance, which I could generally avoid by altering my shape. I discovered that a certain degree of dissonance between myself and another offered only very slight resistance and no pain. Indeed, such contacts might be pleasurable. But harsh discords were a torture and could not be maintained.</p>
<p>I soon found that there was a limit to my possible movement up and down the scale. At a point many octaves below my normal situation I began to feel oppressed and sluggish. As I toiled downwards my discomfort increased, until, in a sort of swoon, I floated up again to my native musical plane. Ascending far above this plane, I felt at first exhilaration; but after many octaves a sort of light-headedness and vertigo overtook me, and presently I sank reeling to the few octaves of my normal habitat.</p>
<p>In the “level” dimension there seemed to be no limit to my power of locomotion, and it was in this dimension chiefly that I sought the vanished nymph. I pressed forward through ever-changing tonal landscapes. Sometimes they opened out into “level” vistas of remote, dim, musical objects, or into “tonal” vistas, deep and lofty, revealing hundreds of octaves above and below me. Sometimes the view narrowed, by reason of the dense musical “vegetation,” to a mere tunnel, no more than a couple of octaves in height. Only with difficulty could I work my way along such a passage. Sometimes, in order to avoid impenetrable objects, I had to clamber far into the treble or the bass. Sometimes, in empty regions, I had to leap from perch to perch.</p>
<p>At last I began to weary. Movement became repugnant, perception uncertain. Moreover the very form of my body lost something of its pleasant fullness. Instinct now impelled me to an act which surprised my intellect though I performed it without hesitation. Approaching certain luscious little musical objects, certain very simple but vigorous little enduring patterns of timbre and harmony, I devoured them. That is, I broke down the sound-pattern of each one into simpler patterns; and these I incorporated into my own harmonious form. Then I passed on, refreshed.</p>
<p>Presently I was confronted by a crowd of the intelligent beings tumbling helter-skelter toward me and jostling one another in their haste. Their emotional timbre expressed such fear and horror that my own musical form was infected with it. Hastily moving myself several octaves toward the bass to avoid their frantic course, which was mostly in the treble, I shouted to them to tell me what was the matter. As they fled past I distinguished only a cry which might be translated, “The Big Bad Wolf.”</p>
<p>My fear left me, for now I recognized that this was a flock of very young creatures. So I laughed reassuringly and asked if they had encountered the lovely being whom I was seeking. And I laughed to myself at the ease and sweetness with which her musical name came to me when I needed it. They answered only with an augmented scream of infantile grief, as they faded into the distance.</p>
<p>Disturbed, I pursued my journey. Presently I came into a great empty region where I could hear a very remote but ominous growl. I halted, to listen to the thing more clearly. It was approaching. Its form emerged from the distance and was heard in detail. Soon I recognized it as no mere childish bogey but a huge and ferocious brute. With lumbering motion in the bass, its limbs propelled it at a surprising speed. Its harsh tentacles of sound, flickering hither and thither far up into the treble, nosed in search of prey.</p>
<p>Realizing at last the fate that had probably befallen my dear companion, I turned sick with horror. My whole musical body trembled and wavered with faintness.</p>
<p>Before I had decided what to do, the brute caught sight of me, or rather sound of me, and came pounding toward me with the roar and scream of a train, or an approaching shell. I fled. But soon realizing that I was losing ground, I plunged into a thicket of chaotic sound, which I heard ahead of me and well up in the treble. Adapting my musical form and colour as best I could to the surrounding wilderness, I continued to climb. Thus I hoped both to conceal myself and escape from the reach of the creature’s tentacles. Almost fainting from the altitude, I chose a perch, integrating my musical limbs with the pattern of the fixed objects in that locality. Thus anchored, I waited, motionless.</p>
<p>The brute was now moving more slowly, nosing in search of me as it approached. Presently it lay immediately below me, far down in the bass. Its body was now all too clearly heard as a grim cacophony of growling and belching. Its strident tentacles moved beneath me like the waving tops of trees beneath a man clinging to a cliff face. Still searching, it passed on beneath me. Such was my relief that I lost consciousness for a moment and slipped several octaves down before I could recover myself. The movement revealed my position. The beast of prey returned, and began clambering awkwardly toward me. Altitude soon checked its progress, but it reached me with one tentacle, one shrieking arpeggio. Desperately I tried to withdraw myself farther into the treble, but the monster’s limb knit itself into the sound-pattern of my flesh. Frantically struggling, I was dragged down, down into the suffocating bass. There, fangs and talons of sound tore me agonizingly limb from limb.</p>
<p>Then suddenly I woke in the concert hall to a great confusion of scraping chairs. The audience was making ready to leave.</p>
