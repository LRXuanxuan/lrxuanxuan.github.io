<h2>第十九章</h2>
<p>“你好。”霸王龙说。“我叫埃米，今年六岁。你叫什么名字？”</p>
<p>“卡莫迪。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“我是他的奖品。”奖品说。</p>
<p>“你们两个看起来都好奇怪。”埃米说。“你们不像我以前见过的任何人。我见过异齿龙、似鸵龙、甲龙，还有好多好多别的。你们住在这附近吗？”</p>
<p>“嗯，也算吧。”卡莫迪说。随后，他想到时间本身也具有维度，又补充道，“不过严格来说，其实不算。”</p>
<p>“哦。”埃米说。他像孩子一样盯着他们，随后陷入沉默。卡莫迪也盯着他看，被那颗巨大而阴森的脑袋迷住了。那脑袋比老虎机或者啤酒桶还大，狭长的嘴里密密排列着牙齿，活像一排排细长匕首。确实可怕！只有那双眼睛与恐龙其余部分的不祥外貌唱反调——圆圆的，温和的，蓝色的，满是信任。</p>
<p>“那么，”埃米终于说，“你们在公园里干什么？”</p>
<p>“这里是公园？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“当然是公园！”埃米说。“这是给小孩子玩的公园。你虽然特别小，可我觉得你不像小孩子。”</p>
<p>“你说得对，我不是小孩子。”卡莫迪说。“我是误打误撞闯进你们公园的。我想，也许我应该和你爸爸谈谈。”</p>
<p>“好呀。”埃米说。“爬到我背上来，我带你去找他。还有，别忘了，是我发现你们的。把你那个朋友也带上，他真奇怪！”</p>
<p>卡莫迪把奖品塞进口袋，爬上霸王龙的背，在埃米坚硬得像铁一样的皮肤褶皱里找到手脚可以攀住的地方。等他在恐龙颈部坐稳，埃米立刻转身，朝西南方向小跑起来。</p>
<p>“我们去哪儿？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“去找我爸爸。”</p>
<p>“对，可你爸爸在哪里？”</p>
<p>“他在城里上班啊。不然还能在哪儿？”</p>
<p>“当然，还能在哪儿呢。”卡莫迪说。埃米开始飞奔，他赶紧抓得更紧了一些。</p>
<p>奖品从卡莫迪口袋里闷声说道：“这一切实在太奇怪了。”</p>
<p>“在这里最奇怪的明明是你。”卡莫迪提醒它。随后，他放松下来，开始享受这趟旅程。</p>
<p>当地人当然不把那座城市叫作“恐龙城”，可卡莫迪实在想不出别的称呼。它距离公园大约两英里。首先，他们来到一条道路——严格说是一条宽阔的土路，被无数恐龙的脚踩得像混凝土一样坚硬。他们沿路前进，看见许多鸭嘴龙躺在路旁柳树下睡觉，偶尔还会用低沉甜美的嗓音合唱几声。卡莫迪问起它们，埃米却只肯说，他爸爸认为这些家伙“是个真正的大问题”。</p>
<p>道路经过一片片桦树、枫树、月桂和冬青树林。每片树林下面都有十来只恐龙，有目的地来回活动，有的刨土，有的清走垃圾。卡莫迪问它们在干什么。</p>
<p>“收拾家务呗。”埃米轻蔑地说。“家庭主妇除了这个还能干什么。”</p>
<p>他们来到一片高地台地。越过最后一处独立的小树林以后，他们突然一头扎进一片森林。</p>
<p>这片森林显然并非自然生长；到处都能看出人为栽植的痕迹，而且规划时显然颇有远见。森林外围是一条很宽的无花果、面包果、榛树和胡桃树带。再往里，是几排间距漂亮、高大而树干纤细的银杏。之后，就只剩松树，偶尔夹杂一棵云杉。</p>
<p>他们越往森林深处走，恐龙就越密集。其中大多数都是兽脚类——像埃米一样的肉食性霸王龙。不过奖品也指出了几种鸟脚类，以及数以百计的角龙类分支成员，其中最显眼的当然是长着巨角的三角龙。几乎所有恐龙都在树林里小跑。大地在它们脚下震动，树木不断颤抖，一团团尘土被扬进空中。侧腹擦过披甲的侧腹，碰撞只能靠突然转弯、急刹或者猛然加速来避免。四处都是争抢路权的咆哮。几千只赶路恐龙同时出现的景象，几乎和它们的气味一样可怕——而后者简直压倒一切。</p>
<p>“到了。”埃米说。他停得太突然，卡莫迪差点从脖子上飞出去。“这就是我爸爸工作的地方！”</p>
<p>卡莫迪四下看了看，发现埃米把他带到一小片红杉林。那些巨树在整片森林中形成一座绿洲。两三只恐龙在红杉之间缓慢移动，步态几乎懒洋洋的，完全无视五十码外的喧嚣。卡莫迪判断，自己大概可以下地而不被踩死，于是小心翼翼地从埃米颈上滑下来。</p>
<p>“爸爸！”埃米大喊。“喂，爸爸，快来看我发现了什么！快看呀，爸爸！”</p>
<p>一只恐龙抬起头。他也是霸王龙，比埃米略大，蓝色皮肤上横着白色条纹。眼睛灰白而布满血丝。他非常缓慢而郑重地转过身。</p>
<p>“我究竟跟你说过多少次，”他问，“不要在这里飞奔？”</p>
<p>“对不起，爸爸，可是你看，我发现了——”</p>
<p>“你永远都‘对不起’。”霸王龙说。“但你从来不肯据此改变自己的行为。我已经和你妈妈讨论过这件事了，埃米，我们基本意见一致。我们谁都不想养出一个举止粗鲁、嗓门震天、只知道飙车，连雷龙那点礼貌都没有的家伙。我爱你，我的儿子，可你必须学会——”</p>
<p>“爸爸！训话能不能以后再讲？你先看看，求你了，看看我到底发现了什么！”</p>
<p>年长霸王龙的嘴绷紧了，尾巴不祥地甩了一下。但他还是低下头，顺着儿子伸出的前爪看过去，于是看见了卡莫迪。</p>
<p>“我的老天保佑！”他叫道。</p>
<p>“您好，先生。”卡莫迪说。“我叫托马斯·卡莫迪，是个人类。我想现在地球上应该没有别的人类，甚至还没有别的灵长类。至于我怎么来到这里，解释起来有点复杂。不过我是怀着和平目的来的，还有——呃——诸如此类。”他说到最后已经相当无力。</p>
<p>“不可思议！”埃米的父亲说。他转过头。“巴克斯利！你看见我看见的东西了吗？你听见我听见的东西了吗？”</p>
<p>巴克斯利是另一只霸王龙，年纪和埃米的父亲差不多。他说：“我看见了，博格，但我不相信。”</p>
<p>“一只会说话的哺乳动物！”博格惊叫道。</p>
<p>“我还是不信。”巴克斯利说。</p>
</section>
<section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="en" lang="en" aria-label="English original" hidden>
<p><b>CHAPTER 19</b></p>
<p>‘Hello,’ said the tyrannosaurus. ‘My name is Emie and I am six years old. What’s your name?’</p>
<p>‘Carmody,’ said Carmody.</p>
<p>‘And I’m his Prize,’ said the Prize.</p>
<p>‘Well, you both look very strange,’ said Emie. ‘You don’t look like anybody I’ve ever met before, and I’ve met a dimetrodon, and a struthiomimus, and a scolosaurus, and lots of others. Do you come from around here?’</p>
<p>‘Well, sort of,’ Carmody said. Then, reflecting on the dimensionality of time, he said, ‘But not really, actually.’</p>
<p>‘Oh,’ said Emie. Childlike, he stared at them and fell into a silence. Carmody stared back, fascinated by that huge, grim head, larger than a slot machine or a beer keg, the narrow mouth studded with teeth like rows of stilettos. Fearsome indeed! Only the eyes – which were round, mild, blue and trusting – refuted the rest of the dinosaur’s ominous appearance.</p>
<p>‘Well, so,’ Emie said at last, ‘what are you doing here in the park?’</p>
<p>‘Is this a park?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Sure, it’s a park!’ Emie said. ‘It’s a park for <i>kids,</i> and I don’t think you’re a kid, even though you are very small.’</p>
<p>‘You’re right, I’m not a kid,’ Carmody said. ‘I stumbled into your park by mistake. I think perhaps I should speak to your father.’</p>
<p>‘Hokay,’ Emie said. ‘Climb on my back and I’ll take you to him. And don’t forget, I discovered you. And bring along your friend. He’s <i>really</i> strange!’</p>
<p>Carmody slipped the Prize into his pocket and mounted the tyrannosaurus, finding hand and foot holds on the folds of Emie’s iron-tough skin. As soon as he was securely in place on the dinosaur’s neck, Emie wheeled and began to lope towards the southwest.</p>
<p>‘Where are we going?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘To see my father.’</p>
<p>‘Yes, but where <i>is</i> your father?’</p>
<p>‘He’s in the city, working at his job. Where else would he be?’</p>
<p>‘Of course, where else indeed?’ Carmody said, taking a firmer grip as Emie broke into a gallop.</p>
<p>From Carmody’s pocket, the Prize said, in a muffled voice, ‘This is all exceedingly strange.’</p>
<p>‘You’re the strange one around here,’ Carmody reminded him. Then he settled back to enjoy the ride.</p>
<p>They didn’t call it Dinosaursville, but Carmody could think of it in no other way. It lay about two miles from the park. First they came to a road, a wide trail, actually, stamped to the firmness of concrete by countless dinosaur feet. They followed it and passed many hadrosaurs sleeping beneath willow trees by the side of the road and occasionally harmonizing in low, sweet voices. Carmody asked about them, but Emie would only say that his father considered them a real problem.</p>
<p>The road went past groves of birch, maple, laurel and holly. Each grove had its dozen or so dinosaurs, moving purposefully beneath the branches, digging at the ground or pushing away refuse. Carmody asked what they were doing.</p>
<p>‘They’re tidying up,’ Emie said scornfully. ‘That’s all that housewives ever do.’</p>
<p>They had come to an upland plateau. They left the last individual grove behind and plunged abruptly into a forest.</p>
<p>Evidently it was not a natural growth; it showed many signs of having been planted purposefully and with considerable foresight. Its outer trees consisted of a broad belt of fig, breadfruit, hazelnut and walnut. Past that there were several nicely spaced rows of tall, slim-trunked gingkos. Then, there was nothing but pine trees and an occasional spruce.</p>
<p>As they moved deeper into the forest, it became more and more crowded with dinosaurs. Most of these were theropods – carnivorous tyrannosaurs like Emie. But the Prize also pointed out several ornithopods, and literally hundreds of the ceratopsia offshoot represented by the massively horned triceratops. Nearly all of them moved through the trees at a canter. The ground shook beneath their feet, the trees trembled, and clouds of dust were flung into the air. Flank scraped against armoured flank, collisions were avoided only by quick turns, abrupt halts and sudden accelerations. There was much bellowing for right-of-way. The sight of several thousand hurrying dinosaurs was almost as fearsome as their smell, which was overpowering.</p>
<p>‘Here we are,’ said Emie, stopping so quickly that Carmody was nearly thrown off his neck. ‘This is my dad’s place!’</p>
<p>Carmody looked around and saw that Emie had, brought him to a small grove of sequoias. The big trees formed an oasis within the forest. Two or three dinosaurs moved among the redwoods with a slow, almost languid pace, ignoring the turmoil fifty yards away. Carmody decided that he could get down without being trampled upon. Warily he slid off Emie’s neck.</p>
<p>‘Dad!’ Emie shouted. ‘Hey, Dad, just look what I found, look, Dad!’</p>
<p>One of the dinosaurs looked up. He was a tyrannosaur, somewhat larger than Emie, with white striations across his blue hide. His eyes were grey and bloodshot. He turned around with great deliberation.</p>
<p>‘How often,’ he asked, ‘have I asked you not to gallop here?’</p>
<p>‘I’m sorry, Dad, but look, I found –’</p>
<p>‘You are always “sorry,”’ the tyrannosaur said, ‘but you never see fit to modify your behaviour. I have spoken with your mother about this, Emie, and we are in, substantial agreement. Neither of us wishes to raise a graceless, loud-mouthed hot-rodder who doesn’t possess the manners of a brontosaurus. I love you, my son, but you must learn –’</p>
<p>‘Dad! Will you please save the lecture for later and look, just <i>look,</i> at what I’ve found!’</p>
<p>The elderly tyrannosaur’s mouth tightened and his tail flicked ominously. But he lowered his head, following the direction of his son’s outstretched forepaw, and saw Carmody.</p>
<p>‘Well bless my soul!’ he cried.</p>
<p>‘Good day, sir,’ Carmody said. ‘My name is Thomas Carmody. I am a human being. I don’t think there are any other humans on Earth just now, or even any primates. How I got here is a little difficult to explain, but I come in peace, and – and that sort of thing,’ he finished lamely.</p>
<p>‘Fantastic!’ Emie’s father said. He turned his head. ‘Baxley! Do you see what I see? Do you hear what I hear?’</p>
<p>Baxley was a tyrannosaur of about the same age as Emie’s father. He said, ‘I see it, Borg, but I don’t believe it.’</p>
<p>‘A talking mammal!’ Borg exclaimed.</p>
<p>‘I still don’t believe it,’ said Baxley.</p>
