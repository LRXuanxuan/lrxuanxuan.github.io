<h2>序言</h2>
<p>虽然这是一部虚构作品，但它并不自称是一部小说。它唯一的主人公是“人类”。既然本书的目的并非刻画一个个具体的人，也就没有刻意让书中寥寥几个人物各具鲜明个性。这里没有通常意义上的情节，只有一个主题：在这个进退失据的时代，人类如何努力掌握自身，并设法与宇宙相处。我试着用这样一个设想来呈现这一主题：二十亿年后的海王星上，生活着一个远比我们发达的人类物种，其中的一员进入我们的心灵，借我们的眼睛观察地球上的世界，却以他自己的智慧加以理解。他选中我们中的一个人作为传声筒，设法把自己的一些发现告诉我们。至于这份报告中的种种缺陷，则必须归咎于他所使用的这件“地球工具”的局限。</p>
<p>即使没有读过我两年前写的另一部幻想作品《最后与最初的人》，本书也完全可以独立理解。不过，读过前书的读者会发现，《伦敦的最后人》与它彼此补充。两部书中发言的都是同一个海王星人：上一次，他讲述从我们的时代直到他的时代之间人类的整个历程；这一次，他则要描述一种精神上的戏剧——照他的说法，这出戏隐藏在我们这个物种全部混乱的历史之下，并在今天走到危机关头。依照本书的设定，这次通讯发生在海王星历史上的一个时点：晚于前书主体叙事的时代，却早于前书的尾声。</p>
<p>“战争”一章的最后一节虽在一定程度上利用了我个人的经历，但仍然是虚构作品。</p>
<p>许多读者想必会很容易看出，我受到了杰拉尔德·赫德先生（Gerald Heard）那些极富启发性的著作的影响。我希望他能原谅我为了自己的目的而把他的一些思想作了变形。</p>
<p>我还要再次感谢 E. V. 里厄先生（E. V. Rieu），他提出了许多宝贵的批评和建议；也要感谢 L. C. 马丁教授及夫人——他们读过那份凌乱的手稿——若没有他们的批评与鼓励，这本书一定会比现在糟得多。最后，我要感谢我的妻子，既感谢她付出的辛劳，也感谢她给予的另一些帮助，而她本人显然并不懂得这些帮助究竟有多么重要。</p>
<p>请读者记住：从这里开始，直到“尾声”开篇之前，发言者都是一个来自极遥远未来的海王星人。</p>
<p>W. O. S. 1932年9月</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" class="stapledon-body" lang="en" hidden>
<h2>PREFACE</h2>
<p>THOUGH THIS IS a work of fiction, it does not pretend to be a novel. It has no hero but Man. Since its purpose is not the characterization of individual human beings, no effort has been made to endow its few persons with distinctive personalities. There is no plot, except the theme of man’s struggle in this awkward age to master himself and to come to terms with the universe. This theme I seek to present by imagining that a member of a much more developed human species, living on Neptune two thousand million years hence, enters into our minds to observe the Terrestrial field through our eyes but with his own intelligence. Using one of us as a mouthpiece, he contrives to tell us something of his findings. The shortcomings of his report must be attributed to the limitations of his Terrestrial instrument.</p>
<p>This book is intelligible without reference to another fantasy, which I produced two years ago, and called Last and First Men. But readers of that earlier book will find that Last Men in London is complementary to it. In both, the same Neptunian being speaks, formerly to tell the story of man’s career between our day and his, now to describe the spiritual drama which, he tells us, underlies the whole confused history of our species, and comes to its crisis today. The present book is supposed to be communicated from a date in Neptunian history later than the body of the earlier book, but before its epilogue.</p>
<p>The last section of the chapter on the War, though it makes use to some extent of personal experience, is none the less fiction.</p>
<p>It will be obvious to many readers that I have been influenced by the very suggestive work of Mr Gerald Heard. I hope he will forgive me for distorting some of his ideas for my own purpose.</p>
<p>My thanks are due once more to Mr E. V. Rieu for many valuable criticisms and suggestions; and to Professor and Mrs L. C. Martin (who read the untidy manuscript) for condemnation and encouragement without which the book would have been much worse than it is. Finally I would thank my wife both for hard labour, and for other help which she is apparently incapable of appreciating.</p>
<p>Let me remind the reader that henceforth and up to the opening of the Epilogue the speaker is a Neptunian man of the very remote future.</p>
<p>W. O. S. September 1932</p>
</section>
