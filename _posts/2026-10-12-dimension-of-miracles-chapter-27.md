<h2>第二十七章</h2>
<p>卡莫迪一边走一边看，一边看一边观察。</p>
<p>这里看起来，就像他认为这里应该看起来的样子。</p>
<p>梅普尔伍德剧院在他右手边。今天上映的是《巨象传奇》，一部意法合拍冒险片，由才华横溢的年轻导演雅克·马拉执导。正是这位导演给世界带来了感人至深的《我的伤口之歌》，以及节奏飞快的喜剧《巴黎十四次》。舞台上还有一项限时演出：新晋声乐组合“亚科嫩与真菌”。</p>
<p>“听起来像部挺好玩的电影。”卡莫迪评价道。</p>
<p>“不是我的菜。”奖品说。</p>
<p>卡莫迪在马文男装店门口停下，朝橱窗里看去。他看见乐福鞋、鞍鞋、犬牙格夹克、宽大的花纹领带，以及配宽角领的白衬衫。隔壁文具店里，他扫了一眼最新一期《柯利尔周刊》，随手翻了翻《自由》，又看见《芒西》《黑猫》和《间谍》。当天早版的《太阳报》刚刚上市。</p>
<p>“怎么样？”奖品问。“是这里吗？”</p>
<p>“还在核对。”卡莫迪说。“不过目前看起来很有希望。”</p>
<p>他穿过街道，朝埃德加午餐店里面看去。</p>
<p>一点没变。</p>
<p>柜台边坐着一个漂亮姑娘，正用吸管喝汽水。卡莫迪一眼就认出了她。</p>
<p>“拉娜·特纳！嘿，拉娜，最近怎么样？”</p>
<p>“挺好的，汤姆。”拉娜说。“好久不见。”</p>
<p>“我高中时跟她约会过。”两人继续往前走时，卡莫迪向奖品解释。“真怪，过去的事一下子全回来了。”</p>
<p>“我想是吧。”奖品将信将疑地说。</p>
<p>下一个街角，是梅普尔伍德大道和南山路的交叉口。一名警察正在那里指挥交通，不过他还是抽空冲卡莫迪咧嘴一笑。</p>
<p>“那是伯特·兰卡斯特。”卡莫迪说。“哥伦比亚高中史上最强那支球队的全州明星后卫。还有，看那边！正在进五金店的那个人——刚才朝我挥手的那个！那是克利夫顿·韦布，我们高中校长。再往街那头看，看见那个金发女人了吗？那是珍·哈露。以前她在梅普尔伍德餐厅当服务员。”</p>
<p>他压低声音。</p>
<p>“大家都说她作风很开放。”</p>
<p>“你认识的人还真不少。”奖品说。</p>
<p>“那当然！我就是在这里长大的！哈露小姐正要进皮埃尔美容院。”</p>
<p>“皮埃尔你也认识？”</p>
<p>“当然。他现在是理发师，不过战争期间参加过法国抵抗运动。他全名叫什么来着？让-皮埃尔·奥蒙，对，就是这个！他娶了我们本地姑娘卡洛尔·隆巴德。”</p>
<p>“有意思。”奖品用一种明显无聊的语气说。</p>
<p>“反正对我来说很有意思。你看，那边又来了个我认识的人……您好，市长先生。”</p>
<p>“你好，汤姆。”那人说着，摘帽致意，继续往前走。</p>
<p>“那是弗雷德里克·马奇，我们的市长。”卡莫迪说。“这人可了不起！我现在还记得他和我们本地激进分子保罗·穆尼的那场辩论。老天，你绝对没听过那么精彩的！”</p>
<p>“嗯。”奖品说。“卡莫迪，这里面有点古怪。有种诡异的感觉，有什么地方不对。你真的没感觉吗？”</p>
<p>“没有。”卡莫迪说。“我都告诉你了，我是跟这些人一起长大的。我了解他们，比了解我自己还清楚。嘿，那边是宝莲·高黛。她是图书馆助理。嗨，宝莲！”</p>
<p>“嗨，汤姆。”那个女人说。</p>
<p>“我不喜欢这里。”奖品说。</p>
<p>“我和她其实不算特别熟。”卡莫迪说。“她以前跟米尔本一个叫亨弗莱·鲍嘉的男孩子交往。你能想象吗？那家伙永远戴领结。他有一次还和学校管理员朗·钱尼打了一架，而且把他揍赢了。我记得这事，是因为当时我正在跟琼·哈沃克约会，而她最好的朋友是玛娜·洛伊，玛娜又认识鲍嘉，所以——”</p>
<p>“卡莫迪！”奖品急切地说。“小心！你听说过<strong>伪适应</strong>吗？”</p>
<p>“别胡说。”卡莫迪说。“我告诉你，我认识这些人！我就是在这里长大的，而且这里曾经是个他妈特别适合长大的地方！那时候的人不像现在，全是一团一团没区别的肉块；人是真正代表某些东西的。那时候人人都是独立个体，不是一群人挤成的乌合之众！”</p>
<p>“你真的确定吗？你的捕食者——”</p>
<p>“少来，我不想再听那一套。”卡莫迪说。“看！那是大卫·尼文！他爸妈是英国人。”</p>
<p>“这些人正在朝你走过来。”奖品说。</p>
<p>“当然会啊。”卡莫迪说。“他们已经很久没见我了。”</p>
<p>他站在街角。朋友们从人行道、马路、商店和店铺里不断涌出来，朝他走来。</p>
<p>足足有几百个人。</p>
<p>每个人都在微笑。</p>
<p>每个人都是老朋友。</p>
<p>他看见艾伦·拉德、多萝西·拉穆尔和巴斯特·克拉布。那边还有斯宾塞·屈塞、莱昂内尔·巴里摩尔、弗雷迪·巴塞洛缪、约翰·韦恩、弗朗西斯·法默——</p>
<p>“这里肯定有问题。”奖品说。</p>
<p>“什么问题都没有。”卡莫迪坚持。</p>
<p>他的朋友们全来了。他们不断靠近，向他伸出双手。自从离开家以后，他从未像现在这样快乐。他简直不敢相信，自己居然曾经忘记这里原本是什么样子。</p>
<p>可现在，他全想起来了。</p>
<p>“卡莫迪！”奖品大喊。</p>
<p>“干什么？”</p>
<p>“你们的世界里，平时永远都有这种音乐吗？”</p>
<p>“你说什么？”</p>
<p>“我说音乐。”奖品说。“你没听见？”</p>
<p>卡莫迪第一次注意到它。</p>
<p>一支交响乐团正在演奏，可他完全判断不出声音来自哪里。</p>
<p>“这音乐响多久了？”</p>
<p>“从我们到这里开始就一直有。”奖品告诉他。“你刚沿街往下走的时候，是轻柔低沉的鼓声；经过电影院时，小号吹起一段欢快旋律；等你往午餐店里看的时候，又变成了几百把小提琴演奏的甜腻抒情曲。然后——”</p>
<p>“那是背景音乐。”卡莫迪呆呆地说。“这一整套鬼东西都有配乐，而我居然一点没注意。”</p>
<p>法兰奇·汤恩伸手碰了碰他的袖子。</p>
<p>加里·库珀一只大手落在他的肩上。</p>
<p>莱尔德·克雷加给了他一个亲热的熊抱。</p>
<p>秀兰·邓波儿一把抱住他的右脚。</p>
<p>其他人也越围越紧。</p>
<p>所有人仍然微笑着。</p>
<p>“西斯赖特！”卡莫迪大喊。“看在上帝的份上，西斯赖特！”</p>
<p>随后发生的一切快得有些超过了他的理解能力。</p>
</section>
<section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="en" lang="en" aria-label="English original" hidden>
<p><b>CHAPTER 27</b></p>
<p>Carmody looked as he walked, and observed as he looked. It seemed like the place he thought it should seem like. The Maplewood Theatre was on his right; today’s feature was <i>The Saga of Elephantine,</i> an Italian-French adventure film directed by Jacques Marat, the brilliant young director who had given the world the deeply moving <i>Song of My Wounds</i> and the swiftly paced comedy <i>Paris Times Fourteen.</i> On the stage, for a limited engagement only, was the new vocal group, Iakonnen and the Fungi.</p>
<p>‘Sounds like a fun film,’ Carmody remarked.</p>
<p>‘Not my sort of thing,’ the Prize said.</p>
<p>Carmody stopped at Marvin’s Haberdashery and looked in the window. He saw loafers and saddle shoes, hound’s-tooth check jackets, wide, boldly patterned neckties, white shirts with spread collars. Next to it, at the stationery store, he glanced at the current <i>Colliers,</i> leafed through <i>Liberty,</i> noticed <i>Munsey’s, Black Cat,</i> and <i>The Spy.</i> The morning edition of <i>The Sun</i> had just come out.</p>
<p>‘Well?’ the Prize asked. ‘Is this the place?’</p>
<p>‘I’m still checking,’ Carmody said. ‘But it looks pretty favourable so far.’</p>
<p>He crossed the street and looked into Edgar’s Luncheonette. It hadn’t changed. There was a pretty girl sitting at the counter, sipping a soda. Carmody recognized her at once.</p>
<p>‘Lana Turner! Hey, how are you, Lana?’</p>
<p>‘I’m fine, Tom,’ Lana said. ‘Long time no see.’</p>
<p>‘I used to date her in high school,’ he explained to the Prize as they walked on. ‘It’s funny how it all comes back to you.’</p>
<p>‘I suppose so,’ the Prize said doubtfully.</p>
<p>At the next corner, the intersection of Maplewood Avenue and South Mountain Road, there was a policeman. He was directing traffic, but he took time to grin at Carmody.</p>
<p>‘That’s Burt Lancaster,’ Carmody said. ‘He was all-state fullback on the best team Columbia High School ever had. And look, over there! That man going into the hardware store, the one who waved at me! That’s Clifton Webb, our high-school principal. And down the block, do you see that blonde woman? That’s Jean Harlow. She used to be the waitress at the Maplewood Restaurant.’ He lowered his voice. ‘Everybody said she was <i>fast.’</i></p>
<p>‘You seem to know a lot of people,’ the Prize said.</p>
<p>‘Well, of course I do! I was raised here! Miss Harlow is going into Pierre’s Beauty Parlour.’</p>
<p>‘Do you know Pierre, also?’</p>
<p>‘Sure. He’s a hairdresser now, but during the war he was in the French Resistance. What was his name again? Jean-Pierre Aumont, that’s it! He married one of our local girls, Carole Lombard.’</p>
<p>‘Interesting,’ the Prize said in a bored voice.</p>
<p>‘Well, it’s interesting for me. Here comes a man I know … Good day, Mr Mayor.’</p>
<p>‘Good day, Tom,’ the man said, and tipped his hat and walked on.</p>
<p>‘That’s Fredric March, our mayor,’ Carmody said. ‘He’s a tremendous person! I can still remember the debate between him and our local radical, Paul Muni. Boy, you never heard anything like it!’</p>
<p>‘Hmm,’ said the Prize. ‘There is something strange about all this, Carmody. Something uncanny, something not right. Don’t you feel it?’</p>
<p>‘No, I don’t,’ Carmody said. ‘I’m telling you, I grew up with these people, I know them better than I know myself. Hey, there’s Paulette Goddard over there. She’s the assistant librarian. Hi, Paulette!’</p>
<p>‘Hi, Tom,’ the woman said.</p>
<p>‘I don’t like this,’ the Prize said.</p>
<p>‘I never knew her very well,’ Carmody said. ‘She used to go with a boy from Millburn named Humphrey Bogart. He always wore bow ties, can you imagine that? He had a fight once with Lon Chaney, the school janitor. Licked him, too. I remember that because I was dating June Havoc at the time, and her best friend was Myrna Loy, and Myrna knew Bogart, and –’</p>
<p>‘Carmody!’ the Prize said urgently. ‘Watch yourself! Have you ever heard of pseudo-acclimatization?’</p>
<p>‘Don’t be ridiculous,’ Carmody said. ‘I tell you, I know these people! grew up here, and it was a damned good place to grow up in! People weren’t just blobs like they are now; people really stood for something. People were <i>individuals</i> then, not crowds!’</p>
<p>‘Are you quite sure of this? Your predator –’</p>
<p>‘Rats, I don’t want to hear any more about it,’ Carmody said. ‘Look! There’s David Niven! His parents are English.’</p>
<p>‘These people are coming towards you,’ the Prize said.</p>
<p>‘Well, sure they are,’ Carmody said. ‘They haven’t seen me for a long time.’</p>
<p>He stood on the corner and his friends came down the pavement and the street, out of stores and shops. There were literally hundreds of them, all smiling, all old friends. He spotted Alan Ladd, Dorothy Lamour and Larry Buster Crabbe. And over there he saw Spencer Tracy, Lionel Barrymore, Freddy Bartholomew, John Wayne, Frances Farmer –</p>
<p>‘There’s something wrong with this,’ the Prize said.</p>
<p>‘Nothing’s wrong,’ Carmody insisted. His friends were all present, they were moving closer to him, holding out their hands, and he was happier than he had ever been since leaving his home. He was amazed that he could have forgotten how it had been. But he remembered now.</p>
<p>‘Carmody!’ the Prize shouted.</p>
<p>‘What is it?’</p>
<p>‘Is there always this music in your world?’</p>
<p>‘What are you talking about?’</p>
<p>‘I’m talking about the music,’ the Prize said. ‘Don’t you hear it?’</p>
<p>Carmody noticed it for the first time. A symphony orchestra was playing, but he couldn’t tell where it was coming from.</p>
<p>‘How long has that been going on?’</p>
<p>‘Ever since we got here,’ the Prize told him. ‘When you started down the street there was a soft thunder of drums. Then, when you passed the theatre, a lively air was played on a trumpet. This changed, when you looked into the luncheonette, to a rather saccharine melody played by several hundred violins. Then –’</p>
<p>‘That was background music,’ Carmody said dully. ‘This whole damned thing was scored, and I didn’t even notice it.’</p>
<p>Franchot Tone reached out and touched his sleeve. Gary Cooper dropped a big hand on his shoulder. Laird Cregar gave him an affectionate bear hug. Shirley Temple seized his right foot. The others pressed closer, all still smiling.</p>
<p>‘Seethwright!’ Carmody shouted. ‘For God’s sake, Seethwright!’</p>
<p>After that, things happened a little too fast for his comprehension.</p>
