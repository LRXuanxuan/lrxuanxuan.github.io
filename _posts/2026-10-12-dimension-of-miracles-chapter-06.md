<h2>第六章</h2>
<p>也许这是人类历史上的第一次：一个人真正、字面意义上地“把场景劈开然后离场”。从卡莫迪自己的角度看，他压根没动；动的是其余一切。使者和办事员融进背景。银河中心扁了下去，看起来无可置疑地像一幅巨大而画工拙劣的壁画。</p>
<p>接着，壁画左上角出现一道裂缝。裂缝扩大、延长，一路奔向右下角。两边的边缘向后卷起，露出彻底的黑暗。随后，那幅壁画——或者说银河中心——像两幅卷帘一样分别卷起，连一缕残迹都没留下。</p>
<p>“别担心，他们是靠镜子做出来的。”奖品在他耳边低语。</p>
<p>这个解释比刚才发生的事情本身更让卡莫迪担心。不过他严密控制住自己，又更加严密地攥住奖品。黑暗变得彻底、绝对，没有声音，也没有光线，是深空的典范。卡莫迪忍受了它，至于究竟持续了多久，则无法理解。</p>
<p>随后，场景骤然成形。他正站在地面上，呼吸着空气。他看见白骨色的荒凉群山，还有一条冻结的熔岩河。一阵微弱、死滞的风吹到脸上。头顶悬着三颗很小的红太阳。</p>
<p>这个地方给人的异星感，比银河中心更加直接；即便如此，卡莫迪仍觉得松了口气。他在梦里见过类似的地方；银河中心却是噩梦才有的东西。</p>
<p>他突然一惊，意识到奖品已经不在手里。自己怎么会把它弄丢？他慌忙四下寻找，最后发现一条小小的绿色束带蛇正盘在他的脖子上。</p>
<p>“是我，”蛇说道。“我是你的奖品。只不过换了个形态。你看，形态是总体环境的函数，而我们奖品对环境影响尤其敏感。别害怕。我还跟你在一起，小子；我们将并肩把墨西哥从那个花花公子马克西米连阴沉的外国魔掌中解放出来。”</p>
<p>“啊？”</p>
<p>“类比啊！”奖品要求道。“你看，博士，尽管我们奖品具有很高的智能，却没有自己的语言。我们也根本不需要什么独有语言，因为我们总是被颁发给各种各样的外星人。解决说话问题其实很简单，只是偶尔会有点让人不安：我直接接一条支线到你的联想库里，从里面抽取需要的词，拼出来表达我的意思。我刚才那些词有没有把意思表达清楚？”</p>
<p>“什么都谈不上特别清楚，”卡莫迪坦白道。“不过我想我明白了。”</p>
<p>“乖孩纸，”奖品说。“概念偶尔可能搅成一团，不过你最终肯定能解码出来。毕竟那都是你自己的联想。我正好有个跟这有关的有趣故事，不过恐怕得以后再讲。有件事马上就会发生，快得来不及。”</p>
<p>“等等，什么事？”</p>
<p>“卡莫迪，mon vieux，我没有时间解释全部。甚至可能连维持你生命运作所绝对必须知道的东西，都来不及解释。办事员和使者非常好心地把你送到了——”</p>
<p>“那两个杀人混蛋！”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“不要如此轻率地谴责谋杀，”奖品责备道。“这会暴露一种草率的性格。我记得有一首恰好谈到这点的酒神颂，改天背给你听。我说到哪儿了？哦，对，办事员和使者。他们俩付出了相当大的个人代价，把你送到了银河系中唯一一个或许——只是或许——能帮到你的地方。要知道，他们根本没有义务这么做。他们完全可以因为你未来会犯下的罪行，当场把你杀掉；也可以把你寄往你们行星最后一次已知的位置——而它现在肯定已经不在那里；还可以外推你们行星最可能的当前位置，把你送过去。不过他们的外推能力很差，那样做的结果多半会糟糕透顶。所以你看——”</p>
<p>“我到底在哪里？”卡莫迪问。“这里又会发生什么？”</p>
<p>“我正要说。”奖品回答。“这颗行星叫路尔西斯，想必一眼就看得出来。这里只住着一个居民——本土原生的梅利克隆。他在这里待了多久，没人记得；以后还会待多久，也没人能推算到尽头。梅利克隆可谓独一无二，而且独一无二得登峰造极、变本加厉。作为一个原生者，他无可模仿；作为一个种族，他无所不在；作为一个个体，他又与众不同。有人这样写他：‘看哪，那孤独而以自身命名的英雄！他与自己交配，而他自己又愤怒地反抗自己对自己发动的猛烈进攻！’”</p>
<p>“该死，”卡莫迪喊道，“你说起来跟参议院小组委员会似的没完没了，可就是一句有用的话都没说！”</p>
<p>“因为我慌了，”奖品说，声音里明显带着委屈。“老天爷啊，伙计，你以为我当初谈条件时料到会碰上这种事吗？我吓坏了，真的吓坏了，相信我。我只是想把事情说明白，因为要是我不赶紧掌舵，这一整团该死的东西就会像一座凝乳（curds）搭的房子一样轰然倒塌。”</p>
<p>“纸牌（cards）。”卡莫迪心不在焉地纠正。</p>
<p>“凝乳！”奖品冲他尖叫。“伙计，你见过一座凝乳房子倒下来吗？我见过，那场面一点也不好看。”</p>
<p>“听起来真够‘乳清（whey）离谱’的。”卡莫迪说着，莫名其妙地大笑起来。</p>
<p>“控制住自己！”奖品忽然急切地低声说。“整合！来一次提神醒脑的暂停！把你的丘脑拴到一颗星星上！因为他来了——梅利克隆本人！”</p>
<p>卡莫迪发现自己反倒出奇平静。他望向扭曲的地貌，没有看见任何先前没见过的东西。</p>
<p>“他在哪儿？”他问奖品。</p>
<p>“梅利克隆正在进化，以便能够同你说话。回答他时要大胆，但要讲策略。不要提及他的残障，那只会惹怒他。一定要——”</p>
<p>“什么残障？”</p>
<p>“一定要记住他唯一的限制。最重要的是，当他提出那个‘问题’时，要极其谨慎地回答。”</p>
<p>“等等！”卡莫迪说。“你除了把我弄得更糊涂，什么都没干！什么残障？什么限制？他的‘问题’又是什么？”</p>
<p>“别没完没了地缠我！”奖品说。“我受不了！而且现在我再也维持不了清醒了。为了你，我已经把冬眠推迟到了无法忍受的程度。再会，小子，别让人卖给你什么木头离心机。”</p>
<p>说完，束带蛇调整了一下自己的盘绕姿势，把尾巴塞进嘴里，睡着了。</p>
<p>“你这个该死的逃避大师，”卡莫迪怒道。“还好意思叫奖品？你就像死人眼皮上压的那几枚硬币一样——这种奖品！”</p>
<p>但奖品已经睡着，既听不见，也可能根本不愿听卡莫迪的咒骂。而事实上，也没有时间再做这种事了，因为下一刻，卡莫迪左侧那座荒山突然变成了一座狂暴喷发的火山。</p>
</section>
<section class="stapledon-body" data-story-lang="en" lang="en" aria-label="English original" hidden>
<p><b>CHAPTER 6</b></p>
<p>Perhaps for the first time in the history of the human race, a man actually and literally split a scene. From Carmody’s point of view, he didn’t move at all; it was everything else that moved. The Messenger and the Clerk melted into the background. The Galactic Centre went flat and took on an unmistakable resemblance to a large, poorly executed mural.</p>
<p>Then a crack appeared in the upper left-hand corner of the mural, widened and lengthened, and raced down to the lower right-hand corner. The edges curled back, revealing utter blackness. The mural, or Galactic Centre, rolled itself up like two windowshades and left not a wrack behind.</p>
<p>‘Don’t worry, they do it with mirrors,’ the Prize whispered to him.</p>
<p>The explanation worried Carmody more than the occurrence. But he kept a tight control on himself and a somewhat tighter control on the Prize. The blackness became complete and utter, soundless and sightless, a paradigm of deep space. Carmody endured it for as long as it lasted, which was incomprehensible.</p>
<p>Then, abruptly, the scene resolved. He was standing on ground breathing air. He could see barren mountains the colour of white bone and a river of frozen lava. A faint, stagnant breeze blew in his face. Overhead there were three tiny red suns.</p>
<p>This place seemed more immediately alien than the Galactic Centre; still, it was a relief to Carmody. He had encountered places like this in dreams; but the Galactic Centre was the stuff of nightmares.</p>
<p>With a sudden start he realized that the Prize was no longer in his hand. How could he have mislaid it? He looked around frantically, and found a small green garter snake curled around his neck.</p>
<p>‘It’s me,’ it said. ‘I’m your Prize. I am merely in a different shape. Form, you see, is a function of total environment, and we Prizes are peculiarly sensitive to environmental influences. You mustn’t let it alarm you. I’m still with you, keed, and together we will free Mexico from the sullen foreigner’s hand of the dandy Maximilian.’</p>
<p>‘Huh?’</p>
<p>‘Analogize!’ the Prize demanded. ‘You see, Doctor, despite our high intelligence, we Prizes have no language of our own. Nor do we have any need of an individual tongue, since we are always being awarded to various aliens. Solving the talk-problem is quite simple, but sometimes disconcerting; I merely run a tapline into your association bank and draw out what words I need to make my meaning clear. Have my words made my meaning clear?’</p>
<p>‘Nothing is very clear,’ Carmody confessed. ‘But I think I understand.’</p>
<p>‘Good bhoy,’ the Prize said. ‘The concepts may get a little jangled from time to time, but you will inevitably decipher them. After all, they are yours. I have a rather amusing story to tell in that regard, but I fear it must wait. Something is about to happen too quickly.’</p>
<p>‘Wait? What is it?’</p>
<p>‘Carmody, <i>mon vieux,</i> there is not time to explain all. There may not even be time to explain what you absolutely <i>must</i> know in order to maintain the operation of your life. The Clerk and the Messenger have very kindly sent you –’</p>
<p>‘Those murderous bastards!’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘You must not condemn murder so lightly,’ the Prize said reprovingly. ‘It bespeaks a careless nature. I remember a pertinent dithyramb to that effect, which I will recite later. Where was I? Oh yes, the Clerk and the Messenger. At considerable personal expense, those two worthies have sent you to the one place in Galaxy where you may – just possibly – be helped. They didn’t have to do it, you know. They could have killed you on the spot for future crimes; or they could have shipped you to your planet’s last known location, where it most assuredly is <i>not</i> now. Or they could have extrapolated its most likely present location and sent you there. But since they are poor extrapolators, the results of that would have been very bad indeed, in all likelihood. So you see –’</p>
<p>‘Where am I?’ Carmody asked, ‘and what is supposed to happen here?’</p>
<p>‘I was coming to that,’ the Prize said. ‘The planet is called Lursis, as is probably evident. It has only a single inhabitant – the autochthonous Melichrone, who has been here as long as anyone can remember, and will be here as long as anyone can project. Melichrone is <i>sui generis</i> in spades and with a vengeance. As an autochthone he is inimitable; as a race he is ubiquitous; as an individual he is different. Of him it has been written: “Lo, the lonely eponymous hero, mating himself with himself while furiously himself resists the angry onslaught of himself!”’</p>
<p>‘Damn you,’ Carmody shouted, ‘you’re talking away like a Senate subcommittee but you’re not saying anything!’</p>
<p>‘That’s because I’m flustered,’ the Prize said, with a noticeable whine. ‘Great Scott, man, d’ye think I bargained for anything like this? I’m shook, man, I’m real shook, believe you me, and I’m only trying to explicate because, if I don’t put my hand to the helm, this whole damned ball of wax will come crashing down like a house of kurds.’</p>
<p>‘Cards,’ Carmody corrected absentmindedly.</p>
<p>‘Kurds!’ the Prize screamed at him. ‘Man, have you ever <i>seen</i> a house of kurds come tumbling down? Well, I have, and it’s not a pretty sight.’</p>
<p>‘It sounds like a whey-out spectacle,’ Carmody said, and giggled immoderately.</p>
<p>‘Get hold of yourself!’ the Prize whispered with sudden urgency. ‘Integrate! Perform the pause that refreshes! Hitch your thalamus to a star! For now it comes, even Melichrone!’</p>
<p>Carmody found himself strangely calm. He looked out over the twisted landscape and saw nothing that he had not seen before.</p>
<p>‘Where is he?’ he asked the Prize.</p>
<p>‘Melichrone is evolving in order to be able to speak to you. Answer him boldly but with tact. Do not make any reference to his disability; that will only get him angry. Be sure –’</p>
<p>‘What disability?’</p>
<p>‘Be sure that you remember his one limitation. And above all, when he asks his Question, answer it with extreme care.’</p>
<p>‘Wait!’ Carmody said. ‘All you’ve done is to confuse me! What disability? What limitation? And what will his Question be?’</p>
<p>‘Stop nagging at me!’ the Prize said. ‘I cannot abide it! And now I can retain consciousness no longer. I have delayed my hibernation unbearably, and all for your sake. So long, keed, and don’t let them sell you any wooden centrifuges.’</p>
<p>And with that, the garter snake adjusted his coils, put his tail in his mouth and went to sleep.</p>
<p>‘You damned cop-out artist,’ Carmody fumed. ‘Call yourself a Prize? Like pennies on a dead man’s eyes, that’s the kind of Prize you are.’</p>
<p>But the Prize was asleep and unable or unwilling to hear Carmody’s invective. And there really was no time for that sort of thing, because in the next moment the barren mountain to Carmody’s left turned into a raging volcano.</p>
</section>
