<h2>ii. 困难与危险</h2>
<p>探索者在恍惚状态开始的一瞬间，究竟为什么能够摆脱自己所处年代与地点的限制，我无法向你们解释。只需知道，这个过程既依赖大脑中的一个特殊器官，也依赖一套必须经过漫长学徒期才能掌握的特殊技巧。就在那一刻的“醒来”中，探索者似乎会混乱地经历从远古直到自己时代为止，人类精神一切重要而连续的历史阶段。但那个至高瞬间的内容一过去便几乎完全从记忆中消失，因此我们无法对它作出明确说明。探索者似乎会在一道闪光中，从另一个时间维度观看事件的全部历史秩序。当然，他看到的只能是纲要式的结构；无比庞杂的细节绝不可能在一瞬间被把握。那种经验只能被描述为——恐怕这个说法本身仍然难懂——一切发生之事的总和，一切物质、心灵与精神流变的总和。人还会忍不住说，在这整个流变的人类一面，他仿佛看见了精神的一场漫长沉睡，其间偶尔被清醒时刻打断；又像看见一片巨大停滞，其中这里那里会掠过高度紧张活动的震颤。尽管这一切都在瞬间被看见，它却并不显得静止，而是真正活着，带着真实的经过与变化。</p>
<p>这个崇高时刻绝不能任其延续，因为它会致命。探索者必须立刻开始选择自己希望研究的那一部分历史秩序。为此，他必须采取一种基本的心态，或者说一种气质上的“味道”；他事先知道，这种心态正是目标时代所特有的。这个过程要求极高技巧，并伴随着大量生硬、古怪而令人神经紧绷的经验。一旦他开始成功进入适当的精神姿态，除了被选中的时代之外，其他一切时代都会从意识中退去；而那个被选中的时代则会越来越细致。接下来，他还必须进一步使自己的心态专门化，从那个时代中选出某一特定阶段或某种群体文化。这个过程还可以继续收窄，直到他把自己引入某个特定时刻、某个特定个体的心灵。一旦在个体心灵中取得立足点，此后他便可以从内部追随那个心灵的一切经验；或者，如果愿意，也可以停留在那个心灵的某一瞬间，对其中的微观细节进行研究。</p>
<p>这就是我们方法的核心。首先，我们必须获得那一瞬对“永恒”的瞥见；说得更准确些，是必须有一瞬间站到永恒的视角上。然后，我们要借助想象与同感，重新进入时间之流：让自己的心灵采取我们希望观察的那些心灵，或那个心灵，最根本的形式。这个过程中，我们必须运用一种极精细的“选择性”；它并非完全不像你们接收某一特定波长的以太信息时所用的物理选择性。不过，捕捉过去心灵的过程要精细得多，因为基本心灵模式构成的系统，远比一维排列的波频复杂。</p>
<p>我们的祖先刚刚获得“进入永恒视角”的能力时，由于不了解其中规律，曾发生过许多灾难。最早期的大量探索者，仅仅因为没能在恍惚期间维持身体生命而死去：他们的睡眠直接变成了死亡。另一些人则陷入极猛烈的抽搐，对自己造成无法挽回的损伤，最后由监管者出于仁慈结束生命。还有些案例中，原本正常的恍惚状态无限期延续，身体虽然活着，却连续几百万年毫无反应。直到不久以前，地下设施中还有一整片区域塞满这些人；照料者始终维护着他们，希望某一天他们能够醒来。但几千年前，我们终于决定结束其中一些人的生命，并把另一些人用于实验。</p>
<p>早期探索者中，那些成功从“永恒视角”进入某个过去时代的人，也有许多回来后发疯。另一些人醒来时还算清醒，却会一次又一次不由自主重新陷入恍惚。还有些人被自己的经历弄得极端愤世嫉俗，成了共同体里的瘟疫源，只好被要求自行停止心脏。少数人拒绝了这个要求，继续在社会中活动，造成了严重破坏，最终不得不被处死。在最早的先驱中有一两人，在我的同时代人中也有一两人，从恍惚中归来时患上了一种特殊的疯狂；种种迹象仿佛说明，他们实际上闯入了“未来”，而且此后仍把我们眼下的世界看成遥远过去中的一个片段。不幸的是，他们无法把自己的经历讲清楚。不过，在一个极早期案例中留下的疯话记录里，似乎提到了狂星——而那时距离这颗星真正获得这个名称还早得很。</p>
<p>这项工作早期只有少数人参加，他们所能做的不过是从非常近的过去中搜集零散事件。后来，技术逐渐得到巨大改进，我们终于能够检查本物种历史中几乎任何一段事件序列。之所以可能做到这一点，是因为心理学家与历史学家此前已经相当充分地研究清楚了本物种各个人类种族的基本心灵模式。再后来，心理学家开始建立一套庞大的理论体系，列出种种“可能存在”的基本心灵模式。经历一段毫无成果的试验之后，探索者终于学会在恍惚中让自己采取其中某些模式。</p>
<p>结果令人震惊。他们不仅如预料那样进入了海王星上许多原始文化，有时甚至发现自己身处完全陌生的世界，而且那些世界似乎比他们熟悉的行星小得多。一切普通事物的比例都改变了：海洋会掀起巨浪，陆地常常皱褶成巨大山系；当地生物虽然没有人工原子的强化，却能长到很大，仍保持纤细与灵活。更令人惊讶的是，这些事实都是借助此前从未有人猜想存在过的人类类型来观察的。至于这些种族居住的究竟是哪几颗行星，人们起初根本无法辨认。这也难怪，因为把人类赶往海王星的那次太阳碰撞，早已把地球和金星的一切痕迹都抹掉了。</p>
<p>发现这些过去世界，对我们的震动甚至比你们祖先发现美洲还大，因为它还顺带推翻了一套原本很有根据的理论——那套理论曾把人类的演化追溯到某种原始海王星生物。于是，对过去探索的兴趣急剧增长，技术也发展到早期先驱做梦都想不到的程度。人类在地球和金星上的整部历史轮廓，一点点被绘制出来；其中一段又一段区域，又被进一步补充了相当丰富的细节。</p>
<p>在观察这些已经灭绝的物种时，探索者遇见了与自己极不相同的经验。当然，为了能够与这些原始存在建立接触，探索者必须事先能够设想，甚至在某种程度上亲自采取他们的基本心灵模式或气质底图；可一旦真正接触成功，他仍会觉得自己闯进了一个全新的精神世界。比如，他必须借用感官能力远弱于自己的心灵。透过那些原始眼睛看东西时，细节远少于使用自己的眼睛；于是，虽然他已经把寄主体粗糙视觉所能提供的一切信息看得极其精确，整个世界在他眼中仍显得模糊、失焦。物体的颜色也会被稀释、简化，因为我们熟悉的好几种颜色，对更原始的眼睛根本不存在。因此，刚开始透过这些眼睛观看世界时，我们会觉得一切异常灰暗，几乎单色，仿佛观察者自己忽然变成了部分色盲。</p>
<p>其他感官同样被削弱。比如，触摸到的形状与质地总显得奇怪地含混、迟钝。甚至性交的感觉也如此：在我们这里，那种感觉丰富多样、具有高度表现力；到了原始人身上，却被压缩成一种令人作呕的单调与无定形。你们无法想象，这种一切感觉领域的粗糙与空洞，对探索者——尤其对尚未学会慷慨地把自己的精神交给原始存在的新手——会造成多么刺耳、甚至令人发狂的效果。</p>
<p>在思想领域，我们发现所有原始人类物种，不论彼此差别多大，与我们之间的距离都同样遥远。哪怕最先进原始物种中最先进的成员，也生活在一种在我们看来天真而怪诞的思想世界里。探索者发现，自己的心灵仿佛被迫折起双翼，困在某个粗制滥造的理论或神话牢笼中；而只要他愿意，这个牢笼本来轻轻一碰就能粉碎。偶尔也会有极少数情况：某座原始思想建筑的基础布局，恰好与宇宙最简单的基本事实一致，例如你们自己的相对论；但即便如此，本应建立在这套基础之上的整个上层结构仍然完全缺席。于是探索者会觉得，宇宙连同身处其中的自己，都被压扁成了一张二维地图。</p>
<p>在欲望领域，探索者还必须面对许多极陌生、极不协调的经验。所有人类物种最根本的动物欲望当然大体相同：安全、食物、配偶与陪伴。但原始人类心灵所追求的具体食物、具体陪伴等等，常常令我们感到陌生甚至厌恶。以食物为例。假如探索者正寄居在过去某个贪吃者心中，他本人躺在海王星地下设施里的身体当然不会真的消化不良——偶尔受到暗示影响的情况除外。然而，他仍可能遭受强烈痛苦，因为他的大脑被迫接受一整套“过量进食”的感觉经验，而这些经验在正常生活中本来就会令他反胃；现在也同样令他反胃。即使并没有暴食，食物的味觉和嗅觉表象也常常刺激我们，让我们觉得恶心。</p>
<p>个人美感同样如此。起初，探索者往往会被原始物种视为“完美”的那些怪异的人类讽刺性变形 所排斥，就像你们可能会因猿类那种过分近似人类的动物性而不适。探索者跟随你们时代某种原始恋爱情感逐渐生长时，常常会对那个被爱慕的对象感到恶心，也会对自己被迫卷入的亲密关系感到恶心。仿佛心灵的一部分正在看着另一部分坠入对一只雌猿的浪漫崇拜；仿佛自己怀着无限厌恶，却仍不得不紧贴那双毛茸茸的大腿。然而，说到底，这里和别处一样，真正引发厌恶的并不是单纯的动物性，而是“未能成为真正的人”；因此，这种刺激对探索者的冒犯，反而比被迫与真正的猿交配更强。</p>
<p>不过，随着熟悉程度增加，这些排斥是可以克服的。你们厌恶猿类对人的近似，其实是你们自身的一种弱点，源于眼界与同感不足；同样，我们厌恶你们这种对“真正人类”的粗糙近似，也是我们的弱点，必须学会把它转化为一种虽常带反讽、却仍怀敬意的同感。正如外科医生会习惯在内脏之间操作，甚至能从中看出美，探索者也能够、并且必须让自己习惯这些原始形态，并在其中发现美。当然，他在那个既受爱慕、又令人反感的“猿女”身上发现的美，绝不会简单等同于那个“猿男”所迷恋的美；它是一种更复杂的美，其中同时包含了令原始心灵愉悦的东西，也包含了令发达心灵厌恶的东西。</p>
<p>因此，对于早期种族的一切关切、一切生活方式和一切理想，探索者都必须以一种能够战胜轻蔑和厌恶的欣赏去回应。当然，这一领域里有许多东西确实可以让他全心喜爱，因为原始生活中有相当一部分只是动物本性尚未遭破坏的自然行为，还有相当一部分已经比动物状态更进一步。然而，原始人类很早便开始把自己的天性折磨成怪异形状，有时无意，有时故意；就像他会无意中折磨家畜，又会有意把宠物培育成畸形和荒诞变形 一样。随着力量增加，他对自身天性的这种侵犯也愈演愈烈，并在你们这种原始工业社会里达到顶点。在这样的阶段，个体的身体与精神越来越扭曲、贫乏而污浊。早期探索者研究这些时期时，必须竭尽全力，才能不让压倒性的愤怒与恶心毁掉研究。因为他当然必须像事情正发生在自己身上一样去观察一切——他正是透过受害者痛苦的心灵观察这一切的。于是，他几乎像一个患病的医生：若要让精神取胜，他就必须学会在研究自己的疾病时也获得某种智性的满足。</p>
<p>对探索者而言，诸如渴望商业成功、渴望任何形式的经济权力这类怪异情感，以及一切纯粹以自我为中心的激情——从社会攀附者的欲望，到一心求个人得救的苦行者的欲望——都会引发一种羞耻感，仿佛一个刚进入青春期的孩子，仍发现自己对已经过时的玩具恋恋不舍；或者引发一种厌恶，仿佛青年从某段不值得的性迷恋中清醒。然而，探索者必须学会超越这种羞耻与厌恶，就像外科医生必须超越对鲜血的厌恶一样。甚至面对仇恨和无缘无故的残酷——这些情感在你们以及所有其他原始物种中都极其普遍——他也必须学会以同感去接受它们，尽管本能上仍会反感，而且在道德上对它们的谴责也完全有充分理由。</p>
<p>我们与绝大多数原始心灵之间还有一个巨大差别：在我们这里，一切动机都完全进入意识，都能够被彻底内省；而在他们那里，几乎没有任何较复杂的动机会真正完全暴露在意识中。因此，当探索者追随一个原始心灵的某段行动时，他常常会看见一种巨大落差：这个心灵自己以为自己出于某种动机行动，但探索者却能看清，真正推动行为的来源完全是另一种动机。一个持续一生的心理情结，会在每天、每小时制造种种扭曲；如果不仅通过临床式外部观察，而是以最亲密的方式亲身经历这些扭曲，探索者将承受极其严酷的压力。因为在他观察的心灵中，每一次内在冲突都会同时撕扯他的心灵；而在他这里，这场冲突反倒完全进入意识，因此格外具有摧毁力。我们早期有不少观察者被自己研究的紊乱“感染”，等回到原来的世界后，已经无法再保持完全理智的行为，只能被毁灭。</p>
<p>总而言之，早期探索者常常被原始存在的单调，以及原始心灵彼此之间的高度相似，弄得极度疲惫；同时，他们又会陷入对原始人的粗糙、迟钝和愚蠢近乎痛苦的厌恶。从一个角度说，海王星人仿佛被判处去筛选一片沙漠里几乎完全相同的沙粒，而且还必须记录每一粒沙子细小的独特之处；从另一个角度说，他又仿佛被逐出成年世界，扔进育儿室或丛林里，而且真正被囚禁在婴儿或野兽的心灵中。因为一旦进入自己选择研究的心灵，探索者在研究需要他离开以前，确实就是一个囚徒。</p>
<p>他固然仍会思考自己的思想，却只能感知对方所感知的东西，并被迫忍受对方每一种最微小的感觉、思想、欲望与情绪，无论它们多么平庸。就像一个人意识到自己心里正在萌生某种躁狂或强迫念头，明知它既不理性又卑下，却无法控制；探索者也注定要以同感方式经历研究对象全部的思考与欲望，哪怕同时恶心得想吐。难怪那么多早期探索者会被厌恶或无聊折磨。</p>
<p>早在我的时代以前，这些危险和烦恼中的大多数都已经大幅减轻。不过，仍有一个严重问题存在，而且反而加剧了。我们当然培育出了一支庞大的工作者队伍，他们在超越时间的经验方面具有特殊天赋，也格外善于理解原始心灵类型。与此同时，我们还特意赋予这些新人对过去的特殊热情与同感——危险恰恰藏在这里。到了我的时代，整个种族每个人都多少具有这种热情与同感；而我们这些探索者，则把它们发展到了极端。</p>
<p>过去即使充满单调与污秽，对我们仍如此迷人，以至已经有许多人屈服于它的魔力，彻底失去了在当下世界的立足点。探索者若沉浸在某场宏大历史运动，或某个个体的人生故事中，可能一点点丧失对自己原本所属未来世界的一切记忆。他实际上不再是一个未来心灵检查过去心灵，而会退化成那个过去心灵本身的一道低沉伴音、一种古怪倾向；甚至可能同时寄存在许多个过去心灵里。再过些时候，连这点差异也会消失，于是探索者与被探索者彻底同一。如果发生这种事，他那位于未来海王星上的身体，就会逐渐解体并死亡。</p>
<p>即使技术已经改进，现代探索者仍会受到另一些问题阻碍。我说过，我们进入过去时代的方法，就是把自己的心灵塑造成所研究时代的基本模式或基调。然而，当探索者想进入某个特定个体时，他必须尽量采取那个个体特有的复杂精神形式或气质；或者抓住某个独特欲望或念头——他猜想那是这个人在生命某一时期独有的东西。可是，这种精神感染或联想过程，并不一定总有利于他。探索者在努力把自己固定进某个心灵时，甚至在已经驻留其中很久之后，自己思维里一次偶然联想，都可能突然把他从研究对象中扯走，抛进另一个心灵。</p>
<p>这个新心灵有时与刚才的研究对象属于同时代；但也常常来自另一个时代，甚至另一个世界。发生这种情况时，不仅研究会突然中断，探索者本人也可能受到严重损伤。因为他位于海王星的大脑被迫进行极其根本、极其迅速的重新调节，受到强烈震荡与拉扯，甚至可能永远无法恢复。哪怕没有真正垮掉，他也可能不得不休一个很长的假来复原。所幸，真正危险的通常只是最夸张的错位；偶尔被猛地撞进另一个与原研究对象具有同样基本模式的心灵，往往只是令人恼火，而不会造成大伤害。</p>
<p>探索者驻留在某个个体心灵中时，常常会通过那个人的感知遇见另一个人，并判断后者值得立刻研究。这时，他必须借助第一个人的感知仔细观察第二个人，尽量找出进入后者心灵的入口。这可能极其困难，因为一个原始心灵对另一个原始心灵的认识，往往错误而带偏见；真正能够使人理解对方的那些感知，根本没有出现。除此以外，每当探索者试图这样“换坐骑”时，总有一种危险：他可能从两者之间掉下去，被猛烈甩回自己原本的时空位置。又或者，在关键一刻，一个偶然联想可能把他抢走，扔到另一个时代或另一个世界。这类事故当然会造成严重损伤，有时甚至致命。</p>
<p>这里还可以提到，有少数心灵零散分布在漫长年代中，能够挫败一切进入它们的尝试。它们极其罕见，几百万乘几百万个心灵里也未必出现一个；可偏偏它们又正是我们最渴望进入的对象。每一个这样的罕见存在，都曾毁掉我们一大批最优秀的探索者。它们在某个关键方面一定与我们异质，以至我们无法把自己的本性调节得足够准确来进入它们。也许，它们自身正受到某种甚至比我们还更未来的影响；也许，它们被遥远恒星系中某个世界的心灵占据，并被悄然改造；甚至也有可能，它们正直接受宇宙心灵影响——我们希望，这个宇宙心灵会在一切未来中最遥远的时刻苏醒。</p>
<p>过去探索者还要承担另一种危险。偶尔，被观察的过去个体会在探索者预见死亡、及时脱离之前突然死去。当然，在许多情况下，探索者事先知道对方会在哪一天死亡，因此可以为正常退出做好准备，把事件一直观察到临死那一刻，然后在太迟以前脱身。但有时，尤其是在开拓历史中从未探索过的新区域时，探索者对下一刻将发生什么，和被观察的心灵本人一样无知。此时，一把匕首、一颗子弹、一道闪电，甚至一次完全出乎意料的心脏衰竭，都可能把他用粉碎性的猛力甩回自己的世界，给大脑造成无法修复的损伤，甚至当场杀死他。</p>
<p>最早观察你们那场最近的欧洲大战时，就有许多人这样遇难。观察者寄居在前线某个士兵心中时，摧毁被观察者的那枚炮弹，也同时“消灭”了观察者。如果埋葬死者是我们的习俗，那么我们也许也会像你们一样拥有1914至1918年的战争墓地——只不过，那些墓穴要到二十亿年后才会被挖开。它们不会装饰民族徽记，也不会立起十字架。</p>
</section>

<h2>ii. DIFFICULTIES AND DANGERS</h2>
<p>HOW IT COMES that at the moment of the onset of the trance the explorer is freed from the limitations of his own date and place I cannot tell you. Suffice it that the process involves both a special organ in the brain and a special technique, which has to be learned through a long apprenticeship. In that moment of awakening the worker has seemingly a confused experience of all the great successive epochal phases of the human spirit, up to his own date. But as the content of that supreme moment almost wholly escapes his memory as soon as it is past, it is impossible to say anything definite about it. He seems, indeed, to see in a flash, as though from another dimension of time, the whole historical order of events. Of course, he sees them only schematically. Their vast complexity of detail cannot be grasped in an instantaneous view. What he experiences can only be described (unintelligibly I fear) as a summation of all happening, of all physical, mental and spiritual flux. One is tempted to say also that he is aware of the human aspect of this flux as a vast slumber of the spirit, punctuated with moments of watchfulness, a vast stagnation troubled here and there with tremors of tense activity. Though all this is seen in an instant, it does not appear static, but alive with real passage and change.</p>
<p>This sublime moment must not be permitted to endure, for it is lethal. Immediately the explorer must begin to select that part of the historical order which he desires to study. To do this he must adopt the fundamental attitude of mind, or temperamental flavour, which he knows to be distinctive of the desired period. This process demands very great skill, and involves a host of uncouth and nerve-racking experiences. As soon as he begins to succeed in assuming the appropriate mental attitude, all the periods save that which he has chosen fade out of his consciousness, and the chosen period becomes increasingly detailed. He has then to specialize his mental attitude still further, so as to select a particular phase or group-culture within his period. And this specialization he may carry further again, till he has brought himself into the mind of some particular individual at a particular moment. Having once gained a footing in the individual mind, he can henceforth follow all its experiences from within; or, if he prefers, he can remain in one moment of that mind, and study its microscopic detail.</p>
<p>Such is the essence of our method. First we have to attain the momentary glimpse of eternity, or, more precisely, to take up for one instant the point of view of eternity. Then by imagination and sympathy we have to re-enter the stream of time by assuming the fundamental form of the minds or the mind that we wish to observe. In this process we have to work by means of a very delicate ‘selectivity’, not wholly unlike that physical selectivity which you exercise when you pick up ethereal messages on a particular wavelength. But this process of picking up past minds is far more delicate, since the system of basic mental patterns is very much more complex than the one-dimensional series of wave-frequencies.</p>
<p>When our ancestors first acquired the power of ‘entering into the point of view of eternity’ they suffered many disasters through ignorance of its principles. Very many of the earliest explorers succumbed simply by failing to keep their bodies alive during the trance. Their sleep turned into death. Others fell into such violent convulsions that they damaged themselves irreparably, and were mercifully killed by the superintendents. In other cases the normal trance lasted indefinitely, the body remaining alive but unresponsive for millions of years. A section of the catacombs was until recently filled with these persons, who were looked after by the attendants in the hope that some day they might wake. But a few thousand years ago it was decided to do away with some, and use others for experimental purposes.</p>
<p>Of the early explorers who successfully emerged from ‘the point of view of eternity’ into some past epoch, many returned insane. Others, though sane when they woke, kept falling into the trance again and again. Others were so embittered by their experiences that they became plague-spots in the community, and had to be requested to stop their hearts. Of these, some few refused, and remained at large, doing so much damage that finally they were put to death. One or two of the pioneers, and one or two of my contemporaries also, have returned from the trance with a peculiar kind of insanity, which suggested that they had actually found their way into the future, and that they still regarded our contemporary world as an episode in the remote past. Unfortunately they could give no account of their experiences; but in one of the earliest cases the recorded ravings seem to refer to the Mad Star, which did not earn its name until long afterwards.</p>
<p>In the early stages of the work few persons were engaged on it, and all that they could do was to collect random incidents from the very recent past. Gradually, however, the technique was greatly improved. It became possible to inspect almost any sequence of events in the history of our own species. This could only be done because the basic mental patterns of all the races of our own species had already been fairly well worked out by our psychologists and historians. Later, however, the psychologists began to formulate a vast theoretical system of possible basic mental patterns; and after some barren experimenting the explorers finally learned to assume certain of these patterns in the trance. The result was startling. Not only did they, as was expected, gain access to many primitive cultures upon Neptune, but also they began to find themselves sometimes in alien worlds, apparently much smaller than their familiar planet. The proportions of common things were all altered. The seas rose into great waves; the lands were often buckled into huge mountain ranges. The native organisms, though unfortified by artificial atoms, were able to attain great size and yet remain slender and agile. And these facts were observed through the medium of human types the existence of which had never been guessed. The very planets which these races inhabited could not be identified. No wonder, since all traces of Earth and Venus had long ago been wiped out by the solar collision that had driven man to Neptune.</p>
<p>This discovery of Past worlds Was even more exciting to us than the discovery of America to your own ancestors; for it entailed incidentally the overthrow of a well-grounded theory, which traced the evolution of the human race to a primitive Neptunian organism. Interest in the exploration of the past now greatly increased. The technique was developed far beyond the dreams of the early explorers. Little by little the outline of man’s whole history on Earth and Venus Was plotted, and tract after tract of it was elaborated in some detail.</p>
<p>In observing these extinct species, the explorers found traces of experience very different from their own. Although, of course, the basic mental pattern or temperamental ground-plan was in every case one which the explorer himself had been able to conceive, and even in a manner assume, in order to make contact with these primitive beings at all, yet when the contact had been made, he seemed to enter into a new mental world. For instance, he had to deal with minds whose sensory powers were much more limited than his own. Looking through those Primitive eyes, he saw things in much less detail than through his own eyes, so that, though he observed everything with all the precision that his host’s crude vision could afford, yet everything seemed to him blurred, and out of focus. The colours of objects, too, were diluted and simplified; for several colours familiar to us are hidden from more primitive eyes. Consequently the world as seen through those eyes appears to us at first strangely drab, almost monochromatic, as though the observer himself had become partially colour-blind. The other senses also are impoverished. For instance, all touched shapes and textures seem curiously vague and muffled. The sensations of sexual intercourse, too, which with us are richly variegated and expressive, are reduced in the primitive to a nauseating sameness and formlessness. It is impossible for you to realize the jarring, maddening effects of this coarseness and emptiness of all the sensory fields, especially to the inexperienced explorer, who has not yet learned to submit his spirit generously to the primitive.</p>
<p>In the sphere of thought, we find all the primitive species of man, however different from one another, equally remote from ourselves. Even the most advanced members of the most advanced primitive species inhabit worlds of thought which to us seem naive and grotesque. The explorer finds himself condemned to cramp the wings of his mind within the caging of some gimcrack theory or myth, which, if he willed, he could easily shatter. Even in those rare cases in which the ground-plan of some edifice of primitive thought happens to be true to the simplest basic facts of the cosmos, as in your own theory of relativity, the superstructure which it ought to support is wholly absent. The explorer thus has the impression that the cosmos, with himself in it, has been flattened into a two-dimensional map.</p>
<p>In the sphere of desire the explorer has to deal with very unfamiliar and very jarring kinds of experience. In all human species, of course, the most fundamental animal desires are much the same, the desires for safety, food, a mate, and companionship; but the kind of food, the kind of companionship, and so on, which the primitive human mind seeks, are often very foreign and distasteful to us. Take the case of food. Of course the explorer’s body, lying in the catacombs on Neptune, does not, if perchance he is inhabiting some primitive glutton of the past, suffer dyspepsia; save occasionally, through the influence of suggestion. But none the less he may experience acute distress, for his brain is forced to accept sensory complexes of overeating which in normal life would disgust him. And they disgust him now. Even when overeating does not occur, the food percepts are often repulsive to our centres of taste and odour. In the matter of personal beauty, too, the explorer is at first repelled by the grotesque caricature of humanity which among primitive species passes for perfection, much as you yourselves may be repelled by the too-human animality of apes. Often, when he is following the growth of some primitive love-sentiment in your epoch, he is nauseated by the adored object, and by the intimacy in which he is reluctantly entangled. It is much as though a part of the mind were to watch the other part entrapped into romantic adoration of a female ape; as if with infinite disgust one were to find himself pressing against those hairy thighs. But indeed in this matter, as in others, since it is not mere animality that disgusts, but the failure to be human, the explorer is far more outraged than if he were compelled to cohabit with an ape. With familiarity, however, these repulsions can be surmounted. Just as your disgust of the ape’s approximation to the human is a weakness in you, due to lack of vision and sympathy, so our tendency to a disgust of your own crude approximation to the human is a weakness in us, which we have to learn to transform into a reverent, though often ironical, sympathy. Just as the surgeon may become accustomed to delving among viscera, and may even find beauty in them, so the explorer may and must accustom himself to all these primitive forms, and find beauty in them too. Of course the beauty which he discovers in the adored yet repulsive ape-woman is never simply identical with the beauty which delights the adoring ape-man; for it is a beauty which includes within itself both that which delights the primitive and that which disgusts the developed mind.</p>
<p>To all the preoccupations then, and to all the ways of life, and all the ideals, of the early races, the explorer must react with that delight which triumphs over contempt and disgust. Of course there is very much in this sphere which he can whole-heartedly enjoy, since much in primitive life is the unspoiled behaviour of the animal, and much also improves upon the animal. But at a very early stage primitive man begins to torture his own nature into grotesque forms, inadvertently or by intent; much as, inadvertently, he tortures his domestic animals, and by intent he turns his pets into freaks and caricatures. This violation of his own nature increases as he gains power, and reaches its height in primitive industrial communities such as your own. In such phases the individual body and spirit become more and more distorted, impoverished, noisome. The early explorer, studying these phases, was hard put to it to prevent his overwhelming indignation and nausea from spoiling his study. He had, of course, to observe it all as though it were happening to himself, since he observed it through the suffering minds of its victims. And so he was almost in the position of a sick doctor, whose spirit must triumph by delighting in the study of his own disease.</p>
<p>Grotesque sentiments such as the lust of business success or economic power of any kind, and indeed every purely self-regarding passion, from that of the social climber to that of the salvation-seeking ascetic, are experienced by the explorer with something of that shame which the child, emerging into adolescence, may feel toward the still-clinging fascination of his outgrown toys, or with such disgust as the youth may feel when he wakes from some unworthy sexual infatuation. But this shame and disgust the explorer must learn to transcend as the surgeon the disgust of blood. Even the passions of hate and gratuitous cruelty, so widespread in your own and all other primitive species, he must learn to accept with sympathy, in spite of his spontaneous revulsion from them and his well-justified moral condemnation of them.</p>
<p>One great difference between ourselves and most primitive minds is that, while in us all motives are fully conscious, fully open to introspection, in them scarcely any of their more complex motives are ever brought fully to light. Thus when the explorer is following some train of action in a primitive mind, he very often observes an immense discrepancy between the mind’s own view of its motives and the real motives which he himself sees to be in fact the source of the activity. To experience all the daily and hourly perversities of a life-long complex, to experience them not merely through clinical observation but in the most intimate manner, puts him to an extremely severe strain. At every turn his own mind is wrenched by the conflict in the mind that he is observing. And in him the conflict is wholly conscious and shattering. Not a few of our early observers became infected by the disorder which they had been studying, so that when they returned to their native world they could no longer behave with perfect sanity, and had to be destroyed.</p>
<p>To sum up, then, the earlier explorers were often desperately fatigued by the monotony of primitive existence and the sameness of primitive minds; and also they fell into disgust, an agony of disgust, with the crudity, insensitivity and folly of the primitive. From the one point of view it might be said that the Neptunian found himself condemned to sift the almost identical sand-grains of a desert, and even to record the minute distinctive features of each grain. From the other point of view, it was as though he had been banished from the adult world to the nursery or the jungle, and was actually imprisoned in the mind of babe or beast. For, once settled in the mind which he has chosen to study, the explorer is indeed a captive until study calls him elsewhere. Though he thinks his own thoughts, he perceives only what the other perceives, and is forced to endure every least sensation, thought, desire and emotion of the other, be it never so banal. Like one who feels within his own mind the beginnings of some mania or obsession which, though he recognizes that it is irrational or base, he cannot control, so the explorer is doomed to experience sympathetically all his subject’s thinking and desiring, even while he is nauseated by it. No wonder, then, that many early explorers were tortured by disgust or ennui.</p>
<p>Long before my time, most of these dangers and irks of exploration had been greatly reduced. One serious trouble, however, remains, and has even increased. A great army of workers was of course bred with special aptitudes for supra-temporal experience, and with special insight into the primitive types of mind. These new workers were given also a special enthusiasm and sympathy in respect of the past; and herein lay their danger. In my day every member of the race has something of this enthusiasm and sympathy; and we explorers have them in an extreme degree. So enthralling do we find the past, even in all its monotony and squalor, that many have succumbed to its spell, and lost all footing in the present world. Rapt in some great movement of history, or in some individual life-story, the explorer may lose, little by little, all memory of the future world of which he is a native, may in fact cease to be a future mind inspecting a past mind, and become instead a mere undertone or freakish propensity in the past mind itself, or in many Past minds. In time even this may vanish, so that the explorer becomes identical with the explored. If this occurs, his own body, situated in the future and in Neptune, gradually disintegrates and dies.</p>
<p>Certain other troubles hamper even the most modern explorers, in spite of improved technique. The method by which we enter a past epoch is, as I have said, this process of shaping our minds to the basic pattern or ground tone of the epoch to be studied. But when the explorer desires to enter a particular individual, he must try to assume the complex form or temperament which is distinctive of that individual; or else he must seize on one unique desire or thought, which he supposes to be peculiar to that individual at a certain date of his life. Now this process of mental infection or association does not necessarily work in his favour. Often, when he is trying to establish himself in some mind, or even when he has long been established, some chance association in his own thought-process may suddenly snatch him away from the object of his study and fling him into some other mind. Sometimes this other is a contemporary of the recent object of study; but often it is a mind in some different epoch or world. When this happens, not only is the study broken short, but also the explorer may be very seriously damaged. His brain, on Neptune, suffers such a fundamental and rapid readjustment that it is grievously jarred and strained, and may never recover. Even if he does not actually succumb, he may have to take a long holiday for recuperation. Fortunately, however, it is only the more extravagant dislocations that are really dangerous. Occasional jolts into minds of the same basic pattern as the original object of study are more exasperating than harmful.</p>
<p>Often when the explorer is resident in a particular individual he encounters through that individual’s perception another individual, who, he thinks, would repay immediate study. He has then to observe this other carefully through the perceptions of the first, so as to discover, if possible, some entry into his mind. This may be very difficult, since one primitive mind’s awareness of another is often so erroneous and biased that the perceptions which would make for true understanding of the other fail to occur. Moreover there is always the danger that, when the explorer attempts this ‘change of mounts’ he may fall between them, and be flung violently once more into his native location in time and space. Or again, he may at the critical moment be snatched by some chance association into some other epoch or world. Such accidents are of course very damaging, and may prove fatal.</p>
<p>Here I may mention that some minds, scattered up and down the ages, defeat all attempts to enter them. They are very rare, only one in millions of millions; but they are such as we should most desire to enter. Each one of these rare beings has caused the ruin of a great company of our most able explorers. They must be in some vital respect alien to us, so that we cannot assume their nature accurately enough to enter them. Possibly they are themselves subject to an influence future even to us. Possibly they are possessed and subtly transformed by minds native to some world in a remote stellar system. Possibly, even, they are under the direct influence of the cosmical mind, which, we hope, will awaken in the most remote of all futures.</p>
<p>Explorers of the past incur one other danger which I may mention. Sometimes the past individual under observation dies suddenly, before the explorer can foresee the death, and free himself. In many cases, of course, he knows the date at which the other’s death will occur, and can therefore, having prepared himself for a normal departure, observe the course of events right up to the moment of death, and yet escape before it is too late. But sometimes, especially in pioneering in some unexplored region of history, the explorer is as ignorant of the immediate future as the observed mind itself.. In such cases a dagger, a bullet, a flash of lightning, even an unforeseen heart-failure, may fling him back to his own world with a shattering jerk, which may irreparably damage his brain, or even kill him outright. Many of the early observers of your recent European War were caught in this manner. Resident in the mind of some soldier in action, the observer himself was annihilated by the shell that destroyed the observed. If burial were one of our practices, we, like you, might have our war graves of 1914 to 1918, though they would not have been dug till two thousand million years later. Nor would they be decorated by national emblems. Nor would they bear the cross.</p>
