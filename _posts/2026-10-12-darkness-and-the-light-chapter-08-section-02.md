<h2>二、官僚问题</h2>
<p>早期世界国家所遭遇的另一场严重冲突，直到美洲问题解决两个世纪之后才真正爆发。这是一种新的阶级战争：世界性的官僚体系同普通公民大众之间的斗争。</p>
<p>世界官僚体系通过心理测验选拔成员，标准是组织才能与道德正直。人们已经知道，较强的组织才能主要集中在某些家族或生物谱系中。于是，一种以血统为基础的贵族制开始显露清晰迹象，仿佛水正在结冰时出现的那种疏松晶体网络。官僚队伍从未对那些出身于大官僚家族之外、却确有才能的人关闭大门，但经过长期考验的家族后裔在种种微妙方面占有优势。某些姓氏逐渐成了‘具有官僚才能’的标签。那些更骄傲的家族非常珍惜自己的姓氏。凡能力没有达到家族高标准的成员，便被剥夺家族姓氏；家族女性同较低门第者结婚后所生的聪明子女，则可获准使用母系姓氏。新进入官僚体系的人也会受到老家族威望的微妙影响，模仿他们的举止和观念，并努力让自己的姓氏也积累起类似的声望。</p>
<p>于是，一种新贵族制一点一点在世界社会的表面结晶。它不是单纯的血统贵族，也不是财富贵族，而是真正以能力为依据的贵族；不过，这是一种特殊的能力，即组织事务和管理人的能力。它的工作做得很好，其他种类的优秀头脑——比如科学家和文学家——也很愿意让这些天生的组织者掌权。然而，后来人们开始抱怨，官僚们似乎越来越自负，也越来越爱管闲事。没有人否认他们的统治大体上高效而诚实，但人们日益怀疑他们变得过分迷恋权力；他们对世界共同体的忠诚，也越来越被一种无意识的关注所修正——他们在意的不是个人名望，而是整个阶级的威望。当然，他们的职位在名义上受联邦议会和各国议会的意志制约。不幸的是，政治人物自己也属于官僚阶级，很少愿意对那些越权的官员采取行动。因此，官僚的力量从一个先例扩张到另一个先例，一点点膨胀起来。他们越来越反感批评，越来越抱团，并逐渐发展出一种独特的生活方式和一套独特的道德准则。</p>
<p>事情的导火索，是俄罗斯一座大型物理研究实验室接到世界研究部命令：停止研究恒星内部物质状态，转而集中解决把亚原子能应用于工业这一实际问题。著名的俄罗斯物理学家提出抗议，拒绝服从，又向世界总统上诉，结果遭到逮捕。全世界科学界一片愤怒。许多研究人员为声援被捕同事而罢工。工业工人的工资虽高、工时虽短，也借此机会控诉工厂纪律过严，以及政府对家庭生活的干涉。那一小群已经稳固存在的工业开拓型资本家——他们因当年的美国实验而被纳入世界国家——则抱怨工厂监察员无所不用其极地妨碍他们工作，想把他们这一职业彻底消灭。一些作家声称，他们的书无法出版，仅仅因为国家或联邦出版部门不喜欢。这些作家指出，出版部门最初建立的职责不是审查，而恰恰是鼓励对制度的批评，如今的做法违背了其原有职能。无线电部门也遭到类似指控。</p>
<p>抗议运动最先在不列颠群岛爆发，后来虽然遍及全球，英国人与爱尔兰人仍是最积极的支持者。岛民通过群众大会、游行、广播、给报纸的投书、给议员和内阁部长的信件来表达不满，而最重要的表现，则是对每一个具体的官僚暴政实例进行坚决抵抗。最流行的口号是‘少一点效率，多一点自由’、‘少一点生产，多一点生活’，尤其是‘我们不要当机器人’。当我把这个时代同胞们的抱怨，与我自己那个时代公民自由所遭受的那些令人沮丧、却远没有激起如此强烈反抗的侵蚀相比较时，我不禁发笑。这场运动的核心，是对个性的坚持。‘典型英国人’游行又为运动提供了喜剧色彩。游行者——或者更准确地说，是杂乱无章地拖拖拉拉前进的人——把自己装扮成当时世界以及古今各时代那些‘无法标准化的类型’与古怪人物。十九世纪流浪汉以及各个时代的无业游民最受欢迎，他们被扮得胡子拉碴、衣衫褴褛、肮脏、醉醺醺，却又友好可亲，而且每个人都竭力打扮得与别人不同。他们同中世纪吟游诗人、修士和弄臣挤在一起，还有心不在焉的哲学家、艺术家，以及缠在电线和试管堆里的科学家。这支由古今怪人组成的杂色队伍故意毫无秩序地在街上游荡，高唱自由之歌，快活地拒绝服从一旁忙得团团转、试图把他们排成整齐队列的滑稽‘官员’。与这群乌合之众形成对比的，可能是一队训练有素的‘机器人’：他们被装扮得像机器，并由红色胶带或电缆彼此连接。真正的官僚们对这一切滑稽表演既鄙夷又愤怒。在他们看来，这象征着社会士气正在危险地衰弱，是一种神经质的无政府冲动，是对人类尊严的否定。</p>
<p>抗议与这些滑稽表演在各国不断涌现。各国政府被迫承诺立即实施若干改革，世界政府则设立一个独立委员会调查整个问题。人类境况已经改善的一个典型表现是，委员会只用了三个月就提交报告；而且报告虽然严厉谴责官僚们不必要的官样作风，却也因为真正理解他们的立场而赢得了其尊重。然而，官僚们仍强烈反对报告提出的改革方案。方案要求建立一套庞大的特别上诉法院体系，专门处理涉嫌官僚主义和干涉自由的案件；各国最臭名昭著的官僚要被免职；最糟糕的一条是，从今以后，任何一个家族同时在官僚体系中任职的人数不得超过三人。经过长时间辩论，世界政府决定接受这一方案，只作少量修改。于是，官僚们——他们真诚相信自身的重要性，也真诚相信自己的理想正确——宣布，只有他们这些经过严格筛选、又接受专门教育的人，才可能真正知道世界社会需要什么。他们公开宣称自己是真正的贵族阶层；在当前紧急状态下，他们被迫暂时中止宪法，恢复独裁权力。世界议会和如蜂群般散布各地的国家议会几乎完全由官僚阶级成员组成，暗地里又同情这套主张，因此只进行了半心半意的抵抗。除英国、爱尔兰和西藏——自由最古老与最新的两个故乡——之外，政变在所有国家立刻成功，因为世界警察的首脑当然也是官僚体系成员。爱尔兰地方政府发生分裂，全国陷入混乱。英国与西藏政府则站出来保卫自由。他们依靠本国非武装警察保护自己，逮捕当地官僚领袖，并呼吁当地世界警察保卫宪法。然而，世界警察执行的是总警监的命令。武装部队出现在两个‘叛乱’议会前。令警察十分苦恼的是，叛乱者试图抵抗，只好动用枪械。两个议会有数名成员腿部中弹，受了轻伤。随后，两国政府及其支持者正式被捕。</p>
<p>可是，地球上的人民此时已经太有精神，绝不会接受独裁，即使这种独裁从其自身标准看来显然是仁慈的。总罢工首先在英国爆发，西藏、冰岛、美国、新西兰相继响应，最终发展为一场普遍的公民不服从运动。在官僚看来，人类简直集体发疯了。因为这些公务员与政治家非常清楚自己的正直，也非常清楚自己对全人类的根本忠诚。他们不是纳粹，不是‘邪恶资本家’，而是有良知的人类公仆，而且确实属于人类中能力较优越的一群。他们唯一的过错，只不过是‘服务得不是不够好，而是太好了’。然而，这一项过错恰恰是他们无法认识的。他们把整个抗议运动归咎于‘颠覆分子’，归咎于那些除了惹事什么也不会的无赖。但抗议仍不断扩大。社会只维持最低限度的必要服务。在一个财富几乎无限的世界里，人们甘愿过贫乏生活，直到自由得到恢复。与此同时，言论依旧完全自由。各地举行大规模示威和抗议集会，骚乱者与警察之间发生许多严重冲突。尽管情绪已经十分激烈，却几乎没有流血，因为人类的性情确实已经有所改善。不过，这种新精神仍然脆弱。</p>
<p>随着冲突发展，双方都变得更加恼怒，也更加严厉。事情终于在伦敦达到顶点。巨大人群涌向白厅，打碎世界政府大楼的窗户。世界首席特使本人走上阳台试图安抚群众，不巧有人扔出一只瓶子，正砸在他脸上，鲜血立刻流满面部。突然之间，双方一直被压抑的野蛮性都汹涌而出，冲破了一切约束。凡穿得像官僚的人都遭到粗暴殴打。政府不得不展示枪械，这反而激起暴民的狂怒。他们冲向大楼。卫兵朝他们腿部开枪，但大多数人仍继续前冲，淹没警卫，闯进大楼并将其纵火焚烧。官员遭到严重殴打，可即便到这一步，仍无人受到真正致命的伤害。新一支世界警察部队被调到现场。新来的警察没有意识到自己面对的只是一场失控的斗殴，而不是血腥革命，于是使用了机枪。由于执行低位射击，最初严重伤亡仍很少，但人群不但没有退缩，反而不顾进一步伤亡继续向前。于是屠杀发生了。然而，成千上万愤怒的市民又从四面八方涌来。警察彻底被包围，为了活命开始不加区别地射击。死者与垂死者堆成墙壁，将他们围在中央。此刻伦敦市民已经完全被野蛮、鲁莽的仇恨控制。过去三个世纪中被驯服得如此彻底的所有野蛮冲动，一下子重新掌权。尸墙越垒越高，新的进攻者就踩着尸体翻过去，随即用自己的尸体把墙垒得更高。终于，警察的弹药耗尽。暴民冲破防线，把所有守卫者全部杀死。此时大批世界警察增援正在向伦敦汇集，郊区爆发了绝望的战斗。</p>
<p>就在这时，伦敦市长通过街头扩音器发表广播讲话，呼吁世界警察撤退，也呼吁市民回家。与此同时，在伦敦群众中颇受欢迎的大都会非武装警察被派往各个危险地点，冷静地接管那些已经相当疲惫的市民。看到人群终于受到控制，武装警察便撤离了。</p>
<p>伦敦那场狂暴事件的消息通过无线电传遍世界。其他城市也相继因愤怒而爆发，随后又一个接一个被劝说着恢复平静。最后，各国世界警察中的很大一部分共同发表广播声明，宣布他们不再执行官僚首脑的命令。到这一步，官僚们已经明白大势已去。世界政府辞职，许多国家政府也随之下台。在日本，部长们以切腹自尽。许多大型公共事业——无论国家级还是国际级——的负责人也交出职权。大多数人重申自己的理想，却承认人类还没有准备好按照如此高的目标生活；另一些人则公开放弃原有主张。整整一个月，除西藏、英国和冰岛的地方政府外，世界上几乎不存在任何公共权威。没有世界政府；警察和公务部门没有行政首脑。然而，并没有出现混乱。所有事务仍照常运作，仿佛处于一种仁慈的无政府状态。这种局面不可能无限维持，但谁也没有权威去改变它。无线电上展开了严肃讨论；最终，仿佛一次覆盖全世界的贵格会聚会，从讨论中逐渐形成了‘会议的共同感受’：恢复旧政府以及大体上的旧官僚阶级，把让世界重新站稳脚跟的任务交还给他们；与此同时，再详细设计新的政治与社会宪制。因此，经过这场教训洗礼的旧统治阶级暂时保住了职位，只有少数顽固分子和投机者被清除。倘若一个社会尚未远远超过我们这个时代的正直和智慧水平，革命绝不可能以这种方式结束。</p>
<p>于是，人类成功避开了走向重新建立阶级统治的第一步危险。由于始终牢记刚刚发生的动乱，人类逐渐在公共生活中形成一种新的性情和新的道德传统。它不过是此前在人际关系中形成、并促使光明意志普遍而轻微增长的那套新性情与传统的延伸。一旦真正扎根，这种新风气便以惊人速度成长。过去，诚实与慷慨被视为难以达到的理想，人们大体上预期邻人会恶劣对待自己，预期统治者会专横腐败；如今，诚实与慷慨却越来越成为社会空气本身的一部分。无论私人事务还是公共事务，人们都开始理所当然地期待自己受到体面的对待。</p>

<h2>ii. DIFFICULTIES WITH THE BUREAUCRATS</h2>
<p>THE OTHER SERIOUS conflict which troubled the early World State did not come to a head until a couple of centuries after the solution of the American trouble. This was a new kind of class war, a worldwide struggle between the bureaucracy and the mass of ordinary citizens.</p>
<p>The world bureaucracy was selected by psychological tests for organizing ability and moral integrity. It was known that superior organizing ability ran mainly in certain families or biological strains. Consequently there began to emerge strong traces of an aristocracy of birth, rather in the manner of the loose network of crystals which appears in water in the act of freezing. The ranks of the bureaucracy were never closed to suitable candidates from outside the great bureaucratic families, but in subtle ways scions of the well-tried stocks had the advantage. Certain family names became labels promising bureaucratic ability. The prouder families guarded their names very jealously. Members who failed to come up to the family’s high standard of ability were deprived of the family name. Able children of female members of the family who married into humbler stocks were granted the name of the maternal family. Newcomers into the bureaucracy were subtly influenced by the prestige of the old families, imitating their manners and ideas, and seeking to gather similar prestige for their Own family names.</p>
<p>Thus, little by little, the new aristocracy crystallized upon the surface of the world-society. It was an aristocracy not of mere birth, nor of wealth, but of genuine ability; but of a special kind of ability, namely the aptitude for organization and for managing human beings. It did its work well; and superior intelligences of other kinds, such as the scientific and the literary, were well content to leave the born organizers in power. But there carne a time when people began to murmur that the bureaucrats were becoming rather self- important and meddlesome. No one denied that their rule was in the main efficient and honest, but there was a growing suspicion that they were growing too fond of power, and that their loyalty to the world community was increasingly tempered by unwitting preoccupation with their own prestige, not as individuals but as a class. They held their position, of course, under the will of the federal and national assemblies. Unfortunately the politicians were themselves members of the bureaucratic class, and would seldom take action against officials who exceeded their powers. Thus, little by little, the strength of the bureaucrats broadened out from precedent to precedent. Increasingly they resented criticism. Increasingly they hung together, developing little by little the beginnings of a distinctive way of life and a distinctive moral code.</p>
<p>Matters came to a head when a great physical research-laboratory in Russia was ordered by the World Research Ministry to give up its inquiry into the condition of matter in the interior of stars and to concentrate on the practical problem of applying sub-atomic energy to industry. The eminent Russian physicists protested, refused, appealed to the World President, and were arrested. There was great indignation in scientific circles throughout the world. Many research workers went out on strike in defence of their arrested colleagues. Industrial workers, though their pay was good and their hours were short, took this opportunity of complaining of excessive discipline in the factories and of interference in their home life. The small but well- established class of pioneering industrial capitalists (incorporated in the World State as a result of the American experiment) complained that factory inspectors used every means to hamper their work and destroy their profession. Certain writers affirmed that they could not get their books published because the national or federal ministry of publication disliked them. This, they said, was a violation of the original function of the ministries, which had been founded not to censor but to foster matter critical of the regime. Similar charges were made against the ministries of radio.</p>
<p>The movement of protest began in the British Isles, and, though it spread throughout the world, the British and Irish peoples were its most vigorous upholders. The islanders expressed their discontent in mass meetings, processions, broadcasts, letters to the press, letters to members of parliament and cabinet ministers, and above all in hearty resistance to particular instances 01 bureaucratic tyranny. The most popular slogans were, ‘Less efficiency, more freedom’, and ‘Less producing, more living’, and above all ‘We won’t be robots’. I could not but smile when I compared the grievances of my countrymen of this period with the disheartening inroads into civil liberty which had occurred in my own time and had been far less indignantly resisted. The dominant note of this movement was the insistence on individuality. Comic relief was given by processions of ‘typical Englishmen’. The marchers, or rather the disorderly stragglers, were persons made up to represent ‘unstereotyped types’ and odd individuals in the present world and in all ages. Nineteenth-century tramps, and vagabonds of every period were the most popular figures. They were represented as unshaven, ragged, filthy, drunk, and friendly. Each was got up to be as unlike as possible to every other. These jostled with medieval minstrels, friars, and fools, scatter-brained philosophers, artists, research scientists entangled in electric wires and test- tubes. This motley host of ancient and modern eccentrics strayed along the street in studied disorder, singing songs of freedom, blithely recalcitrant to the efforts of the comic ‘officials’ who fussed beside them, trying to get them into regular formation. In contrast with this rabble might come a batch of well-drilled robots, made up to look like machinery and linked together by red tape or electric cables. All this buffoonery the real bureaucrats regarded with contempt and indignation. In their view it was a symptom of a sinister weakening of social morale, a neurotic craving for anarchy, a denial of the dignity of the human species.</p>
<p>The agitation and the comic relief welled up in every country. The governments were forced to promise certain immediate reforms, and the World Government set up an independent commission to investigate the whole matter. It was characteristic of the improved condition of the human race that the commission’s report was issued within three months, and that, although it firmly condemned the bureaucrats for their unnecessary officialism, it also won their respect by its insight into their point of view. But its proposals for reform they strongly condemned. There was to be a vast system of special courts of appeal to deal with cases of alleged officialism and interference with liberty. The most notorious bureaucrats in every country were to be dismissed. Worst of all, in future no family should have more than three members in the bureaucracy at any time. After much debate the World Government decided to accept the plan, with a few modifications. Thereupon the bureaucrats, honestly convinced of their own importance and the rightness of their ideals, announced that they alone, who were carefully selected and carefully educated for their task, could possibly know what was needed in the life of the world society. They frankly claimed to be a true aristocracy; and in this emergency they were forced, they said, to suspend the constitution and resume dictatorial power. The World Parliament and the swarm of national parliaments, composed almost entirely of members of the bureaucratic class, and secretly in sympathy with their claims, put up only a half- hearted resistance. In all the states except Britain, Ireland, and Tibet, the oldest and the newest homes of freedom, the coup d’etat was at once successful, for the chiefs of the World Police were of course members of the bureaucracy. In Ireland the local government split, and the country boiled up in disorder. The British and Tibetan governments made a stand for freedom. Guarding themselves with their unarmed police, they arrested the local bureaucratic leaders and appealed to the local World Police to defend the constitution. But the World Police carried out the instructions of its Chief Constable. Armed forces appeared at the two ‘rebel’ parliaments. Much to the distress of the police, the rebels made an effort to resist, and fire-arms had to be used against them. Several members of the two parliaments were slightly damaged by shots fired at their legs. The governments were duly arrested, along with their supporters.</p>
<p>But the peoples of the earth were by now far too spirited to accept dictatorship, even a dictatorship which was manifestly benevolent according to its lights. A general strike started in Britain, was taken up in Tibet, Iceland, America, New Zealand, and developed into a universal campaign of civil disobedience. From the point of view of the bureaucrats the human race had gone quite mad. For these hosts of civil servants and politicians were very conscious of their own integrity and fundamental human loyalty. They were not Nazis or ‘wicked capitalists’ but conscientious servants of mankind, and, moreover, demonstrably superior members of it. Their only fault was that they had served not wisely but too well. This one fault, however, they could not recognize. They attributed the whole agitation to ‘subversive elements’, to ne’er-do-weels who could do nothing but stir up trouble. But the agitation increased. Only minimum services were maintained. In a world of limitless wealth people settled down to a life of penury till liberty could be restored. Meanwhile there was still complete freedom of expression. There were great demonstrations and protest meetings, and many serious clashes between rioters and police. Yet, though feeling was now very strong, there was practically no bloodshed, for the temper of mankind had indeed improved. But the new spirit was still frail.</p>
<p>As the conflict developed, both sides became more exasperated and harsh. Matters came to a head in London. Huge crowds converged on Whitehall and broke the windows of the World Government Building. The Chief World Emissary himself appeared on a balcony to appease the crowd, but as luck had it some one threw a bottle which hit him in the face and covered him with blood. Suddenly the repressed brutishness of both sides surged up and broke away all restraint. Anyone dressed as a bureaucrat was roughly handled. The authorities were forced to make a display of their fire-arms. This merely roused the mob to fury. They charged the building. The guards fired at their legs, but the majority rushed on, overwhelmed the guards, broke into the building, and set fire to it. The officials were badly knocked about, but even at this stage no serious hurt was committed. A fresh force of the World Police was brought to the spot. Not realizing that they were confronted by a brawl rather than a bloody revolution, the new-comers used machine guns. Owing to the practise of low firing there were very few serious casualties, but the crowd, far from being quelled, rushed forward, regardless of further casualties. There was a massacre. But thousands upon thousands of furious citizens now poured in from all directions. The police, now completely surrounded and fighting for their lives, fired indiscriminately. Walls of dead and dying surrounded them. But the people of London were by now possessed by savage and reckless hate. All the barbarous impulses that had been so thoroughly tamed during the last three centuries suddenly took charge. As the wall of dead rose, new attackers climbed over it, only to add their own dead bodies to its height. Presently ammunition ran out. The mob broke in and murdered everyone of the defenders. By now large reinforcements of World Police were converging on London. Desperate struggles took place in the suburbs.</p>
<p>At this stage the Lord Mayor of London made a radio appeal by loud speakers in the streets, urging the World Police to retire, and the people to go home. Meanwhile the metropolitan unarmed police, who were popular with the London crowds, were sent out to all the danger spots and coolly took charge of their rather weary fellow citizens. Seeing that the mobs were now well in hand, the armed police retired.</p>
<p>The news of London’s orgy spread by radio over the world. Other cities flared up in rage, and one by one were persuaded into quietness again. At last a statement was broadcast by a large section of the World Police in every country saying that they would no longer carry out the orders of their bureaucratic chiefs. It was now clear to the bureaucrats that the game was up. The World Government resigned, and many national governments followed its example. In Japan the ministers committed hara-kiri. Many of the chiefs of the great public services, national and international, surrendered their offices. Most of them reaffirmed their ideals but recognized that mankind was not yet ready to live up to so high an aim. Others recanted. For a whole month there was scarcely any public authority anywhere in the world except the local governments of Tibet, Britain, and Iceland. There was no world government. The police and the civil services were without their administrative heads. Yet there was no disorder. Everything functioned normally, in the spirit of benevolent anarchy. This condition could not last indefinitely, but no one had any authority to alter it. Earnest discussion took place by radio; and from this, as in a world- wide Friends’ Meeting, it emerged that the ‘feeling of the meeting’ was in favour of reinstating the old governments and the old bureaucratic class in general, and charging them with the task of putting the world on its feet again. Meanwhile the new political and social constitution could be thought out in detail. Thus for the time being the old governing class, chastened by its experience, retained its position, save for a small number of fanatics and adventurers who were dismissed. It is impossible that a revolution should end in this manner in any community that had not already far surpassed our present level of integrity and intelligence.</p>
<p>Thus the human race successfully avoided the danger of taking the first step towards reviving class dominance. With the warning of the recent troubles constantly in mind mankind gradually acquired a new temper and tradition of morality in public life. It was but an extension of the new temper and tradition of personal relations which had resulted in the slight but general increase in the will for the light. Once it had become firmly rooted, this new temper grew with surprising vigour. Whereas formerly honesty and generosity had been regarded as ideals difficult to attain, and men had on the whole expected their neighbours to treat them scurvily and their rulers to be tyrannical and corrupt, now honesty and generosity were increasingly ‘in the air’. Both in private and in public affairs men confidently expected to be treated decently.</p>
