<h3>第十八章　“滑行者号”的第一次航行</h3>
<p>从此以后，帕克斯在洛身边自在了一些。她照料过洛，得到过她的信任，也终于有了怜惜她的理由。即便如此，洛继续待在家里显然仍让帕克斯承受着压力。游艇下水后，约翰自己对我说：“我们现在必须尽快走。洛快把帕克斯耗死了，虽然她已经尽力不去这样做。可怜的帕克斯！她到底还是被逼着老起来了。”这是真的。她的头发已经开始失去光泽，嘴角也显得紧绷。</p>
<p>得知自己不会参加即将开始的航行、不会同行去寻找殖民地的新成员时，我心情很复杂。我终于可以过自己的生活，可以结婚、安顿下来，只要随时准备在约翰需要我时为他服务就行。可是，没有约翰，我该怎么生活？我试图说服他，说我对他仍有必要。一艘碟子似的怪船由三个孩子驾驶着在海上乱跑，如果船上至少有一个成年人，总归不那么引人注意。可我的建议被驳回。约翰说自己如今已经不太像孩子，还声称只要稍微修饰一下脸，就能让自己看起来至少二十五岁。</p>
<p>这三个年轻怪人为了使自己适合这场冒险而做的准备，我没有必要逐项详述。恩贡科和洛都得学会驾驶飞机，三个人还必须彻底熟悉他们那架怪飞机和那艘怪游艇的全部脾气。帕克斯在克莱德河为船主持下水，并把它命名为“滑行者号”（Skid）。这个名字虽古怪，却非常贴切，船也以此正式登记。顺便说一句，为了通过贸易委员会的检验，船上最初装着一台普通内燃机；检查之后，这台机器便被拆掉，把位置让给心身动力单元和电机。</p>
<p>游艇和飞机都准备妥当后，他们在西部群岛间进行了一次试航。这一趟，我被容许以客人身份同行。这次经历已经足够治好我乘这种魔鬼船进行更长航程的一切欲望。那艘三英尺模型不知怎么竟没让我真正想象到实船会有多么不舒服。巨大的船宽使它还算稳定，可吃水极浅，船身因此也极低，稍大一点的浪就会整个泼过甲板，天气一坏，船上几乎总是泡在水里。好在这并不算太严重，因为所有航行操纵装置都藏在一间流线型甲板室里，样子很像一辆运动型封闭轿车。天气晴好时，人还可以在甲板上伸展一下腿脚；可甲板下几乎没有活动空间，到处塞满机器、铺位和储物。还有那架飞机。按普通标准它小得不可思议，折起来像一把扇子，却仍占掉船内相当大的一部分空间。</p>
<p>驶出格里诺克以后，我们舒舒服服地沿克莱德河向下“滑”去，经过阿伦岛，再绕过金泰尔岬。随后遇上恶劣天气，我开始剧烈晕船。让我颇感快意的是，恩贡科也吐得一塌糊涂。事实上，他病得太厉害，约翰甚至决定找地方避风，免得这个家伙真把自己吐死。可恩贡科突然学会了控制呕吐反射。他停止呕吐，静静躺了十分钟，随后大喊一声胜利地从铺位上跳起来——紧接着船身一歪，他整个人又被甩进了厨房。</p>
<p>据说全部试验都非常成功。“滑行者号”全速前进时，整个船艏会完全抬出水面，在船体两侧各掀起一道像山脉一样的水墙和白沫。天气虽然颇为狂野，他们还是把飞机也试了一遍。飞机先由船尾吊杆放到水面，再在漂浮状态下展开。三名船员轮流驾驶。它最惊人的地方是，由于设计极为巧妙，而且动力储备惊人，飞机根本不必在水面滑跑，便能直接从水上升空。</p>
<p>一星期后，“滑行者号”开始第一次长航。我们在码头告别。韦恩赖特夫妇对小儿子离家远航的反应截然不同。博士真心担忧这种船本身的危险，也不太信任这支少年船员的能力。帕克斯却没有表现出焦虑，她对约翰的信心就是这样彻底。不过，很明显，要看着他离开而不流露难过，对她仍很困难。约翰抱住她，说：“亲爱的帕克斯。”然后便跳上船。已经道过别的洛又走回来，握住帕克斯两只手，竟然微笑着说：“亲爱的重要约翰之母！”面对这句奇怪的话，帕克斯只是吻了她一下。</p>
<p>我对那次航行所知不多，一部分来自约翰言简意赅的来信，另一部分来自他回国后的谈话。他们的行程主要由约翰的心灵感应研究决定。距离本身似乎并不影响他捕捉其他超常者心理活动的难易。能否成功，完全取决于他有没有能力“调谐”到对方的精神“频段”，也就是对方体验世界的方式；而这一点又取决于对方的心智模式同他自己的相似程度。因此，他已经能同西藏的一名超常者以及中国的另外两名超常者清楚交流；至于其他可能成为殖民地成员的人，他只能对他们是否存在以及大概位于何处作极其模糊的猜测。</p>
<p>来信告诉我们，“滑行者号”在非洲西海岸白白耗了三个星期。约翰曾驾驶飞机深入内陆，追踪据说出现在撒哈拉某处绿洲里的一个超常者痕迹。他撞上一场异常猛烈的沙暴，只得在沙漠中迫降，发动机也被沙子彻底堵住。“风停以后我把她肚子里的沙全掏干净，”约翰写道，“然后飞回‘滑行者号’，嘴里还在嚼沙子。”这件事背后究竟有多少堪称史诗的挣扎，我只能猜。</p>
<p>“滑行者号”抵达开普敦后进坞维修，三个年轻人则分头梳理南非各地，追踪一些非常微弱的超常心灵迹象。约翰和洛都很快空手而归。约翰在信中写道：“看白人把黑人当成劣等物种，真是妙极了。洛说，这让她想起母亲讲过的沙皇俄国旧事。”</p>
<p>约翰和洛不耐烦地等了几个星期。恩贡科无疑正为自己重新回到熟悉的原始环境而乐不可支，在恩加米兰偏远的林地和盐沼间到处钻探。他一直同约翰保持心灵感应联系，可他的行动似乎有某种神秘之处。约翰渐渐担心起来，因为这孩子实在太幼稚，而且性格或许不像自己那么平衡。最后，他不得不警告恩贡科：如果再“不收起那些鬼把戏”，“滑行者号”就会丢下他直接开走。回音只是兴高采烈地保证，说一两天内就动身返程。一星期后，消息终于来了，却同时像胜利欢呼和求救信号。他已经抓到了自己的“猎物”，正带着它穿过荒野返回文明地区；问题是，他没钱买返程火车票。于是约翰驾机前往他报告的位置，而洛独自一人把“滑行者号”开往德班。</p>
<p>约翰已经在那个简陋聚居地等了几天，恩贡科才终于出现。他累得半死，却满脸放光。他从背上卸下一个包裹，掀开一端，向恼火的约翰展示出一个小得可怜的黑人婴儿——明显早产，四肢抽动，呼吸急促。</p>
<p>看来，恩贡科一路把那些心灵感应上的暗示追踪到某个部落，又锁定其中一个女人。他在非洲的生活经验，使他从这个女人对森林生活的态度中察觉到某些同自己相似的东西。进一步探查后，他开始相信，这女人本人虽然也有轻微的超常性，但自己一直追踪的那些模糊信号，真正来源并不是母亲，而是她尚未出生的孩子；而在这个胎儿的产前体验中，恩贡科已经辨认出超常强度的萌芽。一个心灵在出生以前竟然就能产生任何心灵感应影响，本身已经极其惊人。那位母亲已经怀胎十一月。恩贡科知道，自己当年也是过期才出生的，直到部落里的智者妇女对他母亲使用了某些促产手段，他才降生。于是，他说服那个已经疲惫不堪的黑人妇人接受同样处理，并尽自己所能，照着记忆里的方法操作。孩子出生了，母亲却死了。恩贡科带着自己的“战利品”逃走。约翰问他这一路到底怎么喂孩子，恩贡科解释说，在阿比西尼亚生活时，他和其他孩子常常会给野羚羊挤奶。他们先悄悄接近母羚羊，再用一种让我想起徒手“逗”鳟鱼的技巧，使母兽愿意让人挤奶。这招在旅途中派上了用场。那婴儿当然长得很不好，不过总算活着。</p>
<p>这个绑架者发现自己的壮举不仅没有受到赞扬，反而遭到谴责和嘲笑，显得颇为受伤。约翰追问，他们到底能拿这东西怎么办？而且，它真的值得费这么大力气吗？恩贡科坚信自己弄到手的是一个婴儿超人，将来会把他们所有人都远远甩在后面。随着时间过去，约翰亲自用心灵感应探查这个新来的婴儿，也开始认真重视恩贡科的判断。</p>
<p>飞机飞往德班时，婴儿一直被恩贡科抱在怀里。照理说，照料它的责任似乎应该落到洛身上，可洛对孩子始终保持疏离。何况恩贡科自己也明确表示，这个新成员的一切责任都由他承担。那孩子不知怎么得了个“桑博”的名字。恩贡科很快就像母亲对待第一个孩子，又像男学生对待自己养的小白鼠一样，全心全意迷上了桑博。</p>
<p>“滑行者号”随后驶向孟买。在赤道以北某处，他们遇上极恶劣的天气。对这样适航性极强的船来说，这本身问题不大，虽然船员必定因此遭受了更多不适。很久以后，我才得知那几天的暴风中还发生过一件阴森的事，而约翰在信里从未提过。“滑行者号”发现一艘遇险的英国小汽船“弗鲁姆号”。它的舵机失灵，正被风浪推得横着迎击暴风。“滑行者号”一直在旁守候，直到“弗鲁姆号”的处境显然已经无可挽回，船员才登上仅存的两艘救生艇弃船。“滑行者号”试图同时拖带两艇。这一操作显然极其危险：一个巨浪竟把其中一艘救生艇整个抛上游艇后甲板，压得船艉沉入水中，几乎令“滑行者号”翻沉。当时正处理拖缆的恩贡科，一只脚被严重压伤。随后救生艇又从甲板上漂开，翻覆了。艇上人员只有两名获救，都是被“滑行者号”救起。另一艘救生艇则成功拖在后面。几天后天气转好，“滑行者号”拖着救生艇顺利向孟买前进。可是这时，船上的两个陌生人开始显露出强烈的好奇心。眼前是三个怪异的孩子和一个黑人婴儿，驾驶着一艘极其古怪的船，动力来源又完全无法理解。两名海员不断高声赞美自己的救命恩人，并向约翰保证，等到为“弗鲁姆号”失事举行公开调查时，他们一定会站出来替他说话。</p>
<p>这就非常麻烦了。三个超常者通过心灵感应讨论局面，一致认为必须采取激烈措施。约翰拿出一支自动手枪，把船上两个客人打死。枪声使被拖在后面的救生艇一阵骚乱。恩贡科解开拖缆，约翰驾驶游艇绕着救生艇盘旋；恩贡科和洛则趴在甲板上，用步枪把“弗鲁姆号”剩下的幸存者全部杀掉。这项阴森的工作结束后，尸体被扔给鲨鱼。救生艇上的血迹被彻底刷洗干净，随后整条艇被凿沉。“滑行者号”继续前往孟买。</p>
<p>很久以后，约翰亲口把这桩骇人事件告诉我时，我既厌恶，又困惑不解。我问他：既然他们连一点公众注意都不敢冒险，为什么当初又肯冒着船毁人亡的风险去救那些救生艇？为什么在救援过程中，他没有意识到曝光几乎不可避免？我还质问他：世上究竟有没有任何事业——哪怕是建立一个新物种——能够为这种冷冰冰地屠杀人类辩护？我说，如果这就是“高等人类”的做法，那么感谢上帝，我属于另一个物种。我们也许软弱，也许愚蠢，但至少有时还能感受到人的生命具有神圣性。这种暴行同智人历史上无数司法杀人、政治杀人、宗教杀人究竟有什么本质区别？我说，那些刽子手在当时也总认为自己做的是正义之举，可我们这个物种中更有人性的人却始终把它们视为野蛮。</p>
<p>约翰回答我时，带着一种温和而认真思考的态度——只有在极少数几次，我真的提出了值得他严肃考虑的问题时，他才会这样对待我。他首先指出，“滑行者号”此后仍不得不同智人的世界接触很长时间。他们在印度、西藏和中国都有工作要做。因此，一旦他们参与“弗鲁姆号”事件的事情公开，他们必然会被迫出席调查作证。他进一步强调，一旦他们真正的身份暴露，整个计划就会毁掉。假如在那个早期阶段，他们已经拥有后来那种通过催眠控制低等物种的知识，也许确实可以把“弗鲁姆号”幸存者脑中关于获救的一切记忆抹去。“可你要明白，”他说，“当时我们还做不到。我们是有意识地冒了曝光的风险，就像有意识地冒了被暴风摧毁的风险一样，只是希望最后能避过去。我们试着让那些客人发生一种‘遗忘化’，可失败了。至于那件事是否邪恶，菲多，你当然会本能地反感，可你漏掉了一点。假如我们也是你这个物种的成员，所关心的也只是正常心灵那些梦境般的目的，那么我们做的确实是犯罪。因为今天你们物种最重要的一课，就是必须学会：与其杀死同自己处于相同心智层级的存在，不如自己去死；哪怕牺牲智人所能追求的最高尚目的，也强过杀他们。可是，就像你们为了让远比狼和虎更明亮的人类精神能够繁荣而杀死狼虎一样，我们杀死了那些不幸的、刚刚被我们救起的生物。他们当然无辜，却很危险。他们在毫无恶意、甚至完全不自知的情况下，威胁到了这个星球迄今出现过的最高贵的实践事业。想想看！如果是你和伯莎被丢进一个由大型猿类统治的世界，它们自有它们的聪明，也很可爱，可又盲目、粗野、暴烈——你会拒绝杀吗？你会为了不杀猿而牺牲建立一个人类世界的机会吗？那种拒绝不是身体上的懦弱，而是精神上的懦弱。说到底，如果我们有能力把你们整个物种抹掉，坦率地讲，我们会这么做。因为只要你们发现我们的存在，并多少明白我们究竟是什么，你们就一定会毁灭我们。还有一件事你必须记住：我们知道，智人已经没有多少东西能够继续贡献给这个星球的音乐；事实上，剩下的只会是徒劳的重复。现在该让更精良的乐器接过主题了。”</p>
<p>说完以后，约翰几乎带着恳求的神情望着我。他仿佛非常渴望得到我的赞同——一个半人类生物、他忠诚猎犬的赞同。他到底还是感到内疚吗？我想不是。我认为，他这样强烈地想说服我，单纯是因为感情。至于我自己，我对约翰的信任竟到了这样一种程度：我虽然无法赞同他，却也无法谴责他。这里一定有某个层面，是我太愚笨、或太迟钝，根本无法把握的。我总觉得约翰一定是对的。他做的事，如果换成我们中任何一个人来做，都会绝对错误；可我仍带着近乎激情的信念相信，在约翰所处的处境中，由约翰做出这件可怕的事，就是正确的。</p>
<p>不过还是回到故事。在孟买，约翰和洛花了一段时间学习印度与西藏的语言，并做其他准备，以便同东方民族接触。终于离开“滑行者号”和孟买时，恩贡科留下照料桑博，也养自己的伤脚。两个探险者一同乘飞机出发；但洛乔装成尼泊尔男孩，在印度一处山地避暑站落地。据推测，那里存在某个超常者，他们希望洛能发展出此前捕捉到的那种心灵感应联系。约翰则继续飞越大山，前往西藏，去见那名同自己早已多次交流的年轻佛教僧侣。</p>
<p>约翰那封简短的西藏来信几乎没有描述实际旅程，虽然即便对超人驾驶的超常飞机而言，飞越喜马拉雅山也必定是极其苛刻的挑战。他只写道：“她越过去的时候漂亮极了，然后又被风整个吹回印度，还是翻着跟头吹回去的。她把我的保温瓶掉了。返程时我在山脊上看见它，但没管。”</p>
<p>由于那名西藏僧侣能够通过心灵感应为他指路，约翰很容易便找到了寺院。约翰把朗阿策（Langatse）描述为一个四十岁的超常者，但在身体上才刚刚越过成年门槛。他天生没有眼睛。失明迫使他把全部注意力集中在心灵感应能力上，而他在这方面的发展远远超过约翰。他随时都能通过心灵感应“看见”别人正在看什么；因此，阅读时只需借用别人的眼睛。对方让视线沿书页移动，朗阿策便在心灵层面跟着读。他训练了几个年轻人专门为自己承担这项工作，配合熟练到让他阅读速度几乎能赶上约翰。失明还有一个奇怪后果：因为他能同时使用许多双眼睛，从多个方向一起看一个物体，所以他的心理表象属于普通人根本无法想象的类型。照约翰的话说，他不是仅仅获得物体的某一个视觉侧面，而是直接在视觉上把握“整个东西”。在心灵中，他能够同时从所有角度看见它。</p>
<p>约翰原本希望说服朗阿策加入他的伟大冒险，却很快发现这根本不可能。西藏人看待整个计划的态度，同阿德兰非常相似。他感兴趣，也愿意鼓励，却始终保持超然。在他看来，建立一个新世界当然总有一天必须由某个人完成，却并非眼下最紧迫的事情，更不能诱使他偏离自己更高层次的精神侍奉。不过，他很高兴地答应担任殖民地的精神顾问；与此同时，他还愿意把自己掌握的全部心灵感应技术，以及其他超常活动的方法，都传授给约翰。有一阵子，朗阿策甚至建议约翰干脆放弃计划，留在西藏，同自己一起投身那些更艰难也更崇高的精神冒险。但发现约翰绝不会轻易被说服以后，他很快便不再坚持。约翰在寺院住了一周。返程飞行途中，他收到朗阿策的讯息：经过一次严肃的精神修炼后，朗阿策决定帮助约翰，负责搜寻亚洲各地适合参加这场冒险的年轻超常者，并提前训练他们。</p>
<p>约翰还收到洛的消息。她发现了一对很不寻常的姐妹，两人都比她年轻。她们愿意加入探险；但姐姐眼下健康极差，妹妹又还只是个小孩子，所以暂时必须留在原地。</p>
<p>“滑行者号”随后驶向中国海。在广州，约翰见到了沈括（Shên Kuo），那个此前已经同他有过一些交流的中国超常男孩。沈括很爽快地同意前往内陆，同朗阿策在遥远的四川发现的另外两个男孩和两个女孩会合。随后，这五个人将一同前往西藏朗阿策的寺院，在开始新生活之前接受一段精神训练。朗阿策还报告说，他已经为殖民地找到三个西藏男孩和一个女孩，也会在寺院里为他们作准备。</p>
<p>他们还招募到另一个新人。这是一个住在旧金山的中美混血女孩，名叫华盛顿妮娅·钟（Washingtonia Jong）。这个孩子同样是朗阿策通过心灵感应发现的。“滑行者号”横渡太平洋去接她，她随即直接成为船员。我直到很久以后才见到她；不过不妨先说，我最初觉得“华希”（大家这样叫她）完全是个普通年轻姑娘——一个精力充沛的小美国摩登女郎，眼睛略带中国人的形状，黑发剪得短短的。但后来我才发现，她绝不只是这样。</p>
<p>约翰接下来的任务，是为殖民地找到一座合适的岛。气候必须温带或亚热带；土壤必须肥沃；地理条件要适合捕鱼；而且必须远离任何常用轮船航线。最后一点极其重要，因为绝对保密是整个计划的根本。即使再偏僻、再不起眼的岛，迟早也一定会有人造访；所以约翰已经设计出一些办法，用来阻止船只接近他的岛，也设计了另一些办法，确保任何真正登陆的来客都不会把殖民地的消息传回正常人类世界。这些手段我以后再说。</p>
<p>“滑行者号”越过赤道，开始系统勘察南太平洋。航行许多星期后，他们终于发现了一座合适但极小的岛，大致位于新西兰—巴拿马航线与新西兰—合恩角航线之间的夹角区域，而且离两条航线都很远。能找到它简直幸运得难以置信，甚至完全可以说是一种天意。因为这座岛根本没有标在任何海图上，而且有明显迹象表明，它不过是在最近二十年左右才因海底地质扰动被抬升出海面。岛上没有非人哺乳动物，也没有爬行动物。植被仍很稀疏，种类也非常单调。然而，岛上已经有人居住。一小群土著先行占据了这里，靠在沿岸捕鱼生活。他们还从原来的家乡带来许多种植物和树木，在岛上种植成活。</p>
<p>直到很久以后，我亲自去了那座岛，才听说这些最初居民的事情。“他们都是些单纯而讨人喜欢的生物，”约翰说，“不过当然，我们不能允许他们妨碍计划。理论上，也许可以把他们脑中关于这座岛和关于我们的一切记忆全部抹掉，再把他们运走。可虽然我从朗阿策那里学到了很多，我们的‘遗忘化’技术仍不可靠。何况，又能把这些土著送去哪里而不引发抗议与好奇？我们也可以把他们留在岛上，当作家畜一样养着，可那会破坏我们的计划，也会在精神上毁掉他们自己。所以我们决定消灭他们。有一种催眠技巧——你要愿意，也可以叫魔法——我当时已经相当有把握，能够成功作用于那些具有强烈宗教信念的正常心灵。我们决定就用这个。土著欢迎我们来到岛上，还为我们举行宴会。宴会以后有仪式舞蹈和宗教礼仪。等到情绪达到最高点时，我让洛为他们跳舞。她跳完以后，我用他们自己的语言告诉他们，我们是神，我们需要这座岛，因此他们必须为自己搭起一座巨大的火葬柴堆，然后所有人一起走上去，一起躺下，并且欣然死去。他们照做了，而且做得极其欢喜——男人、女人和孩子全都如此。等他们全部死去以后，我们点燃柴堆，把尸体烧掉。”</p>
<p>我无法为这一行为辩护。不过，我倒可以指出：假如入侵者是正常人类，他们多半会先给土著施洗，再塞给他们祈祷书和欧洲服装，外加朗姆酒以及白人携带的一切疾病。他们还会在经济上奴役这些人，并随着时间推移，在每一个生活环节里用白人那点微不足道的优势反复压迫他们，最终把他们的精神也碾碎。等这些土著终于全都死于酒精或苦闷以后，白人又会为他们哀悼。</p>
<p>也许，对这些超常者夺取岛屿时实施的“心理谋杀”，唯一可能的辩护只能这样说：既然他们已经决定，不惜任何代价也必须让这座岛完全属于自己，而且不能有原住民牵累，那么他们就没有逃避这一决定的后果。他们睁着眼睛承担自己的选择，并以他们认为最干净利落的方式完成了任务。至于他们如此无情追求的目的，究竟是否真的足以为这些手段辩护，我完全不觉得自己有资格作判断。我的一切同情都倾向于这样一种观点：无论所追求的目的多么崇高，谋杀都永远无法得到正当化。毫无疑问，如果这种杀戮由我自己的同类实施，就理应受到最严厉的谴责。可我又算什么，竟能审判那些每天同我接触时都不断证明自己不仅智力远胜于我、甚至道德洞察也高于我的存在？</p>
<p>当五个高等存在——约翰、洛、恩贡科、华盛顿妮娅，以及婴儿桑博——占有这座岛以后，他们休息了几个星期，一面从长途旅行中恢复，一面整理未来定居点的场地，并同朗阿策以及受他指导的那些人保持联络。他们约定，一旦亚洲的成员完成精神准备，就各自设法前往法属波利尼西亚群岛中的某一座岛，再由“滑行者号”去接。不过在此之前，“滑行者号”会先经麦哲伦海峡匆匆返回英国一趟，采购建立殖民地所需的物资，并把剩余的欧洲超常者接来。</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" lang="en" hidden>
<h3><span id="a348"></span>CHAPTER XVIII. THE SKID’S FIRST VOYAGE</h3>
<p><span class="t28">H</span><span class="t31">ENCEFORTH</span><span class="t30"> P</span><span class="t31">AX</span><span class="t30"> </span><span class="t31">WAS</span><span class="t30"> </span>more at ease with Lo. She had nursed her, received her confidence, and found occasion to pity her. All the same it was clear that the continued presence of Lo was a strain on Pax. When the yacht was launched, John himself said to me, “We must get away as soon as possible now. Lo is killing Pax, though she does her best not to. Poor Pax! She’s being driven into old age at last.” It was true. Her hair was fading, and her mouth drawn.</p>
<p>It was with mixed feelings that I learned that I was not to take part in the coming voyage in search of additional members for the colony. I could live my own life. I could marry and settle down, holding myself in readiness to serve John when he should need me. But how could I live without John? I tried to persuade him that I was necessary to him. A saucer-like craft wandering the oceans with a crew of three children would attract less attention if she carried one adult. But my suggestion was dismissed. John claimed that he no longer looked a child, and further declared that he could touch up his face so as to appear at least twenty-five.</p>
<p>I need not describe in detail the preparations which these three young eccentrics undertook in order to fit themselves for their adventure. Both Ng-Gunko and Lo had to learn to fly; and all three had to become familiar with the mannerisms of their own queer aeroplane and their own queer yacht. The vessel was launched on the Clyde by Pax, and christened Skid, under which odd but appropriate name she was duly registered. I may mention that for the Board of Trade inspection she was fitted with a normal motor-engine, which was subsequently removed to make room for the psycho-physical power unit and motor.</p>
<p>When both yacht and plane were ready for use, a trial trip was made among the Western Isles. On this trip I was tolerated as a guest. The experience was enough to cure me of any desire for a longer voyage in such a diabolical vessel. The three-foot model had somehow failed to make me imagine the discomforts of the actual boat. Her great beam made her fairly steady, but she was so shallow, and therefore low in the water, that every considerable wave splashed over her, and in rough weather she was always awash. This did not greatly matter, as her navigating controls were all under cover in a sort of stream-lined deck-house reminiscent of a sporting saloon motor-car. In fine weather one could stretch one’s legs on deck, but below deck there was scarcely room to move, as she was a mass of machinery, bunks, stores. And there was the plane. This strange instrument, minute by ordinary standards, and folded up like a fan, occupied a large amount of her space.</p>
<p>After emerging from Greenock we skidded comfortably down the Clyde, past Arran, and round the Mull of Kintyre. Then we struck heavy weather, and I was violently sick. So also, much to my satisfaction, was Ng-Gunko. Indeed, he was so ill that John decided to make for shelter, lest he should die. But quite suddenly Ng-Gunko learned to control his vomiting reflexes. He stopped being sick, lay still for ten minutes, then leapt from his bunk with a shout of triumph, only to be hurled into the galley by a lurch of the ship.</p>
<p>The trials were said to be entirely successful. When she was going full speed the Skid lifted the whole part of herself right out of the water and set up a mountain-range of water and foam on either side of her. Though the weather was rather wild, the plane also was tested. It was heaved out aft on a derrick and unfolded while afloat. All three members of the crew took a turn at flying it. The most surprising thing about it was that, owing to its cunning design and its immense reserve of power, it rose straight from the water without taxi-ing.</p>
<p>A week later the Skid set out on her first long voyage. Our farewells were made at the dock. The Wainwright parents reacted very differently to the departure of their youngest son. Doc was genuinely anxious about the dangers of the voyage in such a vessel, and mistrustful of the abilities of the juvenile crew. Pax showed no anxiety, so complete was her confidence in John. But clearly she found it difficult to face his departure without showing distress. Hugging her, he said, “Dear Pax,” then sprang on board. Lo, who had already made her farewells, came back to Pax, took both her hands, and said, actually smiling, “Dear mother of important John!” To this odd remark Pax replied simply with a kiss.</p>
<p>My slender knowledge of the voyage is derived partly from John’s laconic letters, partly from conversation after his return. The programme was determined by his telepathic researches. Distance, apparently, made no difference to the ease with which he could pick up the psychic processes of other supernormals. Success depended entirely on his ability to “tune in” to their mental “setting” or mode of experience, and this depended on the degree of similarity of their mode to his own. Thus he was already in clear communication with a supernormal in Tibet, and two others in China, but for the rest he could only make the vaguest guesses as to the existence and location of possible members of the colony.</p>
<p>Letters told us that the Skid had spent an unprofitable three weeks on the West Coast of Africa. John had flown into the hinterland, pursuing traces of a supernormal in some oasis in the Sahara. He struck a sand storm of peculiar violence, and made a forced landing in the desert, with his engine choked with sand. “When the wind had dropped I cleaned her guts,” said John, “and then flew back to the Skid, still chewing sand.” What epic struggles were involved in this adventure I can only guess.</p>
<p>At Cape Town the Skid was docked, and the three young people set off to comb South Africa, following up certain meagre traces of the supernormal mentality. Both John and Lo soon returned empty-handed. In his letter John remarked, “Delicious to watch the Whites treating the Blacks as an inferior species. Lo says it reminds her of her mother’s stories of Tsarist Russia.”</p>
<p>John and Lo waited impatiently for some weeks while Ng-Gunko, doubtless revelling in his return to native conditions, nosed about in the remote woodlands and saltpans of Ngamiland. He was in telepathic communication with John, but there seemed to be some mystery about his activities. John grew anxious, for the lad was dangerously juvenile, and possibly of a less balanced type than himself. At last he was driven to tell Ng-Gunko that if he did not “chuck his antics” the Skid would sail without him. The reply was merely a cheerful assurance that he would be starting back in a day or two. A week later came a message that combined a cry of triumph and an S.O.S. He had secured his prey, and was making his way through the wilds to civilization, but had no money for the return railway journey. John therefore set out to fly to the spot indicated, while Lo, single handed, took the Skid round to Durban.</p>
<p>John had already been waiting some days at the primitive settlement when Ng-Gunko appeared, dead beat, but radiant. He removed a bundle from his back, uncovered the end of it, and displayed to the indignant John a minute black infant, immature, twitching and gasping.</p>
<p>Ng-Gunko, it seems, had traced the telepathic intimations to a certain tribe and a certain woman. His African experiences had enabled him to detect in this woman’s attitude to the life of the forest something akin to his own. Further investigation led him to believe that though she herself was in some slight degree supernormal, the main source of those obscure hints which he had been pursuing was not the mother but her unborn child, in whose pre-natal experiences Ng-Gunko recognized the rudiments of supernormal intensity. It was indeed remarkable that before birth a mind should have any telepathic influence at all. The mother had already carried her baby for eleven months. Now Ng-Gunko knew that he himself had been born late, and that his mother had not been delivered of him till certain incentives had been used upon her by the wise women of the tribe. This treatment he persuaded the black matron to undergo, for she was weary to death. As best he could, Ng-Gunko applied what he knew of the technique. The baby was born, but the mother died. Ng-Gunko fled with his prize. When John asked how he had fed the baby on the long journey, Ng-Gunko explained that in his Abyssinian days he and other youngsters used to milk wild antelopes. They stalked them, and by means of a process that reminded me of “tickling” trout, they persuaded the mothers to let themselves be milked. This trick had served on the journey. The infant, of course, had not thrived, but it was alive.</p>
<p>The kidnapper was pained to find that his exploit, far from being applauded, was condemned and ridiculed. What on earth, John demanded, could they possibly do with the creature? And anyhow, was he really at all worth bothering about? Ng-Gunko was convinced that he had secured an infant superman who would outclass them all; and in time John himself was impressed by his telepathic explorations of the new-comer.</p>
<p>The plane set out for Durban with the baby in Ng-Gunko’s arms. One would have expected the care and maintenance of it to fall to Lo, but her attitude toward it was aloof. Moreover, Ng-Gunko himself made it clear that he would bear all responsibility for the new-comer, who somehow acquired the name Sambo. Ng-Gunko became as devoted to Sambo as a mother to her first-born or a schoolboy to his white mice.</p>
<p>The Skid now headed for Bombay. Somewhere north of the Equator she ran into very heavy weather. This was a matter of small importance to a craft of her seaworthiness, though it must have greatly increased the discomfort of her crew. At a much later date I learned of a sinister incident that occurred in those wild days, an incident to which John made no reference in his letters. The Skid sighted a small British steamer, the Frome, in distress. Her steering engine was out of action, and she was labouring broadside-on to the storm. The Skid stood by, till, when the Frome’s plight was obviously hopeless, the crew took to the two remaining boats. The Skid attempted to take them both in tow. This operation was evidently very dangerous, for a sea flung one of the boats bodily on to the after deck of the yacht, thrusting her stern under water, and threatening to sink her. Ng-Gunko, who was dealing with the tow rope, had a foot rather badly crushed. The boat then floated off, and capsized. Of her crew only two were rescued, both by the Skid. The other boat was successfully taken in tow. A few days later the weather improved, and the Skid and her charge made good progress toward Bombay. But now the two strangers on board the Skid began to show great curiosity. Here were three eccentric children and a black baby cruising the ocean in a most eccentric craft driven by some unintelligible source of power. The two seamen were loud in praise of their rescuers. They assured John that they would speak up for him in the public inquiry which would be held over the loss of the Frome.</p>
<p>This was all very inconvenient. The three supernormals discussed the situation telepathically, and agreed that drastic action was demanded. John produced an automatic pistol and shot the two guests. The noise caused great excitement in the boat. Ng-Gunko slipped the tow rope, and John cruised round while Ng-Gunko and Lo, lying on the deck with rifles, disposed of all the Frome’s survivors. When this grim task was finished, the corpses were thrown to the sharks. The boat was scrubbed of blood stains, and then scuttled. The Skid proceeded to Bombay.</p>
<p>Long afterwards, when John told me about this shocking incident, I was as much perplexed as revolted. Why, I asked, if he dared not risk a little publicity, had he been willing to risk destruction in the work of rescuing the ship’s boats? And how did he fail to realize, during that operation, that publicity was inevitable? And was there, I demanded, any enterprise whatever, even the founding of a new species, that could justify such cold butchery of human beings? If this was the way of Homo superior, I said, thank God I was of another species. We might be weak and stupid, but at least we were able sometimes to feel the sanctity of human life. Was not this piece of brutality on exactly the same footing as the innumerable judicial murders, political murders, religious murders, that had sullied the record of Homo sapiens? These, I declared, had always seemed to their perpetrators righteous acts, but were regarded by the more human of our kind as barbarous.</p>
<p>John answered with that mildness and thoughtfulness with which he treated me only on those rare occasions when I gave him matter for serious consideration. He first pointed out that the Skid had still to spend much time in contact with the world of Homo sapiens. Her crew had work to do in India, Tibet and China. It was certain, therefore, that if their part in the Frome incident became public, they would be forced to give evidence at the inquiry. He insisted, further, that if they were discovered, their whole venture would be ruined. Had they at that early stage known all that they knew later about hypnotically controlling the humbler species, they might indeed have abolished from the minds of the Frome’s survivors all memory of the rescue. “But you see,” he said, “we could not do it. We had deliberately risked publicity, as we had risked destruction by the storm, hoping to avoid it. We tried to set up a process of ‘oblivifaction’ in our guests, but we failed. As for the wickedness of the act, Fido, it naturally revolts you, but you are leaving something out of account. Had we been members of your species, concerned only with the dreamlike purposes of the normal mind, what we did would have been a crime. For to-day the chief lesson which your species has to learn is that it is far better to die, far better to sacrifice even the loftiest of all ‘sapient’ purposes, than to kill beings of one’s own mental order. But just as you kill wolves and tigers so that the far brighter spirits of men may flourish, so we killed those unfortunate creatures that we had rescued. Innocent as they were, they were dangerous. Unwittingly they threatened the noblest practical venture that has yet occurred on this planet. Think! If you, and Bertha, had found yourselves in a world of great apes, clever in their own way, lovable too, but blind, brutish, and violent, would you have refused to kill? Would you have sacrificed the founding of a human world? To refuse would be cowardly, not physically, but spiritually. Well, if we could wipe out your whole species, frankly, we would. For if your species discovers us, and realizes at all what we are, it will certainly destroy us. And we know, you must remember, that Homo sapiens has little more to contribute to the music of this planet, nothing in fact but vain repetition. It is time for finer instruments to take up the theme.”</p>
<p>When he had done, John looked at me almost pleadingly. He seemed to long for my approval, the approval of a half-human thing, his faithful hound. Did he, after all, feel guilty? I think not. I think this strong desire to persuade me sprang simply from affection. For my part, such is my faith in John, that though I cannot approve, I cannot condemn. There must surely be some aspect that I am too stupid or insensitive to grasp. John, I feel, must be right. Though he did what would have been utterly wrong if it had been done by any of us, I have an almost passionate faith that, done by John, and in John’s circumstances, the terrible deed was right.</p>
<p>But to return to the story. At Bombay John and Lo spent some time studying Indian and Tibetan languages, and otherwise preparing themselves for contact with Eastern races. When at length they left the Skid, and Bombay, Ng-Gunko remained behind to nurse Sambo and his damaged foot. The two explorers set out together in the plane; but Lo, disguised as a Nepalese boy, was put down at an Indian hill station. There, it was hoped, she might develop a telepathic contact which was thought to indicate a supernormal in some such environment. John himself continued his flight over the great mountains to Tibet to meet the young Buddhist monk with whom he had often been in communication.</p>
<p>In his brief letter describing his expedition to Tibet John scarcely mentioned the actual journey, though the flight over the Himalayas must have been an exacting task even for a superman in a superplane. He said only, “She took the jump splendidly, and then was blown right back again into India, head over heels, too. She dropped my thermos flask. Coming back I saw it on the ridge, but let it lie.”</p>
<p>As the Tibetan monk was able to guide him telepathically, he found the monastery quite easily. John described Langatse as a supernormal of forty years, physically but little advanced beyond the threshold of manhood. He had been born without eyes. Blindness had forced him to concentrate on his telepathic powers, which he had developed far beyond John’s own attainment. He could always see telepathically what other people were seeing; consequently, for reading he had simply to use some one else’s eyes. The other would cast his eye over the page while Langatse followed telepathically. He had trained several young men to perform this task for him so well that he could read almost as quickly as John. One curious effect of his blindness was that, since he could use many pairs of eyes at a time, and could see all round an object at once, his mental imagery was of a kind quite inconceivable to ordinary persons. As John put it, he grasped things visually, instead of merely having a single aspect of them. He saw things mentally from every point of view at once.</p>
<p>John had originally hoped to persuade Langatse to join his great adventure, but he soon found that this was out of the question. The Tibetan regarded the whole matter much as Adlan had done. He was interested, encouraging, but aloof. To him the founding of a new world, though it must indeed some day be accomplished by some one, was not a matter of urgency, and must not tempt him from his own more lofty spiritual services. Nevertheless he consented gladly to be the spiritual adviser of the colony, and meanwhile he would impart to John all that he knew of the telepathic technique and other supernormal activities. At one time Langatse suggested that John should give up his enterprise and settle in Tibet to share the more exacting and more exalted spiritual adventures on which he himself was engaged. But, finding that John was not to be easily persuaded, he soon desisted. John stayed at the monastery a week. During his return flight he received a message from Langatse to the effect that, after grave spiritual exercise, he had decided to help John by seeking out and preparing any young supernormals that were in Asia and suited to the adventure.</p>
<p>John received a communication also from Lo. She had discovered two remarkable sisters, both younger than herself. They would join the expedition; but as the elder was just now in a very poor state of health, and the younger a little child, they must stay where they were for the present.</p>
<p>The Skid now set her course for the China Seas. In Canton John met Shên Kuo, the Chinese supernormal boy with whom he had already had some communication. Shên Kuo readily agreed to make his way inland to join the two other boys and two girls whom Langatse had discovered in the remote Eastern Province of Sze Chwan. Thence the five would journey to Tibet to Langatse’s monastery, to undergo a course of spiritual discipline in preparation for their new life. Langatse reported that he had also secured three Tibetan boys and a girl for the colony, and that these also would be prepared at the monastery.</p>
<p>One other convert was made. This was a half-caste Chinese American girl resident in San Francisco. This child, who went by the name of Washingtonia Jong, was also discovered by Langatse telepathically. The Skid crossed the Pacific to pick her up, and she straightway became a member of the crew. I did not meet her till a much later date; but I may as well say at once that “Washy,” as she was called, appeared to me at first a quite normal young person, a keen little American flapper with rather Chinese eyes and black cropped hair. But I was to find that there was more in her than that.</p>
<p>John’s next task was to discover a suitable island for the colony. It must have a temperate or subtropical climate. It must have a fertile soil, and be well situated for fishing. It must be remote from any frequented steamer route. This last requirement was extremely important, for complete secrecy was essential. Even the most remote and unconsidered island would certainly be visited sooner or later; so John had thought out certain steps to prevent ships from reaching his island, and certain others to ensure that any visitors who should make a landing should not spread news of the colony among the normal species. Of these devices I shall speak in due season.</p>
<p>The Skid crossed the Equator and began a systematic exploration of the South Seas. After many weeks of cruising a suitable though minute island was discovered somewhere in the angle between the routes from New Zealand to Panama and New Zealand to Cape Horn, and well away from both courses. This discovery was an incredible stroke of luck, indeed it might well have been an act of Providence. For the island was one which was not marked on any charts, and there were clear signs that it had only within the last twenty years or so been thrown up by sub-oceanic disturbance. There were no non-human mammals on it, and no reptiles. Vegetation was still scanty and undiversified. Yet the island was inhabited. A small native group had taken possession of it, and were living by fishing around its coasts. Many varieties of plants and trees they themselves had brought over from their original home and established on the island.</p>
<p>I did not hear about these original inhabitants till much later, when I visited the island. “They were simple and attractive creatures,” said John, “but, of course, we could not allow them to interfere with our plans. It might have been possible to obliterate from their minds every recollection of the island and of ourselves, and then to transport them. But though I had learned much from Langatse, our technique of oblivifaction was still unreliable. Moreover, where could we have deposited the natives without rousing protests, and curiosity? We might have kept them alive on the island, as domestic animals, but this would have wrecked our plans. It would also have undermined the natives spiritually. So we decided to destroy them. One bit of hypnotic technique (or magic, if you like) I felt sure I could now perform successfully on normal minds in which there were strong religious convictions. This we decided to use. The natives had welcomed us to their island and arranged a feast for us. After the feast there were ritual dances and religious rites. When the excitement was at a climax, I made Lo dance for them. And when she had done, I said to them, in their own language, that we were gods, that we needed their island, that they must therefore make a great funeral pyre for themselves, mount it together, lie down together, and gladly die. This they did, most gladly, men, women and children. When they had all died we set fire to the faggots and their bodies were burnt.”</p>
<p>I cannot defend this act. But I may point out that, had the invaders been members of the normal species, they would probably have baptised the natives, given them prayer books and European clothes, rum and all the diseases of the White Man. They would also have enslaved them economically, and in time they would have crushed their spirits by confronting them at every turn with the White Man’s trivial superiority. Finally, when all had died of drink or bitterness, they would have mourned for them.</p>
<p>Perhaps the only defence of the psychological murders which the supernormals committed when they took possession of the island would run as follows. Having made up their minds that at all costs the island must be theirs, and unencumbered, they did not shirk the consequences of their decision. With open eyes they went about their task, and fulfilled it in the cleanest possible way. Whether the end which they so ruthlessly pursued did in fact justify the means, I simply do not feel competent to decide. All my sympathies lie with the view that murder can never be justified, however lofty the end at stake. Certainly, had the killing been perpetrated by members of my own kind, such a deed would have deserved the sternest condemnation. But who am I that I should judge beings who in daily contact with me constantly proved themselves my superiors not only in intelligence but in moral insight?</p>
<p>When the five superior beings, John and Lo, Ng-Gunko and Washingtonia and the infant Sambo, had taken possession of the island, they spent some weeks resting from their travels, preparing the site for future settlement, and conferring with Langatse and those who were under his guidance. It was arranged that as soon as the Asiatics were spiritually equipped they should find their way as best they could to one of the French Polynesian islands, whence the Skid would fetch them. Meanwhile, however, the Skid would make a hurried trip to England via the Straits of Magellan to secure materials for the founding of the colony, and to fetch the remaining European supernormals.</p>
