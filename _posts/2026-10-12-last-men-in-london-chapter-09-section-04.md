<h2>iv. 本书地球作者的后记</h2>
<p>读过我早先那本《最后与最初的人》的读者也许还记得，那本书最后有一篇后记。我的海王星控制者声称，那篇后记是在该书主体内容传达之后数千年的某个海王星历史时刻传给我的。显然，本书的发端处于一个比前一本书主体传达时间稍晚的海王星年代，却又远远早于那篇后记。如今我有理由相信，在前一篇后记本身传达之后很久的某个时候，我的控制者曾试图再向我传送一篇属于本书的后记；但由于最后人类最精细的脑神经通路已经严重解体，他们的世界社会也在逐渐崩溃，结果极度混乱而支离破碎。</p>
<p>现在，我将试着把那些似乎来自海王星控制者、偶尔进入我头脑的零散思想、激情，以及少数明确陈述拼接起来；再谨慎地动用我自己的想象作为补充，试图重建这样一幅图景：当灾难已经发展到很深程度时，海王星上的情形究竟如何。</p>
<p>从传入我心中的那股模糊暗示洪流来看，我推断：他们希望在灾难彻底消灭人类能力之前，把生命种子散播到群星之间，这一希望并没有实现。播撒实际上曾经开始，可人们随后发现，那些生命种子在散播过程中一直遭到毁坏。这个问题，以及其他意料之外的困难——似乎都发生在此前那篇后记传达之后的某个时期——不仅拖延了整个事业，甚至导致它被放弃了几千年。后来，心理物理学中的某项新发现重新带来希望：也许可以通过一种完全不同的方法完成“大播种”。于是，一个全新的宏伟工程开始了；但气候日益残酷，心智能力不断退化，国家战争与社会混乱又从未止息，这些因素严重妨碍了它。到了这最后一次、断断续续的通信发生之时，播种事业似乎已经永远停止。不过，这一推断并不确定。我无法判断，它究竟是因为已经成功完成而停止，还是因为物理上再无可能，抑或仅仅因为继续追求它的意志已经崩溃。大概对于一个已经退化、失去组织的世界共同体而言，这项工作已经超出了能力范围。</p>
<p>这个注定灭亡的族群在最终崩溃之前还决心完成另一项伟大任务，即“探索人类的过去”。这项工作似乎在某种意义上已经完成，只是细致程度低于原计划。几次通信都暗示，那些仍有能力、也仍愿意进入过去的工作者，正设法把衰退中的力量集中于人类历程的若干关键时刻，至少把这些片段以充分细密的方式重建出来。然而，种族式的心智模式已经不再可能，而个体探索者本身显然也已经严重退化，因此可以肯定，那些仍被收集的数据再也不可能并入那个单一的种族意识，也不可能进入完全觉醒的人类精神对宇宙的审美把握。事实上，对过去的探索之所以还继续着，似乎已经变得像我们地球上一些历史学家毫无目的的研究一样，机械而非理性。</p>
<p>前一本书的后记曾经报告：随着更高级、形成更晚的脑神经通路被狂暴的太阳紫外线腐蚀，那些幸存的共同体成员经历了一场令人痛苦的斗争——高贵天性与卑劣天性彼此厮杀，而最终不可避免地，是他们身上一切最优秀的东西失败了。到了本书后记所处的时代，这个种族似乎已经只剩下一小群惊惶失措、近乎亚人的残民，聚集在南极附近。可以想见，那里大概是整个星球最后还能勉强支持人类生存的地区。这些残余者的生活必定已经彻底失去目的，而且极其卑贱。似乎只剩下一点敷衍的农业，偶尔出发去猎杀那些被酷热逼向南方的野兽，以及邻近群体之间频繁的掠夺性袭击，用来抢夺彼此的食物和女人。</p>
<p>这群人显然被死亡、饥饿，尤其是疯狂的恐惧牢牢支配。死亡率必定极高，但出生率同样惊人。生育已经不再受控制。残疾或病弱儿童似乎占人口中相当大的比例。生育和性显然在大众心灵中占据了极大位置。存在疯狂的性狂欢，而且这些活动似乎获得了某种宗教意义；此外还有一整套庞大的迷信禁忌仪式，多数看起来都是为了净化灵魂，使它为永生作好准备。似乎有一个被称为“人之神”的神祇，人们相信他将以烈火毁灭世界，却会把那些遵守所有禁忌的人摄入永恒福乐。他的“弟弟”据说已经在海王星出现过，却被敌人杀死。这个传说也许应当以某种方式同前一本书后记中提到的那个高级存在联系起来，那里称他为“我们这位年轻的弟弟”。</p>
<p>并非所有人都屈服于这些迷信。我的控制者本人就拒绝了它们，并且对古老智慧保持着一种令人心酸的忠诚；那些智慧的碎片，他偶尔还能够记起。但我有理由相信，很多时候，他对它们的记忆只剩下没有洞察内容的语言公式。</p>
<p>显然，对于那些像我的控制者一样，心中还保留着一点昔日种族目的的人来说，如今已经没有任何理由继续回避种族自杀。为了完成那两项事业，他们才曾经推迟安乐死；可现在两项事业都已不可能再有效推进。然而，那些曾经属于所有人类共同体中最高贵者的不幸成员，如今已经既没有勇气，也没有常识去自行终结。接受流行迷信的人宣称，神已经禁止人类在自己的时辰到来之前主动求死；拒绝救赎学说的人，则说服自己相信，他们之所以不自杀，是因为逃走乃是懦弱。</p>
<p>这里，我要引述一段零碎的言语通信。它很能代表我收到的大多数讯息，也似乎印证了前面的许多推断：</p>
<p>“活着无益。可生命坚持。工作坚持。成千上万太多的人，男人，女人，眼睛溃烂、在地上爬的孩子。食物太少。蓝色太阳穿透深厚云层，烤焦田野。锅里煮着孩子肉。吃那些疯掉、摔死的人。疯子太多。拿刀的疯男人。微笑的疯女人，骷髅一样的脸。（而我还记得美。）劳作太多，恐惧太多。太热。热烘烘的人挤在身边，令人憎恶。彼此相爱？中间隔着汗！最好在水里相爱，在凉水里，水让身体变轻。水池，雨，连房屋都拔起的暴风，永恒的云，刺目的紫色。他们说，神很快会来。把世界烧掉，把他的朋友带走。神的梦。太美好，太廉价。辐射才是唯一的神，无处不在，残酷。快点死，睡下，再也不醒！可生命坚持，工作坚持。无益的工作坚持。为什么？遥远的旧日，无神，却美丽。辉煌的世界。男人女人的美丽世界，还有那单一的觉知。宇宙之眼。宇宙之心。灵巧的手。那时，我们快活地面对一切。甚至这种腐坏，当我们预见到它时，还把它叫作‘人这段短暂音乐的一个美丽终止’。这也配！琴弦全都走样，尖叫着跑调——这也叫美丽终止！”</p>
<p>这段话很典型，尽管已经比大多数通信连贯得多。还有一些更加惊人的片段，却太过违反我同时代人的趣味，我不敢复述。它们描述这个人口已经退化到何等兽性的程度：两性之间除最粗陋的方式之外，再也无法彼此引起兴趣。性生活之所以如此贫乏，是因为身体健康和美已经被摧毁，对经验中更精致的审美与精神侧面几乎再无感受能力，把其他个体真正认识为有生命的人格，也已十分罕见。几乎所有行为都成了野兽式的冲动行为——只是这些野兽的本性里，还残存着许多从早已被遗忘的人性那里继承来的习惯动作。</p>
<p>不过仍有少数男女，没有永久沉沦到这种兽性水平。几次通信都清楚表明：虽然最后人类的文化和实际心智能力此时已经严重退化，仍有极少数人——一个分散而不断缩小的贵族群体——至少能够间歇性地体验个人之爱，甚至还能困惑地忠于某些行为理想。证据就在下面这样的片段中。它短暂恢复了昔日的智力，又茫然摸索着寻找已经遗忘的狂喜，也许正适合用来作为本书的结尾：</p>
<p>“她一动不动地躺在浅水里。海浪像手指一样抚过滚烫的沙，又冒着蒸汽退回。她的肉体从前半透明，如今蒙上了一层灰膜，盲了，如同她那同样蒙膜的眼睛。我们曾经深深地互相取得彼此。酷热有时捉弄我们，使我们不能容忍对方不再年轻的身体、失去柔韧的心灵；然而，每个人又都是另一个人呼吸所必需的空气。我们的工作曾把我们带向极遥远而不同的地方：她走入天文空间，我走入史前时代。但现在，我们会一直留在一起，直到终点。我们已经没有别的事可做，只能记忆，只能忍耐，只能彼此寻找力量，尽可能长久地保持精神清明。人类那无数个纪元！那千百万、千万万的自我；蜉蝣一般，每一个对自己而言，都是宇宙唯一短暂而敏锐的焦点，是一切宇宙努力交汇的关节点。如今全部失败！那单一的、极少真正实现的精神！我们两个，如今已经如此混乱，却曾参与过人类最清明的自我阐释。它已经遗忘了。它只留下黑暗，而对昔日光明的盲目回忆，又使这黑暗更深。很快，会有更大的黑暗！人类像一只被吸入熔炉的飞蛾，消失；而后熔炉本身也消失，因为它也不过是广阔、永恒黑暗中一粒孤立的火星。如果这一切有某种意义，那也不是人的意义。然而在这全部混乱中，仍有一样东西独立其外，不可侵犯，而且美丽：对昔日光明的盲目回忆。”</p>
<p>全书完。</p>

<h2>iv. EPILOGUE BY THE TERRESTRIAL AUTHOR OF THIS BOOK</h2>
<p>READERS OF MY earlier book, Last and First Men, may remember that it closed with an epilogue which my Neptunian controller claimed to have transmitted to me from a date in Neptunian history many thousands of years after the communication of the body of the book. The present book was obviously originated at a Neptunian date shortly after the transmission of the main part of the earlier book, but very long before its epilogue. Now I have reason to believe that at some date long after the communication of that former epilogue itself, my controller attempted to give me an epilogue to the present volume, but that owing to the serious disintegration of the most delicate brain tracts of the Last Men, and the gradual break-up of their world society, the result has been extremely confused and fragmentary.</p>
<p>I shall now attempt to piece together the random thoughts and passions, and occasional definite statements, which have come into my mind seemingly from my Neptunian controller. By supplementing them with a careful use of my own imagination, I shall try to construct a picture of the state of affairs on Neptune when the disaster was already far advanced.</p>
<p>From the vague flood of intimations that has come through to me I gather that the hope of disseminating a seed of life abroad among the stars, before the disaster should have extinguished man’s powers, was not fulfilled. The dissemination had indeed actually been begun, when it was discovered that the vital seed was all the while being destroyed in the process of scattering. This trouble, and other unexpected difficulties, which occurred seemingly at some date after the communicating of the earlier epilogue, not merely delayed the enterprise, but even led to its abandonment for some thousands of years. Then some new discovery in psychophysics raised a hope of achieving the Dissemination by an entirely new method. A vast new enterprise was therefore set on foot; but was seriously hampered by the increasingly cruel climate, the steady deterioration of mental calibre, the ceaseless national wars and civil confusion. It would seem, though the inference is by no means certain, that at the date of this final and intermittent communication, the Dissemination had already ceased for ever; whether because it had been successfully accomplished or because it was no longer physically possible, or because the will to pursue it had already been broken, I cannot ascertain. Probably the work was now beyond the powers of a degenerate and disorganized world-community.</p>
<p>The other great task which the doomed population had undertaken to complete before its final downfall, the Exploration of the Human Past, seems to have been in a manner finished, though in less detail than was originally planned. Several communications suggest that such workers as remained able and willing to enter into the past were trying to concentrate their failing powers on certain critical points of Man’s career, so as to reconstruct at least these moments in full minuteness. But as the racial mode of mentality had already become impossible, and as the individual explorers had evidently very seriously deteriorated, it seems clear that such data as were still being collected would never be incorporated in the single racial consciousness, in the aesthetic apprehension of the Cosmos by the fully awakened Spirit of Man. In fact the continuation of the work of exploring the past seems to have become as automatic and irrational as the aimless researches of some of our Terrestrial historians.</p>
<p>In the epilogue to the earlier book it was reported that, as the higher and more recent brain-tracts were corroded by the fury of ultra-violet solar radiation, the surviving members of the community suffered a harrowing struggle between their nobler and their baser natures, a struggle which issued inevitably in the defeat of all that was most excellent in them. At the date of the epilogue to the present book, the race seems to have been reduced to a remnant of distraught and almost sub-human beings clustering round the South Pole, which, one may suppose, was the only region of the planet still capable of supporting human existence at all. The life of this remnant must have been completely aimless and abject. It apparently consisted of half-hearted agriculture, occasional hunting expeditions against the wild creatures that had been driven south by the heat, and frequent predatory raids of neighbouring groups to capture one another’s food and women.</p>
<p>The population was evidently obsessed by the terror of death, of starvation, and above all of insanity. The death-rate must have been very high, but there was also a huge birthrate. Conception was no longer controlled. Invalid children seem to have composed a large proportion of the population. Parenthood and sex evidently bulked very largely in the popular mind. There were wild sexual orgies, which apparently had acquired some religious significance; and there was a vast ritual of superstitious taboos, most of which seem to have been intended to purify the soul and prepare it for eternal life. Apparently some divinity, referred to as the God of Man, was expected to destroy the world by fire, but to snatch into eternal bliss those who had kept all the taboos. His Younger Brother, it seems, had already appeared on Neptune, but had been killed by his enemies. This legend should, perhaps, be connected in some way or other with the superior being who, in the epilogue to the previous book is referred to as ‘this younger brother of ours’.</p>
<p>Not every one succumbed to these superstitions. My controller himself rejected them, and retained a pathetic loyalty to the ancient wisdom, some shreds of which he now and then remembered. But I have reason to believe that very often his memory of them was but a verbal formula, without insight.</p>
<p>Clearly, for those who, like my controller, had kept alive in themselves something of the former purpose of the race, there was now no longer any reason to refrain from racial suicide. The two enterprises for which euthanasia had been postponed could no longer be usefully continued. But the unhappy members of what had once been the noblest of all human communities had no longer the courage or the common sense to destroy themselves. Those who accepted the popular superstitions declared that the God had forbidden men to seek death before their time. Those who rejected the doctrine of salvation persuaded themselves that they refrained from suicide because to flee was cowardly.</p>
<p>At this point I will report a verbal fragment of communication which is typical of most that I have received, and seems to bear out many of the foregoing inferences.</p>
<p>‘Vain to live. Yet Life insists. Work insists. Too many thousands of men, of women, of crawling children with eyes festering. Too little food. Blue sun piercing the deep cloud-zone, parching the fields. Child meat in the flesh pots. Eat the mad, who come to grief. The many mad. Mad men with knives. Mad women with smiles, with skull-faces. (And I remember beauty.) Too many labours and fears. Too hot. Hateful proximity of men hot. Love one another? With sweat between! Love best in the water, the cooling water, that makes the body lighter. The pools, the rains, the house-uprooting storms, the everlasting cloud, blinding purple. God will come soon, they say it. To burn the world, to take away his friends. The God-dream. Too good, too cheap. Radiation the one God, all pervading, cruel. To die quickly, to sleep and not wake! But life insists, work insists. Vain work insists. Yet why? The old far days, Godless, beautiful. The glorious world. The fair world of men and women and of the single awareness. Eye of the cosmos. Heart of the cosmos. Cunning hand. Then, we faced all things gaily. Even this corruption, when we foresaw it, we called “a fair end to the brief music that is man”. This! The strings all awry, screaming out of tune, a fair end!’</p>
<p>This passage is typical, though more coherent than most. There are others more striking, but so repugnant to the taste of my contemporaries that I dare not repeat them, passages describing the prevailing brutishness of sexual relations in a population that had already so far degenerated that neither sex could interest the other save in the crudest manner. This impoverishment of sexual life was due to the fact that physical health and beauty had been undermined, that apprehension of the more delicate, aesthetic and spiritual aspects of experience had become almost impossible, that the realization of other individuals as living personalities was very uncommon. Nearly all behaviour was the impulsive behaviour of beasts, though of beasts in whose nature were incorporated many tricks derived from a forgotten humanity.</p>
<p>Some men and women there still were who had not permanently sunk to this brutish level. Several communications make it clear that, although the culture and the actual mental capacity of the Last Men were by now so degenerate, some few, a scattered and dwindling aristocracy, were still capable, at least intermittently, of personal love, and even of perplexed loyalty to certain ideals of conduct. The evidence for this lies in such passages as the following, which with its fleeting recovery of the earlier intelligence, and its bewildered groping after a forgotten ecstasy, affords perhaps the right conclusion to this book.</p>
<p>‘She lies motionless in the shallow water. The waves, fingering, the hot sand, recoil steaming. Her flesh, translucent formerly, now is filmed and grey, blind like her filmed eyes. We have taken of each other very deeply. The heat tricks us sometimes into intolerance each of the other’s unyouthful body, unpliant mind; yet each is the other’s needed air for breathing. We have ranged in our work very far apart, she into the astronomical spaces, I into the pre-historical times. But now we will remain together until the end. There is nothing more for us to do but to remember, to tolerate, to find strength together, to keep the spirit clear so long as may be. The many aeons of man! The many million, million selves; ephemeridae, each to itself, the universe’s one quick point, the crux of all cosmical endeavour. And all defeated! The single, the very seldom achieved, spirit! We two, now so confused, participated once in man’s clearest elucidation. It is forgotten. It leaves only a darkness, deepened by blind recollection of past light. Soon, a greater darkness! Man, a moth sucked into a furnace, vanishes; and then the furnace also, since it is but a spark islanded in the wide, the everlasting darkness. If there is a meaning, it is no human meaning. Yet one thing in all this welter stands apart, unassailable, fair, the blind recollection of past light.’</p>
<p>THE END</p>
