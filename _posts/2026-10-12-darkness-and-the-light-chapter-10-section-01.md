<h2>一、和平的成长</h2>
<p>我已经讲过，光明意志取得胜利以后，人类经历了一段爆炸式进步时期，随后渐渐进入一个漫长得多的乌托邦稳定阶段。在这一阶段，物质文明只有一些细微变化，整个时期大概持续了许多世纪。人类的文化生活也是如此：各种小型实验和改进从未停止，却没有发生革命性变化。这个物种最优秀的头脑，都在探索新秩序建立以后为理智与情感打开的那些新视野。至于他们究竟取得了怎样的文化成就，我自然只能说“确有成就”，无法进一步描述。在这一阶段较早的时候，那些新的文化探索，我想还没有真正超出现代人的智力范围；只是我们缺少理解它们所必需的经验背景。一个复活的古埃及人若想理解现代科学，大概也会陷入同样困境。能够确定的是，在整个时期内，文化最活跃的生长点不断从一个领域转向另一个领域。有时它位于纯科学，有时位于科学的工业应用或优生学，有时在某一种艺术里，有时在哲学里，有时在具体人际关系那些极其细微的层面，也有时在宗教情感中。文化领导地位则不断易手：一时属于这个民族或社会阶层，一时又转到另一个。</p>
<p>随着世纪流逝，各种新视野都被越来越充分地探索和利用。黄金时代渐渐变成白银时代；人们不再大规模开垦新土地，而是对那片已经反复收获的人类经验土壤进行精细而密集的耕作。只有平均智力和高端智力虽然缓慢却稳定的提高，才使文化免于停滞，因为人们因此能够在熟悉的土地上挖得越来越深。</p>
<p>偶尔，会有某个卓越头脑在特别富于刺激性的环境里，对某一文化领域发动一场小规模革命；这场革命产生的结果，足够那些原创性较弱的人继续进行几十年更细小的探索。但总的说来，由于社会条件长期稳定，文化越来越具有传统性。</p>
<p>在这一整个时期，文化发展的主要意义为全体人类所共同理解。民族之间、阶级之间、职业之间、政党之间当然仍不断出现各种紧张和冲突，但所有冲突都从属于一个普遍得到承认的前提：人类已经构成世界共同体。战争与革命从来不会真正进入人们的考虑范围。个人交往领域也大体如此。人与人之间的竞争和冲突并不比我们少，但人们很少允许这些矛盾严重妨碍对公共事业的合作。出于报复心的迫害几乎无人知晓。</p>
<p>对于生活在一个异常动荡时代的我们而言，很难想象这个未来世界那种温和的幸福与从容的进步。每个人都能确保自己的个性获得表达，同时每个人又都感到自己在那项伟大的共同事业中负有责任——发展人的能力，完善整个人类，使这个物种成为精神越来越精美的容器。</p>
</section>
<section data-story-lang="en" class="stapledon-body" lang="en" hidden>
<h2>i. PEACEFUL GROWTH</h2>
<p>IHAVE TOLD how, after the victory of the will for the light, there followed a period of explosive progress which gradually gave place to a much longer phase of Utopian stability. This phase, in which material civilization changed only in minor ways, must have lasted for many centuries. In the cultural life of the race also, though minor experiments and advances were constantly being made, no revolutionary changes occurred. The best minds of the race were busy exploring the new vistas which had been opened up for intellect and feeling by the founding of the new order. Of these cultural achievements naturally I can say no more than that achievement did occur. In the earlier part of this phase the new cultural ventures were not, I think, beyond the range of our contemporary human intelligence, but we have not the necessary background of experience to comprehend them. As well might a resuscitated ancient Egyptian understand modern science. Suffice it that throughout this period the growing point of culture kept shifting from one field to another. At one time it lay in pure science, at another in the application of science to industry or eugenics, at another in one or other of the arts, or in philosophy, or in the minutiae of concrete personal relations, or in religious feeling. Cultural leadership would pass now to one people or one social class, now to another.</p>
<p>As the centuries passed, the various new vistas became more and more fully explored and exploited. The golden age gave place to a silver age devoted to minute intensive cultivation of the heavily cropped ground of human experience. Only the steady though slow rise in average and superior intelligence prevented stagnation by making it possible to dig more thoroughly into the familiar soil.</p>
<p>Occasionally some outstanding mind in peculiarly stimulating circumstances would cause a minor revolution in some branch of culture, the consequences of which might afford to less original workers decades of minuter exploration. But in the main, since social circumstances remained stable, culture became more and more traditional.</p>
<p>Throughout this period the main purport of cultural development was grasped by every member of the race. And though all kinds of strains and conflicts occurred between peoples, between classes, vocations, political parties, these conflicts were subordinated to the universal acceptance of world-community. Wars and revolutions were never contemplated. Similarly in the sphere of personal contacts, though rivalries and conflicts were no less common than with us, they were seldom permitted to interfere seriously with co-operation in the public cause. Vindictive persecution was almost unknown.</p>
<p>It is difficult for us, who live in an exceptionally tumultuous age, to conceive of the bland happiness and leisurely progress of this future world. All men were assured of personal expression, and all were blessed with a sense of responsibility within the great common enterprise, the development of the capacity of man, the perfecting of the human race to become an ever finer vessel of the spirit.</p>
</section>
