<h2>i. 通往过去之门</h2>
<p>种族觉醒结束后，我怀着一种压倒一切的感受——存在本身既神秘，又拥有令人敬畏的美——匆匆赶往北极的一处聚居地，我们称那里为“过去之门”。这次我乘飞行艇前往，很快便抵达冰原；到中午时，标志着目的地的高塔已像一排硬毛，竖在地平线上。我已经遇见许多其他飞行艇，以及不用艇、直接飞行的人，他们也都朝同一个目标前进。没过多久我便抵达那里，把自己的小艇停进指定机库，然后走进与我有关的那座高塔宽大的白色入口。我向几名同事打过招呼，穿过门厅，进入众多升降机中的一部。几分钟内，我已经下降到地下一千英尺。随后我沿着一条高大明亮的走廊前进，走廊两侧每隔一百码便有一扇门。我打开其中一扇。里面是一间舒适的房间，有椅子、桌子、橱柜和床。房间另一端是一扇从天花板一直延伸到地面的窗户，窗外竟是一座阳光灿烂、赏心悦目的花园。</p>
<p>你们一定会问：地下一千英尺，怎么可能有这种景象？如果你真的走进花园，就会看见明亮的蓝色“天空”上燃着一轮人工太阳，还会感到一阵清爽微风，从灌木之间吹来。</p>
<p>地下众多走廊里，每一扇门后面都有类似的房间与花园，只在细节上依照住客的喜好有所不同。我们这些探索过去的人，正是在这样的寓所里进行那些奇异冒险。你们也许又会问：为什么偏偏在北极？又为什么必须深入地下？因为我们的工作要求心灵与同时代的人类世界彻底隔绝；只有来到遥远地区，并藏到地表以下，我们才可能摆脱整个世界人口的心灵感应影响。我们的心灵感应交流，本质上依赖以太振动从一个大脑传到另一个大脑，并由后者接收。</p>
<p>平时，我们会严格控制自己的接收能力，因此只有在愿意的时候才接收信息。但在那种作为过去探索媒介的特殊恍惚状态中，这种控制会失效。于是，探索者如果没有被隔离，他的心灵就会像暴雨倾泻下的一池水一样，被无数信号抽打得翻腾不休。</p>
<p>进入房间、关上门后，我先走进自己的花园，来回踱了一阵，为即将开始的冒险调整心境。随后我从房内拖出一个抽屉，里面装满了卷成卷、布满显微级微小符号的带子；在我的世界里，它们取代了书籍。正常情况下，这些小卷带的使用方式，是有规律地中断某种特殊仪器发出的辐射。辐射穿入“读者”的大脑，再由脑内的心灵感应器官直接解译。不过，在这些地下深层设施中，除了正常太阳波长之外，不允许存在任何其他辐射，因此我们这些探索者必须学会借助显微镜，真正用眼睛去“阅读”那些卷带。</p>
<p>接下来许多个小时，我躺在阳光下的草坪上，阅读自己即将造访的时代资料。过了一阵，我回到房间，按下墙上的一个按钮。一只托盘随即出现，上面放着令人食欲大开的饭菜。吃完并让托盘退走之后，我又回到花园继续学习。大约相当于十天地球时间的一段时期里，我一直如此生活：学习、沉思，偶尔进食，却完全不睡觉。有时我会中断学习，照料花园；也常常跳进灌木间的小池塘游上一会儿。但绝大多数时间，我只是浏览那些“书”，或翻阅自己从前关于过去的记录。</p>
<p>到了准备期后段，我已经困得近乎绝望。为了保持清醒，我不得不越来越频繁地来回走动，也越来越常跳进池塘。出发的时候快到了。我把那张小床从房间拖进花园，安置妥当，准备让它承接我的身体。随后我按下另一个按钮，让白昼缓慢变成黑夜。我最后游了一次，又最后走了一圈，然后踉踉跄跄来到床边，在暮色中躺下。</p>
<p>但即便到了这时，我仍不能睡。我必须把注意力集中在自己反复起伏的呼吸上，同时思考时间本身的性质。这个过程要持续大约三十小时，中间不能吃东西、不能睡觉，也不能有任何休息。接近结束时，如果你看见我，大概会说我终于正在睡着；再过一会儿，你会断定我已经陷入极深的睡眠。从某种意义上说确实如此；只是我脑中有一个平时沉睡的器官，此刻反而高度活跃，并准备在大脑通过深睡得到恢复之后，接管我的整个身体。经过两天左右的无意识状态，我确实醒来了——但醒来面对的已经不是熟悉的环境。</p>
<p>从此以后，我的身体可能连续数月、数年、甚至数十年都几乎一动不动地躺在床上，只会周期性起身，完成进食、饮水和排泄等自然功能。它也常常会连续几个小时在花园里走动，或跳进池塘洗浴。但这些活动全都在彻底心不在焉的状态下进行；这时即使有人对我说话，我也不会察觉。因为我大脑的高级中枢已经完全被“过去”占据。任何旁观者如果看见我完成这些例行活动，都会觉得我像一个梦游者。</p>
<p>无论是在这些活动中，还是在漫长的卧床静止期里，观察者都会看见：我的面部，有时甚至整个身体，都持续受到情绪与思想影响。因为在整个恍惚过程中，我的大脑当然正在经历遥远过去里一连串事件，而全身都会依照我正常的情绪反应对那些经验作出回应。于是，在旁观者眼里，我仿佛正在睡眠和做梦；只不过，我的神情会比普通梦中人的神情明确、系统得多。</p>
<p>这里我要顺便提一个具有哲学意味的事实。恍惚状态持续多久，与被观察的过去事件本身持续多久完全没有关系。比如，我可以在这座舒适的“囚室”里躺上一年，而在这一年中观察过去许多年的事件、许多千年的事件，甚至整个人类历史。恍惚持续的长度，只取决于所观察材料的复杂程度。举例说，我可能花整整一年，去观察数量极大的、彼此同时发生，而实际只持续几分钟的事件；也可能用远比某个个体自己亲身意识到的程度更细致的方式，观察他完整的一生；又或者，我可以飞快扫过整个时代，只追踪其中一条极其简单的变化线索。总之，恍惚状态持续多久，只取决于大脑吸收材料的能力，而与所观察事件实际占据多少时间无关。</p>
<p>当搜集到的材料已经多到接近一次能够方便掌握的上限时，探索者会逐步把注意力从过去撤回。随后，他会陷入一场不受打扰的睡眠，可能连续持续数周。在这个阶段，他的身体不再出现主恍惚期所特有的、永无休止的情绪表情，而是一动不动，仿佛被击昏。最后，探索者的注意力开始重新凝聚，再次回到自己的身体以及周围环境。他醒来以后，会先躺一阵，被动接受新出现的视觉印象。</p>
<p>此后，他会在自己的寓所里继续生活数月或数年，把经历先记录成粗略笔记，以待日后整理。有时，记录完成后，他会选择不作休息，立刻再次返回过去搜集更多材料；有时，他会离开房间，与其他工作者商讨；还有些时候，他会决定暂时放下工作，回到同时代世界中恢复精力。</p>
</section>

<h2>i. THE PORTAL TO THE PAST</h2>
<p>IT WAS WITH an overwhelming sense of the mystery and formidable beauty of existence that I hastened, after the racial awakening, toward that Arctic settlement which we call the Portal to the Past. Travelling now by flying-boat, I soon reached the icefields; and by noon the great towers which marked my destination stood like bristles on the horizon. Already I encountered many other boats and boatless flyers, bound for the same goal. Presently I arrived, docked my little vessel in her appointed garage, and entered the great white gate of the particular tower which concerned me. Greeting some of my colleagues, I passed through the entrance hall and stepped into one of the many lifts. In a few moments I had sunk a thousand feet below the ground. I then proceeded along a lofty and well-lit passage, in the walls of which were doors, at intervals of a hundred yards. One of these I opened. Within was a comfortable room, in which were chairs, a table, cupboards, and a bed. At the far side of the room was a window, opening from ceiling to ground, and beyond it a delightful sunny garden. How could this be, you wonder, a thousand feet below ground? Had you walked out into the garden, you would have seen that in the luminous blue ceiling there blazed an artificial sun, and you would have felt a refreshing breeze, which issued from among the bushes.</p>
<p>Behind every one of the doors in the many underground passages you would have found similar rooms and gardens, varying only in detail according to the tastes of their occupiers. Such are the apartments where we explorers of the past undertake our strange adventures. You ask, why in the Arctic, why underground? Our work demands that the mind shall be isolated from the contemporary human world; only in a remote land, and beneath its surface, can we escape the telepathic influence of the world-population. Our telepathic intercourse is based on the transmission and reception of ethereal vibrations from brain to brain.</p>
<p>Normally our powers of reception are kept under strict control, so that we receive messages only when we will. But, in the peculiar trance which is our medium for exploring the past, this control fails; so that, unless the explorer is isolated, his mind is lashed like a pond in which torrential rain is falling.</p>
<p>When I had entered my room and shut my door, I went out into my garden and walked up and down for a while, preparing my mind for its adventure. I then brought out from the room a drawer full of rolls of microscopically figured tape, which in my world take the place of books. In normal circumstances these little rolls work by systematically interrupting the radiation of a special instrument. This radiation penetrates the brain of the’ reader’, and is interpreted telepathically by the telepathic organ of his brain. But since in the catacombs no radiation other than the normal solar wave-lengths is permitted, we explorers have to learn actually to read the rolls, with the aid of a microscope.</p>
<p>I now spent many hours lying on the turf in the sunshine, reading about the epoch which I was to visit. After a while I re-entered the room and pressed a button in the wall. A tray appeared, bearing an appetizing meal. When I had finished this, and dismissed the tray, I returned to the garden and my studies. For a period equivalent to about ten days I lived in this manner, studying, meditating, eating occasionally, but sleeping not at all. Sometimes I would break off my studies to tend my garden. Frequently I would swim about in the pond among the bushes. But for the most part I merely browsed on my books or on my own records of the past. Toward the end of this period of preparation, I grew desperately sleepy. To keep myself awake, I had to walk up and down more and more, and my dips in the pond became more frequent. It was almost time to be gone. I drew my little bed out into the garden and made it ready to receive me. I pressed another button, which would make day slowly become night. I took a final dip and a final walk, then staggered to my bed and lay down in the twilight.</p>
<p>But even yet I must not sleep. Instead, I concentrated my attention on the recurrent rise and fall of my own breathing, and on the nature of time. This process I had to maintain for about thirty hours, without food or sleep or any respite. Toward the end of this period, had you seen me, you would have said that I was at last falling asleep, and finally you would have declared me to be in a very profound slumber. And so in a manner I was, save that a single organ in my brain, usually dormant, was now intensively active, and preparing to take possession of my whole body as soon as my brain should have been refreshed by its deep sleep. After a couple of days of unconsciousness I did indeed wake, but not to the familiar surroundings.</p>
<p>For months, even years or decades, my body might now remain inert upon its bed, save for periodic risings to perform its natural functions of eating, drinking and excretion. Frequently also it would walk for hours at a time in the garden or bathe in the pond. But these activities would be carried out in complete abstraction, and had anyone spoken to me at these times, I should have been unaware. For the higher centres of my brain were wholly possessed by the past. To anyone watching me during these routine activities, I should have appeared as a sleep-walker. At these times, no less than during the long periods of quiescence on my bed, the observer would have seen that my face, and sometimes my whole body, was constantly influenced by emotion and thought. For during the whole trance, of course, my brain would be experiencing sequences of events in the remote past, and my whole body would respond to these experiences with my normal emotional reactions. Thus to the observer I should appear to be asleep and dreaming, save that my expressions would be far more definite and systematic than those of a dreamer.</p>
<p>Here I will mention one point of philosophical interest. The duration of the trance has no relation to the duration of the past events observed. Thus I might lie in my comfortable prison for a year, and in that time I might observe many years of past events, or many thousands of Years, or even the whole span of man’s history. The length of the trance depends only on the complexity of the matter observed. I might for instance spend a year in observing an immense number of simultaneous events which took only a few minutes to occur. Or I might cover the whole life of one individual in far more detail than was afforded by his own consciousness of his life story. Or I might sweep through whole epochs, tracing out only some one simple thread of change. In fact the length of the trance depends simply on the brain’s capacity to assimilate, not upon the actual duration of the events observed.</p>
<p>When as much material has been gathered as can be conveniently grasped, the explorer gradually withdraws his attention from the past. He then sinks into an undisturbed sleep which may last for several weeks. During this phase his body shows none of that ceaseless emotional expression which is characteristic of the main trance. It lies inert, as though stunned. Finally the explorer’s attention begins to concentrate itself again, and to revert once more to his own body and its surroundings. He wakes, and lies for a while passively accepting the new visual impressions. He then lives in his apartments for months or years recording his experiences in rough notes, to be organized at a later date. Sometimes, when this process is finished, he chooses to return at once, without respite, to the past for further data. Sometimes he leaves his room to confer with other workers. Sometimes he decides to drop his work and return to the contemporary world for refreshment.</p>
