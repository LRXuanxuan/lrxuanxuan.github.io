<h2>第十八章</h2>
<p>转换完成以后，卡莫迪先清点了一下自己。简单盘点的结果表明，他仍然拥有四肢、一个身体、一个脑袋和一个心智。当然，最后的清算结果还没出来，不过至少表面上看，他的零件都在。他还注意到奖品也仍然跟着自己；尽管奖品又照例发生了形态变化，他还是不知怎么一眼就认出来了。这一次，它从一个侏儒变成了一支做工很差的长笛。</p>
<p>“目前为止，还算顺利。”卡莫迪对着谁也不是地说道。随后他开始观察周围环境。</p>
<p>“不太顺利。”他立刻补充。</p>
<p>他原本已经做好抵达“错误地球”的心理准备，却没想到能错到这种程度。</p>
<p>他正站在一片沼泽边缘的湿软土地上。停滞的褐色水面升起带瘴气的蒸汽。周围有宽叶蕨类、低矮的细叶灌木、顶部蓬松的棕榈，以及孤零零的一棵山茱萸。空气温暖得像血，而且浓重地充满繁殖与腐败的气味。</p>
<p>“也许我是在佛罗里达。”卡莫迪满怀希望地说。</p>
<p>“恐怕不是。”奖品——或者说那支长笛——用低沉悦耳、但颤音多得过分的嗓音说道。</p>
<p>卡莫迪瞪着奖品。“你怎么还能说话？”他追问。</p>
<p>“我当铜坩埚的时候，你怎么没问？”奖品反问。“不过你真想知道的话，我就告诉你。就在这里，我的吹口里面固定着一只二氧化碳气瓶。它暂时替代肺，不过使用时间有限。剩下的自然一目了然。”</p>
<p>对卡莫迪来说一点也不一目了然。不过他眼下有更重要的问题。“我在哪里？”</p>
<p>“<strong>我们</strong>，”奖品说，“现在位于地球。这片湿乎乎的土地，在你的时代将会成为纽约州斯卡斯代尔镇。”它窃笑起来。“我建议你趁现在房地产价格低迷，赶紧买点地。”</p>
<p>“这里他妈怎么看都不像斯卡斯代尔。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“当然不像。先把‘哪一个（Which）’的问题放到一边，我们至少已经看出来，‘何时（When）’完全错了。”</p>
<p>“那……我们现在到底是什么时候？”</p>
<p>“好问题。”奖品回答。“不过我只能给你一个近似而且附带许多保留条件的答案。很明显，我们处在显生宙；单是这个范围，就覆盖了地球地质时间的六分之一。这个容易。可我们究竟位于显生宙的哪一部分——古生代，还是中生代？这里我只能猜。单从气候判断，我可以排除整个古生代，只留下二叠纪末期这个勉强可能的选项。不过等等，现在连它也能排除了！看头顶，你右边！”</p>
<p>卡莫迪抬头，看见一只形状古怪的鸟笨拙地拍着翅膀，朝远处飞去。</p>
<p>“毫无疑问，是始祖鸟。”奖品说。“看它的羽毛如何从中轴两侧呈羽状分开，一眼就能认出来。大多数科学家认为它属于晚侏罗世和白垩纪，反正绝不可能早于三叠纪。所以整个古生代都可以排除；我们确定处于中生代。”</p>
<p>“那已经倒退得很远了，对吧？”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“非常远。”奖品同意。“不过我们还能更精确一点。我想我们可以锁定自己处在中生代的哪一部分。让我想想。”它想了一会儿。“对，我觉得有答案了。不是三叠纪！恐怕那片沼泽是个假线索。不过，你左脚边那株被子植物开花植物，在地质时期上指向一个毫不含糊的方向。而且这还不是唯一证据。你看见前面那棵山茱萸了吗？很好，转过身，你还会看见两棵杨树和一棵无花果树，混在一小片针叶树中。很有意义，对吧？可你有没有注意到最重要的细节——在你的时代太过寻常，以至于你很可能忽略？我说的是草。这里到处都是。直到侏罗纪还根本没有草！只有蕨类和本内苏铁类！所以事情定了，卡莫迪！我敢把毕生积蓄押上！我们现在就在白垩纪，而且大概距离它的上界不算太远！”</p>
<p>卡莫迪对地球各个地质时期只剩下极其模糊的记忆。“白垩纪。”他说。“离我的时代有多远？”</p>
<p>“哦，大概一亿年，上下差几百万吧。”奖品说。“白垩纪本身持续了七千万年。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪完全不需要适应这个概念；他压根就没有尝试适应。他问奖品：“这些地质学知识你从哪儿学来的？”</p>
<p>“你觉得呢？”奖品精神十足地回答。“我学的。我一想，既然我们要去地球，最好先了解一下那个地方。事实证明，我学得他妈太对了。要不是有我，你现在还会在这里跌跌撞撞地找迈阿密海滩，最后八成让一只异特龙给吃掉。”</p>
<p>“让什么东西吃掉？”</p>
<p>“我说的是，”奖品说道，“蜥臀目里相貌比较丑陋的一员。蜥臀目有一个分支——蜥脚类——最终发展出了著名的雷龙。”</p>
<p>“你是说，这里有恐龙？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“我是说，”奖品用伴奏声部般的语调说道，“这里就是原装正品、独一无二的恐龙城；同时，我也想借此机会欢迎你来到巨型爬行动物的时代。”</p>
<p>卡莫迪发出一声无法辨认的声音。他注意到左边有什么东西在动，于是转过头。</p>
<p>他看见了一只恐龙。</p>
<p>那东西看起来大约二十英尺高，从鼻尖到尾端也许足有五十英尺。它用两条后腿直立行走，身体呈石板蓝色，正快速朝卡莫迪大步走来。</p>
<p>“那是霸王龙吗？”卡莫迪问。</p>
<p>“没错。”奖品说。“<strong>Tyrannosaurus rex</strong>，蜥臀目里最受敬重的成员。你可以看到，它是货真价实的恐齿型，光上门齿就有半英尺长。朝我们走来的这个小伙子，体重至少在九吨以上。”</p>
<p>“而且它吃肉。”卡莫迪说。</p>
<p>“当然。我个人认为，这一时期的霸王龙和其他肉食龙类，主要以性情温和、分布广泛的鸭嘴龙为食。不过这只是我个人特别偏爱的理论。”</p>
<p>巨兽已经离他们不到五十英尺。平坦泥泞的沼泽地上没有任何掩护，没有树可爬，没有洞穴可以钻。卡莫迪问：“我该怎么办？”</p>
<p>“你必须立刻变成一株植物！”奖品急切地说。</p>
<p>“可我不会！”</p>
<p>“你不会？那你的情况确实严重。让我想想：你不会飞，也不会打洞；而且我敢十比一跟你赌，你肯定跑不过它。嗯，这下麻烦了。”</p>
<p>“所以我到底该怎么办？”</p>
<p>“这个嘛，既然是这种情况，我觉得你最好对整件事采取斯多葛主义态度。我可以给你背一段爱比克泰德。如果有帮助，我们还可以一起唱首赞美诗。”</p>
<p>“去你的赞美诗！我想活着离开这里！”</p>
<p>那支长笛已经开始吹奏《更近我主》。卡莫迪握紧双拳。霸王龙如今已经到了他正前方，高高耸在头顶，活像一座长出血肉、突然活过来的起重架。</p>
<p>它张开了那张令人胆寒的大嘴。</p>
</section>

<p><b>CHAPTER 18</b></p>
<p>With the transition completed, Carmody took stock of himself. A brief inventory convinced him that he still had all four limbs, one body, one head, and one mind. Final returns were not in, of course, but he did seem to be all there. He also noted that he still had the Prize, which was somehow recognizable even though it had undergone its usual metamorphosis. This time it had changed from a dwarf into a badly constructed flute.</p>
<p>‘So far, so good,’ Carmody said to nobody in particular. He now surveyed his surroundings.</p>
<p>‘Not so good,’ he said at once. He had been prepared to arrive at the wrong Earth, but he hadn’t expected it would be quite so wrong as this.</p>
<p>He was standing on marshy ground at the edge of a swamp. Miasmic vapours rose from the stagnant brown waters. There were broad-leaved ferns, and low, thin-leaved shrubs, and bushy-headed palms, and a single dogwood tree. The air was blood-warm and heavily laden with odours of fertility and decay.</p>
<p>‘Maybe I’m in Florida,’ Carmody said hopefully.</p>
<p>‘Afraid not,’ said the Prize, or the flute, speaking in a low melodious voice but with an excess of vibrato.</p>
<p>Carmody glared at the Prize. ‘How come you can speak?’ he demanded.</p>
<p>‘How come you didn’t ask me that when I was a cauldron?’ the Prize replied. ‘But I’ll tell you, if you really want to know. Affixed up here, just inside my mouthpiece, is a CO<sub>2</sub> cartridge. That serves me in place of lungs, though for a limited time only. The rest is obvious.’</p>
<p>It wasn’t obvious to Carmody. But he had more important matters on his mind. He asked, ‘Where am I?’</p>
<p>‘<i>We</i>,’ said the Prize, ‘are on the planet Earth. This moist bit of ground upon which we are standing will become, in your day, the township of Scarsdale, New York.’ He snickered. ‘I suggest you buy property now, while the real-estate values are depressed.’</p>
<p>‘It sure as hell doesn’t look like Scarsdale,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Of course not. Leaving aside for a moment the question of <i>Whichness,</i> we can see that the <i>Whenness</i> is all wrong.’</p>
<p>‘Well … <i>When</i> are we?’</p>
<p>‘A good question,’ the Prize replied, ‘but one to which I can only make an approximate and highly qualified reply. Obviously enough, we are in the Phanerozoic Aeon, which in itself covers one sixth of Earth’s geological time. Easy enough; but what <i>part</i> of the Phanerozoic are we in, the Palaeozoic or the Mesozoic Era? Here I must hazard a guess. Just on the basis of climate, I rule out all of the Palaeozoic except, just possibly, the end of the Permian Period. But wait, now I can rule that out, too! Look, overhead and to your right!’</p>
<p>Carmody looked and saw an oddly shaped bird flapping awkwardly into the distance.</p>
<p>‘Definitely an Archaeopteryx,’ the Prize said. ‘You could tell at once from the way its feathers diverged pinnately from its axis. Most scientists consider it a creature of the upper Jurassic and the Cretaceous Periods, but certainly not older than the Triassic. So we can rule out the entire Palaeozoic; we are definitely in the Mesozoic Era.’</p>
<p>‘That’s pretty far back, huh?’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Quite far,’ the Prize agreed. ‘But we can do better than that. I think we can pinpoint what part of the Mesozoic we are in. Let me think for a bit.’ He thought for a bit. ‘Yes, I think I have it. <i>Not</i> the Triassic! That swamp is a false clue, I fear. However, the angiospermous flowering plant near your left foot points an unmistakable direction, periodwise. Nor does it constitute our sole evidence. You noticed the dogwood tree in front of you? Well, turn around and you will see two poplars and a fig tree in the midst of a small group of conifers. Significant, eh? But did you notice the most important detail of all, so commonplace in your time that you would be apt to overlook it? I refer to grass, which we see here in abundance. There was no grass as late as Jurassic times! Just ferns and cycadeoids! And that decides it, Carmody! I’d wager my life savings on it! We are in the Cretaceous Period, and probably not far from its upper limit!’</p>
<p>Carmody had only the vaguest remembrance of the geological periods of the Earth. ‘Cretaceous,’ he said. ‘How far is that from my time?’</p>
<p>‘Oh, about a hundred million years, give or take a few million,’ the Prize said. ‘The Cretaceous age lasted for seventy million years.’</p>
<p>Carmody had no difficulty in adjusting to this concept; he never even tried. He said to the Prize, ‘How did you learn all of this geology stuff?’</p>
<p>‘How do you think?’ the Prize replied spiritedly. ‘I studied. I figured, since we were going to Earth, I’d better find out something about the place. And it’s a damned good thing I did. If it weren’t for me, you’d be stumbling around here looking for Miami Beach, and you’d have probably ended up being eaten by an allosaurus.’</p>
<p>‘Eaten by a who?’</p>
<p>‘I refer,’ the Prize said, ‘to one of the uglier members of the order Saurischia, an offshoot of which – the sauropoda – culminated in the renowned brontosaurus.’</p>
<p>‘You mean to tell me there are dinosaurs here?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘I mean to tell you,’ the Prize said in obbligato, ‘that this is the one and original Dinosaursville, and I would also like to take this opportunity to welcome you to the Age of the Giant Reptiles.’</p>
<p>Carmody made an incoherent noise. He noticed a movement to his left and turned. He saw a dinosaur. It looked about twenty feet high, and might have stretched fifty feet from nose to tail. It stood erect on its hind legs. It was coloured slate-blue, and it was striding rapidly towards Carmody.</p>
<p>‘Is that a tyrannosaurus?’ Carmody asked.</p>
<p>‘Yes, it is,’ the Prize said. ‘<i>Tyrannosaurus rex,</i> most highly respected of the saurischians. A true deinodon, you will note, its upper incisors running half a foot in length. This young chap coming towards us must weigh upwards of nine tons.’</p>
<p>‘And he eats meat,’ Carmody said.</p>
<p>‘Yes, of course. I personally think that tyrannosaurus and other carnosaurs of this period feasted mainly on the inoffensive and widely distributed hadrosaurs. But that is only my own pet theory.’</p>
<p>The giant creature was less than fifty feet away. There was no refuge on the flat, marshy land, no place to climb, no cave to scuttle into. Carmody said, ‘What should I do?’</p>
<p>‘You must change into a plant at once!’ the Prize said urgently.</p>
<p>‘But I can’t!’</p>
<p>‘You can’t? Then your situation is serious indeed. Let me see, you can’t fly or burrow, and I’d wager ten to one you’d never outrun him. Hmm, this becomes difficult.’</p>
<p>‘So what do I do?’</p>
<p>‘Well, under the circumstances, I think you should be stoical about the whole thing. I could quote Epictetus to you. And we could sing a hymn together if that would help.’</p>
<p>‘Damn your hymns! I want to get out of this!’</p>
<p>The flute had already begun to play ‘Nearer My God to Thee.’ Carmody clenched his fists. The tyrannosaurus was now directly in front of him, towering overhead like a fleshed-out and animate derrick. It opened its awesome mouth.</p>
