<h3>第十六章</h3>
<h3>普拉克茜被征调</h3>
<p>接下来的几个月里，我经常到坦-伊-沃尔度周末。我见天狼星越多，就越被他吸引。当然，我们之间始终潜伏着一个双方都心知肚明的矛盾——普拉克茜。不过，我们三个人都决心设法建立一种能够维持的关系；而我同天狼星之间真正的相互好感，也缓解了这种紧张。当然，潜在冲突有时仍会公开爆发；这种时候，只有靠其中一方表现出近乎英雄式的克制与体谅，才能保住彼此的友好。但一点一点地，正如天狼星自己所说，我们身上那种相同的精神，终于战胜了天性和个人利益上的差异。假如我不曾亲身经历过这种紧密纠缠的三人关系，我根本不会相信它可能存在。甚至可以说，如果我从一开始对普拉克茜的爱就带着强烈占有欲，我大概也无法承担自己在其中的角色；而我之所以没有如此，部分正因为我自己也像天狼星以犬类方式那样，曾经在别处爱过别人。</p>
<p>当地有一小群人数不多却很活跃的人敌视我们，这反而把我们三个人拉得更近。欧文·劳埃德-托马斯牧师已经几次从讲坛上发出含蓄警告。另一些牧师也忍不住加入进来；也许他们在无意识中明白，“女孩与狗之间反自然的罪恶”这个题目很可能扩大自己的会众，于是便抵挡不住利用它的诱惑。结果，一小批人数渐增、在某种意义上情感受挫、急需找一个对象迫害的人，开始把普拉克茜和天狼星当成发泄工具，其性质同纳粹拿犹太人作替罪羊并无二致。邻近农户大多太友善，不容易感染这种自以为正义的仇恨；但在更远的地方，事实上几乎整个北威尔士，关于梅里奥尼斯那间孤僻小屋里这对伴侣的“淫乱”以及所谓叛国活动的流言都在蔓延。普拉克茜收到过一些匿名信，使她非常痛苦。夜里，有人把写给“撒旦猎犬”的纸条钉在门上，其中几封威胁说，如果他不释放那个被施了魔法的姑娘，就要把他枪毙。普格的羊有时会被人弄残。有一只甚至被宰杀后扔在小屋门口。空白墙面上被画满女孩与狗的淫秽图画。一家地方报纸还发表社论，号召居民采取行动。荒原上甚至爆发过一场战斗：凯尔·布莱的狗群对上一伙专程来杀天狼星的青年和狗。幸好那些人没有枪，最后被打得溃败而去。</p>
<p>与此同时，另一类事件也正在威胁我们三人的生活。我已经预计自己很快会被派往海外。这个前景使普拉克茜对我变得更加温柔；而天狼星如果是在装难过，那他的表演也实在逼真。不过，比我离开更糟的是，官方命令普拉克茜本人必须参加某种获准的国家服务。我们原本希望，她作为农场工人能够安静留下，可她的身份实在太不合常规。主管部门无法理解：一个受过大学教育的姑娘，独自同一条狗住在乡间深处，为什么仅仅因为自愿帮邻近农场干活，就可以免除国家服务。起初，办事人员态度还很友好，也愿意尽量用合乎人情的方式解释规定。可就在普拉克茜最有希望获准留下的时候，他们的口气却突然、而且莫名其妙地变了。我怀疑，是当地某个天狼星的敌人把这对奇怪伴侣那些“丑闻缠身、据说还有叛国嫌疑”的事情添油加醋地报告了上去。不管原因究竟是什么，普拉克茜最终得到通知：申诉被驳回。普格强烈请求保留她，却被告知，他完全可以另找一个女农工代替；而普拉克茜则应当被投入更符合她能力的国家服务。普格提出正式雇她做女农工，按官方工资支付薪酬。这安排显然太像临时做出来应付审查的把戏。主管部门越发怀疑，也越发不肯通融。普拉克茜必须二选一：加入某个女性军事组织，或者在文职机关找一份临时工作。她选择了后者，希望能被分到某个已经疏散到兰开夏或北威尔士的大型政府部门。</p>
<p>一想到必须离开，普拉克茜非常痛苦。“这就是我的生活，”她对天狼星说，“至少现在，你就是我的生活。我知道战争重要得可怕，可我就是感觉不到它有多重要。它像一件跟我无关的东西。至少，我感觉不到我留下还是离开会对战争产生什么区别。说真的，我在这里做的工作反而更有用，就算从战争角度看也是这样。简直像是整个人类的手正在越来越用力地反对我们。啊，亲爱的，我的宝贝，没有我给你梳毛、给你洗、替你从脚上拔刺，更别说帮你料理羊群，你要怎么办？”天狼星说：“我会对付过去。而且你虽然有一部分非常不想走，另一部分其实会高兴，因为它想重新彻底做一个人。你也会摆脱这些愚蠢的迫害。”她回答：“哦，是的，我确实有一部分想走。可那一部分并不是真正的我。真正的我，完整而真正的普拉克茜，拼命想继续现在这种生活。那个想走的我，只是一个梦里的自我。唯一能安慰我的，就是也许我离开后，那些迫害你的人会把你放过。”</p>
<p>普拉克茜离开的那一天终于来了。从此天狼星要住到普格夫妇家，而坦-伊-沃尔会一直保留着，等普拉克茜休假时让他们回来住。最后一个早晨，天狼星尽力帮她作临行准备；只要想得起来，他就勇敢地把尾巴高高翘着。村里的汽车快来接她去车站之前，她给两人泡了茶。他们并排坐在炉边地毯上，几乎默默无言地喝着。“我真庆幸，”她说，“在加斯最后那个早晨，我作了当时那个决定。”“我也是，”他答道，“只要它没有把你带得离你的同类太远。”他们听见出租车从大路拐进来时按了一声喇叭，接着汽车挂着最低档轰鸣着爬上山坡。普拉克茜深深叹了口气，说：“我的物种来把我从你身边带走了。”随后，她突然激动地抱住他，把脸埋进他颈间那圈倔强浓密的鬃毛里。天狼星在她怀中扭过身，把头依偎到她胸前。“无论以后发生什么，”他说，“我们已经拥有过这几个月。没有任何东西能把它毁掉。”</p>
<p>出租车停在花园门口，又按了一声喇叭。他们亲吻告别。然后普拉克茜站起来，把头发向后一甩，提起一部分行李，到门口迎接司机。上车后，她从车窗探出身子，伸手摸了摸天狼星，只说了一句：“再见，祝你好运！”他们事先已经约定，分别就到这里为止。天狼星不会陪她去车站。</p>
</section>

<h3><span id="a583"></span>CHAPTER XVI</h3>
<p><span class="t28">D</span><span class="t30">URING </span><span class="t31">THE</span><span class="t30"> </span><span class="t31">NEXT</span><span class="t30"> </span>few months I spent frequent week-ends at Tan-y-Voel. The more I saw of Sirius, the more I was drawn towards him. Always, of course, there was a latent but an acknowledged conflict over Plaxy. All three of us, however, were determined to work out a tolerable relationship, and between Sirius and me the strain was eased by a genuine mutual affection. Sometimes, of course, the latent conflict became overt, and only by heroic tact and restraint on one side or the other could friendliness be maintained. But little by little the identical spirit in each of us, as Sirius himself said, triumphed over the diversity of our natures and our private interests. Had I not actually experienced this close-knit triple relationship I should not have believed it possible. Nor should I, perhaps, have been able to sustain my part in it, had not my love for Plaxy been from the onset unpossessive; owing to the fact that I myself, like Sirius in his canine style, had sometimes loved elsewhere.</p>
<p>The three of us were drawn more closely together by the hostility of a small but active section of the local people. The Rev. Owen Lloyd-Thomas had on several occasions issued veiled warnings from his pulpit. A few other ministers, realizing, perhaps subconsciously, that the theme of the “unnatural vice” of a girl and dog was likely to increase their congregations, could not resist the temptation to use it for that end. The result was that a small but increasing number of persons who were in one way or another emotionally frustrated and in need of an object for persecution were using Plaxy and Sirius for the same sort of purpose as the Nazis had used the Jews. The neighbours were mostly far too friendly to be accessible to this disease of self-righteous hate; but farther afield, in fact throughout North Wales, rumours were spreading both about the vice and the supposed treasonable activities of the couple in the lonely cottage in Merioneth. Plaxy received anonymous letters which caused her much distress. Messages for “Satan’s Hound” were pinned on the door at night, including several threats that unless he released the spellbound girl he would be shot. Pugh’s sheep were sometimes found maimed. One was slaughtered and laid at the cottage door. Obscene drawings of a girl and dog were scrawled on blank walls. A local paper published a leading article calling the population to action. A battle occurred on the moors between the canine inhabitants of Caer Blai and a number of youths and dogs who had come out to do Sirius to death. Fortunately they had no firearms, and they were routed.</p>
<p>Meanwhile events of another type were threatening to change the fortunes of all three of us. I was expecting to be sent abroad very soon. The prospect caused Plaxy to treat me with increased tenderness. And if Sirius’s grief was feigned, the imitation was very convincing. But worse than the prospect of my departure was the official order that Plaxy herself must take up some form of approved national service. We had hoped that she might be allowed to remain in peace as a farm worker, but her position was anomalous. The authorities could not see why a girl with a university training, living alone with a dog in the depths of the country, should be let off merely because she voluntarily helped on a neighbouring farm. The officials, however, were at first friendly, and anxious to interpret the regulations in a human manner. But just when it seemed most probable that Plaxy would be allowed to remain with Sirius, there was a marked and unaccountable change of tone. I suspect that some local enemy of Sirius had been telling tales of the scandalous and reputedly treasonous actions of the strange couple. Anyhow, whatever the cause, Plaxy was told that her appeal was rejected. Pugh put in a strong plea for her retention, but it was pointed out that he could easily find a land-girl to take her place. She could then be used for national service more suited to her capacity. Pugh offered to take on Plaxy herself as a land-girl, paying her the official wage. This was too obviously a put-up job. Authority became increasingly suspicious and uncompromising. Plaxy must either join one of the military organizations for women or take a temporary post in the civil service. She chose the latter, hoping to get herself attached to one of the great government offices evacuated into Lancashire or North Wales.</p>
<p>Plaxy was greatly distressed at the prospect of leaving. “This is my life,” she said to Sirius. “You are my life, for the present, anyhow. The war matters terribly, I know; but I can’t feel that it matters. It feels an irrelevance. At least, I can’t feel that it makes any difference to the war whether I stay or go. Surely I’m doing more useful work here, really, even for the war. It’s as though the hand of man were turning more and more against us. And oh, my dear, my sweet, what will you do without me to brush you and wash you and take thorns out of your feet, to say nothing of helping you with the sheep?” “I shall manage,” he said. “And you, though part of you hates to go, another part will be glad of it, wanting to be entirely human again. And you will be freed from all this silly persecution.” She replied, “Oh, yes, part of me wants to go. But that part of me isn’t really me. The real I, the whole real Plaxy, desperately wants to carry on. The bit of me that wants to go is just a dream self. The only consolation is that perhaps when I am gone the persecutors will leave you alone.”</p>
<p>At last the day came for Plaxy to go. Sirius would henceforth live with the Pughs, but Tan-y-Voel was to be kept ready to receive them whenever Plaxy had her leave. On the last morning Sirius did his best to help her with her final preparations, his tail (when he could remember it) kept bravely up. Before the village car was due to take her to the station she made tea for the two of them. They sat together on the hearth-rug, drinking it almost in silence. “How glad I am,” she said, “that I decided as I did on that last morning at Garth!” “And I,” he answered, “if it has not drawn you away too far from your kind.” They heard the taxi hoot as it turned out of the main road. It roared up the hill in bottom gear. Plaxy sighed deeply and said, “My species has come to take me from you.” Then with sudden passion she clung to him and buried her face in the stubborn mane on his neck. Twisting himself round in her embrace, he snuggled to her breast. “Whatever happens now,” he said, “we have had these months together, nothing can destroy that.”</p>
<p>The taxi stopped at the garden gate and hooted again. They kissed. Then she stood up, tossed back her hair, seized some luggage and met the taxi-man at the door. Seated in the car, she leaned out of the window to touch Sirius, saying only “Good-bye, good luck!” It had been arranged that this should be their parting. He would not go with her to the station.</p>
